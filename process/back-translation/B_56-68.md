# Chapter 56

**The chapter as a whole:** Those who know do not speak, and those who speak do not know. Closing off the openings, blunting the sharp, untangling the knots, softening the light and merging with the dust is named "dark sameness". Whoever reaches it cannot be drawn close or pushed away, benefited or harmed, ennobled or debased, and that is why it is the most valued thing in the world.

### Unit 1
> 知者不言，言者不知。 (*Zhī zhě bù yán, yán zhě bù zhī.* — "Those who know do not speak; those who speak do not know.")
- **Says:** A person who knows does not speak, and a person who speaks does not know. Both halves use 不 (*bù* — "not"), negating an action: speaking in the first half, knowing in the second. 者 (*zhě* — "the one who") turns each verb into a type of person.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** "those who know" and "those who speak", people in general. The thing known or spoken about is unstated.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 言 (*yán* — "speak") can mean speaking in general, or holding forth about *it*, with the object (presumably the Way) left unstated. 知 (*zhī* — "know") is also sometimes read 智 (*zhì* — "the wise").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi reads the knower as following what is so: 因自然也 (*yīn zì rán yě* — "it is following what is so of itself"), and the speaker as making trouble: 造事端也 (*zào shì duān yě* — "it is creating the beginnings of affairs"). Heshang Gong reads it ethically: 知者貴行不貴言也 (*zhī zhě guì xíng bù guì yán yě* — "the one who knows values action and does not value words").

### Unit 2
> 塞其兌，閉其門，挫其銳，解其紛，和其光，同其塵，是謂玄同。 (*Sè qí duì, bì qí mén, cuò qí ruì, jiě qí fēn, hé qí guāng, tóng qí chén, shì wèi xuán tóng.* — "Stop up its openings, shut its gates, blunt its sharpness, untie its tangles, soften its light, be one with its dust: this is called dark sameness.")
- **Says:** Six verb–object phrases, each with 其 (*qí* — "its / one's"): block the openings, shut the gates, blunt the sharp point, undo the tangles, temper the light, join the dust. Doing or being all of this is named 玄同 (*xuán tóng* — "dark / mysterious sameness").
- **Act:** names/defines, through 是謂 (*shì wèi* — "this is called"). The six phrases may be commands or descriptions.
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. Most likely the one who knows, from Unit 1, or "one" in general, acting on their own senses and edges.
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and, continuing from Unit 1 with no connective
- **Open:** (a) The six phrases may be imperatives (advice) or a description of the knower. (b) 其 (*qí* — "its / one's") may be one's own (one's senses, one's sharpness) or that of things in the world. (c) 玄 (*xuán* — "dark") may be "dark / obscure", "mysterious / profound", or, for Heshang Gong, "heaven".
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi reads the first three as inward keeping, 含守質也 (*hán shǒu zhì yě* — "holding in and guarding one's plain substance"), and the rest as removing grounds for rivalry: 除争原也 (*chú zhēng yuán yě* — "removing the source of contention"), and for the light, 無所特顯則物無所偏争也 (*wú suǒ tè xiǎn zé wù wú suǒ piān zhēng yě* — "if nothing is singled out for display, things have nothing to contend over one-sidedly"). His lemma reads 解其分 (*jiě qí fēn* — "untie its divisions"), and his note says other editions have 紛 (*fēn* — "tangles"). Heshang Gong reads 銳 (*ruì* — "sharpness") as desire: 情欲有所銳 (*qíng yù yǒu suǒ ruì* — "where the passions have a sharp point"). He glosses 玄 (*xuán* — "dark") as heaven: 玄天也……是謂與天同道也 (*xuán tiān yě … shì wèi yǔ tiān tóng dào yě* — "玄 means heaven … this is called sharing the same Way as heaven").

### Unit 3
> 故不可得而親，不可得而疏； (*Gù bù kě dé ér qīn, bù kě dé ér shū;* — "Therefore one cannot get and be close [to them], cannot get and be distant;")
- **Says:** The one who has reached this sameness cannot be drawn near and cannot be held at a distance. 不可 (*bù kě* — "cannot"), with 不 (*bù* — "not") negating the possibility.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** The would-be doers are unstated: others, the world. The one done to is the person of dark sameness, also unstated.
- **Force:** can/possible (negated)
- **Link:** therefore, through 故 (*gù* — "therefore")
- **Open:** (a) Others cannot get close to or estranged from this person (passive to them). (b) This person cannot be brought to be partial, intimate with some and distant from others (a description of their own evenness).
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 可得而親則可得而疎也 (*kě dé ér qīn zé kě dé ér shū yě* — "if one could get close [to them], one could also get distant"). Heshang Gong reads it as the person's own disposition: 不以榮譽為樂獨立為哀 (*bù yǐ róng yù wéi lè dú lì wéi āi* — "does not take honor and praise as joy, nor standing alone as grief"). His lemma, and the editions Wang Bi's note cites, add 亦 (*yì* — "also") before the second clause.

### Unit 4
> 不可得而利，不可得而害； (*Bù kě dé ér lì, bù kě dé ér hài;* — "cannot get and benefit [them], cannot get and harm [them];")
- **Says:** This person cannot be benefited and cannot be harmed. Both halves use 不可 (*bù kě* — "cannot"), with 不 (*bù* — "not") negating the possibility.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** As in Unit 3: others, unstated, cannot do this to the person of dark sameness.
- **Force:** can/possible (negated)
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** As in Unit 3: whether this is passive to others or describes the person's own indifference to gain and loss.
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong reads it as the person's own lack of desire: 身不欲富貴口不欲五味 (*shēn bù yù fù guì kǒu bù yù wǔ wèi* — "the body does not want wealth and rank, the mouth does not want the five flavors"), and 不與貪爭利 (*bù yǔ tān zhēng lì* — "does not contend with the greedy for profit").

### Unit 5
> 不可得而貴，不可得而賤。 (*Bù kě dé ér guì, bù kě dé ér jiàn.* — "cannot get and ennoble [them], cannot get and debase [them].")
- **Says:** This person cannot be raised to honor and cannot be lowered to baseness. 不可 (*bù kě* — "cannot"), with 不 (*bù* — "not") negating the possibility.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** As in Unit 3.
- **Force:** can/possible (negated)
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** As in Unit 3.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 可得而貴則可得而賤也 (*kě dé ér guì zé kě dé ér jiàn yě* — "if one could be ennobled, one could be debased"). Heshang Gong: 不以乗權故驕不以失志故屈 (*bù yǐ chéng quán gù jiāo, bù yǐ shī zhì gù qū* — "is not proud for riding power, does not bend for failing in their aims").

### Unit 6
> 故為天下貴。 (*Gù wéi tiān xià guì.* — "Therefore [they] are the most valued in all under heaven.")
- **Says:** Because of all this, this person (or this sameness) is what the whole world holds most precious.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. Most likely the person of dark sameness; possibly the sameness itself.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** therefore, through 故 (*gù* — "therefore")
- **Open:** 為天下貴 (*wéi tiān xià guì* — "is the world's valued") may mean "is the most precious thing in the world" or "is honored by the world". It stands in pointed tension with Unit 5, which says this person cannot be ennobled.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 無物可以加之也 (*wú wù kě yǐ jiā zhī yě* — "there is nothing that can be placed above it").

# Chapter 57

**The chapter as a whole:** A state is governed by the straight and troops are used by the unexpected, but the world is won only by having no business with it. The speaker gives the evidence: more prohibitions, sharp tools, cunning and laws bring more poverty, confusion, strange things and thieves. The sage then says that when they do nothing, love stillness, have no undertakings and have no desires, the people transform, straighten, prosper and grow plain of themselves.

### Unit 1
> 以正治國，以奇用兵，以無事取天下。 (*Yǐ zhèng zhì guó, yǐ qí yòng bīng, yǐ wú shì qǔ tiān xià.* — "By the straight govern a state; by the unexpected use troops; by having no affairs take all under heaven.")
- **Says:** Three parallel maxims. A state is governed by means of 正 (*zhèng* — "the straight / correct / regular"). Troops are used by means of 奇 (*qí* — "the irregular / unexpected / crooked"). The world is taken by means of 無事 (*wú shì* — "no affairs / no undertakings"). In the third, 無 (*wú* — "no / without") negates the existence of undertakings.
- **Act:** asserts. The maxims may also be read as advice.
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. A ruler, or one who would rule.
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** (a) All three are endorsed as the right means in their own spheres. (b) The first two are conventional wisdom, set up as a foil for the third, which alone wins the world. 取 (*qǔ* — "take") may mean "seize" or "win over / gain".
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi takes the first two as inferior: 以道治國則國平以正治國則竒正起也 (*yǐ dào zhì guó zé guó píng, yǐ zhèng zhì guó zé qí zhèng qǐ yě* — "govern a state by the Way and the state is at peace; govern it by correctness and then the crooked and the correct both arise"). Heshang Gong reads 以 (*yǐ* — "by") as 至 (*zhì* — "arrive") and makes it heaven's appointment: 天使正身之人使至有國也 (*tiān shǐ zhèng shēn zhī rén shǐ zhì yǒu guó yě* — "heaven lets a person of upright self come to possess a state"). He glosses 竒詐也 (*qí zhà yě* — "奇 means deceit").

### Unit 2
> 吾何以知其然哉？以此： (*Wú hé yǐ zhī qí rán zāi? Yǐ cǐ:* — "How do I know it is so? By this:")
- **Says:** The speaker asks how they know that the world is won through no-affairs, and answers that they know it by what follows.
- **Act:** asks (real question), answered at once
- **Who acts / of whom:** 吾 (*wú* — "I"), the speaker
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none. It turns to evidence for Unit 1.
- **Open:** (a) 此 (*cǐ* — "this") points forward to the evidence that follows. (b) Heshang Gong reads it as "the present": 此今也……以今日所見知 (*cǐ jīn yě … yǐ jīn rì suǒ jiàn zhī* — "此 means 'now' … [I] know by what is seen today"). 其然 (*qí rán* — "its being so") may refer to the third maxim only, or to all three.

### Unit 3
> 天下多忌諱，而民彌貧； (*Tiān xià duō jì huì, ér mín mí pín;* — "The more taboos and prohibitions all under heaven has, the poorer the people;")
- **Says:** Where the world is full of prohibitions and taboos, the people grow poorer and poorer. The correlation runs through 多 (*duō* — "many") and 彌 (*mí* — "ever more").
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天下 (*tiān xià* — "all under heaven") imposes the taboos; 民 (*mín* — "the people") suffer them.
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none. It is the first item of the evidence. 而 (*ér* — "and then") joins the two clauses.
- **Open:** (a) 天下 (*tiān xià* — "all under heaven") is the world at large. (b) Heshang Gong: 天下謂人主也 (*tiān xià wèi rén zhǔ yě* — "'all under heaven' means the ruler of people").

### Unit 4
> 人多利器，國家滋昏； (*Rén duō lì qì, guó jiā zī hūn;* — "The more sharp instruments people have, the more benighted the state;")
- **Says:** When people possess many 利器 (*lì qì* — "sharp tools / weapons"), the state and its households grow darker and more confused.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 人 (*rén* — "people"). Wang Bi's and Heshang Gong's lemmas read 民 (*mín* — "the people").
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 利器 (*lì qì* — "sharp instruments") may be literal weapons and tools, or figurative instruments of advantage and power. 國家 (*guó jiā* — "state-family") may be "the state" or "states and households".
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 利器凡所以利己之器也民强則國家弱 (*lì qì fán suǒ yǐ lì jǐ zhī qì yě, mín qiáng zé guó jiā ruò* — "sharp instruments are any tools by which one benefits oneself; when the people are strong, the state is weak"). Heshang Gong: 利器者權也 (*lì qì zhě quán yě* — "sharp instruments means power").

### Unit 5
> 人多伎巧，奇物滋起； (*Rén duō jì qiǎo, qí wù zī qǐ;* — "The more skill and cunning people have, the more strange things arise;")
- **Says:** The more craft and cleverness people have, the more 奇物 (*qí wù* — "strange / rare things") spring up.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 人 (*rén* — "people")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** (a) 奇物 (*qí wù* — "strange things") may be curiosities and luxury goods. (b) For Wang Bi they are perverse happenings: 巧偽生則邪事起 (*qiǎo wěi shēng zé xié shì qǐ* — "when artful falsehood is born, deviant affairs arise"). Heshang Gong takes 人 (*rén* — "people") as the lords: 人謂人君百里諸侯也 (*rén wèi rén jūn bǎi lǐ zhū hóu yě* — "'people' means lords of people, the feudal lords of a hundred li").

### Unit 6
> 法令滋彰，盜賊多有。 (*Fǎ lìng zī zhāng, dào zéi duō yǒu.* — "The more laws and ordinances are displayed, the more robbers and thieves there are.")
- **Says:** As laws and decrees are made ever more prominent, robbers and bandits multiply.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. The lawgivers; the people become thieves.
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** (a) 法令 (*fǎ lìng* — "laws and ordinances"). (b) Heshang Gong's lemma reads 法物 (*fǎ wù* — "fine objects"), glossed 法物好物也 (*fǎ wù hǎo wù yě* — "法物 means precious things"), so the clause becomes "the more treasures are displayed, the more thieves".
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi sums up Units 3–6: 多皆舍本以治末 (*duō jiē shě běn yǐ zhì mò* — "all of them abandon the root to govern the branches").

### Unit 7
> 故聖人云：我無為，而民自化； (*Gù shèng rén yún: wǒ wú wéi, ér mín zì huà;* — "Therefore the sage says: I do not act, and the people transform of themselves;")
- **Says:** The sage says: when I take no action, the people transform on their own. 無 (*wú* — "no / without") negates the action of doing.
- **Act:** asserts, as quoted speech
- **Who acts / of whom:** The sage, speaking as 我 (*wǒ* — "I"); 民 (*mín* — "the people") transform, by 自 (*zì* — "of themselves").
- **Force:** habitual. This is an implicit when/then.
- **Link:** therefore, through 故 (*gù* — "therefore")
- **Open:** 化 (*huà* — "transform") may mean "change / are transformed" or "are civilized / reformed".

### Unit 8
> 我好靜，而民自正； (*Wǒ hào jìng, ér mín zì zhèng;* — "I love stillness, and the people become straight of themselves;")
- **Says:** When I love stillness, the people set themselves right. 正 (*zhèng* — "straight / correct") is the same word as in Unit 1.
- **Act:** asserts, as quoted speech
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage (我, *wǒ* — "I"); the people (民, *mín*)
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 我好靜不言不教民皆自忠正也 (*wǒ hào jìng bù yán bù jiào, mín jiē zì zhōng zhèng yě* — "I love stillness, do not speak, do not instruct, and the people all become loyal and upright of themselves").

### Unit 9
> 我無事，而民自富； (*Wǒ wú shì, ér mín zì fù;* — "I have no undertakings, and the people grow rich of themselves;")
- **Says:** When I have no business with them, the people prosper on their own. 無 (*wú* — "no") negates the existence of undertakings.
- **Act:** asserts, as quoted speech
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; the people
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong makes the undertakings concrete: 我無徭役徴召之事 (*wǒ wú yáo yì zhēng zhào zhī shì* — "I have no business of corvée labor and levies").

### Unit 10
> 我無欲，而民自樸。 (*Wǒ wú yù, ér mín zì pǔ.* — "I have no desires, and the people become plain of themselves.")
- **Says:** When I am without desire, the people become 樸 (*pǔ* — "plain / uncarved wood") on their own. 無 (*wú* — "no") negates the existence of desire.
- **Act:** asserts, as quoted speech
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; the people
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 上之所欲民從之速也 (*shàng zhī suǒ yù mín cóng zhī sù yě* — "what those above desire, the people follow quickly"). His note records another edition with a fifth line: 我無情而民自清 (*wǒ wú qíng ér mín zì qīng* — "I am without feelings and the people become clear of themselves").

# Chapter 58

**The chapter as a whole:** A dull, muffled government makes the people honest, while a sharp-eyed, scrutinizing one makes them broken. Misfortune and good fortune lie inside each other, no one knows where the turning ends, and there is no fixed norm: the straight turns crooked and the good turns ominous, and people have been confused about this for a long time. So the sage is square without cutting, edged without wounding, straight without overreaching, and bright without dazzling.

### Unit 1
> 其政悶悶，其民淳淳； (*Qí zhèng mèn mèn, qí mín chún chún;* — "When its government is dull and muffled, its people are simple and honest;")
- **Says:** Where a state's governing is 悶悶 (*mèn mèn* — "dull, muffled, unclear"), its people are 淳淳 (*chún chún* — "pure, honest, thick-natured").
- **Act:** asserts, with an implicit when/then
- **Who acts / of whom:** 其 (*qí* — "its"), meaning some state's or ruler's government, and that state's people
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 悶悶然卒至於大治 (*mèn mèn rán zú zhì yú dà zhì* — "muffled, yet in the end it reaches great order"). Heshang Gong: 寛大悶悶昧昧似若不明也 (*kuān dà mèn mèn mèi mèi sì ruò bù míng yě* — "broad and lenient, muffled and dim, as if not clear-sighted"). Heshang Gong reads 醇醇 (*chún chún* — "unadulterated, rich").

### Unit 2
> 其政察察，其民缺缺。 (*Qí zhèng chá chá, qí mín quē quē.* — "When its government is scrutinizing, its people are wanting.")
- **Says:** Where governing is 察察 (*chá chá* — "sharp-eyed, scrutinizing, minutely discerning"), the people are 缺缺 (*quē quē* — "lacking, broken, deficient").
- **Act:** asserts, with an implicit when/then
- **Who acts / of whom:** as in Unit 1
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** but. It stands in contrast with Unit 1, with no connective.
- **Open:** (a) Wang Bi: divided and contentious, 民懐争競 (*mín huái zhēng jìng* — "the people harbor contention"). (b) Heshang Gong: impoverished and estranged, 民不聊生故𡙇𡙇日以踈薄 (*mín bù liáo shēng gù quē quē rì yǐ shū bó* — "the people cannot make a living, so they are 'wanting', daily more estranged and thin").

### Unit 3
> 禍兮福之所倚，福兮禍之所伏。 (*Huò xī fú zhī suǒ yǐ, fú xī huò zhī suǒ fú.* — "Misfortune — what good fortune leans on; good fortune — where misfortune crouches.")
- **Says:** 禍 (*huò* — "misfortune") is what 福 (*fú* — "good fortune") rests on, and good fortune is where misfortune lies hidden. 兮 (*xī* — an exclamatory pause) sets up each topic.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** misfortune and good fortune themselves
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none. The topic turns.
- **Open:** 倚 (*yǐ* — "lean on / rest against") may mean "depend on, arise from" or "lie beside". 伏 (*fú* — "lie hidden / crouch") may mean "lurk" or "lie prostrate beneath".
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 倚因也 (*yǐ yīn yě* — "倚 means 'depends on'"). Han Fei traces a causal chain each way, ending 而福本於有禍 (*ér fú běn yú yǒu huò* — "and good fortune is rooted in having had misfortune") and 而禍本生於有福 (*ér huò běn shēng yú yǒu fú* — "and misfortune is rooted and born in having had good fortune").

### Unit 4
> 孰知其極？ (*Shú zhī qí jí?* — "Who knows its limit?")
- **Says:** Who knows where this ends, or what its extreme or turning point is? The question is asked through 孰 (*shú* — "who").
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical: implies no one knows)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 孰 (*shú* — "who"), no one; 其 (*qí* — "its"), the alternation of fortune and misfortune
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** (a) 極 (*jí* — "limit / ridgepole / utmost") as the end point of the alternation. Heshang Gong: 禍福更相生誰能知其窮極時 (*huò fú gēng xiāng shēng, shuí néng zhī qí qióng jí shí* — "misfortune and fortune give rise to each other in turn: who can know when they will be exhausted?"). (b) Wang Bi: the utmost of good governing, 言誰知善治之極乎 (*yán shuí zhī shàn zhì zhī jí hū* — "it says: who knows the utmost of good governing?").

### Unit 5
> 其無正。 (*Qí wú zhèng.* — "It has no fixed norm.")
- **Says:** There is no 正 (*zhèng* — "fixed / correct standard"). 無 (*wú* — "there is no") negates its existence.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 其 (*qí* — "it"), the alternation, or its limit
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and. It answers Unit 4, with no connective.
- **Open:** (a) The alternation has no settled norm or fixed point. (b) Wang Bi: the utmost lies in there being nothing set up as correct, 唯無可正舉無可形名 (*wéi wú kě zhèng jǔ, wú kě xíng míng* — "only when there is nothing that can be held up as correct, nothing that can be given form and name"). (c) Heshang Gong reads 無 (*wú*) as 不 (*bù* — "not") and 正 (*zhèng*) as a verb: 无不也謂人君不正其身其无國也 (*wú bù yě, wèi rén jūn bù zhèng qí shēn, qí wú guó yě* — "无 means 'not': it says that if the lord does not correct himself, he will have no state"). (d) Wang Bi's note records an edition adding 邪 (*yé* — question particle), which would make it the question "Is there no norm?"

### Unit 6
> 正復為奇，善復為妖。 (*Zhèng fù wéi qí, shàn fù wéi yāo.* — "The straight turns back into the crooked; the good turns back into the ominous.")
- **Says:** 正 (*zhèng* — "the straight / correct") turns into 奇 (*qí* — "the irregular / crooked"), and 善 (*shàn* — "the good") turns into 妖 (*yāo* — "the uncanny / ominous / aberrant").
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the straight and the good themselves
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and. It illustrates Unit 5.
- **Open:** (a) 復 (*fù* — "again / back") may mean "turn back into", a reversal, or "again become", repeatedly. (b) 奇 (*qí*) may be "the irregular" (Wang Bi, echoing chapter 57's use of troops) or "deceit" (Heshang Gong: 竒詐也 (*qí zhà yě* — "奇 means deceit")).
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 以正治國則便復以竒用兵矣 (*yǐ zhèng zhì guó zé biàn fù yǐ qí yòng bīng yǐ* — "govern the state by correctness and you soon revert to using troops by surprise"). Heshang Gong makes it political: 人君不正下雖正復化上為詐 (*rén jūn bù zhèng, xià suī zhèng fù huà shàng wéi zhà* — "if the lord is not upright, those below, though upright, are in turn transformed by the one above into deceit"). His lemma reads 訞 (*yāo* — "ominous").

### Unit 7
> 人之迷，其日固久。 (*Rén zhī mí, qí rì gù jiǔ.* — "People's confusion — its days have surely been long.")
- **Says:** People have been confused about this for a long time indeed.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 人 (*rén* — "people") in general
- **Force:** past, continuing into the present
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** (a) People in general. (b) Heshang Gong: rulers, 人君迷惑失正以來其日已固久 (*rén jūn mí huò shī zhèng yǐ lái, qí rì yǐ gù jiǔ* — "since the lords became confused and lost the correct, the days have long been many").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 不可便正善治以責 (*bù kě biàn zhèng shàn zhì yǐ zé* — "one cannot simply demand [of them] correctness and good governance"). Wang Bi's note says Han Fei reads 其故以久矣 (*qí gù yǐ jiǔ yǐ* — "it has already been long").

### Unit 8
> 是以聖人方而不割，廉而不劌，直而不肆，光而不耀。 (*Shì yǐ shèng rén fāng ér bù gē, lián ér bù guì, zhí ér bù sì, guāng ér bù yào.* — "Therefore the sage is square but does not cut, edged but does not wound, straight but does not overreach, bright but does not dazzle.")
- **Says:** The sage has four qualities, each with its harmful use refused by 不 (*bù* — "not"), which negates the action: 方 (*fāng* — "square") but not 割 (*gē* — "cutting"); 廉 (*lián* — "edged / incorrupt") but not 劌 (*guì* — "wounding"); 直 (*zhí* — "straight") but not 肆 (*sì* — "letting loose / overreaching"); 光 (*guāng* — "bright") but not 耀 (*yào* — "dazzling / flaunting").
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; implicitly, others are the ones not cut
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore, through 是以 (*shì yǐ* — "therefore")
- **Open:** (a) 廉 (*lián*) is "sharp-cornered" (the physical edge) or "incorrupt / honest" (Wang Bi: 亷清亷也 (*lián qīng lián yě* — "廉 means pure and incorrupt")). (b) 肆 (*sì*) is "unrestrained / overbearing", or Heshang Gong's 肆申也 (*sì shēn yě* — "肆 means asserting / stretching oneself"): 聖人雖直曲巳從人 (*shèng rén suī zhí qū jǐ cóng rén* — "though the sage is straight, they bend themselves to follow others"). (c) For 劌 (*guì* — "wound"), Heshang Gong reads 害 (*hài* — "harm") and Han Fei reads 穢 (*huì* — "defile").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi takes each as guiding others without force: 以方導物舍去其邪不以方割物 (*yǐ fāng dǎo wù shě qù qí xié, bù yǐ fāng gē wù* — "with squareness to guide things away from their deviance, not to cut things with squareness"). Han Fei reads 光 (*guāng* — "bright") as worldly splendor: 官爵尊貴，衣裘壯麗也 (*guān jué zūn guì, yī qiú zhuàng lì yě* — "office and rank honored, clothes and furs magnificent").

# Chapter 59

**The chapter as a whole:** In governing people and serving heaven, nothing beats being sparing. Sparingness is early yielding, early yielding is doubled store of potency, and from that store come overcoming everything, a limit no one can see, and the capacity to hold a state. The mother of holding a state lasts long, and this is called the deep root and firm taproot, the way of long life and lasting sight.

### Unit 1
> 治人事天，莫若嗇。 (*Zhì rén shì tiān, mò ruò sè.* — "In governing people and serving heaven, nothing is as good as sparingness.")
- **Says:** For ruling others and serving heaven, nothing is better than 嗇 (*sè* — "sparing, frugal husbanding, holding back"). 莫 (*mò* — "nothing / none") negates the existence of anything better.
- **Act:** asserts. It works as advice.
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. A ruler, or anyone who governs; people and heaven are the objects.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** (a) 嗇 (*sè*) as sparing, frugality, conserving. (b) Wang Bi reads it as farming (the sense of 穡 (*sè* — "harvest")): 嗇農夫 (*sè nóng fū* — "嗇 means the farmer"), who weeds out odd growths and returns the field to evenness. (c) 事天 (*shì tiān* — "serve heaven") is "serve heaven", or Heshang Gong's 事用也 (*shì yòng yě* — "事 means 'use'"): use heaven's way.
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong glosses 嗇貪也 (*sè tān yě* — "嗇 means 'to covet / hoard'") and applies it to state and body: 治國者當愛民……治身者當愛精氣 (*zhì guó zhě dāng ài mín … zhì shēn zhě dāng ài jīng qì* — "one who governs a state should cherish the people … one who governs the body should cherish essence and breath"). Han Fei: 嗇之者，愛其精神，嗇其智識也 (*sè zhī zhě, ài qí jīng shén, sè qí zhì shí yě* — "to be sparing is to cherish one's vital spirit and be sparing with one's intellect").

### Unit 2
> 夫唯嗇，是謂早服； (*Fú wéi sè, shì wèi zǎo fú;* — "It is only sparingness — this is called early yielding;")
- **Says:** Sparingness, and only it, is what is named 早服 (*zǎo fú* — "early submitting / early taking-up").
- **Act:** names/defines, through 是謂 (*shì wèi* — "this is called")
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and. 夫唯 (*fú wéi* — "it is just / only") opens the unit.
- **Open:** (a) 服 (*fú*) as "submit / yield / comply". (b) Heshang Gong: 早先也服得也 (*zǎo xiān yě, fú dé yě* — "早 means 'first', 服 means 'obtain'"), so "obtaining [the heavenly Way] first". (c) Wang Bi: 早服常也 (*zǎo fú cháng yě* — "early 服 is [returning to] the constant"). Han Fei's text writes 蚤服 (*zǎo fú* — "early 服").

### Unit 3
> 早服謂之重積德； (*Zǎo fú wèi zhī chóng jī dé;* — "early yielding is called doubly accumulating potency;")
- **Says:** Early 服 (*fú*) is named 重積德 (*chóng jī dé* — "doubled / heavy accumulating of potency").
- **Act:** names/defines, through 謂之 (*wèi zhī* — "is called")
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and. It is a chain definition.
- **Open:** 重 (*chóng* — "doubly / again") or (*zhòng* — "heavily"). Heshang Gong's sound-gloss gives *chóng*. 德 (*dé* — "potency / virtue") is the thing accumulated.

### Unit 4
> 重積德則無不克； (*Chóng jī dé zé wú bù kè;* — "doubly accumulating potency, then there is nothing not overcome;")
- **Says:** If one doubly stores up potency, there is nothing one cannot master. 無不 (*wú bù* — "there is nothing not") is a double negation: 無 (*wú* — "nothing") plus 不 (*bù* — "not").
- **Act:** if/when … then, through 則 (*zé* — "then")
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. The one who stores potency.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** if-then
- **Open:** 克 (*kè* — "overcome / be equal to") may mean "conquer" or "be capable of". Heshang Gong: 剋勝也 (*kè shèng yě* — "克 means 'win'").

### Unit 5
> 無不克則莫知其極； (*Wú bù kè zé mò zhī qí jí;* — "nothing not overcome, then no one knows its limit;")
- **Says:** If there is nothing one cannot master, no one knows the limit of one's capacity. 莫 (*mò* — "no one") negates the existence of a knower.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** 莫 (*mò* — "no one"); 其 (*qí* — "its / one's") limit
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** if-then
- **Open:** Whose limit: (a) one's own potency (Heshang Gong: 莫有知己德之窮極也 (*mò yǒu zhī jǐ dé zhī qióng jí yě* — "no one knows the end of one's potency")); (b) the Way's (Wang Bi: 道無窮也 (*dào wú qióng yě* — "the Way has no end")).

### Unit 6
> 莫知其極，可以有國； (*Mò zhī qí jí, kě yǐ yǒu guó;* — "no one knows its limit, then one can hold a state;")
- **Says:** When no one can see one's limit, one can possess a state. 莫 (*mò* — "no one") negates the existence of a knower.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. The one who stores potency, who holds the state.
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** if-then
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 以有窮而莅國非能有國也 (*yǐ yǒu qióng ér lì guó fēi néng yǒu guó yě* — "to oversee a state with something that has a limit is not to be able to hold a state").

### Unit 7
> 有國之母，可以長久。 (*Yǒu guó zhī mǔ, kě yǐ cháng jiǔ.* — "The mother of holding a state can last long.")
- **Says:** The 母 (*mǔ* — "mother") of possessing a state can endure.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the mother (of the state); unstated who has it
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** (a) "The mother of the one who holds the state", with 有國 (*yǒu guó* — "possessing a state") as a noun phrase. (b) "Having the state's mother, one can last long", with 有 (*yǒu* — "have") as the verb. The mother is glossed as the Way by Heshang Gong (母道也 (*mǔ dào yě* — "the mother means the Way")) and by Han Fei (母者，道也 (*mǔ zhě, dào yě* — "the mother is the Way")). Wang Bi: 國之所以安謂之母 (*guó zhī suǒ yǐ ān wèi zhī mǔ* — "that by which a state is secure is called its mother").

### Unit 8
> 是謂深根固柢，長生久視之道。 (*Shì wèi shēn gēn gù dǐ, cháng shēng jiǔ shì zhī dào.* — "This is called deep roots and a firm taproot, the way of long life and lasting sight.")
- **Says:** This is named making the roots deep and the 柢 (*dǐ* — "taproot") firm, which is the way to long life and long seeing.
- **Act:** names/defines, through 是謂 (*shì wèi* — "this is called")
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and. It sums up.
- **Open:** (a) 久視 (*jiǔ shì* — "long seeing") as living long, seeing for many years. (b) It may also be read as enduring vision or oversight. 道 (*dào* — "way") here is "the way / method of".
- **Commentators:** Han Fei's text reads 深其根，固其柢 (*shēn qí gēn, gù qí dǐ* — "deepen its roots, firm its taproot"). He distinguishes 曼根 (*màn gēn* — "spreading roots") from 直根 (*zhí gēn* — "the straight root"), which is the 柢 (*dǐ* — "taproot"). Heshang Gong reads it as conserving breath and essence: 人能以氣為根以精為蔕 (*rén néng yǐ qì wéi gēn yǐ jīng wéi dì* — "a person can take breath as root and essence as stem").

# Chapter 60

**The chapter as a whole:** Governing a great state is like cooking small fish. When the world is overseen by the Way, the ghosts lose their numinous power: or rather they keep it but it does not harm people, and the sage does not harm people either. Because the two do no harm to each other, potency flows back to both.

### Unit 1
> 治大國，若烹小鮮。 (*Zhì dà guó, ruò pēng xiǎo xiān.* — "Governing a great state is like cooking small fish.")
- **Says:** Ruling a large state resembles cooking 小鮮 (*xiǎo xiān* — "small fresh fish"), something that falls apart if it is stirred too much.
- **Act:** asserts, as a simile through 若 (*ruò* — "like")
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. A ruler.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 不擾也 (*bù rǎo yě* — "[it means] not disturbing"). Heshang Gong: 烹小魚不去腸不去鱗不敢撓恐其糜也 (*pēng xiǎo yú bù qù cháng bù qù lín bù gǎn náo kǒng qí mí yě* — "in cooking small fish one does not gut them, does not scale them, and dare not stir them, for fear they will turn to mush").

### Unit 2
> 以道莅天下，其鬼不神； (*Yǐ dào lì tiān xià, qí guǐ bù shén;* — "When the Way oversees all under heaven, its ghosts are not numinous;")
- **Says:** If one presides over the world by means of the Way, the world's 鬼 (*guǐ* — "ghosts, the spirits of the dead") are not 神 (*shén* — "numinous, spiritually potent"). 不 (*bù* — "not") negates their being, or acting as, numinous.
- **Act:** if/when … then (implicit)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. The one who presides, a ruler. 其 (*qí* — "its"), the world's ghosts.
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** (a) The ghosts have no numinous power. (b) They do not manifest, do not haunt: Heshang Gong, 則□不敢見其精神以犯人也 (*zé [guǐ] bù gǎn xiàn qí jīng shén yǐ fàn rén yě* — "then the ghosts dare not show their spirit-essence to offend people"). (c) For Han Fei, people cease to treat them as potent: 其輕恬鬼也甚 (*qí qīng tián guǐ yě shèn* — "they take the ghosts very lightly").

### Unit 3
> 非其鬼不神，其神不傷人； (*Fēi qí guǐ bù shén, qí shén bù shāng rén;* — "It is not that its ghosts are not numinous; their numinous power does not harm people;")
- **Says:** This corrects Unit 2: the ghosts do have numinous power, but it does not injure people. 非 (*fēi* — "it is not that") negates the whole proposition "the ghosts are not numinous". 不 (*bù* — "not") negates the harming.
- **Act:** asserts, as a correction
- **Who acts / of whom:** 其神 (*qí shén* — "their numinous power / their spirits") as the doer; 人 (*rén* — "people") as the done-to
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** but. It qualifies Unit 2.
- **Open:** 其神 (*qí shén*) may be "their numinous power" (a quality of the ghosts) or "the spirits", taken as a class.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 神不害自然也 (*shén bù hài zì rán yě* — "the numinous does not harm what is so of itself"). Heshang Gong: 其□非无精神也……不能傷自然之人 (*qí [guǐ] fēi wú jīng shén yě … bù néng shāng zì rán zhī rén* — "the ghosts are not without spirit-essence … they cannot harm a person who is so of themselves").

### Unit 4
> 非其神不傷人，聖人亦不傷人。 (*Fēi qí shén bù shāng rén, shèng rén yì bù shāng rén.* — "It is not that their numinous power does not harm people; the sage too does not harm people.")
- **Says:** A second correction. 非 (*fēi* — "it is not that") negates the proposition, and 不 (*bù* — "not") negates harming. 亦 (*yì* — "also") adds the sage.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 其神 (*qí shén* — "their numinous power"); 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage"); 人 (*rén* — "people") as the done-to
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and. It extends Unit 3.
- **Open:** (a) "Not only do the spirits not harm people; the sage too does not harm them." (b) "It is not that the spirits by nature do no harm; [it is because] the sage does no harm." Heshang Gong reads it this way: 以聖人在位不傷害人故□不敢干之也 (*yǐ shèng rén zài wèi bù shāng hài rén, gù [guǐ] bù gǎn gān zhī yě* — "because the sage in office does not harm people, therefore the ghosts dare not intrude on them"). Han Fei's text reads 聖人亦不傷民 (*shèng rén yì bù shāng mín* — "the sage too does not harm the people").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 道洽則神不傷人……道洽則聖人亦不傷人 (*dào qià zé shén bù shāng rén … dào qià zé shèng rén yì bù shāng rén* — "when the Way pervades, the numinous does not harm people … when the Way pervades, the sage also does not harm people").

### Unit 5
> 夫兩不相傷，故德交歸焉。 (*Fú liǎng bù xiāng shāng, gù dé jiāo guī yān.* — "Now, the two do not harm each other, so potency returns to them together.")
- **Says:** Because the two parties do not injure each other, 德 (*dé* — "potency / virtue") converges and returns to them. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates mutual harm.
- **Act:** asserts, through 故 (*gù* — "so") as cause and effect
- **Who acts / of whom:** 兩 (*liǎng* — "the two"), unstated who they are; 德 (*dé* — "potency") as subject of the second clause
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore. 夫 (*fú* — "now") opens the unit as a summing-up; 故 (*gù* — "so") comes inside it.
- **Open:** Who "the two" are: (a) the numinous and the sage (Wang Bi: 神聖合道交歸之也 (*shén shèng hé dào jiāo guī zhī yě* — "the numinous and the sage both accord with the Way, and [potency] returns to them both")). (b) The ghosts and the sage (Heshang Gong: □與聖人俱兩不相傷也 (*[guǐ] yǔ shèng rén jù liǎng bù xiāng shāng yě* — "the ghosts and the sage, the two, both do not harm each other")). (c) Han Fei has two pairs, ruler with people and ghosts with people, with potency returning to the people: 言其德上下交盛而俱歸於民也 (*yán qí dé shàng xià jiāo shèng ér jù guī yú mín yě* — "it says their potency flourishes in exchange above and below, and all returns to the people"). 交歸 (*jiāo guī* — "return in exchange / converge") may also be "mutually return". Han Fei's lemma reads 則 (*zé* — "then") for 故 (*gù* — "so").

# Chapter 61

**The chapter as a whole:** A great state is the downstream, where the world converges, the world's female, and the female overcomes the male by stillness, taking the low place. When great and small states each place themselves beneath the other, each gains the other. Since the great wants only to gather people in and the small wants only to enter and serve, for both to get what they want, the great one should be the one below.

### Unit 1
> 大國者下流，天下之交，天下之牝。 (*Dà guó zhě xià liú, tiān xià zhī jiāo, tiān xià zhī pìn.* — "A great state is the lower stream, the meeting place of all under heaven, the female of all under heaven.")
- **Says:** A large state is 下流 (*xià liú* — "the lower course of a river, downstream"), the 交 (*jiāo* — "juncture / meeting / intercourse") of the world, and the 牝 (*pìn* — "female (of animals)") of the world. 者 (*zhě*) marks the topic.
- **Act:** names/defines (equational). It may also be normative.
- **Who acts / of whom:** 大國 (*dà guó* — "a great state")
- **Force:** always/universal (or must, if normative)
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** (a) Descriptive: a great state *is* the downstream. (b) Normative: a great state *should be* the downstream. Heshang Gong: 治大國當如居下流 (*zhì dà guó dāng rú jū xià liú* — "governing a great state, one should be as one who dwells downstream"). 交 (*jiāo*) may be "the place where all converges" (Wang Bi: 天下所歸㑹也 (*tiān xià suǒ guī huì yě* — "where all under heaven returns and meets")) or "intercourse", set beside the female.

### Unit 2
> 牝常以靜勝牡，以靜為下。 (*Pìn cháng yǐ jìng shèng mǔ, yǐ jìng wéi xià.* — "The female always overcomes the male by stillness; by stillness she is beneath.")
- **Says:** The female constantly overcomes 牡 (*mǔ* — "the male (of animals)") by means of 靜 (*jìng* — "stillness"), and by stillness she takes the low position.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 牝 (*pìn* — "the female") is the doer in both clauses; 牡 (*mǔ* — "the male") is overcome.
- **Force:** always/universal, through 常 (*cháng* — "always / constantly")
- **Link:** and. It explains Unit 1's "female".
- **Open:** 以靜為下 (*yǐ jìng wéi xià*) may mean (a) "by stillness she is / goes below" or (b) "she takes stillness as [the way of] being low", with 為 (*wéi*) as "to regard as / to make".
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 以其静故能為下也 (*yǐ qí jìng gù néng wéi xià yě* — "because of her stillness she can be below"), and 雄躁動貪欲雌常以静故能勝雄也 (*xióng zào dòng tān yù, cí cháng yǐ jìng gù néng shèng xióng yě* — "the rooster is agitated, restless and greedy; the hen by constant stillness can therefore overcome the rooster"). Heshang Gong: 隂道以安靜爲謙下 (*yīn dào yǐ ān jìng wéi qiān xià* — "the way of yin takes calm stillness as humble lowness").

### Unit 3
> 故大國以下小國，則取小國； (*Gù dà guó yǐ xià xiǎo guó, zé qǔ xiǎo guó;* — "So if a great state goes beneath a small state, it takes the small state;")
- **Says:** If a large state places itself below a small one, it wins the small state.
- **Act:** if/when … then, through 則 (*zé* — "then")
- **Who acts / of whom:** 大國 (*dà guó* — "the great state") acts on 小國 (*xiǎo guó* — "the small state")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore, through 故 (*gù* — "so")
- **Open:** 取 (*qǔ* — "take") may mean "seize, annex" or "win over, gain the allegiance of". Wang Bi: 小國則附之 (*xiǎo guó zé fù zhī* — "the small state then attaches itself to it").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi parses the grammar: 大國以下猶云以大國下小國 (*dà guó yǐ xià yóu yún yǐ dà guó xià xiǎo guó* — "'great state by lowering' is like saying 'taking the great state, go beneath the small state'").

### Unit 4
> 小國以下大國，則取大國。 (*Xiǎo guó yǐ xià dà guó, zé qǔ dà guó.* — "If a small state goes beneath a great state, it takes the great state.")
- **Says:** If a small state places itself below a large one, it "takes" the large state.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** 小國 (*xiǎo guó* — "the small state"); 大國 (*dà guó* — "the great state")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** (a) The small state "takes", in the sense of wins over, the great state. (b) It gains acceptance from, or is taken in by, the great state: Wang Bi, 大國納之也 (*dà guó nà zhī yě* — "the great state receives it"). The surface grammar is identical to Unit 3, but context pulls toward (b).

### Unit 5
> 故或下以取，或下而取。 (*Gù huò xià yǐ qǔ, huò xià ér qǔ.* — "So one goes beneath in order to take, another goes beneath and so takes.")
- **Says:** 或 (*huò* — "some / one") lowers itself and thereby takes, and 或 (*huò* — "another") lowers itself and so takes. The two halves differ only in 以 (*yǐ* — "in order to / by means of") against 而 (*ér* — "and then").
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 或 … 或 (*huò … huò* — "one … another"): the great state and the small state, unspecified which is which
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore, through 故 (*gù* — "so")
- **Open:** (a) The great state lowers itself in order to take, and the small state lowers itself and is taken (accepted). (b) No distinction is meant beyond "both gain by being low". Heshang Gong: 大國以下小國小國以下大國更以義相取 (*dà guó yǐ xià xiǎo guó, xiǎo guó yǐ xià dà guó, gēng yǐ yì xiāng qǔ* — "the great state lowers itself to the small, the small to the great, each in turn taking the other by right"). Wang Bi: 言唯修卑下然後乃各得其所 (*yán wéi xiū bēi xià rán hòu nǎi gè dé qí suǒ* — "it says that only by cultivating lowliness does each find its place").

### Unit 6
> 大國不過欲兼畜人，小國不過欲入事人。 (*Dà guó bù guò yù jiān xù rén, xiǎo guó bù guò yù rù shì rén.* — "A great state wants no more than to gather and keep people; a small state wants no more than to enter and serve others.")
- **Says:** The large state's whole desire is to bring people together and nourish or keep them, and the small state's whole desire is to come in and serve someone. 不過 (*bù guò* — "not exceeding / no more than"), with 不 (*bù* — "not") negating "exceed".
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the great state and its people; the small state and the one it serves
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 兼畜 (*jiān xù* — "gather together and rear") may mean "unite and nurture" or "annex and keep like livestock". Heshang Gong: 兼并人國而牧畜之 (*jiān bìng rén guó ér mù xù zhī* — "annex other people's states and herd them"). For the small state: 使為臣僕 (*shǐ wéi chén pú* — "[seeking to] become subjects and servants").

### Unit 7
> 夫兩者各得所欲，大者宜為下。 (*Fú liǎng zhě gè dé suǒ yù, dà zhě yí wéi xià.* — "Now, for the two each to get what they want, the great one ought to be beneath.")
- **Says:** If both are to get what they want, the larger one should take the lower place. 宜 (*yí* — "fitting / ought").
- **Act:** advises/commands, through 宜 (*yí* — "ought / it is fitting")
- **Who acts / of whom:** 兩者 (*liǎng zhě* — "the two"); 大者 (*dà zhě* — "the great one")
- **Force:** must/necessary (as what is fitting)
- **Link:** none. 夫 (*fú* — "now") opens it as a conclusion.
- **Open:** (a) The first clause is the purpose or condition: "for both to get what they want, the great should be low". (b) It is the result: "both get what they want; [so] the great should be low". 宜 (*yí*) may be "ought to" or "it is fitting / it suits". Wang Bi's lemma reads 各得其所欲 (*gè dé qí suǒ yù* — "each gets what it wants").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi explains why the great one specifically: 小國修下自全而已不能令天下歸之大國修下則天下歸之 (*xiǎo guó xiū xià zì quán ér yǐ, bù néng lìng tiān xià guī zhī; dà guó xiū xià zé tiān xià guī zhī* — "a small state cultivating lowliness merely keeps itself whole and cannot make the world turn to it; a great state cultivating lowliness, the world turns to it"). Heshang Gong: 大國又宜為謙下 (*dà guó yòu yí wéi qiān xià* — "the great state, further, ought to be humble and low").

# Chapter 62

**The chapter as a whole:** The Way is the hidden inner recess of all things: the good treasure it, and those who are not good are kept by it. Fine words and fine conduct can buy honor and raise a person up, but no one who is not good is cast away. So when a ruler is enthroned, sitting and offering this Way beats any tribute of jade and horses. The ancients prized it because by it the seeker finds and the offender is spared, and so it is the world's most precious thing.

### Unit 1
> 道者萬物之奧。 (*Dào zhě wàn wù zhī ào.* — "The Way is the inner recess of the myriad things.")
- **Says:** The Way is the 奧 (*ào* — "the innermost southwest corner of a house; the hidden, sheltered recess") of all things.
- **Act:** names/defines (者 (*zhě*) marks the topic of an equational sentence)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 道 (*dào* — "the Way"); 萬物 (*wàn wù* — "the myriad things")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 奧 (*ào*) as (a) the hidden inner recess or sanctum, (b) shelter (Wang Bi: 奥猶曖也可得庇䕃之辭 (*ào yóu ài yě, kě dé bì yìn zhī cí* — "奧 is like 'shaded', a word for being able to get shelter")), or (c) storehouse (Heshang Gong: 奥藏也道為萬物之藏無所不容也 (*ào zàng yě, dào wéi wàn wù zhī zàng, wú suǒ bù róng yě* — "奧 means storehouse; the Way is the storehouse of the myriad things, there is nothing it does not hold")).

### Unit 2
> 善人之寶，不善人之所保。 (*Shàn rén zhī bǎo, bù shàn rén zhī suǒ bǎo.* — "The treasure of the good, that by which the not-good are kept.")
- **Says:** The Way is what good people treasure, and also what people who are not good hold to or are kept safe by. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates the quality "good".
- **Act:** names/defines (continuing the equation)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 善人 (*shàn rén* — "good people"); 不善人 (*bù shàn rén* — "not-good people")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** (a) 所保 (*suǒ bǎo*) is "what [they] hold on to". (b) It is "that by which [they] are protected". Wang Bi: 保以全也 (*bǎo yǐ quán yě* — "保 means being kept whole by it"). Heshang Gong: 道者不善人之保倚也 (*dào zhě bù shàn rén zhī bǎo yǐ yě* — "the Way is what the not-good rely on"). 善 (*shàn*) may be ethical "good" or "skilled", though the pairing favors "good".

### Unit 3
> 美言可以市尊，美行可以加人。 (*Měi yán kě yǐ shì zūn, měi xíng kě yǐ jiā rén.* — "Fine words can buy honor; fine conduct can raise one above others.")
- **Says:** As the base text punctuates it: beautiful words can purchase esteem, and beautiful conduct can set a person above others or add to them.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. People in general; or, for Wang Bi, words and conduct concerning the Way.
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** (a) Base text: 美言可以市尊 (*měi yán kě yǐ shì zūn* — "fine words can buy honor"), 美行可以加人 (*měi xíng kě yǐ jiā rén* — "fine conduct can be added to people"). (b) Wang Bi's and Heshang Gong's lemmas: 美言可以市，尊行可以加人 (*měi yán kě yǐ shì, zūn xíng kě yǐ jiā rén* — "fine words can be marketed; honored conduct can be added to people"). Further, 加人 (*jiā rén*) may be "benefit others", "raise oneself over others", or Heshang Gong's 加别也 (*jiā bié yě* — "加 means 'set apart'"). And whether the line praises the Way or disparages mere fine words is divided.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi reads it as praise of the Way: 美言之則可以奪衆貨之賈 (*měi yán zhī zé kě yǐ duó zhòng huò zhī jià* — "if one speaks finely of it, it can outbid the price of all goods"), and 尊行之則千里之外應之 (*zūn xíng zhī zé qiān lǐ zhī wài yìng zhī* — "if one honors and practices it, those a thousand li away respond"). Heshang Gong reads it as disparagement: 美言者獨可於市耳 (*měi yán zhě dú kě yú shì ěr* — "fine words are fit only for the market"), and 未足以尊道 (*wèi zú yǐ zūn dào* — "[it is] not yet enough to honor the Way").

### Unit 4
> 人之不善，何棄之有？ (*Rén zhī bù shàn, hé qì zhī yǒu?* — "People's not being good — what casting away is there?")
- **Says:** Even if people are not good, why should they be thrown away? 不 (*bù* — "not") negates "good". 何…之有 (*hé … zhī yǒu* — "what … is there") is a rhetorical formula.
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical: implies they are not to be abandoned)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the abandoner is unstated (the Way? the ruler? anyone?); the people who are not good are the done-to
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** but. It turns against Unit 3, with no connective.
- **Open:** Who does not abandon them: (a) the Way, which keeps them (following Unit 2). (b) The ruler or teacher (Heshang Gong: 人雖不善當以道化之 (*rén suī bù shàn dāng yǐ dào huà zhī* — "though a person is not good, one should transform them by the Way")). Wang Bi puts the burden on them: 不善當保道以免放 (*bù shàn dāng bǎo dào yǐ miǎn fàng* — "the not-good should hold to the Way to escape being cast out").

### Unit 5
> 故立天子，置三公，雖有拱璧以先駟馬，不如坐進此道。 (*Gù lì tiān zǐ, zhì sān gōng, suī yǒu gǒng bì yǐ xiān sì mǎ, bù rú zuò jìn cǐ dào.* — "So when the Son of Heaven is enthroned and the three dukes installed, though there be jade disks held in both arms preceding teams of four horses, it is not as good as sitting and offering this Way.")
- **Says:** At the enthronement of the ruler and the installing of the three highest ministers, even a gift of great jade disks followed by four-horse teams is worth less than sitting still and presenting this Way. 雖 (*suī* — "although") makes it concessive. 不如 (*bù rú* — "not as good as"), with 不 (*bù* — "not") negating the comparison.
- **Act:** asserts (a comparison of value)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. The givers at court; the recipient is the Son of Heaven and the ministers.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** therefore, through 故 (*gù* — "so")
- **Open:** 坐進此道 (*zuò jìn cǐ dào*) may be (a) "sit and offer this Way" as a gift, matching the tribute, or (b) "sit and advance in this Way" (make progress in it). 拱璧 (*gǒng bì*) may be "a jade disk held in both arms", a large disk, or "a disk held with folded hands". Why 故 (*gù* — "so") applies is also open: (a) rulers exist to transform the not-good (Heshang Gong: 欲使教化不善之人 (*yù shǐ jiào huà bù shàn zhī rén* — "wishing to have the not-good taught and transformed")); (b) Wang Bi: 尊其位重其人所以為道也 (*zūn qí wèi zhòng qí rén suǒ yǐ wéi dào yě* — "honoring their position and giving weight to their persons is done for the sake of the Way").

### Unit 6
> 古之所以貴此道者何？ (*Gǔ zhī suǒ yǐ guì cǐ dào zhě hé?* — "Why did the ancients value this Way?")
- **Says:** It asks the reason the people of old prized this Way.
- **Act:** asks (real question), answered in Unit 7
- **Who acts / of whom:** 古 (*gǔ* — "antiquity / the ancients"); 此道 (*cǐ dào* — "this Way")
- **Force:** past
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none

### Unit 7
> 不曰：求以得，有罪以免邪？ (*Bù yuē: qiú yǐ dé, yǒu zuì yǐ miǎn xié?* — "Is it not said: by it, seeking one finds; having offended, one is spared?")
- **Says:** Is it not because, by means of the Way, whoever seeks obtains, and whoever has 罪 (*zuì* — "an offense, a crime") escapes? 不 (*bù* — "not") negates "say", inside the rhetorical frame 不曰…邪 (*bù yuē … xié* — "is it not said … ?").
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical: implies yes, this is why)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. Anyone who seeks; anyone who has offended.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none. It answers Unit 6.
- **Open:** (a) 不曰 (*bù yuē* — "is it not said"). (b) Heshang Gong's lemma reads 不日 (*bù rì* — "not daily"): 不日日逺行求索近得之於身 (*bù rì rì yuǎn xíng qiú suǒ, jìn dé zhī yú shēn* — "not traveling far day after day searching: one gets it nearby, in oneself"). 有罪以免 (*yǒu zuì yǐ miǎn* — "having offense, thereby escape") may mean the guilty are pardoned, or (Heshang Gong) one escapes punishment unjustly dealt: 有罪謂遭亂世闇君妄行刑誅 (*yǒu zuì wèi zāo luàn shì àn jūn wàng xíng xíng zhū* — "'having offense' means meeting a chaotic age and a benighted lord who wildly applies punishment and execution"). Wang Bi's lemma reads 以求得 (*yǐ qiú dé* — "by it, seek and obtain") and ends with 耶 (*yé* — question particle).
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 以求則得求以免則得免 (*yǐ qiú zé dé qiú, yǐ miǎn zé dé miǎn* — "use it to seek and you get what you seek; use it to escape and you get escape").

### Unit 8
> 故為天下貴。 (*Gù wéi tiān xià guì.* — "Therefore it is the most valued in all under heaven.")
- **Says:** For that reason the Way is the most precious thing in the world.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. The Way.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** therefore, through 故 (*gù* — "therefore")
- **Open:** "the most precious thing in the world" or "valued by the world". This is the same closing line as chapter 56.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 無所而不施故為天下貴也 (*wú suǒ ér bù shī, gù wéi tiān xià guì yě* — "there is nowhere it is not applied, so it is the world's most precious").

# Chapter 63

**The chapter as a whole:** Do the not-doing, take up the no-affair, savor the flavorless, and answer grievance with potency. Plan the hard while it is easy and do the great while it is small, because every hard and great thing in the world begins easy and small. So the sage never does the great and thereby completes it. Easy promises earn little trust and treating things as easy brings much difficulty, so the sage treats things as hard and in the end has no hardship.

### Unit 1
> 為無為，事無事，味無味。 (*Wéi wú wéi, shì wú shì, wèi wú wèi.* — "Do not-doing; undertake no-undertaking; taste the tasteless.")
- **Says:** Make non-action your action, make no-affairs your affair, and make the flavorless your flavor. In each phrase the verb takes as its object a noun negated by 無 (*wú* — "no / without"): no action, no affair, no flavor.
- **Act:** advises/commands (or describes, see Open)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. The reader, or the sage.
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** (a) Imperatives addressed to the reader. (b) A description of how the sage acts. (c) Either way, "act without acting" against "do the not-doing" are two readings of the same structure.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 以無為為居以不言為教以恬淡為味治之極也 (*yǐ wú wéi wéi jū, yǐ bù yán wéi jiào, yǐ tián dàn wéi wèi, zhì zhī jí yě* — "taking non-action as one's dwelling, not-speaking as teaching, the calm and bland as flavor: the utmost of governing"). Heshang Gong reads 味無味 (*wèi wú wèi* — "taste the tasteless") as 深思逺慮味道意也 (*shēn sī yuǎn lǜ wèi dào yì yě* — "deep thought and far reflection, tasting the meaning of the Way").

### Unit 2
> 大小多少，報怨以德。 (*Dà xiǎo duō shǎo, bào yuàn yǐ dé.* — "Great small, many few; repay resentment with potency.")
- **Says:** Four bare words, 大小多少 (*dà xiǎo duō shǎo* — "big, small, many, few"), followed by an instruction to answer 怨 (*yuàn* — "resentment, grievance") with 德 (*dé* — "potency / virtue / kindness").
- **Act:** advises/commands (second clause). The first clause is undetermined.
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. The reader or the sage. The one who bears the grievance is unstated.
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 大小多少 (*dà xiǎo duō shǎo*) has several genuine readings. (a) Verbal: "treat the small as great, the few as many". (b) "Whether great or small, many or few", that is, any grievance. (c) Heshang Gong: 欲大反小欲多反少自然之道也 (*yù dà fǎn xiǎo, yù duō fǎn shǎo, zì rán zhī dào yě* — "wanting the great, return to the small; wanting the many, return to the few: the way of what is so of itself"). (d) Wang Bi reads the second clause against the first: 小怨則不足以報大怨則天下之所欲誅順天下之所同者徳也 (*xiǎo yuàn zé bù zú yǐ bào, dà yuàn zé tiān xià zhī suǒ yù zhū, shùn tiān xià zhī suǒ tóng zhě dé yě* — "a small grievance is not worth repaying; a great grievance is what the world wants punished; following what the whole world agrees on is potency"). 德 (*dé*) may be "potency / virtue" or "kindness / favor".

### Unit 3
> 圖難於其易，為大於其細。 (*Tú nán yú qí yì, wéi dà yú qí xì.* — "Plan the difficult in its easy stage; do the great in its minute stage.")
- **Says:** Tackle what is hard while it is still easy, and accomplish what is great while it is still small.
- **Act:** advises/commands
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. The reader, or one.
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none

### Unit 4
> 天下難事，必作於易； (*Tiān xià nán shì, bì zuò yú yì;* — "The difficult affairs of the world necessarily arise from the easy;")
- **Says:** Every hard undertaking in the world has its start in something easy.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天下難事 (*tiān xià nán shì* — "the world's difficult affairs")
- **Force:** must/necessary, through 必 (*bì* — "necessarily")
- **Link:** because. It grounds Unit 3, with no connective.
- **Open:** 作 (*zuò*) may mean (a) "arise / begin" (a fact about how things grow, as Han Fei reads it: 有形之類，大必起於小 (*yǒu xíng zhī lèi, dà bì qǐ yú xiǎo* — "among things with form, the great must arise from the small")) or (b) "be done / be worked" (a rule for acting).

### Unit 5
> 天下大事，必作於細。 (*Tiān xià dà shì, bì zuò yú xì.* — "The great affairs of the world necessarily arise from the minute.")
- **Says:** Every great undertaking in the world starts from something tiny.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天下大事 (*tiān xià dà shì* — "the world's great affairs")
- **Force:** must/necessary, through 必 (*bì* — "necessarily")
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** as in Unit 4

### Unit 6
> 是以聖人終不為大，故能成其大。 (*Shì yǐ shèng rén zhōng bù wéi dà, gù néng chéng qí dà.* — "Therefore the sage never does the great, and so can complete their greatness.")
- **Says:** Because of this, the sage never undertakes the great, and that is why they are able to accomplish the great. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates the action, strengthened by 終 (*zhōng* — "to the end / ultimately").
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore, through 是以 (*shì yǐ* — "therefore"), with 故 (*gù* — "so") inside the unit
- **Open:** (a) 不為大 (*bù wéi dà*) as "does not undertake great things", acting only on the small. (b) "Does not make themselves great / act the great one" (Heshang Gong: 處謙虚也 (*chǔ qiān xū yě* — "dwells in humility and emptiness")). 成其大 (*chéng qí dà* — "complete its / their greatness") may be "accomplish great things" or "achieve their own greatness" (Heshang Gong: 天下共歸之也 (*tiān xià gòng guī zhī yě* — "the whole world turns to them")).

### Unit 7
> 夫輕諾必寡信，多易必多難。 (*Fú qīng nuò bì guǎ xìn, duō yì bì duō nán.* — "Now, light promises necessarily earn little trust; much treating as easy necessarily brings much difficulty.")
- **Says:** Promises given lightly are bound to be little trusted, and treating many things as easy is bound to produce many difficulties.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. Anyone who promises or takes things lightly.
- **Force:** must/necessary, through 必 (*bì* — "necessarily")
- **Link:** none. 夫 (*fú* — "now") opens a general observation.
- **Open:** 寡信 (*guǎ xìn* — "little trust") may be "little trustworthiness" (the promiser fails to keep faith) or "little credence" (others do not believe them). 多易 (*duō yì*) may be "to treat much as easy" or "much easiness".
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 不重言也 (*bù zhòng yán yě* — "not giving weight to one's words"), and 不愼患也 (*bù shèn huàn yě* — "not being careful about trouble").

### Unit 8
> 是以聖人猶難之，故終無難矣。 (*Shì yǐ shèng rén yóu nán zhī, gù zhōng wú nán yǐ.* — "Therefore even the sage treats things as hard, and so in the end has no hardship.")
- **Says:** For this reason the sage still treats them (easy things) as difficult, and so in the end meets no difficulty. 無 (*wú* — "no") negates the existence of difficulty.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; 之 (*zhī* — "it / them"), the easy things or affairs in general
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore, through 是以 (*shì yǐ* — "therefore"), with 故 (*gù* — "so") inside the unit
- **Open:** 猶 (*yóu*) may be (a) "even [the sage]" or (b) "still / all the same", or (c) "as if [hard]". Wang Bi takes (a): 以聖人之才猶尚難於細易况非聖人之才而欲忽於此乎 (*yǐ shèng rén zhī cái yóu shàng nán yú xì yì, kuàng fēi shèng rén zhī cái ér yù hū yú cǐ hū* — "even with a sage's gifts one still treats the minute and easy as hard; how much less may one without a sage's gifts neglect this?").

# Chapter 64

**The chapter as a whole:** What is at rest is easy to hold, and what has not yet shown itself is easy to plan for, so act before things exist and order them before they fall into disorder: great trees, towers and journeys all start from something tiny. Whoever forces things ruins them and whoever grasps loses them, so the sage neither acts nor grasps. People ruin their affairs just short of completion, and care at the end equal to care at the start prevents that. So the sage desires not-desiring, learns not-learning, and supports the self-so of all things without daring to act.

### Unit 1
> 其安易持，其未兆易謀。 (*Qí ān yì chí, qí wèi zhào yì móu.* — "While it is at rest it is easy to hold; while it has not yet given signs it is easy to plan for.")
- **Says:** A thing (a situation) is easy to keep hold of while it is settled, and easy to plan for before it shows any 兆 (*zhào* — "omen, sign, crack"). 未 (*wèi* — "not yet") negates the showing of signs.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 其 (*qí* — "it"), an unstated thing or situation. The one who holds and plans is unstated.
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 以其安不忘危 (*yǐ qí ān bù wàng wēi* — "because in its security one does not forget danger"). Heshang Gong: 治身治國安靜者易守持也 (*zhì shēn zhì guó ān jìng zhě yì shǒu chí yě* — "in governing body or state, what is calm and still is easy to keep hold of").

### Unit 2
> 其脆易泮，其微易散。 (*Qí cuì yì pàn, qí wēi yì sàn.* — "While it is brittle it is easy to break; while it is minute it is easy to scatter.")
- **Says:** A thing is easily split while still brittle, and easily dispersed while still tiny.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 其 (*qí* — "it"), as in Unit 1
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 泮 (*pàn*) may be "to split / break apart" or "to melt / dissolve (as ice)". Heshang Gong reads 破 (*pò* — "break"). Wang Bi's note records 脆 (*cuì* — "brittle") written 膬 (*cuì* — "brittle") in one edition.

### Unit 3
> 為之於未有，治之於未亂。 (*Wéi zhī yú wèi yǒu, zhì zhī yú wèi luàn.* — "Act on it before it exists; order it before it is disordered.")
- **Says:** Deal with a thing before it has come into being, and put it in order before disorder sets in. 未 (*wèi* — "not yet") appears twice, negating existence and disorder.
- **Act:** advises/commands
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. The reader, or one. 之 (*zhī* — "it"), the matter.
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore, drawn from Units 1–2 with no connective
- **Open:** none

### Unit 4
> 合抱之木，生於毫末； (*Hé bào zhī mù, shēng yú háo mò;* — "A tree that fills an embrace grows from a downy tip;")
- **Says:** A tree too thick to get one's arms around grows from something as small as the tip of a fine hair.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the tree
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** because. It illustrates Unit 3, with no connective.
- **Open:** none

### Unit 5
> 九層之臺，起於累土； (*Jiǔ céng zhī tái, qǐ yú lěi tǔ;* — "A terrace of nine tiers rises from heaped earth;")
- **Says:** A nine-story terrace starts from piled-up earth.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the terrace
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none

### Unit 6
> 千里之行，始於足下。 (*Qiān lǐ zhī xíng, shǐ yú zú xià.* — "A journey of a thousand li begins beneath the foot.")
- **Says:** A thousand-league journey starts from the ground under one's own foot.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the journey; implicitly the traveler
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none

### Unit 7
> 為者敗之，執者失之。 (*Wéi zhě bài zhī, zhí zhě shī zhī.* — "One who acts on it ruins it; one who grasps it loses it.")
- **Says:** Whoever acts (forcibly) on a thing spoils it, and whoever clutches a thing loses it.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 為者 (*wéi zhě* — "the one who acts"); 執者 (*zhí zhě* — "the one who grasps"); 之 (*zhī* — "it"), the thing
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none. The topic turns.
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 有為於事廢於自然 (*yǒu wéi yú shì fèi yú zì rán* — "acting on affairs, one wrecks what is so of itself"). Wang Bi: 以施為治之形名執之反生事原 (*yǐ shī wéi zhì zhī, xíng míng zhí zhī, fǎn shēng shì yuán* — "governing it by applied action and holding it by form and name instead gives rise to the sources of trouble").

### Unit 8
> 是以聖人無為故無敗，無執故無失。 (*Shì yǐ shèng rén wú wéi gù wú bài, wú zhí gù wú shī.* — "Therefore the sage does not act, and so does not ruin; does not grasp, and so does not lose.")
- **Says:** The sage takes no action and so ruins nothing, and grasps nothing and so loses nothing. 無 (*wú* — "no / without") appears four times, negating action, ruin, grasping and loss.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore, through 是以 (*shì yǐ* — "therefore"), with 故 (*gù* — "so") inside the unit
- **Open:** none

### Unit 9
> 民之從事，常於幾成而敗之。 (*Mín zhī cóng shì, cháng yú jī chéng ér bài zhī.* — "People, in pursuing their affairs, always ruin them when they are nearly complete.")
- **Says:** When people take up a task, they commonly wreck it just as it is about to be finished.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 民 (*mín* — "the people"); 之 (*zhī* — "it"), their affairs
- **Force:** habitual, through 常 (*cháng* — "always / commonly")
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 不慎終也 (*bù shèn zhōng yě* — "[it is] not being careful at the end"). Heshang Gong: 貪位好名奢泰盈滿而自敗也 (*tān wèi hào míng shē tài yíng mǎn ér zì bài yě* — "they crave position and love fame, grow extravagant and overfull, and ruin themselves").

### Unit 10
> 慎終如始，則無敗事。 (*Shèn zhōng rú shǐ, zé wú bài shì.* — "Be as careful at the end as at the beginning, then there are no ruined affairs.")
- **Says:** If one takes as much care at the end as at the start, nothing will be spoiled. 無 (*wú* — "no") negates the existence of ruined affairs.
- **Act:** if/when … then, through 則 (*zé* — "then"). It works as advice.
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. The reader, or anyone.
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and. It is the remedy for Unit 9.
- **Open:** none

### Unit 11
> 是以聖人欲不欲，不貴難得之貨； (*Shì yǐ shèng rén yù bù yù, bù guì nán dé zhī huò;* — "Therefore the sage desires not-desiring, and does not value goods hard to get;")
- **Says:** The sage desires 不欲 (*bù yù* — "not-desiring"), and does not prize rare goods. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates desiring (inside the object), and negates valuing.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore, through 是以 (*shì yǐ* — "therefore")
- **Open:** 欲不欲 (*yù bù yù*) may be (a) "desires to be without desire" or (b) "desires what [others] do not desire". Heshang Gong takes (b): 聖人欲人所不欲 (*shèng rén yù rén suǒ bù yù* — "the sage desires what people do not desire"). Han Fei's illustration, a man who treasures not accepting a jade, fits both.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 好欲雖㣲争尚為之興 (*hào yù suī wēi zhēng shàng wèi zhī xīng* — "fondness and desire, however slight, are enough to stir up contention").

### Unit 12
> 學不學，復眾人之所過。 (*Xué bù xué, fù zhòng rén zhī suǒ guò.* — "Learns not-learning, and returns to what the many pass by.")
- **Says:** The sage learns 不學 (*bù xué* — "not-learning"), and goes back to, or restores, what the multitude have gone past. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates "learn" inside the object.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; 眾人 (*zhòng rén* — "the many / the multitude")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** (a) 學不學 (*xué bù xué*) as "learns not-learning" or "learns what [others] do not learn". Heshang Gong takes the latter: 聖人學人所不能學 (*shèng rén xué rén suǒ bù néng xué* — "the sage learns what people cannot learn"). (b) 復 (*fù*) as "return to" or "restore / redress". (c) 所過 (*suǒ guò*) as "what [they] pass by / overlook" or "their errors / excesses". Han Fei's text reads 復歸衆人之所過 (*fù guī zhòng rén zhī suǒ guò* — "returns back to what the many pass by"), and in his story the sage takes up what the world neglects. Heshang Gong reads 過 (*guò*) as error: 衆人學問反過本為末過實為華 (*zhòng rén xué wèn fǎn guò běn wéi mò, guò shí wéi huá* — "the many, in their learning, go past the root to the branches, past the fruit to the flowers"). He reads 復 (*fù*) as restoring: 復之者使反本也 (*fù zhī zhě shǐ fǎn běn yě* — "to 'restore' them is to make them return to the root").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 不學而能者自然也……故學不學以復衆人之過 (*bù xué ér néng zhě zì rán yě … gù xué bù xué yǐ fù zhòng rén zhī guò* — "being able without learning is what is so of itself … so one learns not-learning in order to restore the faults of the many").

### Unit 13
> 以輔萬物之自然，而不敢為。 (*Yǐ fǔ wàn wù zhī zì rán, ér bù gǎn wéi.* — "In this way [the sage] supports the self-so of the myriad things, and does not dare to act.")
- **Says:** The sage thereby helps all things be what they are of themselves, and does not presume to act. 不敢 (*bù gǎn* — "not dare"), with 不 (*bù* — "not") negating daring to act.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; 萬物 (*wàn wù* — "the myriad things"), whose 自然 (*zì rán* — "so-of-itself") is supported
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and. 以 (*yǐ* — "thereby / in order to") introduces it, and 而 (*ér* — "and / but") joins the clauses.
- **Open:** (a) 以 (*yǐ*) as purpose: "in order to support". (b) As result or means: "thereby supports". Han Fei's text reads 恃 (*shì* — "rely on"): "relies on the self-so of the myriad things".

# Chapter 65

**The chapter as a whole:** Those of old who were skilled in the Way did not use it to make the people clever but to keep them simple, since people with much cleverness are hard to govern. Governing by cleverness robs a state, and governing without it blesses it. Knowing these two is a standing model, and constantly knowing it is called dark potency, which is deep and far, turns with things, and only then reaches the great accord.

### Unit 1
> 古之善為道者，非以明民，將以愚之。 (*Gǔ zhī shàn wéi dào zhě, fēi yǐ míng mín, jiāng yǐ yú zhī.* — "Those of old who were good at practicing the Way did not use it to make the people bright, but to make them simple.")
- **Says:** The ancients skilled in the Way did not aim at making the people 明 (*míng* — "bright, clear-sighted, clever"). Rather they meant to make them 愚 (*yú* — "simple, artless, foolish"). 非 (*fēi* — "it was not [to]") negates the purpose.
- **Act:** describes a person (the ancients)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 古之善為道者 (*gǔ zhī shàn wéi dào zhě* — "the ancients good at practicing the Way") act on 民 (*mín* — "the people")
- **Force:** past
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 愚 (*yú*) may be (a) "keep ignorant / make foolish" or (b) "make plain, artless, unscheming". Both commentators take (b). Wang Bi: 愚謂無知守真順自然也 (*yú wèi wú zhī shǒu zhēn shùn zì rán yě* — "'simple' means without [scheming] knowledge, keeping to the genuine, following what is so of itself"), and 明謂多見巧詐蔽其樸也 (*míng wèi duō jiàn qiǎo zhà bì qí pǔ yě* — "'bright' means seeing much cunning and deceit, which hides their plainness"). Heshang Gong: 將以道德教民使朴質不詐偽 (*jiāng yǐ dào dé jiào mín shǐ pǔ zhì bù zhà wěi* — "they would teach the people by the Way and potency, making them plain and not false"). 善 (*shàn*) is "good at / skilled in".

### Unit 2
> 民之難治，以其智多。 (*Mín zhī nán zhì, yǐ qí zhì duō.* — "The people are hard to govern because their cleverness is much.")
- **Says:** The reason the people are hard to rule is that they have too much 智 (*zhì* — "cleverness, knowing, scheming").
- **Act:** asserts, through 以 (*yǐ* — "because") as a cause
- **Who acts / of whom:** 民 (*mín* — "the people")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** because. It explains Unit 1, with no connective.
- **Open:** none

### Unit 3
> 故以智治國，國之賊； (*Gù yǐ zhì zhì guó, guó zhī zéi;* — "So governing a state by cleverness is the state's bandit;")
- **Says:** Ruling by cleverness is a robber or injury to the state.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. The ruler, or whoever governs.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** therefore, through 故 (*gù* — "so")
- **Open:** (a) 以智 (*yǐ zhì*) as "by means of cleverness". (b) Heshang Gong: by means of clever men, 使智惠之人治國之政事 (*shǐ zhì huì zhī rén zhì guó zhī zhèng shì* — "having clever people run the state's affairs"). 賊 (*zéi*) may be "thief / bandit" or "harm, injury". Wang Bi glosses 智猶治也 (*zhì yóu zhì yě* — "智 'cleverness' is like 治 'managing'"). He explains that clever control provokes clever evasion: 民知其術防隨而避之 (*mín zhī qí shù fáng suí ér bì zhī* — "the people learn the techniques and guard against them, evading them as they go").

### Unit 4
> 不以智治國，國之福。 (*Bù yǐ zhì zhì guó, guó zhī fú.* — "Not governing a state by cleverness is the state's good fortune.")
- **Says:** Ruling without cleverness is a blessing to the state. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates governing by cleverness.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. The ruler.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** but. It stands in contrast with Unit 3.
- **Open:** none

### Unit 5
> 知此兩者亦稽式。 (*Zhī cǐ liǎng zhě yì jī shì.* — "To know these two is also a model.")
- **Says:** Knowing these two (governing with and without cleverness) is itself a 稽式 (*jī shì* — "standard, pattern, model").
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. The knower, the ruler.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** (a) "Knowing these two is also the model." (b) "These two are also the model; know them." (c) "Know these two and you also know the model." 亦 (*yì* — "also") is loose here. Wang Bi glosses 稽同也 (*jī tóng yě* — "稽 means 'the same / shared'"): 今古之所同則不可廢 (*jīn gǔ zhī suǒ tóng zé bù kě fèi* — "what past and present share cannot be discarded"). Heshang Gong reads 楷式 (*kǎi shì* — "model, pattern").

### Unit 6
> 常知稽式，是謂玄德。 (*Cháng zhī jī shì, shì wèi xuán dé.* — "Constantly knowing the model — this is called dark potency.")
- **Says:** Always knowing this pattern is named 玄德 (*xuán dé* — "dark / mysterious potency").
- **Act:** names/defines, through 是謂 (*shì wèi* — "this is called")
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 玄 (*xuán*) may be "dark / mysterious / profound", or, for Heshang Gong, heaven: 玄天也……是謂與天同德也 (*xuán tiān yě … shì wèi yǔ tiān tóng dé yě* — "玄 means heaven … this is called having the same potency as heaven").

### Unit 7
> 玄德深矣，遠矣，與物反矣，然後乃至大順。 (*Xuán dé shēn yǐ, yuǎn yǐ, yǔ wù fǎn yǐ, rán hòu nǎi zhì dà shùn.* — "Dark potency is deep, is far, turns back with things; only then does it reach the great accord.")
- **Says:** Dark potency is deep and distant, and it 反 (*fǎn* — "turns back / reverses") along with things. Only after that does one arrive at 大順 (*dà shùn* — "great accord / great compliance / the great flow").
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 玄德 (*xuán dé* — "dark potency"); 物 (*wù* — "things")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and. Inside the unit, 然後乃 (*rán hòu nǎi* — "only after that") marks sequence.
- **Open:** 與物反 (*yǔ wù fǎn*) may be (a) "returns together with things" (Wang Bi: 反其真也 (*fǎn qí zhēn yě* — "returns to their genuineness")) or (b) "runs contrary to things" (Heshang Gong: 玄德之人與萬物反異萬物欲益已玄徳施與人也 (*xuán dé zhī rén yǔ wàn wù fǎn yì, wàn wù yù yì jǐ, xuán dé shī yǔ rén yě* — "the person of dark potency is the opposite of the myriad things: the myriad things want to increase themselves; dark potency gives to others")). 大順 (*dà shùn*), for Heshang Gong: 順天理也 (*shùn tiān lǐ yě* — "according with heaven's pattern").

# Chapter 66

**The chapter as a whole:** Rivers and seas are kings of the hundred valleys because they are good at being below them. So whoever would be above the people must speak as one below them, and whoever would lead them must put themselves behind. Then the sage stands above and in front without burdening or harming anyone, the world gladly pushes them forward without tiring, and because they do not contend, no one can contend with them.

### Unit 1
> 江海所以能為百谷王者，以其善下之，故能為百谷王。 (*Jiāng hǎi suǒ yǐ néng wéi bǎi gǔ wáng zhě, yǐ qí shàn xià zhī, gù néng wéi bǎi gǔ wáng.* — "The reason rivers and seas can be king of the hundred valleys is that they are good at being below them; so they can be king of the hundred valleys.")
- **Says:** Rivers and seas are able to be 王 (*wáng* — "king") over all the valleys because they are skilled at lying lower than them, and that is why they can be king.
- **Act:** asserts. 所以…者 (*suǒ yǐ … zhě* — "the reason that …") gives an explanation, closed by 故 (*gù* — "so").
- **Who acts / of whom:** 江海 (*jiāng hǎi* — "rivers and seas"); 百谷 (*bǎi gǔ* — "the hundred valleys")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 江海以卑故衆流歸之若民歸就王 (*jiāng hǎi yǐ bēi gù zhòng liú guī zhī ruò mín guī jiù wáng* — "rivers and seas are low, so the many streams return to them, as the people go over to a king").

### Unit 2
> 是以欲上民，必以言下之； (*Shì yǐ yù shàng mín, bì yǐ yán xià zhī;* — "Therefore, wanting to be above the people, one must with one's words go below them;")
- **Says:** If one wishes to stand above the people, one must in one's speech place oneself beneath them.
- **Act:** if/when … then, with 必 (*bì* — "must")
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. Whoever would rule. Heshang Gong's lemma supplies 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage"): 是以聖人欲上民 (*shì yǐ shèng rén yù shàng mín* — "therefore the sage, wanting to be above the people").
- **Force:** must/necessary
- **Link:** therefore, through 是以 (*shì yǐ* — "therefore")
- **Open:** none

### Unit 3
> 欲先民，必以身後之。 (*Yù xiān mín, bì yǐ shēn hòu zhī.* — "Wanting to be ahead of the people, one must with one's person go behind them.")
- **Says:** If one wishes to lead the people, one must with one's own self stand behind them.
- **Act:** if/when … then, with 必 (*bì* — "must")
- **Who acts / of whom:** as in Unit 2
- **Force:** must/necessary
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 先人而後己也 (*xiān rén ér hòu jǐ yě* — "putting others first and oneself after").

### Unit 4
> 是以聖人處上而民不重，處前而民不害。 (*Shì yǐ shèng rén chǔ shàng ér mín bù zhòng, chǔ qián ér mín bù hài.* — "Therefore the sage dwells above and the people do not feel the weight; dwells in front and the people do not feel harm.")
- **Says:** So the sage occupies the high place yet the people do not find them heavy, and occupies the front yet the people do not suffer. 不 (*bù* — "not") appears twice, negating 重 (*zhòng* — "heavy / burdened") and 害 (*hài* — "harm").
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage"); 民 (*mín* — "the people")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore, through 是以 (*shì yǐ* — "therefore")
- **Open:** 民不害 (*mín bù hài*) may be (a) "the people are not harmed", (b) "the people do not regard [the sage] as harmful", or (c) "the people do not seek to harm [the sage]". Heshang Gong takes (c): 民親之若父母無有欲害之心也 (*mín qīn zhī ruò fù mǔ, wú yǒu yù hài zhī xīn yě* — "the people love them as father and mother and have no wish to harm them"). For 重 (*zhòng*), Heshang Gong: 故民戴而不為重 (*gù mín dài ér bù wéi zhòng* — "so the people bear them up and do not find them heavy").

### Unit 5
> 是以天下樂推而不厭。 (*Shì yǐ tiān xià lè tuī ér bù yàn.* — "Therefore all under heaven gladly pushes [them] forward and does not tire.")
- **Says:** The whole world delights in promoting the sage and never wearies of it. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 厭 (*yàn* — "weary of / sated with").
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天下 (*tiān xià* — "all under heaven"); the sage is the unstated object
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore, through 是以 (*shì yǐ* — "therefore")
- **Open:** 厭 (*yàn*) as "tire of / be sated with" or "reject / feel oppressed by".

### Unit 6
> 以其不爭，故天下莫能與之爭。 (*Yǐ qí bù zhēng, gù tiān xià mò néng yǔ zhī zhēng.* — "Because they do not contend, no one in all under heaven can contend with them.")
- **Says:** Since the sage does not compete, nobody in the world is able to compete with them. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates contending. 莫 (*mò* — "no one") negates the existence of a rival.
- **Act:** asserts, through 以…故 (*yǐ … gù* — "because … therefore")
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage (其, *qí* — "they"); 天下 (*tiān xià* — "all under heaven"), no one in it
- **Force:** can/possible (negated)
- **Link:** because / therefore
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 由聖人不與人爭先後也 (*yóu shèng rén bù yǔ rén zhēng xiān hòu yě* — "it is because the sage does not contend with others over first and last").

# Chapter 67

**The chapter as a whole:** The speaker's Way is called great and seems to resemble nothing, and it seems so precisely because it is great: had it resembled something, it would long since have become petty. The speaker keeps three treasures: caring love, frugality, and not daring to be first in the world. These make one brave, broad, and head of the vessels, while dropping them for their opposites is death. Caring love wins in battle and holds in defense, and when heaven means to save someone, it guards them with it.

### Unit 1
> 天下皆謂我道大，似不肖。 (*Tiān xià jiē wèi wǒ dào dà, sì bù xiào.* — "All under heaven say my Way is great, and seems like nothing.")
- **Says:** Everyone says the speaker's Way is great, and that it seems 不肖 (*bù xiào* — "not resembling [anything] / unworthy"). 不 (*bù* — "not") negates resembling.
- **Act:** asserts (reported speech)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天下 (*tiān xià* — "all under heaven") say it; 我道 (*wǒ dào* — "my Way") is described; 我 (*wǒ* — "I"), the speaker
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** (a) 不肖 (*bù xiào*) as "resembles nothing / unlike anything" or "unworthy, worthless" (its common sense, as of an unworthy son). (b) The second clause may be part of what people say ("great, yet seeming unlike anything") or the speaker's own remark. (c) Heshang Gong's lemma lacks 道 (*dào* — "Way"): 天下皆謂我大 (*tiān xià jiē wèi wǒ dà* — "all under heaven say I am great"). He reads it as feigned foolishness: 我則佯愚似不肖 (*wǒ zé yáng yú sì bù xiào* — "so I feign foolishness and seem unworthy").

### Unit 2
> 夫唯大，故似不肖。 (*Fú wéi dà, gù sì bù xiào.* — "It is just because it is great that it seems like nothing.")
- **Says:** Precisely its greatness is why it seems unlike anything. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates resembling.
- **Act:** asserts, through 夫唯…故 (*fú wéi … gù* — "it is just because … therefore")
- **Who acts / of whom:** the Way (or the speaker)
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** because. It explains Unit 1.
- **Open:** as in Unit 1, for 不肖 (*bù xiào*)

### Unit 3
> 若肖，久矣其細也夫！ (*Ruò xiào, jiǔ yǐ qí xì yě fú!* — "If it resembled something, long ago it would have been small!")
- **Says:** Had it resembled anything, it would long since have become petty. The word order is inverted for exclamation, closing on 也夫 (*yě fú* — exclamatory particles).
- **Act:** if/when … then (counterfactual), exclaimed
- **Who acts / of whom:** the Way
- **Force:** past (counterfactual)
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** (a) Wang Bi reads it as a counterfactual: 久矣其細猶曰其細久矣肖則失其所以為大矣 (*jiǔ yǐ qí xì yóu yuē qí xì jiǔ yǐ; xiào zé shī qí suǒ yǐ wéi dà yǐ* — "'long, its smallness' is like saying 'it would have been small for long'; if it resembled, it would lose what makes it great"). (b) Heshang Gong glosses 肖善也謂辨惠也 (*xiào shàn yě, wèi biàn huì yě* — "肖 means 'good', meaning discerning cleverness"). He reads it as a remark on clever people: 言辨惠者唯如小人也 (*yán biàn huì zhě wéi rú xiǎo rén yě* — "it says the clever are only like petty people").

### Unit 4
> 我有三寶，持而保之：一曰慈，二曰儉，三曰不敢為天下先。 (*Wǒ yǒu sān bǎo, chí ér bǎo zhī: yī yuē cí, èr yuē jiǎn, sān yuē bù gǎn wéi tiān xià xiān.* — "I have three treasures, which I hold and keep: the first is called caring love, the second frugality, the third not daring to be first in all under heaven.")
- **Says:** The speaker has three treasures and holds them fast. They are named 慈 (*cí* — "tender love, as a parent's for a child; compassion"), 儉 (*jiǎn* — "frugality, restraint, economy"), and not daring to put oneself ahead of the world. In the third, 不敢 (*bù gǎn* — "not dare"), with 不 (*bù* — "not"), negates daring.
- **Act:** names/defines, through 曰 (*yuē* — "is called") three times
- **Who acts / of whom:** 我 (*wǒ* — "I"), the speaker
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none. The topic turns.
- **Open:** 慈 (*cí*) as "parental / motherly love" in its root sense, or the wider "compassion, kindness". Heshang Gong: 愛百姓若赤子 (*ài bǎi xìng ruò chì zǐ* — "loving the common people as newborn children"). Heshang Gong's lemma reads 持而寳之 (*chí ér bǎo zhī* — "hold and treasure them"), and Han Fei's reads 吾有三寶，持而寶之 (*wú yǒu sān bǎo, chí ér bǎo zhī* — "I have three treasures, I hold and treasure them"). For 儉 (*jiǎn*), Heshang Gong: 賦儉若取之於己也 (*fù jiǎn ruò qǔ zhī yú jǐ yě* — "taxing sparingly, as if taking from oneself"). For the third: 執謙退不爲倡始也 (*zhí qiān tuì bù wéi chàng shǐ yě* — "holding to humble withdrawal, not being the one to start things").

### Unit 5
> 慈故能勇； (*Cí gù néng yǒng;* — "Caring love, therefore able to be brave;")
- **Says:** Because of caring love, one can be courageous.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. The speaker, or whoever holds the treasure.
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** and. 故 (*gù* — "therefore") comes inside the unit.
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 夫慈以陳則勝以守則固故能勇也 (*fú cí yǐ zhèn zé shèng, yǐ shǒu zé gù, gù néng yǒng yě* — "caring love, in battle array, wins, and in defense holds firm; so it can be brave"). Heshang Gong: 以為仁故能勇於忠孝也 (*yǐ wéi rén gù néng yǒng yú zhōng xiào yě* — "being humane, one can be brave in loyalty and filial duty").

### Unit 6
> 儉故能廣； (*Jiǎn gù néng guǎng;* — "Frugal, therefore able to be broad;")
- **Says:** Because of frugality, one can be expansive or ample.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** as in Unit 5
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 廣 (*guǎng* — "broad") may be "generous, giving widely" or "having abundance / reaching widely". Wang Bi: 節儉愛費天下不匱故能廣也 (*jié jiǎn ài fèi, tiān xià bù kuì, gù néng guǎng yě* — "restrained and sparing of expense, the world does not run short; so it can be broad"). Heshang Gong: 天子身能節儉故民日用廣矣 (*tiān zǐ shēn néng jié jiǎn gù mín rì yòng guǎng yǐ* — "if the Son of Heaven himself is frugal, the people's daily means are ample").

### Unit 7
> 不敢為天下先，故能成器長。 (*Bù gǎn wéi tiān xià xiān, gù néng chéng qì zhǎng.* — "Not daring to be first in all under heaven, therefore able to become head of the vessels.")
- **Says:** Because one does not dare to put oneself ahead of the world, one can become 器長 (*qì zhǎng* — "the head of the vessels"). 不 (*bù* — "not") negates daring.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** as in Unit 5
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** (a) 成器長 (*chéng qì zhǎng*) as "head of the completed vessels", with vessels standing for officials, talents or things. (b) "Become a complete vessel and a leader". Wang Bi's note says Han Fei reads 事 (*shì* — "affairs"): 成事長 (*chéng shì zhǎng* — "head of accomplished affairs"). Han Fei glosses it 處大官之謂爲成事長 (*chǔ dà guān zhī wèi wéi chéng shì zhǎng* — "holding high office is what is meant by head of accomplished affairs"). Wang Bi: 乃能立成器為天下利為物之長也 (*nǎi néng lì chéng qì wéi tiān xià lì, wéi wù zhī zhǎng yě* — "then one can set up completed vessels for the world's benefit and be the head of things"). Heshang Gong: 成器長謂得道人也 (*chéng qì zhǎng wèi dé dào rén yě* — "'head of completed vessels' means [the head of] people who have attained the Way").

### Unit 8
> 今捨慈且勇，捨儉且廣，捨後且先，死矣！ (*Jīn shě cí qiě yǒng, shě jiǎn qiě guǎng, shě hòu qiě xiān, sǐ yǐ!* — "Now to abandon caring love and yet be brave, abandon frugality and yet be broad, abandon being behind and yet be first — that is death!")
- **Says:** To discard each treasure while grasping at the result it alone makes possible is fatal.
- **Act:** asserts, exclaimed through 矣 (*yǐ* — emphatic final particle)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. People nowadays (Heshang Gong: 今世人 (*jīn shì rén* — "people of the present age")).
- **Force:** habitual (present), with certainty
- **Link:** but. It stands in contrast with Units 5–7. 今 (*jīn* — "now / nowadays") opens it.
- **Open:** 且 (*qiě*) as "and yet / while still", or Wang Bi's 且猶取也 (*qiě yóu qǔ yě* — "且 is like 'take'"): "abandon caring love and take up bravery". 死矣 (*sǐ yǐ* — "dead!") as "that is death" or "they will die".

### Unit 9
> 夫慈，以戰則勝，以守則固。 (*Fú cí, yǐ zhàn zé shèng, yǐ shǒu zé gù.* — "Now caring love: fight with it and you win; defend with it and you hold firm.")
- **Says:** If caring love is used in battle, one wins; if used in defense, one stands firm.
- **Act:** if/when … then, through 則 (*zé* — "then") twice
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. Whoever fights or defends with it.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none. 夫 (*fú* — "now") resumes the topic.
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 相慜而不避於難故勝也 (*xiāng mǐn ér bù bì yú nàn gù shèng yě* — "they feel for one another and do not shrink from danger, so they win"). Heshang Gong: 百姓親附并心一意 (*bǎi xìng qīn fù bìng xīn yī yì* — "the common people draw close, joined in one heart and one mind"). Han Fei's lemma reads 於戰 (*yú zhàn* — "in battle").

### Unit 10
> 天將救之，以慈衛之。 (*Tiān jiāng jiù zhī, yǐ cí wèi zhī.* — "When heaven is about to save someone, it guards them with caring love.")
- **Says:** Whom heaven means to rescue, it protects by means of caring love.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天 (*tiān* — "heaven"); 之 (*zhī* — "them"), unstated who
- **Force:** will/future, through 將 (*jiāng* — "about to / will")
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** (a) Heaven itself guards them with caring love. (b) Heaven gives them caring love so that they guard themselves. Heshang Gong takes (b): 天將救助善人必與慈仁之性使能自當助也 (*tiān jiāng jiù zhù shàn rén bì yǔ cí rén zhī xìng shǐ néng zì dāng zhù yě* — "when heaven is about to save and help a good person, it surely gives them a nature of caring love and humaneness, so that they can help themselves"). (c) The second clause may be read with a new subject: "[the one heaven saves] guards with caring love".

# Chapter 68

**The chapter as a whole:** The best officer is not martial, the best fighter is not angry, the best victor does not engage, and the best employer of people goes beneath them. This is named the potency of not contending, the use of others' strength, and the matching of heaven, the ultimate of old.

### Unit 1
> 善為士者，不武； (*Shàn wéi shì zhě, bù wǔ;* — "One good at being an officer is not martial;")
- **Says:** One skilled at being a 士 (*shì* — "officer, man-at-arms, knight") is not 武 (*wǔ* — "martial, warlike, aggressive"). 不 (*bù* — "not") negates the quality.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 善為士者 (*shàn wéi shì zhě* — "one good at being a 士")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 士 (*shì*) may be "warrior / officer" (Wang Bi: 士卒之帥也 (*shì zú zhī shuài yě* — "[it means] the commander of soldiers")) or "gentleman, scholar-official". 不武 (*bù wǔ*) may be "not warlike" or "does not use force". Wang Bi: 武尚先陵人也 (*wǔ shàng xiān líng rén yě* — "'martial' means prizing being first and overrunning others").

### Unit 2
> 善戰者，不怒； (*Shàn zhàn zhě, bù nù;* — "One good at fighting does not rage;")
- **Says:** A skilled fighter does not get angry. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates the action.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 善戰者 (*shàn zhàn zhě* — "one good at fighting")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 後而不先應而不唱故不在怒 (*hòu ér bù xiān, yìng ér bù chàng, gù bù zài nù* — "they come after and not first, respond and do not lead, so anger plays no part").

### Unit 3
> 善勝敵者，不與； (*Shàn shèng dí zhě, bù yǔ;* — "One good at defeating the enemy does not engage;")
- **Says:** One skilled at overcoming an enemy does not 與 (*yǔ* — "join with / engage / take on") them. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates the action.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 善勝敵者 (*shàn shèng dí zhě* — "one good at defeating enemies"); the enemy is the implied object
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 不與 (*bù yǔ*) may be (a) "does not engage / contend with them" (Wang Bi: 不與争也 (*bù yǔ zhēng yě* — "does not contend with them"); Heshang Gong: 不與敵爭而敵自服也 (*bù yǔ dí zhēng ér dí zì fú yě* — "does not contend with the enemy, and the enemy submits of itself")), (b) "does not join battle", or (c) "does not ally / take part". Heshang Gong's lemma reads 善勝戰者 (*shàn shèng zhàn zhě* — "one good at winning battles").

### Unit 4
> 善用人者，為之下。 (*Shàn yòng rén zhě, wéi zhī xià.* — "One good at employing people goes beneath them.")
- **Says:** One skilled at using people places themselves below those people.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 善用人者 (*shàn yòng rén zhě* — "one good at employing people"); 之 (*zhī* — "them"), the people employed
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong's lemma lacks 之 (*zhī* — "them"): 善用人者為下 (*shàn yòng rén zhě wéi xià* — "one good at employing people is low"). He reads it 常為人執謙下也 (*cháng wèi rén zhí qiān xià yě* — "constantly holds to humility toward others").

### Unit 5
> 是謂不爭之德，是謂用人之力，是謂配天，古之極。 (*Shì wèi bù zhēng zhī dé, shì wèi yòng rén zhī lì, shì wèi pèi tiān, gǔ zhī jí.* — "This is called the potency of not contending; this is called using others' strength; this is called matching heaven, the ultimate of old.")
- **Says:** Three namings. This is the 德 (*dé* — "potency / virtue") of not contending. It is the use of others' strength. It is 配天 (*pèi tiān* — "matching / being a counterpart to heaven"), the 極 (*jí* — "utmost / ridgepole") of antiquity. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates contending.
- **Act:** names/defines, through 是謂 (*shì wèi* — "this is called") three times
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. The skilled ones of Units 1–4.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none. It sums up.
- **Open:** (a) 用人之力 (*yòng rén zhī lì*) may be "using the strength of others" or "the power of [knowing how to] employ people". Heshang Gong: 能身為人下是謂用人臣之力 (*néng shēn wéi rén xià shì wèi yòng rén chén zhī lì* — "being able to put oneself below others is called using the strength of one's ministers"). (b) 配天古之極 (*pèi tiān gǔ zhī jí*) may be two items, "matching heaven; the ultimate of old", or one phrase, "matching the ancient ultimate of heaven". Heshang Gong splits them: 能行此者德配天地 (*néng xíng cǐ zhě dé pèi tiān dì* — "one who can practice this has potency matching heaven and earth"), and 是乃古之極要道也 (*shì nǎi gǔ zhī jí yào dào yě* — "this is the supreme essential Way of antiquity").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 用人而不為之下則力不為用也 (*yòng rén ér bù wéi zhī xià zé lì bù wéi yòng yě* — "if one employs people but does not go beneath them, their strength is not put to use").
