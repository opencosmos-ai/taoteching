## Summary, deepest first

1. **Ch 30 · CLOSED · major** · 其事好還 (qí shì hào huán — "their affairs tend to turn back") → *"Such undertakings tend to come back on you"*: both commentators read the Tao-holder turning back, and the English takes the recoil reading. Nothing records that.
2. **Ch 53 · CLOSED · major** · 唯施是畏 (wéi shī shì wèi — "only acting is what I fear") → *"my only fear would be to stray from it"*: both commentators read 施 (shī — to act) as taking action, and the English takes the swerve reading. Not logged.
3. **Ch 52 · OPAQUE · major · logged** · 是謂襲常 (shì wèi xí cháng — "this is called cloaking the ever-present") → *"This is called the ever-present worn covered"*: Reader A could not parse it at all.
4. **Ch 32 · FORCE · minor** · 夫亦將知止 (fū yì jiāng zhī zhǐ — "one too will know to stop") → *"one must know when to stop"*: a promise becomes an obligation, against the same 夫亦將 (fū yì jiāng — "they too will") frame at ch 37.
5. **Ch 30 · LINK · minor** · 果而不得已 (guǒ ér bù dé yǐ — "succeed, as what could not be avoided") → *"They succeed because their actions are unavoidable"*: a restriction becomes a cause.
6. **Ch 30 · ACT · minor** · 果而勿▢ (guǒ ér wù ▢ — "succeed, and do not ▢") ×4 → *"They succeed without ▢"*: four prohibitions become a description, and the commentators divide on which it is.
7. **Ch 29 · CLOSED · minor** · 吾見其不得已 (wú jiàn qí bù dé yǐ — "I see they will not get it" / "I see they are driven to it") → *"I see they will not succeed"*, while the same 不得已 (bù dé yǐ — "it cannot be helped") is *unavoidable* at 30 and 31.
8. **Ch 42 · CLOSED · minor** · 人之所教，我亦教之 (rén zhī suǒ jiào, wǒ yì jiào zhī — "what others teach, I also teach") with a colon after it: Heshang Gong reads the speaker teaching the opposite, and the colon shuts that reading out.
9. **Ch 50 · CLOSED · FORCE · minor** · 陸行不遇…入軍不被甲兵 (lù xíng bù yù… rù jūn bù pèi jiǎ bīng — "travels by land and does not meet…, enters an army and does not put on / is not reached by armor and weapons") → *"can travel… untouched by armor or weapons"*: Han Fei's *unarmed* is lost, and *does* becomes *can*.
10. **Ch 54 · CLOSED · minor** · 善建者不拔 (shàn jiàn zhě bù bá — "one masterful at planting is not uprooted") → *"What is masterfully planted cannot be uprooted"*: the person Heshang Gong and Han Fei read becomes a thing.
11. **Ch 28 · ACT · minor** · 復歸於▢ (fù guī yú ▢ — "and [one] returns again to ▢") ×3 → *"Return again to ▢."*: the outcome becomes a fourth command.
12. **Ch 34 · FORCE · minor** · 故能成其大 (gù néng chéng qí dà — "so it can complete its greatness") → *"and so completes its greatness"*: 能 (néng — can) is dropped, as at ch 63, and like ch 7 in the accuracy audit's smaller table.
13. **Ch 37 · OPENED · minor** · 化而欲作 (huà ér yù zuò — "as they transform, desire stirs") → *"When the countless things urge action"*: lets the things press the ruler to act.
14. **Ch 48 · WHO · minor** · *"The world always comes to you by not meddling with it"*: the grammar makes the world the one who does not meddle. The rendering is logged; the dangling modifier is not.
15. **Ch 39 · ACT · minor · logged** · 其致之 (qí zhì zhī — "they brought it about by it" / "the warning") → *"Follow it through:"*: Reader A heard an instruction to the reader, which neither commentator reads.
16. **Ch 29 · OPENED · minor · logged** · 天下神器 (tiān xià shén qì — "the world is a numinous vessel") → *"sacred vessel"*: Reader A heard *holy*, *religious*. Evidence for T2-37.

## The entries

### Ch 30 · Unit refs (A 2 / B 2) · CLOSED · major
- **Chinese:** 其事好還 (qí shì hào huán — "its / their affairs tend to turn back")
- **English:** "Such undertakings tend to come back on you."
- **Reader A heard:** military ventures rebound on whoever undertakes them.
- **Reader B read:** open three ways: (a) war recoils on the one who wages it; (b) Wang Bi: the Tao-holder strives to return to non-doing; (c) Heshang Gong: the Tao-holder turns back and blames themselves.
- **The divergence:** Neither commentator reads recoil. Both take 其 (qí — their) as the Tao-guided helper of the line above, and 還 (huán — to turn back) as that person's turning back. Wang Bi: 為始者務欲立功生事，而有道者務欲還反無為，故云其事好還也 (wéi shǐ zhě wù yù lì gōng shēng shì, ér yǒu dào zhě wù yù huán fǎn wú wéi, gù yún qí shì hào huán yě — "those who start things strive to establish merit and create affairs, but one who has the Tao strives to turn back to non-doing; so it says 'their affairs tend to turn back'"). Heshang Gong: 其舉事好還自責，不怨於人也 (qí jǔ shì hào huán zì zé, bù yuàn yú rén yě — "in what they undertake they tend to turn back and blame themselves, and do not resent others"). The recoil reading is grammatical and the usual modern one, after 兵強天下 (bīng qiáng tiān xià — "forcing the world with arms"). But the English supplies *such undertakings* and *on you*, which closes the line onto that reading. A reader comes away with a warning about consequences. The two oldest readers have a description of the sage's restraint.
- **Logged?** The reading is assumed but not argued. `glossary/shi-事.md` lists it as *"what rebounds on you"*, and `notes/reading.md` → *the Tao is a road* says *"Violence is a road that loops."* Neither mentions that both commentators read it the other way. The chapter's own notes are silent.
- **Direction:** Either log the split in ch 30's notes and keep the line, or render the bare figure, as in *"Such affairs tend to turn back"*, which holds both readings.

### Ch 53 · Unit refs (A 1 / B 1) · CLOSED · major
- **Chinese:** 使我介然有知，行於大道，唯施是畏 (shǐ wǒ jiè rán yǒu zhī, xíng yú dà dào, wéi shī shì wèi — "if I had a [slight / firm] knowing, walking the great Tao, only 施 (shī — acting) is what I would fear")
- **English:** "If I had just a scrap of knowledge, walking the path of the great Tao, my only fear would be to stray from it."
- **Reader A heard:** I would fear only going off the path.
- **Reader B read:** both commentators read 施 (shī) as acting. The swerve sense (a loan for 迤, yǐ — to slant, to veer) is supported only by 徑 (jìng — byway) in the next line.
- **The divergence:** The commentators agree with each other, against the English. Wang Bi: 唯施為之是畏也 (wéi shī wéi zhī shì wèi yě — "only taking action is what I fear"). On the next line he adds 況復施為以塞大道之中乎 (kuàng fù shī wéi yǐ sè dà dào zhī zhōng hū — "how much worse to go further and take action, blocking the middle of the great Tao"). Heshang Gong: 獨畏有所施為，失道意 (dú wèi yǒu suǒ shī wéi, shī dào yì — "I fear only that taking some action would lose the Tao's intent"). On their reading the line is about non-doing: even someone on the road should fear acting. The English makes it a fear of getting lost. It is a defensible philological choice, but it is a choice against every commentary in the repository, and nothing records it. A second, smaller choice rides in the same line: 介然 (jiè rán — slight / firm). Heshang Gong glosses it 介，大也 (jiè, dà yě — "*jiè* means great"), and the English *"just a scrap"* takes the opposite pole without a note.
- **Logged?** No. `glossary/wei-唯.md` cites the line only for the grammar of 唯 (wéi — only).
- **Direction:** Either follow the commentators (*"my only fear would be to act"*), or keep *stray* and log the 迤 (yǐ — to veer) reading, with 徑 (jìng — byway) as its evidence and both commentators against it. Note 介然 (jiè rán — slight / firm) while there.

### Ch 52 · Unit refs (A 9 / B 8) · OPAQUE · major · logged
- **Chinese:** 是謂襲常 (shì wèi xí cháng — "this is called cloaking / inheriting the ever-present")
- **English:** "This is called the ever-present worn covered."
- **Reader A heard:** nothing settled: *"grammatically opaque… Reader cannot settle it"*. A offered three guesses: a garment worn underneath, practising the constant under cover, and *worn* as *habitual*.
- **Reader B read:** cloaking oneself in, inheriting, or stealing into the ever-present. Both commentaries print 習 (xí — to practise).
- **The divergence:** The choice is argued: 襲 (xí — a robe worn over another) gives *covered*, matching ch 27's 是謂襲明 (shì wèi xí míng — "this is called clarity worn covered"). But the English phrase does not parse as a phrase. *Worn* reads as *worn out* as easily as *worn as clothing*, and *covered* hangs loose. The chapter's closing name reaches a careful reader as noise. This is evidence that the choice costs the line its claim, not that the choice is wrong.
- **Logged?** Yes: ch 52's notes (Pass C, T2-10; Pass E, T2-19), `sources/variants.yaml`, `notes/manuscript.md`.
- **Direction:** Keep 襲 (xí) and *covered*, but give English a syntax it can parse, for example *"This is called wearing the ever-present beneath"*. Change ch 27 with it.

### Ch 32 · Unit refs (A 7 / B 5) · FORCE · minor
- **Chinese:** 名亦既有，夫亦將知止 (míng yì jì yǒu, fū yì jiāng zhī zhǐ — "names having come to exist, one too will know to stop")
- **English:** "Since names already exist, one must know when to stop."
- **Reader A heard:** an obligation (*"must/necessary"*).
- **Reader B read:** will/future (將, jiāng — will), open between *will know* and *must know*.
- **The divergence:** 將 (jiāng — will) is a prediction, and *must* turns it into a duty. 亦 (yì — too) is also dropped. The same three-character frame, 夫亦將 (fū yì jiāng — "they too will"), opens ch 37's 夫亦將無欲 (fū yì jiāng wú yù — "they too will be without desire"), where the accuracy audit (finding 7) proposes restoring *will*. If that lands, the book will read 夫亦將 (fū yì jiāng — "they too will") as *must* at 32 and *will* at 37. Wang Bi gives the line as a consequence of what comes past the first cut: 過此以往，將爭錐刀之末，故曰名亦既有，夫亦將知止也 (guò cǐ yǐ wǎng, jiāng zhēng zhuī dāo zhī mò, gù yuē míng yì jì yǒu, fū yì jiāng zhī zhǐ yě — "go on past this and they will fight over the tip of an awl; so it says: names having come to exist, one too will know to stop").
- **Logged?** No. The source table's literal gloss already prints *"one must"*. The 可以 (kě yǐ — can) → 所以 (suǒ yǐ — that by which) fork in the next clause is logged and is not this.
- **Direction:** *"Since names already exist, one too will know when to stop."* Let it ride with audit finding 7.

### Ch 30 · Unit refs (A 7 / B 7) · LINK · minor
- **Chinese:** 果而不得已 (guǒ ér bù dé yǐ — "succeed, as what could not be avoided")
- **English:** "They succeed because their actions are unavoidable."
- **Reader A heard:** three readings: they succeed because they act only when they must; their success is itself inevitable; they succeed only where it cannot be avoided.
- **Reader B read:** get the result only because there was no choice.
- **The divergence:** The Chinese restricts. The masterful one succeeds only where force cannot be avoided. Wang Bi: 然時故不得已當復用者 (rán shí gù bù dé yǐ dāng fù yòng zhě — "but when the times leave no choice and troops must be used again"). The English *because* makes unavoidability the cause of success, and *their actions are unavoidable* states that everything they do is necessary. One of A's three readings, fatalism, is not in the line. The form audit glosses the clause *"succeed only because it is unavoidable"*, which shows the restricting *only* that the verse lacks.
- **Logged?** No.
- **Direction:** *"They succeed only where it cannot be avoided."*

### Ch 30 · Unit refs (A 6, 8 / B 6, 7) · ACT · minor
- **Chinese:** 果而勿矜，果而勿伐，果而勿驕…果而勿強 (guǒ ér wù jīn, guǒ ér wù fá, guǒ ér wù jiāo … guǒ ér wù qiáng — "succeed, and do not preen; succeed, and do not boast; succeed, and do not be arrogant … succeed, and do not force")
- **English:** "They succeed without boasting, succeed without bragging, succeed without arrogance. … They succeed without using excessive force."
- **Reader A heard:** a description of the masterful one.
- **Reader B read:** prohibitions, from 勿 (wù — do not), with a descriptive reading possible.
- **The divergence:** 勿 (wù — do not) is the prohibitive, and the commentators divide. Heshang Gong is prescriptive: 當果敢謙卑，勿自矜大也 (dāng guǒ gǎn qiān bēi, wù zì jīn dà yě — "one should be resolute and humble, and not puff oneself up"). Wang Bi is descriptive: 吾不以師道為尚，不得已而用，何矜驕之有也 (wú bù yǐ shī dào wéi shàng, bù dé yǐ ér yòng, hé jīn jiāo zhī yǒu yě — "I do not esteem the way of armies; using it only when unavoidable, what pride or arrogance would there be?"). The English takes Wang Bi's side without saying so. The form audit's own gloss for the frame is the imperative *"succeed, and do not ▢"*.
- **Logged?** No. Accuracy-audit finding 5's proposed *"They succeed without forcing"* keeps the description, so it would carry this choice forward.
- **Direction:** Keep the description and add a note naming Heshang Gong's prohibitive reading. Or turn the series: *"Succeed, but do not preen…"*.

### Ch 29 · Unit refs (A 1 / B 1) · CLOSED · minor
- **Chinese:** 將欲取天下而為之，吾見其不得已 (jiāng yù qǔ tiān xià ér wéi zhī, wú jiàn qí bù dé yǐ — "those who would take the world and handle it, I see they will not get it" / "I see they are driven to it")
- **English:** "Those who would take the world and handle it, / I see they will not succeed."
- **Reader A heard:** they will fail.
- **Reader B read:** open. (a) 已 (yǐ) is a final particle: *will not get it*, which is Heshang Gong's 不得天道人心 (bù dé tiān dào rén xīn — "they do not get heaven's way and people's hearts"). (b) The fixed phrase *cannot help it, has no choice*.
- **The divergence:** The English takes (a), with Heshang Gong, and the reading is sound. But the same three characters, 不得已 (bù dé yǐ — "it cannot be helped"), are rendered *unavoidable* in the next two chapters (30: *"their actions are unavoidable"*; 31: *"Used only when unavoidable"*). A reader of the Chinese meets one phrase three times. A reader of the English meets two unrelated ideas, and nothing says the first was read differently on purpose.
- **Logged?** No.
- **Direction:** Keep (a) and note the fork in ch 29's notes, naming the 30/31 phrase. One line would do.

### Ch 42 · Unit refs (A 6–7 / B 5–6) · CLOSED · minor
- **Chinese:** 人之所教，我亦教之。強梁者不得其死 (rén zhī suǒ jiào, wǒ yì jiào zhī. qiáng liáng zhě bù dé qí sǐ — "what others teach, I also teach. The violent do not get their [natural] death")
- **English:** "What others teach, I also teach: / The strong and rigid do not die a natural death."
- **Reader A heard:** the speaker teaches the same lesson as everyone else, and the lesson is that the strong die badly.
- **Reader B read:** open. (a) The same lesson. (b) Heshang Gong: I teach the crowd the opposite.
- **The divergence:** The commentators divide. Heshang Gong: 謂衆人所以教去弱為強，去柔為剛 (wèi zhòng rén suǒ yǐ jiào qù ruò wéi qiáng, qù róu wéi gāng — "what the crowd teaches: leave the weak to become strong, leave the soft to become hard"), and 言我教衆人使去強為弱，去剛為柔 (yán wǒ jiào zhòng rén shǐ qù qiáng wéi ruò, qù gāng wéi róu — "I teach the crowd to leave the strong for the weak, the hard for the soft"). Wang Bi reads one lesson shared: 人相教違之，自取其凶也，亦如我之教人勿違之也 (rén xiāng jiào wéi zhī, zì qǔ qí xiōng yě, yì rú wǒ zhī jiào rén wù wéi zhī yě — "people teach one another to go against it and bring on their own misfortune; it is like my teaching people not to go against it"). The colon goes further than either: it makes the strong-die line the content of what *others* teach.
- **Logged?** No. `glossary/qiang-強.md` → *The commentators name the quartet* quotes Heshang Gong's two opposite curricula under the English *"what others teach, I also teach"*, without noting that they contradict it.
- **Direction:** A full stop for the colon lets the strong-die line stand as the speaker's own teaching. Note Heshang Gong in ch 42.

### Ch 50 · Unit refs (A 7 / B 6) · CLOSED · FORCE · minor
- **Chinese:** 陸行不遇兕虎，入軍不被甲兵 (lù xíng bù yù sì hǔ, rù jūn bù pèi jiǎ bīng — "travelling by land, does not meet rhinoceros or tiger; entering an army, does not put on / is not reached by armor and weapons")
- **English:** "can travel the land without meeting rhinos or tigers, / and can enter into battle untouched by armor or weapons."
- **Reader A heard:** an ability to go unharmed. A also noticed *untouched by armor* could mean *not needing to wear armor*.
- **Reader B read:** 被 (pèi / bèi) is either *to wear* or *to suffer*, and the witnesses divide.
- **The divergence:** There are two shifts. (1) The witnesses split, and the English takes Wang Bi's side, unharmed: 雖入軍而不害 (suī rù jūn ér bù hài — "though entering an army, unharmed"). Han Fei quotes the line as 入軍不備甲兵 (rù jūn bù bèi jiǎ bīng — "enters an army without being equipped with armor and weapons") and explains: 無忿爭之心，則無所用救害之備 (wú fèn zhēng zhī xīn, zé wú suǒ yòng jiù hài zhī bèi — "having no heart for angry contention, one has no use for defenses against harm"). Heshang Gong reads 不避 (bù bì — does not avoid), glossed 不好戰以殺人 (bù hào zhàn yǐ shā rén — "does not love war in order to kill people"). On Han Fei's reading the person is unarmed, not invulnerable, and that is the chapter's point about not living life too thickly. (2) The Chinese states a fact (*does not meet*), and the English twice adds *can*.
- **Logged?** Partly. Ch 50's note (2026-10-14) restores 甲 (jiǎ — armor) and records Heshang Gong's 不避 (bù bì — does not avoid), but not the wear/suffer fork or Han Fei.
- **Direction:** Record the fork. If Han Fei's reading is wanted: *"travels the land without meeting rhinos or tigers, / goes into battle without armor or weapons."*

### Ch 54 · Unit refs (A 1–2 / B 1) · CLOSED · minor
- **Chinese:** 善建者不拔，善抱者不脫 (shàn jiàn zhě bù bá, shàn bào zhě bù tuō — "one masterful at planting is not uprooted; one masterful at embracing does not slip loose" / "what is well planted…")
- **English:** "What is masterfully planted cannot be uprooted. / What is masterfully embraced cannot slip away."
- **Reader A heard:** a well-established thing.
- **Reader B read:** 者 (zhě) makes either the person or the thing.
- **The divergence:** Two old readers make it the person. Heshang Gong: 善以道立身立國者，不可得引而拔也 (shàn yǐ dào lì shēn lì guó zhě, bù kě dé yǐn ér bá yě — "one masterful at establishing self and state by the Tao cannot be pulled up"). Wang Bi's 固其根而後營其末 (gù qí gēn ér hòu yíng qí mò — "make the root firm, then tend the branches") fits the thing. The English takes the thing without a note, and the adverb *masterfully* departs from the lock, which lists ch 54 under 善 (shàn — masterful) + verb → *masterful at* (`glossary/shan-善.md`). 不 (bù — not) also becomes *cannot*.
- **Logged?** No.
- **Direction:** *"One masterful at planting is not uprooted; / one masterful at embracing does not slip loose."* Or keep the thing and log the persons.

### Ch 28 · Unit refs (A 3, 6, 9 / B 2, 4, 6) · ACT · minor

- **Direction:** Join it to the result: *"…never leaves you, / and you return again to the infant."*

### Ch 48 · Unit refs (A 5 / B 4) · WHO · minor
