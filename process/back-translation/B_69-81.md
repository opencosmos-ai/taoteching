# Reader B — chapters 69–81

# Chapter 69

**The chapter as a whole:** A military saying is quoted: the speaker will not be the one who starts the fight, and would rather give ground than take it. This posture is named a moving without moving, an arm with no arm, an enemy with no enemy, weapons with no weapons. The worst disaster is making light of an enemy, which nearly costs "my treasure"; so when armies are matched, the side that grieves wins.

### Unit 1
> 用兵有言：吾不敢為主，而為客； (*Yòng bīng yǒu yán: wú bù gǎn wéi zhǔ, ér wéi kè;* — "Those who use arms have a saying: I do not dare to be host, but act as guest;")
- **Says:** A saying among those who wage war: "I do not dare to act as the host (the one who takes the initiative); instead I act as the guest (the one who responds)." 不 (*bù* — "not") negates the daring.
- **Act:** asserts (quotes a saying)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 吾 (*wú* — "I"), the speaker of the military saying: a commander or strategist
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 主 (*zhǔ* — "host / master"): the one who strikes first, or the home side. Heshang Gong takes it as "first": 主先也，不敢先舉兵 (*zhǔ xiān yě, bù gǎn xiān jǔ bīng* — "host means first: not daring to be first to raise troops"); 客者和而不倡 (*kè zhě hè ér bù chàng* — "the guest answers and does not lead the song").

### Unit 2
> 不敢進寸，而退尺。 (*Bù gǎn jìn cùn, ér tuì chǐ.* — "do not dare advance an inch, but withdraw a foot.")
- **Says:** Continuing the saying: rather than daring to advance an inch, I withdraw a foot. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates daring to advance.
- **Act:** asserts (still the quoted saying)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the same "I"
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and (parallel to Unit 1; 而 (*ér* — "and / but") is contrastive inside the line)
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong makes it concrete: 侵人境界…為進，閉門守城為退 (*qīn rén jìng jiè … wéi jìn, bì mén shǒu chéng wéi tuì* — "invading others' borders … is advancing; closing the gates and guarding the city is withdrawing").

### Unit 3
> 是謂行無行，攘無臂，扔無敵，執無兵。 (*Shì wèi xíng wú xíng, rǎng wú bì, rēng wú dí, zhí wú bīng.* — "This is called: going where there is no going, baring the arm where there is no arm, pulling in where there is no enemy, gripping where there are no weapons.")
- **Says:** This posture is named four ways: advancing with no advancing (or: ranks with no ranks), pushing up the sleeve with no arm, drawing in with no opponent, holding with no weapon. Each 無 (*wú* — "there is no / without") negates the existence of the thing.
- **Act:** names/defines (是謂 (*shì wèi* — "this is called"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; the one who takes the guest's stance
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore (the naming sums up Units 1–2)
- **Open:** (1) 行無行 (*xíng wú háng* — "march with no battle-line") vs (*xíng wú xíng* — "go without going"); Wang Bi: 行謂行陳也 (*xíng wèi háng zhèn yě* — "*háng* means the battle array"). (2) Whose lack: it looks as though the guest has no arm to raise in anger (Heshang Gong: 雖欲大怒，若無臂可攘也 (*suī yù dà nù, ruò wú bì kě rǎng yě* — "though one may wish great anger, it is as if there were no arm to bare")), or the result is that nobody can stand against them (Wang Bi: 言無有與之抗也 (*yán wú yǒu yǔ zhī kàng yě* — "meaning there is no one who resists them")). 扔 (*rēng* — "pull / throw"); Heshang Gong's comment has 仍 (*réng* — "draw toward").

### Unit 4
> 禍莫大於輕敵，輕敵幾喪吾寶。 (*Huò mò dà yú qīng dí, qīng dí jī sàng wú bǎo.* — "No calamity is greater than making light of the enemy; making light of the enemy nearly loses my treasure.")
- **Says:** No disaster is greater than making light of an enemy; to make light of an enemy comes close to losing my treasure. 莫 (*mò* — "none / nothing") gives "no disaster is greater".
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the one who makes light is unstated; the treasure is the speaker's ("my")
- **Force:** universal; 幾 (*jī* — "nearly") gives near-possibility
- **Link:** none (a new point)
- **Open:** 輕敵 (*qīng dí* — "slight the enemy"): underestimating an opponent, or going lightly to war (Heshang Gong: 輕戰貪財 (*qīng zhàn tān cái* — "making light of war, coveting wealth")). 寶 (*bǎo* — "treasure"): Wang Bi: 寳三寳也 (*bǎo sān bǎo yě* — "treasure means the three treasures"); Heshang Gong: 寳身也 (*bǎo shēn yě* — "treasure means the body / one's life").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi reads the danger as ending up with no enemy at all: 卒至於無敵，斯乃吾之所以為大禍也 (*zú zhì yú wú dí, sī nǎi wú zhī suǒ yǐ wéi dà huò yě* — "ending up with no enemy: this is what I count as great calamity").

### Unit 5
> 故抗兵相若，哀者勝矣。 (*Gù kàng bīng xiāng ruò, āi zhě shèng yǐ.* — "So when raised armies are equal, the one who grieves wins.")
- **Says:** Therefore, when opposing armies are evenly matched, the side that grieves is the one that wins.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** two armies; 哀者 (*āi zhě* — "the one who grieves") wins
- **Force:** universal; will
- **Link:** therefore (故 (*gù* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** 哀 (*āi* — "grieve, mourn / pity"): grief at war vs compassion. Wang Bi: 哀者必相惜 (*āi zhě bì xiāng xī* — "those who grieve surely cherish one another"); Heshang Gong: 哀者慈仁士卒 (*āi zhě cí rén shì zú* — "the grieving one is kind and humane to the troops"). Wang Bi's and Heshang Gong's text reads 相加 (*xiāng jiā* — "are brought against each other") for the base 相若 (*xiāng ruò* — "are equal to each other"), so: "when armies clash" vs "when armies are matched".

# Chapter 70

**The chapter as a whole:** The speaker's words are very easy to understand and to practise, yet no one in the world can do either. The words have an ancestor and the affairs a lord; people fail to know the speaker because they lack that knowing. Those who know the speaker are few, and so the sage wears coarse cloth with jade hidden inside.

### Unit 1
> 吾言甚易知，甚易行。 (*Wú yán shèn yì zhī, shèn yì xíng.* — "My words are very easy to know, very easy to practise.")
- **Says:** What I say is very easy to understand and very easy to put into practice.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 吾 (*wú* — "I"), the speaker of the book
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none

### Unit 2
> 天下莫能知，莫能行。 (*Tiān xià mò néng zhī, mò néng xíng.* — "Under heaven none can know them, none can practise them.")
- **Says:** Yet no one in the world can understand them, and no one can practise them. 莫 (*mò* — "no one") twice.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天下 (*tiān xià* — "all under heaven", everyone)
- **Force:** can/possible (negated)
- **Link:** but (unmarked contrast)
- **Open:** none

### Unit 3
> 言有宗，事有君。 (*Yán yǒu zōng, shì yǒu jūn.* — "Words have an ancestor, affairs have a lord.")
- **Says:** Words have a source they come back to, and deeds have a ruling principle.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** words and affairs; whose is not stated
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** these are the speaker's own words (Heshang Gong: 我所言有宗祖根本 (*wǒ suǒ yán yǒu zōng zǔ gēn běn* — "what I say has an ancestral root")) or words and affairs in general. Wang Bi: 宗萬物之宗也，君萬物之主也 (*zōng wàn wù zhī zōng yě, jūn wàn wù zhī zhǔ yě* — "the ancestor is the ancestor of the myriad things; the lord is the master of the myriad things").

### Unit 4
> 夫唯無知，是以不我知。 (*Fú wéi wú zhī, shì yǐ bù wǒ zhī.* — "It is just because of no knowing that they do not know me.")
- **Says:** Only because there is no understanding do people fail to know me. 無 (*wú* — "there is no") negates the existence of knowing; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates "know me" (object 我 (*wǒ* — "me") placed before the verb).
- **Act:** if/when … then (夫唯 … 是以 (*fú wéi … shì yǐ* — "just because … therefore"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** the subject of 無知 (*wú zhī* — "lack knowing") is unstated: most likely the people of the world; "me" is the speaker
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none (an explanation of Unit 2)
- **Open:** whose "no knowing": the world's ignorance (Wang Bi: 有知之人不得不知之也 (*yǒu zhī zhī rén bù dé bù zhī zhī yě* — "those who have knowing cannot fail to know it")), or the speaker's own hidden not-knowing (Heshang Gong: 我德之暗不見於外…故无知也 (*wǒ dé zhī àn bù xiàn yú wài … gù wú zhī yě* — "my virtue is dark and does not show outside … so there is no knowing [it]")).

### Unit 5
> 知我者希，則我者貴。 (*Zhī wǒ zhě xī, zé wǒ zhě guì.* — "Those who know me are rare; those who pattern on me are precious.")
- **Says:** Few know me; those who take me as their pattern are rare and valued.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** those who know / follow the speaker
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 則 (*zé* — "model on" / "then"): as a verb parallel to 知 (*zhī* — "know"), "those who model on me are prized/rare"; as a connective, "those who know me are few, so I am precious" (Wang Bi: 知我益希，我亦無匹 (*zhī wǒ yì xī, wǒ yì wú pǐ* — "the rarer those who know me, the more I too have no match")). 貴 (*guì* — "valued / dear / scarce").

### Unit 6
> 是以聖人被褐而懷玉。 (*Shì yǐ shèng rén pī hè ér huái yù.* — "Therefore the sage wears coarse cloth and holds jade at the breast.")
- **Says:** Therefore the sage dresses in rough cloth and keeps jade hidden in the bosom.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore (是以 (*shì yǐ* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** none. Wang Bi (his lemma's first two characters are lost): 同其塵 … 寳其真 (*tóng qí chén … bǎo qí zhēn* — "one with the dust … treasuring their genuineness"); Heshang Gong: 薄外 … 厚內 (*bó wài … hòu nèi* — "thin outside … thick within").

# Chapter 71

**The chapter as a whole:** Knowing that one does not know is highest; not knowing yet taking oneself to know is a sickness. Only by treating the sickness as a sickness is one free of it, and the sage is free of it for exactly that reason.

### Unit 1
> 知不知，上； (*Zhī bù zhī, shàng;* — "Knowing not-knowing: highest;")
- **Says:** To know, while counting oneself as not knowing (or: to know that one does not know), is the highest. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates knowing.
- **Act:** asserts (evaluative)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: "one" in general
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** (a) "to know that one does not know"; (b) "to know yet [present oneself as] not knowing" (Heshang Gong: 知道言不知 (*zhī dào yán bù zhī* — "knowing the Tao, saying one does not know")); (c) "to know what cannot be known".

### Unit 2
> 不知知，病。 (*Bù zhī zhī, bìng.* — "Not knowing [yet] knowing: sickness.")
- **Says:** Not knowing, yet taking oneself to know, is a sickness. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates knowing.
- **Act:** asserts (evaluative)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: "one"
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** and (contrasting pair)
- **Open:** (a) "not knowing, yet claiming to know" (Heshang Gong: 不知道言知 (*bù zhī dào yán zhī* — "not knowing the Tao, saying one knows")); (b) "not knowing [the limits of] knowing" (Wang Bi: 不知知之不足任則病也 (*bù zhī zhī zhī bù zú rèn zé bìng yě* — "not knowing that knowing is not fit to be relied on: that is sickness")). 病 (*bìng* — "sickness / flaw").

### Unit 3
> 夫唯病病，是以不病。 (*Fú wéi bìng bìng, shì yǐ bù bìng.* — "Just by being sick of sickness, one is therefore not sick.")
- **Says:** Only by treating the sickness as a sickness is one free of it. The first 病 (*bìng* — "be troubled by / regard as sick") is a verb; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates being sick.
- **Act:** if/when … then (夫唯 … 是以 (*fú wéi … shì yǐ* — "just because … therefore"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: "one"
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** whose sickness: one's own, recognised (plain grammar), or others' (Heshang Gong: 病苦衆人有強知之病 (*bìng kǔ zhòng rén yǒu qiǎng zhī zhī bìng* — "being pained that the crowd has the sickness of forced knowing")).

### Unit 4
> 聖人不病，以其病病，是以不病。 (*Shèng rén bù bìng, yǐ qí bìng bìng, shì yǐ bù bìng.* — "The sage is not sick, because they are sick of sickness; therefore not sick.")
- **Says:** The sage is not sick, because they treat the sickness as a sickness; that is why they are not sick. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates being sick, twice.
- **Act:** describes a person (with a causal clause, 以 (*yǐ* — "because"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none (applies Unit 3 to the sage)
- **Open:** none. Wang Bi notes that Han Fei's text reads 聖人之不病也，以其病病，是以無病也 (*shèng rén zhī bù bìng yě, yǐ qí bìng bìng, shì yǐ wú bìng yě* — "the sage's not being sick is because they are sick of sickness; therefore there is no sickness").

# Chapter 72

**The chapter as a whole:** When the people no longer fear dread power, a greater dread comes. So do not crowd their dwellings or press down on their livelihood: if one does not oppress, one is not cast off. Therefore the sage knows themselves without display and cherishes themselves without self-exaltation, discarding the one and taking the other.

### Unit 1
> 民不畏威，則大威至。 (*Mín bù wèi wēi, zé dà wēi zhì.* — "When the people do not fear the dread, then the great dread arrives.")
- **Says:** If the people do not fear dread power, then a great dread arrives. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates fearing.
- **Act:** if/when … then (則 (*zé* — "then"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** 民 (*mín* — "the people") fail to fear; the source of the great dread is unstated (the ruler's force, revolt, or Heaven)
- **Force:** universal; will
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 威 (*wēi* — "dread / majesty / intimidating force"). Heshang Gong: 威害也 (*wēi hài yě* — "dread means harm"): failing to fear small harms brings great harm, that is, death. Wang Bi reads it politically: when the people cannot bear the ruler's dread power, 上下大潰矣，天誅將至 (*shàng xià dà kuì yǐ, tiān zhū jiāng zhì* — "high and low collapse completely; Heaven's punishment is about to come").

### Unit 2
> 無狎其所居，無厭其所生。 (*Wú xiá qí suǒ jū, wú yàn qí suǒ shēng.* — "Do not encroach on where they dwell; do not press on what they live by.")
- **Says:** Do not crowd or slight the places where they live; do not oppress the means by which they live. 無 (*wú* — "do not") is a prohibition, twice.
- **Act:** advises/commands (prohibition)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the addressee is unstated: most likely the ruler; 其 (*qí* — "their"): most likely the people's
- **Force:** must/necessary
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 狎 (*xiá* — "be over-familiar with / slight"); Heshang Gong's text has 狹 (*xiá* — "narrow, constrict"). 厭 (*yàn* — "press down, oppress" / "be sated with, weary of"). Heshang Gong turns it inward: 心居神當寛柔 (*xīn jū shén dāng kuān róu* — "the heart, where the spirit dwells, should be broad and soft"), so 其 (*qí* — "their") means one's own.

### Unit 3
> 夫唯不厭，是以不厭。 (*Fú wéi bù yàn, shì yǐ bù yàn.* — "Just because one does not press, one is therefore not spurned.")
- **Says:** Only because the ruler does not oppress is the ruler not rejected. The same verb, 厭 (*yàn* — "oppress / weary of"), plays on two senses; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates it each time.
- **Act:** if/when … then (夫唯 … 是以 (*fú wéi … shì yǐ* — "just because … therefore"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: first the ruler (who does not press), then the people (who do not reject the ruler)
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** because (explains Unit 2)
- **Open:** political pun (not oppressing, so not rejected) vs self-cultivation (Wang Bi: 不自厭也 … 天下莫之厭 (*bù zì yàn yě … tiān xià mò zhī yàn* — "not pressing on oneself … so no one under heaven wearies of them"); Heshang Gong: whoever does not weary their vital spirit keeps it dwelling in them).

### Unit 4
> 是以聖人自知，不自見； (*Shì yǐ shèng rén zì zhī, bù zì xiàn;* — "Therefore the sage knows themselves, does not display themselves;")
- **Says:** Therefore the sage knows themselves but does not put themselves on show. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates self-display.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage"), reflexively
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore (是以 (*shì yǐ* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** none. 見 (*xiàn* — "display") is read as "display" (the packet's pinyin gives *jiàn*, "see"); Heshang Gong's sound gloss and both comments support "display". Wang Bi: 不自見其所知以耀光行威也 (*bù zì xiàn qí suǒ zhī yǐ yào guāng xíng wēi yě* — "does not display what they know so as to flash brilliance and wield dread").

### Unit 5
> 自愛，不自貴。 (*Zì ài, bù zì guì.* — "Cherishes themselves, does not exalt themselves.")
- **Says:** The sage loves and cares for themselves but does not set themselves high. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates self-exaltation.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage, reflexively
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** none. Wang Bi ties it back to Unit 2: 自貴則物狎厭居生 (*zì guì zé wù xiá yàn jū shēng* — "exalting oneself, things then crowd and press on dwelling and livelihood").

### Unit 6
> 故去彼取此。 (*Gù qù bǐ qǔ cǐ.* — "Therefore they let go of that and take this.")
- **Says:** So the sage discards the one (display, self-exaltation) and takes the other (self-knowledge, self-care).
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore (故 (*gù* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** none

# Chapter 73

**The chapter as a whole:** Boldness in daring leads to killing, boldness in not daring leads to living; one helps and one harms, and no one knows why Heaven hates what it hates, so even the sage treats it as hard. The way of Heaven wins without contending, answers without speaking, draws things without summoning, plans while at ease. Its net is vast and wide-meshed, yet nothing slips through.

### Unit 1
> 勇於敢則殺，勇於不敢則活。 (*Yǒng yú gǎn zé shā, yǒng yú bù gǎn zé huó.* — "Bold in daring, then killing; bold in not daring, then living.")
- **Says:** Courage put into daring leads to killing or being killed; courage put into not daring leads to living. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates daring.
- **Act:** if/when … then (則 (*zé* — "then"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: anyone brave
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 殺 (*shā* — "kill"): kill others vs be killed; 活 (*huó* — "live"): survive vs let others live. Both commentators read it as the bold person's own fate: Heshang Gong 殺身 (*shā shēn* — "the body is killed"); Wang Bi 必不得其死也 (*bì bù dé qí sǐ yě* — "will surely not die their proper death").

### Unit 2
> 此兩者，或利或害。 (*Cǐ liǎng zhě, huò lì huò hài.* — "These two: one benefits, one harms.")
- **Says:** Of these two kinds of courage, one is beneficial and the other harmful.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the two courages
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 或 … 或 (*huò … huò* — "one … the other" / "sometimes … sometimes"): one helps and one harms (Wang Bi: 利害不同 (*lì hài bù tóng* — "benefit and harm are not the same")), or each sometimes helps and sometimes harms.

### Unit 3
> 天之所惡，孰知其故？ (*Tiān zhī suǒ wù, shú zhī qí gù?* — "What Heaven hates: who knows its reason?")
- **Says:** As for what Heaven hates, who knows why it hates it?
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical: implies no one knows); 孰 (*shú* — "who")
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天 (*tiān* — "Heaven") hates; the knower is "no one"
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong names it: 惡有為也 (*wù yǒu wéi yě* — "it hates contrived action").

### Unit 4
> 是以聖人猶難之。 (*Shì yǐ shèng rén yóu nán zhī.* — "Therefore even the sage treats it as hard.")
- **Says:** Therefore even the sage treats this as difficult.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage"); 之 (*zhī* — "it"): the matter of daring and Heaven's reasons
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore (是以 (*shì yǐ* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** 猶 (*yóu* — "even / still"): "even the sage finds it hard" vs "the sage still handles it as hard". Both commentators: 聖人之明猶難於勇敢 (*shèng rén zhī míng yóu nán yú yǒng gǎn* — "even the sage's clarity finds bold daring hard").

### Unit 5
> 天之道，不爭而善勝，不言而善應，不召而自來，繟然而善謀。 (*Tiān zhī dào, bù zhēng ér shàn shèng, bù yán ér shàn yìng, bù zhào ér zì lái, chǎn rán ér shàn móu.* — "The way of Heaven: not contending yet good at winning, not speaking yet good at answering, not summoning yet [things] come of themselves, slack yet good at planning.")
- **Says:** The way of Heaven wins without contending, responds without speaking, has things come without calling them, and plans well while unhurried. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates contending, speaking, summoning.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天之道 (*tiān zhī dào* — "the way of Heaven"); what comes of itself is unstated: things (Wang Bi: 處下則物自歸 (*chǔ xià zé wù zì guī* — "staying low, things return of themselves"))
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 繟然 (*chǎn rán* — "slack, loose, at ease"; Heshang Gong: 繟寛也 (*chǎn kuān yě* — "slack means broad")). 善 (*shàn* — "good at, skilled").

### Unit 6
> 天網恢恢，疏而不失。 (*Tiān wǎng huī huī, shū ér bù shī.* — "Heaven's net is vast, wide-meshed yet loses nothing.")
- **Says:** Heaven's net is huge and coarse-meshed, yet nothing gets lost from it. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates losing.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** Heaven's net
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong makes it moral oversight: 司察人善惡無有所失 (*sī chá rén shàn è wú yǒu suǒ shī* — "it oversees people's good and evil, missing nothing").

# Chapter 74

**The chapter as a whole:** If the people do not fear death, threatening them with death is pointless; and even if they did fear it, the question is who dares to do the killing. There is always an appointed killer; to kill in that killer's place is like hewing wood in place of a master carpenter, and one who does that seldom escapes cutting their own hand.

### Unit 1
> 民不畏死，奈何以死懼之？ (*Mín bù wèi sǐ, nài hé yǐ sǐ jù zhī?* — "The people do not fear death; how then use death to frighten them?")
- **Says:** The people do not fear death, so what is the use of trying to frighten them with death? 不 (*bù* — "not") negates fearing.
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical: implies it is futile); 奈何 (*nài hé* — "how / what use")
- **Who acts / of whom:** 民 (*mín* — "the people"); whoever frightens them is unstated: the ruler
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** a statement of fact vs a condition ("if the people do not fear death"). Heshang Gong gives the cause: 刑罰酷深，民不聊生 (*xíng fá kù shēn, mín bù liáo shēng* — "punishments are cruel and deep, the people cannot bear to live").

### Unit 2
> 若使民常畏死，而為奇者，吾得執而殺之，孰敢？ (*Ruò shǐ mín cháng wèi sǐ, ér wéi qí zhě, wú dé zhí ér shā zhī, shú gǎn?* — "If the people were made always to fear death, and those who do the odd I could seize and kill, who would dare?")
- **Says:** Suppose the people always feared death, and I could seize and kill anyone who did something deviant: who would dare?
- **Act:** if/when … then (若 (*ruò* — "if")), ending in a rhetorical question, 孰 (*shú* — "who"): implies no one
- **Who acts / of whom:** 吾 (*wú* — "I"), in the person of a ruler; 為奇者 (*wéi qí zhě* — "those who do the odd / deviant")
- **Force:** can/possible (hypothetical)
- **Link:** but (turns from fact to supposition)
- **Open:** "who would dare [to deviate]?" (deterrence works; Heshang Gong: 誰敢有犯者 (*shuí gǎn yǒu fàn zhě* — "who would dare offend")) vs "who would dare [to do the killing]?", which leads into Unit 3. Wang Bi on 奇 (*qí* — "odd"): 詭異亂羣謂之竒也 (*guǐ yì luàn qún wèi zhī qí yě* — "the bizarre that disorders the crowd is called odd").

### Unit 3
> 常有司殺者殺。 (*Cháng yǒu sī shā zhě shā.* — "There is always the one in charge of killing who kills.")
- **Says:** There is always an appointed killer who does the killing.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 司殺者 (*sī shā zhě* — "the one in charge of killing"), not identified in the text
- **Force:** always (常 (*cháng* — "always"))
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** who the killer is: Heaven or the natural order (Heshang Gong: 司殺者天 (*sī shā zhě tiān* — "the one in charge of killing is Heaven")) or an office of state.

### Unit 4
> 夫代司殺者殺，是謂代大匠斫。 (*Fú dài sī shā zhě shā, shì wèi dài dà jiàng zhuó.* — "Now, to kill in place of the one in charge of killing is called hewing in place of the great carpenter.")
- **Says:** Killing in place of the appointed killer is called chopping wood in place of the master carpenter.
- **Act:** names/defines (是謂 (*shì wèi* — "this is called"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** whoever takes over the killing is unstated: the ruler
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none (夫 (*fú* — "now"), opening a topic)
- **Open:** none. Wang Bi notes Heshang Gong's text lacks the second 殺 (*shā* — "kill").

### Unit 5
> 夫代大匠斫，希有不傷其手矣。 (*Fú dài dà jiàng zhuó, xī yǒu bù shāng qí shǒu yǐ.* — "Now, of those who hew in place of the great carpenter, few do not hurt their hands.")
- **Says:** Those who chop in place of the master carpenter rarely avoid cutting their own hands. 希 (*xī* — "rarely") with 不 (*bù* — "not"): almost all are hurt.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the substitute
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none (夫 (*fú* — "now"))
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong: 代天殺者…還受其殃也 (*dài tiān shā zhě … huán shòu qí yāng yě* — "one who kills in Heaven's place … in turn suffers its calamity").

# Chapter 75

**The chapter as a whole:** The people starve because those above eat up so much in taxes; they are hard to govern because those above contrive; they take death lightly because those above pursue life so lavishly. Each ill of the people is traced to the people above them. The one who does not make living their aim is worthier than the one who prizes life.

### Unit 1
> 民之飢，以其上食稅之多，是以飢。 (*Mín zhī jī, yǐ qí shàng shí shuì zhī duō, shì yǐ jī.* — "The people's hunger: because their superiors eat so much tax, therefore hunger.")
- **Says:** The people go hungry because those above them consume so much in taxes; that is why they go hungry.
- **Act:** asserts (cause: 以 (*yǐ* — "because"); 是以 (*shì yǐ* — "therefore"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** 上 (*shàng* — "those above", the rulers) consume; 民 (*mín* — "the people") suffer
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none (Heshang Gong reads 食稅 (*shí shuì* — "eat tax") as 稅食下太多 (*shuì shí xià tài duō* — "tax and eat from those below too much"); same sense)

### Unit 2
> 民之難治，以其上之有為，是以難治。 (*Mín zhī nán zhì, yǐ qí shàng zhī yǒu wéi, shì yǐ nán zhì.* — "The people's being hard to govern: because their superiors act, therefore hard to govern.")
- **Says:** The people are hard to govern because those above them meddle with purposeful action; that is why they are hard to govern.
- **Act:** asserts (causal)
- **Who acts / of whom:** those above act; the people resist
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** 有為 (*yǒu wéi* — "have doing": purposeful, contrived action). Heshang Gong: 多欲好有為 (*duō yù hào yǒu wéi* — "many desires, fond of contrived action"), and the people 化上有為 (*huà shàng yǒu wéi* — "are transformed by the superiors' contriving").

### Unit 3
> 民之輕死，以其上求生之厚，是以輕死。 (*Mín zhī qīng sǐ, yǐ qí shàng qiú shēng zhī hòu, shì yǐ qīng sǐ.* — "The people's taking death lightly: because their superiors seek life so richly, therefore they take death lightly.")
- **Says:** The people make light of death because those above them pursue life so lavishly; that is why they make light of death.
- **Act:** asserts (causal)
- **Who acts / of whom:** in the base text, those above seek life richly; the people take death lightly
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** Wang Bi's and Heshang Gong's text lacks 上 (*shàng* — "those above"): 以其求生之厚 (*yǐ qí qiú shēng zhī hòu* — "because of their seeking life so richly"), which makes the over-seekers the people themselves. Heshang Gong: 人民輕犯死者，以其求生活之道太厚 (*rén mín qīng fàn sǐ zhě, yǐ qí qiú shēng huó zhī dào tài hòu* — "people lightly risk death because they seek the means of living too richly"). Base: the rulers' luxury; lemma: the people's own greed.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi, on the chapter: 皆由上不由其下也 (*jiē yóu shàng bù yóu qí xià yě* — "all of it comes from above, not from below").

### Unit 4
> 夫唯無以生為者，是賢於貴生。 (*Fú wéi wú yǐ shēng wéi zhě, shì xián yú guì shēng.* — "Only one who does not make life their doing: this is worthier than prizing life.")
- **Says:** One who does not make living their business is better than one who sets a high value on life. 無 (*wú* — "not / have nothing") negates taking life as one's aim.
- **Act:** asserts (comparison, 賢於 (*xián yú* — "worthier than"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: the ruler (continuing "those above"), or anyone
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none (夫唯 (*fú wéi* — "only / it is just"), drawing the conclusion)
- **Open:** whom it describes: a ruler who does not live for luxury, or a person who does not cling to life (Heshang Gong: 無以生為務者，爵禄不干於意 (*wú yǐ shēng wéi wù zhě, jué lù bù gān yú yì* — "one who does not make living their task: rank and salary do not trouble their mind")).

# Chapter 76

**The chapter as a whole:** Living people and plants are soft and supple; dead ones are hard and dried. So hardness belongs with death, softness with life: a strong army does not win, a strong tree snaps. The strong and great end up below, the soft and weak above.

### Unit 1
> 人之生也柔弱，其死也堅強。 (*Rén zhī shēng yě róu ruò, qí sǐ yě jiān qiáng.* — "A person alive is soft and weak; dead, hard and strong.")
- **Says:** While alive a human body is soft and pliant; in death it is hard and stiff.
- **Act:** asserts (observation)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 人 (*rén* — "a person, people")
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 生 (*shēng* — "be born / be alive"): at birth vs while living.

### Unit 2
> 萬物草木之生也柔脆，其死也枯槁。 (*Wàn wù cǎo mù zhī shēng yě róu cuì, qí sǐ yě kū gǎo.* — "The myriad things, grasses and trees, alive are soft and tender; dead, withered and dry.")
- **Says:** All things, grass and trees, are soft and tender while alive, and withered and dry when dead.
- **Act:** asserts (observation)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 萬物草木 (*wàn wù cǎo mù* — "the myriad things, grass and trees")
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** 脆 (*cuì* — "tender / crisp / brittle"): here beside 柔 (*róu* — "soft"), most likely "tender".

### Unit 3
> 故堅強者死之徒，柔弱者生之徒。 (*Gù jiān qiáng zhě sǐ zhī tú, róu ruò zhě shēng zhī tú.* — "So the hard and strong are death's companions; the soft and weak are life's companions.")
- **Says:** Therefore what is hard and strong belongs with death, and what is soft and weak belongs with life.
- **Act:** asserts (conclusion)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the hard and strong; the soft and weak
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** therefore (故 (*gù* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** 徒 (*tú* — "followers, companions / kind, class").

### Unit 4
> 是以兵強則不勝，木強則折。 (*Shì yǐ bīng qiáng zé bù shèng, mù qiáng zé zhé.* — "Therefore when an army is strong it does not win; when a tree is strong it snaps.")
- **Says:** That is why a strong army fails to win, and a strong tree breaks. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates winning.
- **Act:** if/when … then (則 (*zé* — "then"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** army; tree
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** therefore (是以 (*shì yǐ* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** none in the base. Wang Bi on the army: 物之所惡也，故必不得勝 (*wù zhī suǒ wù yě, gù bì bù dé shèng* — "it is what things hate, so it surely cannot win").
- **Commentators:** the tree clause differs in both lemmas. Wang Bi: 木強則兵 (*mù qiáng zé bīng* — "a tree strong, then the axe"), with 物所加也 (*wù suǒ jiā yě* — "it is what things are brought against"). Heshang Gong: 木強則共 (*mù qiáng zé gòng* — "a tree strong, then [others grow] together [on it]"), with 枝弱共生其上也 (*zhī ruò gòng shēng qí shàng yě* — "weak branches grow together on top of it"). The base 折 (*zhé* — "snap") is reported.

### Unit 5
> 強大處下，柔弱處上。 (*Qiáng dà chǔ xià, róu ruò chǔ shàng.* — "The strong and great dwell below; the soft and weak dwell above.")
- **Says:** The strong and great take the lower place; the soft and weak take the upper.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the strong and great; the soft and weak
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** (a) a verdict on rank: the strong end up beneath, the soft come out on top (Heshang Gong: 大道抑強扶弱 (*dà dào yì qiáng fú ruò* — "the great Way suppresses the strong and supports the weak")); (b) the tree picture: thick trunk below, slender twigs above (Wang Bi: 木之本也 … 枝條是也 (*mù zhī běn yě … zhī tiáo shì yě* — "the trunk of the tree … the twigs are it")).

# Chapter 77

**The chapter as a whole:** The way of Heaven is like a bow being drawn: it lowers what is high, raises what is low, takes from surplus and adds to lack. The human way does the reverse, taking from those who lack to serve those who have more. Only one with the Tao can give their surplus to the world, so the sage acts without relying on it, achieves without dwelling in it, and does not wish to show their worth.

### Unit 1
> 天之道，其猶張弓與？ (*Tiān zhī dào, qí yóu zhāng gōng yǔ?* — "The way of Heaven: is it not like drawing a bow?")
- **Says:** Is the way of Heaven not like the drawing of a bow?
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical: implies yes); 其 … 與 (*qí … yǔ* — tentative question frame)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天之道 (*tiān zhī dào* — "the way of Heaven")
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 張弓 (*zhāng gōng* — "stretch a bow"): drawing and aiming it, or stringing and adjusting it (Heshang Gong: 言張弓和調之如是乃可用 (*yán zhāng gōng hé tiáo zhī rú shì nǎi kě yòng* — "it means that a stretched bow, tuned and adjusted like this, can then be used")).

### Unit 2
> 高者抑之，下者舉之；有餘者損之，不足者補之。 (*Gāo zhě yì zhī, xià zhě jǔ zhī; yǒu yú zhě sǔn zhī, bù zú zhě bǔ zhī.* — "The high, press it down; the low, raise it; what has surplus, reduce it; what lacks, add to it.")
- **Says:** What is high is pressed down, what is low is lifted; what has too much is reduced, what has too little is filled out. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates sufficiency.
- **Act:** describes (the bow's handling, by analogy Heaven's way)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: the one handling the bow, standing for the way of Heaven
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none (elaborates the image)
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong's text has 與之 (*yǔ zhī* — "give to it") for 補之 (*bǔ zhī* — "patch / supplement it").

### Unit 3
> 天之道，損有餘而補不足。 (*Tiān zhī dào, sǔn yǒu yú ér bǔ bù zú.* — "The way of Heaven reduces surplus and adds to lack.")
- **Says:** The way of Heaven takes from what has excess and supplies what is lacking. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates sufficiency.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the way of Heaven
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** therefore (draws the image's point)
- **Open:** none

### Unit 4
> 人之道，則不然，損不足以奉有餘。 (*Rén zhī dào, zé bù rán, sǔn bù zú yǐ fèng yǒu yú.* — "The way of humans is not so: it reduces the lacking to offer up to those with surplus.")
- **Says:** The human way is otherwise: it takes from those who lack in order to serve those who already have more. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates "so" (不然 (*bù rán* — "not so")) and sufficiency.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 人之道 (*rén zhī dào* — "the way of humans"); done to the lacking, for the benefit of the surplus-holders
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** but (則 (*zé* — "as for / then"), contrastive)
- **Open:** none. 奉 (*fèng* — "present respectfully, serve"). Heshang Gong: 損貧以奉富 (*sǔn pín yǐ fèng fù* — "diminish the poor to serve the rich").

### Unit 5
> 孰能有餘以奉天下？唯有道者。 (*Shú néng yǒu yú yǐ fèng tiān xià? Wéi yǒu dào zhě.* — "Who can have surplus and offer it to all under heaven? Only one who has the Tao.")
- **Says:** Who is able to take their surplus and give it to the world? Only one who has the Tao.
- **Act:** asks (real question, answered in the text) then asserts; 孰 (*shú* — "who"); 唯 (*wéi* — "only")
- **Who acts / of whom:** 有道者 (*yǒu dào zhě* — "one who has the Tao"); Heshang Gong: 唯有道之君能行也 (*wéi yǒu dào zhī jūn néng xíng yě* — "only a ruler who has the Way can do it")
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none

### Unit 6
> 是以聖人為而不恃，功成而不處，其不欲見賢。 (*Shì yǐ shèng rén wéi ér bù shì, gōng chéng ér bù chù, qí bù yù xiàn xián.* — "Therefore the sage acts yet does not rely on it, achieves yet does not dwell in it; they do not wish to show their worth.")
- **Says:** Therefore the sage acts without depending on the result, completes the work without staying in the achievement, and has no wish to display their own excellence. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates relying, dwelling, wishing.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore (是以 (*shì yǐ* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** 恃 (*shì* — "rely on / presume on"): not counting on repayment (Heshang Gong: 不恃其報 (*bù shì qí bào* — "does not count on its return")) vs not claiming the act. 其 (*qí* — "they / it is that"): pronoun vs modal particle. 見 (*xiàn* — "display") is read here as "display", though the packet's pinyin gives *jiàn* ("see"); Heshang Gong reads "display": 不欲使人知己之賢 (*bù yù shǐ rén zhī jǐ zhī xián* — "does not wish to let people know their worth").

# Chapter 78

**The chapter as a whole:** Nothing is softer and weaker than water, yet nothing beats it at wearing down the hard and strong, because nothing can take its place. Everyone knows the weak overcomes the strong, yet no one can practise it. So the sage says that whoever takes on the state's filth and misfortune is its true lord and king; straight words sound like their opposite.

### Unit 1
> 天下莫柔弱於水，而攻堅強者莫之能勝，以其無以易之。 (*Tiān xià mò róu ruò yú shuǐ, ér gōng jiān qiáng zhě mò zhī néng shèng, yǐ qí wú yǐ yì zhī.* — "Under heaven nothing is softer and weaker than water, yet among attackers of the hard and strong none can surpass it, because nothing can replace it.")
- **Says:** Nothing in the world is softer or weaker than water, yet for attacking the hard and strong nothing can outdo it, because nothing can substitute for it. 莫 (*mò* — "nothing / none") twice; 無 (*wú* — "there is nothing") once.
- **Act:** asserts (with a reason: 以 (*yǐ* — "because"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** 水 (*shuǐ* — "water"); 其 (*qí* — "its"): water's
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** (1) 莫之能勝 (*mò zhī néng shèng* — "none can overcome it"): nothing surpasses water (Heshang Gong: 莫能勝水 (*mò néng shèng shuǐ* — "none can surpass water")) vs nothing hard can withstand it. (2) 易 (*yì* — "replace / change"): nothing can replace it (Wang Bi: 無物可以易之也 (*wú wù kě yǐ yì zhī yě* — "no thing can replace it")) vs nothing can alter it. Wang Bi takes 以 (*yǐ* — "use"): "in using water's softness, nothing replaces it". Wang Bi notes Heshang Gong's text reads 天下柔弱莫過於水 (*tiān xià róu ruò mò guò yú shuǐ* — "under heaven, of the soft and weak nothing exceeds water").

### Unit 2
> 弱之勝強，柔之勝剛，天下莫不知，莫能行。 (*Ruò zhī shèng qiáng, róu zhī shèng gāng, tiān xià mò bù zhī, mò néng xíng.* — "That the weak beats the strong and the soft beats the hard, no one under heaven does not know; no one can practise it.")
- **Says:** That the weak overcomes the strong and the soft overcomes the hard, everyone in the world knows, and no one can act on it. 莫不 (*mò bù* — "none not": everyone) is a double negative; 莫 (*mò* — "no one") negates ability.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天下 (*tiān xià* — "everyone under heaven")
- **Force:** universal; can (negated)
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none

### Unit 3
> 是以聖人云：受國之垢，是謂社稷主； (*Shì yǐ shèng rén yún: shòu guó zhī gòu, shì wèi shè jì zhǔ;* — "Therefore the sage says: one who receives the state's filth is called lord of the altars of soil and grain;")
- **Says:** So the sage says: whoever takes on the dirt of the state is called master of its altars of soil and grain (its ruler).
- **Act:** names/defines (是謂 (*shì wèi* — "is called")), inside a quotation (云 (*yún* — "says"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage speaks; the one who receives is unstated: the ruler
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** therefore (是以 (*shì yǐ* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** 垢 (*gòu* — "dirt, defilement / disgrace"): filth vs humiliation. Heshang Gong: like rivers and sea that 不逆小流 (*bù nì xiǎo liú* — "do not refuse small streams").

### Unit 4
> 受國之不祥，是謂天下王。 (*Shòu guó zhī bù xiáng, shì wèi tiān xià wáng.* — "One who receives the state's ill-omened is called king of all under heaven.")
- **Says:** Whoever takes on the state's misfortunes is called king of the world. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 祥 (*xiáng* — "auspicious"): the unlucky, the ill-omened.
- **Act:** names/defines (是謂 (*shì wèi* — "is called"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: the ruler
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong: 代民受不祥之殃 (*dài mín shòu bù xiáng zhī yāng* — "bears the calamity of misfortune in the people's place").

### Unit 5
> 正言若反。 (*Zhèng yán ruò fǎn.* — "Straight words seem reversed.")
- **Says:** True words sound like their opposite.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 正言 (*zhèng yán* — "straight / correct words")
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** a comment on the sage's sayings just quoted (Heshang Gong: 此乃正直之言，世人不知以為反言 (*cǐ nǎi zhèng zhí zhī yán, shì rén bù zhī yǐ wéi fǎn yán* — "these are upright words; the world does not recognise them and takes them as contrary words")) vs a general maxim. 反 (*fǎn* — "reverse / the opposite / turn back").

# Chapter 79

**The chapter as a whole:** Settling a great grievance always leaves some grievance behind, so that cannot count as good. So the sage holds the left half of the tally and makes no demands on others: the one with virtue keeps the tally, the one without it keeps the levy. Heaven's Tao has no favourites, yet it always goes with the good person.

### Unit 1
> 和大怨，必有餘怨； (*Hé dà yuàn, bì yǒu yú yuàn;* — "Settle a great grievance, and there will surely be grievance left over;")
- **Says:** When a great resentment is reconciled, some resentment is bound to remain.
- **Act:** if/when … then (by juxtaposition)
- **Who acts / of whom:** whoever reconciles is unstated: a ruler or mediator
- **Force:** must/necessary (必 (*bì* — "surely"))
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 和 (*hé* — "harmonise, settle"): Heshang Gong reads it as settlement by punishment: 殺人者死，傷人者刑，以相和報 (*shā rén zhě sǐ, shāng rén zhě xíng, yǐ xiāng hé bào* — "the killer dies, the injurer is punished: by this they are balanced and requited"); Wang Bi as reconciling with virtue after neglect: 其傷不復 (*qí shāng bù fù* — "its injury does not heal").

### Unit 2
> 安可以為善？ (*Ān kě yǐ wéi shàn?* — "How can that be taken as good?")
- **Says:** How can that be considered good?
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical: implies it cannot); 安 (*ān* — "how")
- **Who acts / of whom:** the settling of grievance
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** therefore (draws the consequence of Unit 1)
- **Open:** "how can [such settling] count as good?" vs "how can one thereby do good?" Wang Bi's lemma joins this to the next line.

### Unit 3
> 是以聖人執左契，而不責於人。 (*Shì yǐ shèng rén zhí zuǒ qì, ér bù zé yú rén.* — "Therefore the sage holds the left tally and does not exact from others.")
- **Says:** Therefore the sage holds the left half of the tally and does not press others for payment. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates demanding.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage"); 人 (*rén* — "others")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore (是以 (*shì yǐ* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** 左契 (*zuǒ qì* — "left tally"): the creditor's half, held without calling in the debt, vs a token of trust (Heshang Gong: 刻契合符以為信也 (*kè qì hé fú yǐ wéi xìn yě* — "carving tallies and matching tokens as a pledge")). 責 (*zé* — "demand payment / blame"). Wang Bi: 左契防怨之所由生也 (*zuǒ qì fáng yuàn zhī suǒ yóu shēng yě* — "the left tally guards against where resentment arises").

### Unit 4
> 有德司契，無德司徹。 (*Yǒu dé sī qì, wú dé sī chè.* — "Having virtue, one keeps the tally; lacking virtue, one keeps the levy.")
- **Says:** One who has virtue takes charge of the tally; one without virtue takes charge of collecting the levy. 無 (*wú* — "lack") negates having virtue.
- **Act:** asserts (contrasting two types)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 有德 (*yǒu dé* — "one with virtue / potency") and 無德 (*wú dé* — "one without")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none (explains Unit 3)
- **Open:** 徹 (*chè* — "the Zhou tithe / penetrate, remove"): tax collection (plain sense) vs fault-finding (Wang Bi: 徹司人之過也 (*chè sī rén zhī guò yě* — "the levy means overseeing people's faults"); Heshang Gong: 背其契言，司人所失 (*bèi qí qì yán, sī rén suǒ shī* — "turns against the word of the tally and oversees what people fail in")).

### Unit 5
> 天道無親，常與善人。 (*Tiān dào wú qīn, cháng yǔ shàn rén.* — "Heaven's Tao has no kin; it always goes with the good person.")
- **Says:** The Tao of Heaven has no favourites, yet it is always with the good person. 無 (*wú* — "has no") negates having kin.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天道 (*tiān dào* — "Heaven's Tao"); 善人 (*shàn rén* — "the good / skilled person")
- **Force:** always (常 (*cháng* — "always"))
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 與 (*yǔ* — "be with / give to / side with"). The tension between 無親 (*wú qīn* — "no kin, no favourites") and 常與 (*cháng yǔ* — "always with") stands in the text. Heshang Gong resolves it: 唯與善人，則與司契者也 (*wéi yǔ shàn rén, zé yǔ sī qì zhě yě* — "it sides only with good people, that is, with those who keep the tally").

# Chapter 80

**The chapter as a whole:** A small state with few people: it has labour-multiplying tools but does not use them, its people value their lives and do not move far, and its boats, carts, armour and weapons sit idle. The people go back to knotted cords, relish their food, clothes, homes and customs. Neighbouring states are within sight and earshot, yet the people grow old and die without visiting each other.

### Unit 1
> 小國寡民。 (*Xiǎo guó guǎ mín.* — "Small state, few people.")
- **Says:** A small state with few people.
- **Act:** describes (or advises, see Open)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the state and its people; any actor is unstated
- **Force:** wish (an ideal), or habitual if read as a description
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** a picture ("a small state with few people") vs a prescription ("make the state small and the people few", with 小 (*xiǎo* — "small") and 寡 (*guǎ* — "few") as causative verbs). Heshang Gong reads it as an attitude: 聖人雖治大國猶以為小 (*shèng rén suī zhì dà guó yóu yǐ wéi xiǎo* — "the sage, though governing a large state, still treats it as small"); Wang Bi as an argument from the smaller case: 况國大民衆乎 (*kuàng guó dà mín zhòng hū* — "how much more a large state with many people").

### Unit 2
> 使有什伯之器而不用； (*Shǐ yǒu shí bǎi zhī qì ér bù yòng;* — "Let there be implements of tens and hundreds, yet not used;")
- **Says:** Let there be tools that do the work of ten or a hundred, and let them go unused. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates using.
- **Act:** advises/commands (causative 使 (*shǐ* — "let / cause"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** the one who causes is unstated: the ruler; the users are the people
- **Force:** wish
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 什伯之器 (*shí bǎi zhī qì* — "implements of tens and hundreds"): tools multiplying human labour tenfold or a hundredfold vs military or household units of ten and a hundred (Heshang Gong, whose text reads 什伯人之器 (*shí bǎi rén zhī qì* — "implements of tens and hundreds of people"): 使民各有部曲什伯 (*shǐ mín gè yǒu bù qǔ shí bǎi* — "let the people each have their units of ten and hundred"), and 器謂農人之器 (*qì wèi nóng rén zhī qì* — "implements means farmers' tools")). Wang Bi notes other editions add 人 (*rén* — "people"). 使 (*shǐ*) also read as "even if": Wang Bi: 使民雖有什伯之器而無所用 (*shǐ mín suī yǒu shí bǎi zhī qì ér wú suǒ yòng* — "let the people, though they have such implements, have nothing to use them for").

### Unit 3
> 使民重死而不遠徙。 (*Shǐ mín zhòng sǐ ér bù yuǎn xǐ.* — "Let the people weigh death heavily and not move far.")
- **Says:** Let the people take death seriously and not migrate to distant places. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates moving far.
- **Act:** advises/commands (causative 使 (*shǐ* — "let / cause"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated causer (the ruler); 民 (*mín* — "the people")
- **Force:** wish
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong: 民重死而貪生 (*mín zhòng sǐ ér tān shēng* — "the people weigh death heavily and cling to life").

### Unit 4
> 雖有舟輿，無所乘之；雖有甲兵，無所陳之。 (*Suī yǒu zhōu yú, wú suǒ chéng zhī; suī yǒu jiǎ bīng, wú suǒ chén zhī.* — "Though there are boats and carts, there is nowhere to ride them; though there are armour and weapons, there is nowhere to deploy them.")
- **Says:** Even with boats and carriages, there is no occasion to ride them; even with armour and weapons, there is no occasion to set them out. 無所 (*wú suǒ* — "nothing for / nowhere to") negates any occasion for use.
- **Act:** describes (concessive, 雖 (*suī* — "though"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** the people of the small state
- **Force:** habitual (within the ideal)
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none

### Unit 5
> 使民復結繩而用之。 (*Shǐ mín fù jié shéng ér yòng zhī.* — "Let the people return to knotting cords and using them.")
- **Says:** Let the people go back to keeping records with knotted cords.
- **Act:** advises/commands (causative 使 (*shǐ* — "let / cause"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated causer; the people
- **Force:** wish
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong: 去文反質 (*qù wén fǎn zhì* — "discard ornament, return to plainness").

### Unit 6
> 甘其食，美其服，安其居，樂其俗。 (*Gān qí shí, měi qí fú, ān qí jū, lè qí sú.* — "Sweet their food, fine their clothes, at ease their homes, joyful their customs.")
- **Says:** They find their food sweet, their clothes fine, their homes peaceful, their customs a joy.
- **Act:** describes
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: most likely the people, toward their own food, clothes, homes, customs
- **Force:** habitual (within the ideal)
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** putative ("they relish their food …") vs causative ("[the ruler] makes their food sweet …"). Heshang Gong reads it putatively, of plain things: 甘其蔬食 (*gān qí shū shí* — "relish their vegetable fare"), 美其惡衣 (*měi qí è yī* — "find their coarse clothes fine"), 安其茅茨 (*ān qí máo cí* — "rest content in their thatched huts").

### Unit 7
> 隣國相望，雞犬之聲相聞，民至老死，不相往來。 (*Lín guó xiāng wàng, jī quǎn zhī shēng xiāng wén, mín zhì lǎo sǐ, bù xiāng wǎng lái.* — "Neighbouring states look across at each other, the sounds of chickens and dogs are heard between them, yet the people reach old age and death without going back and forth.")
- **Says:** The next state is within sight, its roosters and dogs within earshot, yet the people live to old age and die without ever visiting one another. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates the coming and going.
- **Act:** describes
- **Who acts / of whom:** 民 (*mín* — "the people")
- **Force:** habitual (within the ideal)
- **Link:** and (with an unmarked "yet" before the last clause)
- **Open:** none. Wang Bi: 無所欲求 (*wú suǒ yù qiú* — "there is nothing they desire or seek"); Heshang Gong: 其無情欲 (*qí wú qíng yù* — "they are without passions and desires").

# Chapter 81

**The chapter as a whole:** Three chiasmi: trustworthy words are not beautiful and beautiful ones not trustworthy; the good do not argue and arguers are not good; the knowing are not broadly learned and the broadly learned do not know. The sage does not hoard: the more they do for and give to others, the more they have. Heaven's way benefits without harming; the sage's way acts without contending.

### Unit 1
> 信言不美，美言不信。 (*Xìn yán bù měi, měi yán bù xìn.* — "Trustworthy words are not beautiful; beautiful words are not trustworthy.")
- **Says:** True words are not pretty, and pretty words are not true. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates the quality, twice.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** words
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none. 信 (*xìn* — "true, trustworthy").

### Unit 2
> 善者不辯，辯者不善。 (*Shàn zhě bù biàn, biàn zhě bù shàn.* — "The good do not argue; those who argue are not good.")
- **Says:** Those who are good do not dispute cleverly; those who dispute cleverly are not good. 不 (*bù* — "not") twice.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** people of each kind
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** 善 (*shàn* — "good / skilled"); 辯 (*biàn* — "argue, be glib / distinguish"). Heshang Gong: 辯者謂巧言也 (*biàn zhě wèi qiǎo yán yě* — "arguers means clever talk").

### Unit 3
> 知者不博，博者不知。 (*Zhī zhě bù bó, bó zhě bù zhī.* — "Those who know are not broadly learned; the broadly learned do not know.")
- **Says:** Those who truly know are not widely read; the widely read do not truly know. 不 (*bù* — "not") twice.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** people of each kind
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** 知者 (*zhī zhě* — "those who know" / *zhì zhě* — "the wise"). Heshang Gong: 知者謂知道之士 (*zhī zhě wèi zhī dào zhī shì* — "those who know means people who know the Tao").

### Unit 4
> 聖人不積。 (*Shèng rén bù jī.* — "The sage does not accumulate.")
- **Says:** The sage does not hoard. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates accumulating.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none in the text. Heshang Gong adds an object: 積德不積財 (*jī dé bù jī cái* — "accumulates virtue, does not accumulate wealth").

### Unit 5
> 既以為人己愈有，既以與人己愈多。 (*Jì yǐ wèi rén jǐ yù yǒu, jì yǐ yǔ rén jǐ yù duō.* — "Having used it all for others, oneself has more; having given it all to others, oneself has more still.")
- **Says:** The more the sage spends on others, the more they have; the more they give to others, the more they possess.
- **Act:** describes a person (paradox)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; 己 (*jǐ* — "oneself"), the sage's own self; 人 (*rén* — "others")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** because (explains Unit 4)
- **Open:** 既 (*jì* — "having already / entirely"); 為人 (*wèi rén* — "for others" / *wéi rén* — "act for people"). Heshang Gong splits the halves: the first is spreading virtue (施設德化 (*shī shè dé huà* — "bestowing virtue's transforming")), the second giving wealth.

### Unit 6
> 天之道，利而不害； (*Tiān zhī dào, lì ér bù hài;* — "The way of Heaven benefits and does not harm;")
- **Says:** Heaven's way brings benefit and does no harm. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates harming.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天之道 (*tiān zhī dào* — "the way of Heaven")
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none

### Unit 7
> 聖人之道，為而不爭。 (*Shèng rén zhī dào, wéi ér bù zhēng.* — "The way of the sage acts and does not contend.")
- **Says:** The sage's way is to act without contending. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates contending.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 聖人之道 (*shèng rén zhī dào* — "the way of the sage")
- **Force:** universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong: 不與下爭功名 (*bù yǔ xià zhēng gōng míng* — "does not contend with those below for merit and fame").
