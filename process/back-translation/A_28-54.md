# Chapter 28

**The chapter as a whole:** Three parallel stanzas advise knowing one of a pair (rooster, white) while holding to the other (hen, black, soiled), and so serving as a low or patterning place for the world; each time, a lasting integrity stays with you, and there is a return to an original state (infant, the endless, uncarved wood). A coda says the uncarved wood breaks up into vessels that the sage uses to become head of officials, and concludes that the great carving cuts nothing apart.

### Unit 1
> Know the rooster, hold to the hen, serve as the world's ravine.
- **Says:** Know the male bird, keep hold of the female bird, and act as a ravine for the world.
- **Act:** advises/commands
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated "you" (the reader); objects: rooster, hen, the world
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** Imperative series, or a description of one stance whose result is "serve as the ravine." Rooster/hen carry male/female and possibly assertive/receptive connotations; whether the reader is to become the hen or merely keep to her qualities is open. "Ravine" suggests a low place things flow into.

### Unit 2
> Serve as the world's ravine, and the ever-present integrity never leaves you.
- **Says:** If you serve as the world's ravine, the constant integrity will never leave you.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** "you"; integrity stays with you
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** if-then (repeats prior clause as condition)
- **Open:** "Integrity" may be heard as moral honesty or as wholeness/inner power. "The ever-present integrity" could be a general quality or yours specifically.

### Unit 3
> Return again to the infant.
- **Says:** Go back again to being (or to) an infant.
- **Act:** advises/commands
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; most likely "you", possibly the integrity
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (reads as a result or next step)
- **Open:** Grammatically an imperative to the reader; could also be read as describing what follows (you return / integrity returns to the infant state). "The infant" may be the state of infancy or an infant figure.

### Unit 4
> Know the white, hold to the black, serve as the world's pattern.
- **Says:** Know the white, keep to the black, act as the world's pattern.
- **Act:** advises/commands
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated "you"
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (parallel to Unit 1)
- **Open:** White/black may be light/dark, known/unknown, or carry moral (good/bad) or other connotations. "Pattern" may mean model/template or design.

### Unit 5
> Serve as the world's pattern, and the ever-present integrity never varies.
- **Says:** If you serve as the world's pattern, the constant integrity never changes.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** "you"; integrity
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** if-then
- **Open:** "Never varies" could mean is never in error or never fluctuates.

### Unit 6
> Return again to the endless.
- **Says:** Go back again to the limitless.
- **Act:** advises/commands
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; most likely "you"
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** As Unit 3: imperative or resulting description. "The endless" is unspecified: infinity, boundlessness, or a state without limits.

### Unit 7
> Know the white, hold to the soiled, serve as the world's valley.
- **Says:** Know the white (clean), keep to the soiled (dirty), act as the world's valley.
- **Act:** advises/commands
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated "you"
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (parallel)
- **Open:** "Soiled" may mean physically dirty or disgraced/dishonored. A reader notices "white" repeats from Unit 4 with a different partner.

### Unit 8
> Serve as the world's valley, and the ever-present integrity is enough at last.
- **Says:** If you serve as the world's valley, the constant integrity is finally sufficient.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** "you"; integrity
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** if-then
- **Open:** "Enough at last" could mean finally complete/full, or sufficient in the end.

### Unit 9
> Return again to the uncarved wood.
- **Says:** Go back again to the state of wood not yet carved.
- **Act:** advises/commands
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; most likely "you"
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** Imperative or result. "Uncarved wood" as a literal material or a figure for an unshaped, natural state.

### Unit 10
> The uncarved wood scatters, and becomes vessels.
- **Says:** The uncarved wood breaks up/disperses and turns into vessels.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the uncarved wood (subject of its own change)
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none (picks up "uncarved wood")
- **Open:** "Scatters" may mean is split up, or spreads out. "Vessels" may be literal containers or tools/persons fit for use (officials).

### Unit 11
> The sage puts them to use and becomes chief of the officers.
- **Says:** The sage uses the vessels and so becomes head of the officials.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; "them" = the vessels
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** Whether the sage becomes chief by using them, or uses them as chief; whether "vessels" here means people/officials.

### Unit 12
> So the great carving severs nothing.
- **Says:** Therefore the greatest carving cuts nothing apart.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** "the great carving" (unstated carver; possibly the sage's shaping/governing)
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** therefore/so
- **Open:** Paradox: carving that does not cut, or the greatest shaping/government that does not divide. How it follows from Unit 11 is not spelled out.

# Chapter 53

**The chapter as a whole:** The speaker says that with even a little knowledge, walking the great Tao, they would fear only straying, for the Tao is level but people prefer shortcuts. A court that is immaculate while fields lie weedy and granaries empty, with finery, swords, gluttony and excess wealth, exposes a chief of thieves: it is not the Tao.

### Unit 1
> If I had just a scrap of knowledge, walking the path of the great Tao, my only fear would be to stray from it.
- **Says:** If I had even a little knowledge, as I walked the great Tao I would fear only going off it.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** "I" (speaker)
- **Force:** wish (counterfactual conditional)
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** Counterfactual may imply modesty (I lack knowledge) or that a little knowledge suffices. "Stray" = deviate, or take a side path.

### Unit 2
> The great Tao is very level, yet people love to take shortcuts.
- **Says:** The great Tao is very even, but people like side paths.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the great Tao; people
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** "People" = common folk, or rulers. "Shortcuts" = byways/devious paths.

### Unit 3
> The courts are immaculate, the fields are choked with weeds, the granaries are empty;
- **Says:** The court is spotless while the fields are overgrown and the storehouses empty.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** courts, fields, granaries
- **Force:** habitual (present scene)
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** "Immaculate" = elegant/well kept, or possibly emptied/cleaned out. "Courts" = palace court, or courtyards.

### Unit 4
> Wearing ornate, colorful clothes, carrying sharp swords, gorged on food and drink, possessing excess wealth and goods — This exposes the ringleader of thieves.
- **Says:** Dressing finely, bearing swords, overeating, and holding surplus wealth reveal one as a chief of thieves.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated wearers (rulers/courtiers); "the ringleader of thieves"
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** "This" = the whole scene or the list. "Exposes" = reveals them as, or is called/is what we call. Who the ringleader is: the ruler or the court collectively.

### Unit 5
> It is not the Tao\!
- **Says:** That is not the Tao!
- **Act:** asserts (exclamation)
- **Who acts / of whom:** it = the described conduct
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** The backslash before "!" is a markup escape; the reader sees "It is not the Tao!".
