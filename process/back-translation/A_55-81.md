# Chapter 55

**The chapter as a whole:** A person of abundant integrity is compared to a newborn: unharmed by dangerous animals, soft yet firm-gripping, full of vitality and harmony. Knowing harmony leads to "the ever-present" and clear-seeing; by contrast, forcing life, the heart driving vital energy, and reaching a prime all lead to decay, called "not-Tao", which ends early.

### Unit 1
> A person with abundant integrity is like a newborn.
- **Says:** someone who has a great deal of integrity resembles a newborn infant.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** a person with abundant integrity; compared to a newborn
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** "integrity" will be heard by most readers as moral honesty/uprightness; it may instead mean wholeness or inner power.

### Unit 2
> Venomous creatures do not sting them,
> fierce beasts do not seize them,
> birds of prey do not attack them.
- **Says:** poisonous creatures, fierce beasts and birds of prey do not harm them.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** venomous creatures, beasts, birds of prey; done to "them"
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none (elaborates the comparison)
- **Open:** "them" may be the person of integrity or the newborn (singular "they"); either way it is a claim of immunity, read literally or figuratively.

### Unit 3
> Their bones are yielding and their muscles soft, yet their grip is firm.
- **Says:** though their bones are pliable and muscles soft, they grip firmly.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** "they" (newborn / person of integrity)
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** "their" may be the newborn literally, or the person of integrity by comparison.

### Unit 4
> They do not yet know the union of female and male, yet their whole being quickens with life — this is vital essence at its utmost.
- **Says:** though they have no experience of sexual union, their whole body is stirred with life; this is the peak of vital essence.
- **Act:** describes a person; then names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** "they" (the newborn most likely, given "not yet")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** "quickens with life" may be general vitality or a discreet reference to physical (sexual) arousal; "this" may point to the quickening or to the whole state.

### Unit 5
> It cries all day and never goes hoarse — this is harmony at its utmost.
- **Says:** it cries the whole day without getting hoarse; this is the peak of harmony.
- **Act:** describes; names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** "it", most likely the newborn (shift from "they" to "it")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** the switch to "it" makes the subject plainly the infant, leaving it unclear whether the person of integrity is still meant.

### Unit 6
> Knowing harmony is called the ever-present.
- **Says:** to know harmony is named "the ever-present".
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated knower (anyone)
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** "the ever-present" may be a constant principle, a permanent state of the knower, or something always at hand; whether knowing harmony *is* the ever-present or is named so is the English's own equation.

### Unit 7
> Knowing the ever-present is called clear-seeing.
- **Says:** to know the ever-present is named clear-seeing.
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated knower
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (chain)
- **Open:** "clear-seeing" may be insight/wisdom or literal clarity of vision.

### Unit 8
> To force life to increase is an ill omen.
- **Says:** to push life to grow beyond its course is a bad sign.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated (anyone who forces); done to life
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (turn to the contrary)
- **Open:** "force life to increase" may mean prolonging life, stimulating vitality, or striving for gain; "ill omen" carries a hint of the supernatural/portentous.

### Unit 9
> When the heart drives the vital energy, that is called strong.
- **Says:** when the heart (will/mind) pushes the vital energy, that is called "strong".
- **Act:** if/when … then; names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** the heart, acting on the vital energy
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** "strong" may be neutral or positive description, or (given the surrounding lines about ill omen and decay) a negative term meaning forced, overbearing.

### Unit 10

- **Says:** once things reach their peak, they decline.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** things
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none

### Unit 11

- **Act:** names/defines

# Chapter 59

**The chapter as a whole:** In governing and serving nature, conserving is best. A chain links conserving to early acceptance, accumulated integrity, overcoming everything, a limit no one knows, and holding the state; having "the mother of the state" lets one last, and this is the Tao of deep roots and long life.

### Unit 1
> When governing people and serving nature, nothing compares to conserving.
- **Says:** in governing people and serving nature, nothing is better than conserving.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated governor (a ruler)
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** "serving nature" may mean serving the natural order/heaven, or tending one's own nature; "conserving" may be frugality, sparing, or storing up.

### Unit 2
> Only conserving is called accepting early,
> accepting early is called a heavy accumulation of integrity,
> a heavy accumulation of integrity is when nothing cannot be overcome,
> when nothing cannot be overcome, no one knows its limit,
> when no one knows its limit, you can have the state.
- **Says:** a chain: conserving is accepting early; that is accumulating integrity; that means everything can be overcome; then no one knows the limit; then you can have the state.
- **Act:** names/defines; if/when … then (chain)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; "you" at the end (the ruler or reader)
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** "accepting early" — what is accepted (the Tao? a course? submission?) is unstated; "Only" may restrict conserving or emphasize; "its limit" may be the integrity's or the person's; "have the state" may mean possess/rule it or be fit to.

### Unit 3
> Have the mother of the state, and you can last long.
- **Says:** if you have the mother of the state, you can endure.
- **Act:** if/when … then (imperative-conditional)
- **Who acts / of whom:** "you"
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** "the mother of the state" is undefined: a source/principle, the root that sustains it, or literally a mother; "last long" may be you or the state.

### Unit 4
> This is called the Tao of deep roots, firm base, long life, and enduring vision.
- **Says:** this is named the way of deep roots, firm foundation, long life and lasting vision.
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** "this"
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** "this" may be Unit 3 or the whole chain; "enduring vision" may be lasting sight or a long-lived outlook.

# Chapter 60

**The chapter as a whole:** Governing a big state is likened to cooking a small fish. When the world is overseen with the Tao, ghosts lose potency and do no harm, and the sage does no harm either; since the two do not harm each other, their integrity comes together and returns.

### Unit 1
> Governing a large state is like cooking a small fish.
- **Says:** running a big state resembles cooking a small fish.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated governor
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** the point of the comparison is not stated: handle gently, don't stir, or don't overcook.

### Unit 2
> Oversee the world with the Tao, and the ghosts lose their potency,
> not only do ghosts lose their potency, their potency causes no harm to people,
> not only does their potency do no harm, the sage, too, does no harm to people.
- **Says:** if the world is overseen by the Tao, ghosts lose potency; and their potency does no harm to people; and the sage also does no harm to people.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** "you"/unstated overseer; ghosts; the sage; done to people
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** logically odd: ghosts "lose" potency yet their potency is then said to do no harm, so "lose their potency" may mean lose effective power, or the second clause corrects the first (not that they lose it, but that it harms no one); "potency" may sound like spiritual/supernatural power.

### Unit 3
> And since the two do no harm to each other, their integrity converges and returns.
- **Says:** because the two do not harm each other, their integrity comes together and goes back.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** "the two" (most likely ghosts and the sage; possibly ghosts' potency and the sage)
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** because
- **Open:** "each other" shifts from "people"; "the two" unclear; "returns" — to whom or where (the people, the source) is unstated.

- **Act:** advises/commands
