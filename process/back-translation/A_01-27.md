# Chapter 1

**The chapter as a whole:** Any Tao that can be turned into a way, and any name that can be named, is not the lasting one. The nameless begins sky and earth, the named mothers all things; the reader is told to look at "its" subtlety without desire and its boundaries with desire. The two share one origin, differ in name, and together are a profound darkness that is the gate of all subtleties.

### Unit 1
> The Tao that can be made a way is not the ever-present Tao.
- **Says:** A Tao that can be made into a way is not the Tao that is always present.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated who "makes" it a way; of the Tao
- **Force:** always/universal (with "can": possible)
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** "made a way" may mean turned into a path to walk, a method or teaching, or put into words; "ever-present" may mean eternal, constant, or always here.

### Unit 2
> The name that can be named is not the ever-present name.
- **Says:** A name that can be given or spoken is not the always-present name.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated namer; of names
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** whether "the ever-present name" is the name of the Tao or a separate, lasting name in general.

### Unit 3
> The nameless is the beginning of sky and earth.
- **Says:** What has no name is where sky and earth begin.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the nameless; of sky and earth
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** "beginning" may be a moment in time or a source; "sky" may be heard as "heaven".

### Unit 4
> The named is the mother of the countless things.
- **Says:** What has a name is the mother of all things.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the named; of the countless things
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel contrast with unit 3)
- **Open:** "mother" literal-figurative (source, nurturer); feminine image.

### Unit 5
> Ever desireless, observe its subtlety;
- **Says:** Remaining always without desire, look at its subtlety.
- **Act:** advises/commands
- **Who acts / of whom:** "you" (reader), observing "it"
- **Force:** habitual ("ever")
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** "its" has no clear antecedent: the Tao, the nameless, the named, or the whole; could also be read as "one who is desireless observes", a statement rather than a command.

### Unit 6
> Ever desiring, observe its boundaries.
- **Says:** Remaining always with desire, look at its boundaries.
- **Act:** advises/commands
- **Who acts / of whom:** "you"; "its" as in unit 5
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and (parallel contrast)
- **Open:** "boundaries" may be edges, limits, or outward manifestations; whether desiring is endorsed or merely allowed as the other mode.

### Unit 7
> These two have the same origin, but differ in name.
- **Says:** The two things just mentioned come from one source but are named differently.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** "these two"
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** "these two" may be the nameless and the named, desireless and desiring, or subtlety and boundaries.

### Unit 8
> Together, they are called profound darkness.
- **Says:** Taken together, the two are named "profound darkness".
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** the two
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** "darkness" may mean mystery/obscurity or literal dark; "together" may mean jointly or their shared origin.

### Unit 9
> Dark, and dark again, the gate of all subtleties.
- **Says:** Darker and darker still, this is the gate through which all subtleties come.
- **Act:** names/defines (verbless)
- **Who acts / of whom:** implied: the profound darkness
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** "gate" as entrance or exit (subtleties emerge or are entered); "dark again" as intensification or as a second, deeper level.

# Chapter 2

**The chapter as a whole:** Recognizing beauty and good brings ugliness and bad into being, since opposites depend on and complete each other. So the sage works by non-doing and teaches without words, lets things arise without starting, owning or relying, and does not stay in the finished work; because the sage does not stay, "it" does not leave.

### Unit 1
> When the world knows beauty as beauty, ugliness arises.
- **Says:** Once everyone recognizes beauty as beauty, ugliness comes into being.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** "the world" (people generally)
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** ugliness may arise as a concept by contrast or as actual ugly things/conduct; "knows as" may mean recognizes or labels.

### Unit 2
> When the world knows good as good, the bad arises.
- **Says:** Once everyone recognizes good as good, bad comes into being.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** the world
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** "good" moral or "good" as skilled/fine; same concept-vs-reality question.

### Unit 3
> Therefore presence and absence give birth to each other,
> Difficult and easy complete each other,
> Long and short measure each other,
> High and low depend on each other,
> Sound and tone harmonize each other,
> Front and back follow each other.
- **Says:** So each pair of opposites produces, completes, measures, depends on, harmonizes with, or follows the other.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the paired opposites, each on the other
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** therefore/so
- **Open:** "presence and absence" as being/non-being or having/lacking; "sound and tone" are near-synonyms to an English reader and the contrast is unclear; whether "therefore" draws a conclusion from units 1–2 or restates them as a general law.

### Unit 4
> Therefore the sage dwells in the work of non-doing
> and carries out the teaching without words.
- **Says:** So the sage abides in a work that consists of not doing and gives the teaching wordlessly.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore/so
- **Open:** "non-doing" as total inaction or as action without striving; "sage" has a reverent, possibly religious ring.

### Unit 5
> The countless things arise, and the sage does not begin them.
- **Says:** All things come into being and the sage does not initiate them.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the countless things arise; the sage does not begin them
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** "does not begin them" as "does not start them off/make them" or "does not presume to take the lead over them".

### Unit 6
> They are born, and the sage does not possess them.
- **Says:** Things are born and the sage does not claim ownership of them.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** things; the sage
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none

### Unit 7
> The sage acts, yet relies on nothing.
- **Says:** The sage does act, but depends on nothing in doing so.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and (with "yet" inside)
- **Open:** "relies on nothing" as needs no support, or does not count on results/rewards; sits in tension with "non-doing" in unit 4.

### Unit 8
> The work is done, and the sage does not stay in it.
- **Says:** When the work is finished, the sage does not remain in it.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; the work
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** "stay in it" as linger, dwell in, or take credit for it.

### Unit 9
> Only because the sage does not stay does it not leave.
- **Says:** Precisely because the sage does not stay, "it" does not depart.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; "it"
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** because
- **Open:** "it" most likely the work or its merit, but could be the things or the sage's standing.

# Chapter 3

**The chapter as a whole:** Not exalting the exceptional, not prizing rare goods and not displaying the desirable keeps the people from contending, stealing and disorder. The sage governs by emptying hearts and filling bellies, weakening ambition and strengthening bones, keeping people unknowing and desireless and the knowers from pressing to act. Doing not-doing leaves nothing ungoverned.

### Unit 1
> By not exalting the exceptional, the people have no cause to contend.
- **Says:** If the exceptional are not exalted, the people have no reason to compete.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated who does not exalt (most likely the ruler/sage); the people
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** grammatically the people are the ones "not exalting"; most readers will take the ruler. "The exceptional" as talented people or exceptional things.

### Unit 2
> By not prizing rare goods, the people do not become thieves.
- **Says:** If rare goods are not valued, the people do not turn into thieves.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated (ruler); the people
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** same dangling-subject question.

### Unit 3
> By not displaying the desirable, the people's hearts are not thrown into disorder.
- **Says:** If desirable things are not shown off, the people's hearts are not disturbed.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated (ruler); the people's hearts
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** "hearts" as minds/feelings; "disorder" as agitation or as social unrest.

### Unit 4
> This is how the sage governs:
> emptying their hearts and filling their bellies,
> weakening their ambition and strengthening their bones,
> so the people are ever unknowing, ever desireless,
> and the knowers never push to act.
- **Says:** The sage rules by emptying people's hearts, filling their bellies, weakening their ambition and strengthening their bones, so that the people stay without knowledge or desire and the knowledgeable never press to act.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; "their" = the people's
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore/so (implicit: this is the method)
- **Open:** "emptying their hearts" may read as calming desire or as dumbing down; "so" as purpose or result; "the knowers" as clever people, officials, or anyone who knows; "never push to act" as do not dare act or do not force their actions; "their" could include the sage's own heart.

### Unit 5
> Do the not-doing, and nothing is left ungoverned.
- **Says:** If one does non-doing, nothing remains unruled.
- **Act:** advises/commands (if/when … then)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; addressed to the reader or a ruler
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** imperative or a general conditional; "ungoverned" as unruled or as in disorder.
