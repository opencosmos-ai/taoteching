# Reader B — chapters 15–28

# Chapter 15

**The chapter as a whole:** The skilled people of old were too deep to be known, so the speaker can only describe them by forced comparison: cautious, wary, grave, loosening, solid, open, murky. Two "who can" questions ask who can let muddiness settle into clarity and stillness stir into life; whoever keeps this way does not want to be full, and because not full, can stay covered (or worn) without being newly made.

### Unit 1
> 古之善為士者，微妙玄通，深不可識。 (*Gǔ zhī shàn wéi shì zhě, wēi miào xuán tōng, shēn bù kě shí* — "Those of antiquity who were good at being officers: minute, subtle, dark, penetrating; deep, not able to be known.")
- **Says:** The ones of old who were skilled at being 士 (*shì* — "officer / man of service") were subtle, fine, dark and penetrating, so deep they could not be known.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the skilled 士 (*shì* — "officer") of antiquity; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates the possibility of their being known (不可識 (*bù kě shí* — "cannot be known/recognized"))
- **Force:** past
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 玄通 (*xuán tōng* — "dark, penetrating") as one phrase "darkly penetrating / in dark communion" or as two of four separate qualities 微、妙、玄、通 (*wēi, miào, xuán, tōng* — "minute, subtle, dark, through-going").
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong takes the 士 (*shì* — "officer") as a ruler: 謂得道之君也 (*wèi dé dào zhī jūn yě* — "it refers to rulers who attained the Tao"), and 玄 (*xuán* — "dark") as heaven: 玄天也 (*xuán tiān yě* — "*xuán* is heaven"), their purpose 精與天通 (*jīng yǔ tiān tōng* — "in essence communing with heaven").

### Unit 2
> 夫唯不可識，故強為之容：豫兮若冬涉川； (*Fú wéi bù kě shí, gù qiǎng wèi zhī róng: yù xī ruò dōng shè chuān* — "Just because they cannot be known, therefore forcing it, for them a description: hesitant, as if crossing a river in winter.")
- **Says:** Precisely because they cannot be known, the speaker forces himself to give an account of their appearance; first: hesitant, like someone crossing a river in winter.
- **Act:** if/when … then (causal 夫唯 … 故 (*fú wéi … gù* — "just because … therefore")), then describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the describer is unstated, most likely the speaking "I" of the book; the described are the skilled ones of Unit 1
- **Force:** past (for the description); the forcing is the speaker's present act
- **Link:** because / therefore
- **Open:** 為之容 (*wèi zhī róng* — "for them [make] a description") vs. *wéi zhī róng* ("make their likeness"); either way 容 (*róng* — "appearance, outward look") is a portrait, not the essence. 冬涉川 (*dōng shè chuān* — "in winter cross a river"): wary as on ice, or reluctant to wade into freezing water.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi reads irresolution: 冬之涉川豫然若欲度若不欲度其情不可得見之貌也 (*dōng zhī shè chuān yù rán ruò yù dù ruò bù yù dù qí qíng bù kě dé jiàn zhī mào yě* — "crossing a river in winter, hesitant, as if wanting to cross, as if not wanting to cross: the look of one whose feelings cannot be seen"). Heshang Gong reads 與 (*yǔ* — "with; a variant graph") for 豫 (*yù* — "hesitant") and takes it as caution in action: 舉事輙加重愼 (*jǔ shì zhé jiā zhòng shèn* — "in undertaking affairs, always adding great care").

### Unit 3
> 猶兮若畏四鄰； (*Yóu xī ruò wèi sì lín* — "Wary, as if fearing the neighbours on four sides.")
- **Says:** Wary, like someone afraid of the neighbours all around.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the skilled ones of old
- **Force:** past
- **Link:** and (list)
- **Open:** fear of being attacked from all sides vs. fear of being seen by all around.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 四鄰合攻中央之主猶然不知所趣向者也 (*sì lín hé gōng zhōng yāng zhī zhǔ yóu rán bù zhī suǒ qù xiàng zhě yě* — "a ruler in the center whom the four neighbours attack together, wavering, not knowing which way to turn"). Heshang Gong: 若人犯法畏四隣知之也 (*ruò rén fàn fǎ wèi sì lín zhī zhī yě* — "like a person who has broken the law, fearing the neighbours on four sides will know of it").

### Unit 4
> 儼兮其若客； (*Yǎn xī qí ruò kè* — "Grave, they are like a guest.")
- **Says:** Grave and dignified, like a guest.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 其 (*qí* — "they / their") = the skilled ones of old
- **Force:** past
- **Link:** and (list)
- **Open:** none in the base. Wang Bi's lemma reads 容 (*róng* — "appearance") for 客 (*kè* — "guest"), with a note that one text has 客 (*kè* — "guest").
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 如客畏主人儼然無所造作也 (*rú kè wèi zhǔ rén yǎn rán wú suǒ zào zuò yě* — "like a guest in awe of the host: grave, initiating nothing").

### Unit 5
> 渙兮其若冰之將釋； (*Huàn xī qí ruò bīng zhī jiāng shì* — "Loosening, they are like ice about to melt.")
- **Says:** Dispersing, loosening, like ice that is about to melt.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the skilled ones of old
- **Force:** past; 將 (*jiāng* — "about to") marks the imminent melt
- **Link:** and (list)
- **Open:** 渙 (*huàn* — "dissolve, disperse; spread loose") as yielding, unrigid ease, or as dissolving of self.
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 渙者解散釋者消亡除情去欲日以空虚 (*huàn zhě jiě sàn, shì zhě xiāo wáng, chú qíng qù yù rì yǐ kōng xū* — "*huàn* is loosening and scattering; *shì* is melting away: removing feelings, shedding desires, daily growing empty").

### Unit 6
> 敦兮其若樸； (*Dūn xī qí ruò pǔ* — "Solid, they are like uncarved wood.")
- **Says:** Thick and solid, like the uncarved block.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the skilled ones of old
- **Force:** past
- **Link:** and (list)
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 敦者質厚朴者形未分 (*dūn zhě zhì hòu, pǔ zhě xíng wèi fēn* — "*dūn* is substantial and thick; *pǔ* is form not yet divided").

### Unit 7
> 曠兮其若谷； (*Kuàng xī qí ruò gǔ* — "Open-wide, they are like a valley.")
- **Says:** Wide and empty, like a valley.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the skilled ones of old
- **Force:** past
- **Link:** and (list)
- **Open:** none

### Unit 8
> 混兮其若濁。 (*Hùn xī qí ruò zhuó* — "Mixed, they are like muddy water.")
- **Says:** Blended, undistinguished, like murky water.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the skilled ones of old
- **Force:** past
- **Link:** and (list)
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong (reading 渾 (*hún* — "turbid, whole")) makes it social: 與衆合同不自尊 (*yǔ zhòng hé tóng bù zì zūn* — "joining in with the crowd, not exalting oneself").

### Unit 9
> 孰能濁以靜之徐清？ (*Shú néng zhuó yǐ jìng zhī xú qīng?* — "Who can, muddy, by stilling it, slowly clear?")
- **Says:** Who is able, being muddy (or taking what is muddy), by stilling it to let it gradually become clear?
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical: implies it is rare and hard; the next lines point to the one who keeps this way)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 孰 (*shú* — "who") open; the "who" is the person, and 之 (*zhī* — "it") the muddiness
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** none (shift from description to question)
- **Open:** intransitive (the person is muddy and settles into clarity) vs. transitive (the person stills muddy things so they clear).
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 孰能者言其難也 (*shú néng zhě yán qí nán yě* — "'who can' speaks of its difficulty"), and reads it as acting on things: 濁以静物則得清 (*zhuó yǐ jìng wù zé dé qīng* — "muddy, by stilling things, then clarity is obtained"). Heshang Gong: 誰能知水之濁止而淨之徐徐自清也 (*shuí néng zhī shuǐ zhī zhuó zhǐ ér jìng zhī xú xú zì qīng yě* — "who can know that muddy water, stopped and calmed, slowly slowly clears of itself").

### Unit 10
> 孰能安以動之徐生？ (*Shú néng ān yǐ dòng zhī xú shēng?* — "Who can, at rest, by stirring it, slowly bring to life?")
- **Says:** Who is able, being at rest, by setting it in motion to let life gradually arise?
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical: implies it is rare and hard)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 孰 (*shú* — "who") open; 之 (*zhī* — "it") the rest/stillness
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** 生 (*shēng* — "life, grow, arise") as things coming to life vs. long life for the person. Both commentators' lemmas add 久 (*jiǔ* — "long"): 安以久動之 (*ān yǐ jiǔ dòng zhī* — "at rest for long, by stirring it"); the base lacks it.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 安以動物則得生 (*ān yǐ dòng wù zé dé shēng* — "at rest, by moving things, then life is obtained"). Heshang Gong: 誰能安靜以久徐徐以長生也 (*shuí néng ān jìng yǐ jiǔ xú xú yǐ cháng shēng yě* — "who can be calm and still for long, slowly slowly thereby living long").

### Unit 11
> 保此道者不欲盈。 (*Bǎo cǐ dào zhě bù yù yíng* — "One who keeps this way does not desire to be full.")
- **Says:** Whoever preserves this way does not want to be full.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 保此道者 (*bǎo cǐ dào zhě* — "the one who keeps this way"), generic; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates the wanting
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (answers the questions implicitly)
- **Open:** 此道 (*cǐ dào* — "this way / this Tao"): the way just described, or the Tao itself.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 盈必溢也 (*yíng bì yì yě* — "what is full must overflow").

### Unit 12
> 夫唯不盈，故能蔽而不新成。 (*Fú wéi bù yíng, gù néng bì ér bù xīn chéng* — "Just because not full, therefore can be covered and not newly completed.")
- **Says:** Precisely because such a one is not full, they can remain covered (or worn) and not be newly completed.
- **Act:** if/when … then (causal 夫唯 … 故 (*fú wéi … gù* — "just because … therefore"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** the one who keeps this way; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates fullness and then 新成 (*xīn chéng* — "new completion")
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** because / therefore
- **Open:** 蔽 (*bì* — "cover, conceal") vs. 敝 (*bì* — "worn out"; the Yongle Dadian reading noted under Wang Bi): "can stay hidden and not achieve anew" vs. "can wear out and not be renewed". The base has 而 (*ér* — "and/yet"); both lemmas lack it.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 蔽覆盖也 (*bì fù gài yě* — "*bì* means covering over"). Heshang Gong: 能守蔽不為新成蔽者匿光榮也新成者貴功名 (*néng shǒu bì bù wéi xīn chéng, bì zhě nì guāng róng yě, xīn chéng zhě guì gōng míng* — "can keep concealed and not make new achievements; *bì* is hiding one's light and glory; *xīn chéng* is prizing achievement and fame").

# Chapter 16

**The chapter as a whole:** Reach the utmost emptiness and hold stillness firmly, and you can watch all things rise and then return to their root. Returning to the root is named stillness, returning to one's 命 (*mìng* — "allotment, life, mandate"), constancy; knowing constancy is clarity, and not knowing it brings disaster. A chain follows: knowing constancy leads to capaciousness, impartiality, kingliness, heaven, the Tao, and lasting, so that to the end of life there is no danger.

### Unit 1
> 致虛極，守靜篤。 (*Zhì xū jí, shǒu jìng dǔ* — "Bring emptiness to the utmost; keep stillness steadfast.")
- **Says:** Carry emptiness to its limit; hold to stillness firmly.
- **Act:** advises/commands (or describes the practice)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; the reader / the practitioner, matched by 吾 (*wú* — "I") in Unit 2
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** imperative vs. description of what the speaker does. 極 (*jí* — "utmost") and 篤 (*dǔ* — "firm, solid") as adverbs ("to the utmost / firmly") or nouns ("the extreme of emptiness / the core of stillness").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi makes them properties of things: 言致虚物之極篤守静物之真正也 (*yán zhì xū wù zhī jí dǔ, shǒu jìng wù zhī zhēn zhèng yě* — "it says: reaching emptiness is the utmost of things; firmly keeping stillness is the true rightness of things"). Heshang Gong (lemma 至 (*zhì* — "arrive")) makes it personal cultivation: 得道之人捐情去欲五内清淨至於虚極 (*dé dào zhī rén juān qíng qù yù wǔ nèi qīng jìng zhì yú xū jí* — "the person who attains the Tao discards feelings, sheds desires, the five inner organs pure and clean, arriving at the extreme of emptiness").

### Unit 2
> 萬物並作，吾以觀復。 (*Wàn wù bìng zuò, wú yǐ guān fù* — "The myriad things arise together; I thereby watch the return.")
- **Says:** All things spring up together, and by this (the emptiness and stillness) I observe their returning.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 萬物 (*wàn wù* — "the ten thousand things") arise; 吾 (*wú* — "I") watches
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and (from the practice to what it shows)
- **Open:** 以 (*yǐ* — "by means of / thereby"): by means of emptiness and stillness, or simply "and so". Wang Bi notes other texts read 觀其復 (*guān qí fù* — "watch their return").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 以虛静觀其反復 (*yǐ xū jìng guān qí fǎn fù* — "with emptiness and stillness, watch their reversion").

### Unit 3
> 夫物芸芸，各復歸其根。 (*Fū wù yún yún, gè fù guī qí gēn* — "Now, things flourish and flourish; each returns again to its root.")
- **Says:** Things, however profusely they grow, each go back to their root.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 物 (*wù* — "things"), each one
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** 夫 (*fū* — "now, as for"), introducing the general case
- **Open:** 復 (*fù* — "again / return") as "again return" or doubling "go back, return".
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong reads a cycle of rebirth: 言萬物無不枯落各復反其根而更生也 (*yán wàn wù wú bù kū luò gè fù fǎn qí gēn ér gēng shēng yě* — "it says the myriad things all wither and fall, each goes back to its root and is born again").

### Unit 4
> 歸根曰靜，是謂復命。 (*Guī gēn yuē jìng, shì wèi fù mìng* — "Returning to the root is called stillness; this is called returning to the mandate.")
- **Says:** Going back to the root is named stillness; that is named returning to 命 (*mìng* — "life, allotment, mandate").
- **Act:** names/defines (曰 (*yuē* — "is called"); 是謂 (*shì wèi* — "this is called"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** things returning
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (naming chain)
- **Open:** 復命 (*fù mìng* — "return-mandate"): return to one's allotted life/nature, or the well-attested sense "report back on a commission completed".
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 復命則得性命之常 (*fù mìng zé dé xìng mìng zhī cháng* — "returning to *mìng*, one obtains the constancy of nature and allotted life"). Heshang Gong: 言安靜者是為復還性命使不死也 (*yán ān jìng zhě shì wéi fù huán xìng mìng shǐ bù sǐ yě* — "it says being calm and still is returning to one's nature and life, so as not to die").

### Unit 5
> 復命曰常，知常曰明。 (*Fù mìng yuē cháng, zhī cháng yuē míng* — "Returning to the mandate is called constancy; knowing constancy is called clarity.")
- **Says:** Returning to 命 (*mìng* — "allotment") is named the constant; knowing the constant is named clarity.
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** the knower is unstated: anyone who knows
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (naming chain)
- **Open:** 明 (*míng* — "bright, clear-seeing"): insight in the knower, or illumination as a state.
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong makes 常 (*cháng* — "constant") "what the Tao constantly does": 能知道之所常行則為明 (*néng zhī dào zhī suǒ cháng xíng zé wéi míng* — "to be able to know what the Tao constantly does is clarity").

### Unit 6
> 不知常，妄作凶。 (*Bù zhī cháng, wàng zuò xiōng* — "Not knowing constancy, acting recklessly: ill-omened.")
- **Says:** If one does not know the constant, one acts blindly, and that is disastrous.
- **Act:** if/when … then (implicit)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; anyone. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates the knowing
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** but (contrast with Unit 5)
- **Open:** "acting recklessly brings disaster" vs. "reckless action, and disaster" as two results. Heshang Gong's lemma has 萎 (*wěi* — "wither") for 妄 (*wàng* — "reckless"), but his comment reads 妄 (*wàng* — "reckless").
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 妄作巧詐則失神明故凶也 (*wàng zuò qiǎo zhà zé shī shén míng gù xiōng yě* — "acting recklessly with cunning and deceit, one loses spirit-brightness, therefore it is ill-omened").

### Unit 7
> 知常容，容乃公，公乃王，王乃天，天乃道，道乃久，沒身不殆。 (*Zhī cháng róng, róng nǎi gōng, gōng nǎi wáng, wáng nǎi tiān, tiān nǎi dào, dào nǎi jiǔ, mò shēn bù dài* — "Knowing constancy, capacious; capacious, then impartial; impartial, then kingly; kingly, then heaven; heaven, then the Tao; the Tao, then lasting; to the end of the body, no danger.")
- **Says:** Knowing the constant makes one all-containing; containing leads to impartiality, impartiality to kingliness, kingliness to heaven, heaven to the Tao, the Tao to lasting, and to the end of one's life one is not in danger.
- **Act:** if/when … then (a chain with 乃 (*nǎi* — "then, thereupon"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; the one who knows the constant. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 殆 (*dài* — "danger")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** but / and (back to the positive case)
- **Open:** 王 (*wáng* — "king, kingly"): literally becoming king, or "all-pervading". 沒身不殆 (*mò shēn bù dài* — "end-body not-danger"): "lifelong without danger" vs. "even when the body perishes, no danger".
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi reads 王 (*wáng* — "kingly") as universality: 無所不周普 (*wú suǒ bù zhōu pǔ* — "nothing it does not reach everywhere"). Heshang Gong reads it as rule: 公正無私可以為天下王 (*gōng zhèng wú sī kě yǐ wéi tiān xià wáng* — "impartial, upright, without self-interest, one can be king of all under heaven"), and the end as 乃與天地俱没不危殆也 (*nǎi yǔ tiān dì jù mò bù wēi dài yě* — "then one ends together with heaven and earth, without danger").

# Chapter 17

**The chapter as a whole:** Rulers are ranked by how those below regard them: the highest are merely known to exist, the next loved and praised, the next feared, the lowest scorned. Where trust is lacking, distrust appears. The best ruler, unhurried, values words; when the work is done the people all say it happened of itself.

### Unit 1
> 太上，下知有之； (*Tài shàng, xià zhī yǒu zhī* — "The very highest: those below know it exists.")
- **Says:** With the highest kind, those below know only that it (the ruler) is there.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 下 (*xià* — "those below") know; 之 (*zhī* — "it / him") = the highest ruler
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 太上 (*tài shàng* — "the very highest"): the best kind of ruler (a ranking) vs. the ruler of highest antiquity. Wang Bi notes the Yongle Dadian and Wu Cheng read 不 (*bù* — "not") for 下 (*xià* — "below"): "the highest: [people] do not know it exists".
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 太上謂大人也 (*tài shàng wèi dà rén yě* — "*tài shàng* means the great person"), who 居無為之事行不言之教 (*jū wú wéi zhī shì xíng bù yán zhī jiào* — "abides in the business of not-doing and practises the teaching without words"). Heshang Gong: 太上謂太古无名之君也 (*tài shàng wèi tài gǔ wú míng zhī jūn yě* — "*tài shàng* means the nameless rulers of high antiquity").

### Unit 2
> 其次，親而譽之； (*Qí cì, qīn ér yù zhī* — "The next: they draw close and praise him.")
- **Says:** With the next kind, the people feel close to him and praise him.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** those below; 之 (*zhī* — "him") the ruler
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (ranking sequence)
- **Open:** none. Wang Bi notes Heshang Gong and others read 之 (*zhī* — "him") for 而 (*ér* — "and"): 親之譽之 (*qīn zhī yù zhī* — "love him, praise him").

### Unit 3
> 其次，畏之； (*Qí cì, wèi zhī* — "The next: they fear him.")
- **Says:** With the next kind, the people fear him.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** those below fear the ruler
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (ranking sequence)
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 設刑法以治之 (*shè xíng fǎ yǐ zhì zhī* — "he sets up punishments and laws to govern them").

### Unit 4
> 其次，侮之。 (*Qí cì, wǔ zhī* — "The next: they scorn him.")
- **Says:** With the lowest kind, the people despise him.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** those below scorn the ruler
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (ranking sequence)
- **Open:** none

### Unit 5
> 信不足焉，有不信焉。 (*Xìn bù zú yān, yǒu bù xìn yān* — "Where trust is not sufficient, there is no trust.")
- **Says:** When trust(worthiness) falls short, distrust appears.
- **Act:** if/when … then (implicit)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; most likely the ruler's trustworthiness, met by the people's distrust. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 足 (*zú* — "suffice") and then 信 (*xìn* — "trust")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (explains the decline)
- **Open:** 信 (*xìn* — "trust / trustworthiness"): the ruler's reliability, or trust in general; the second clause "there is distrust" vs. "there are those who do not trust".
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 君信不足於下下則應之以不信而欺其君也 (*jūn xìn bù zú yú xià, xià zé yìng zhī yǐ bù xìn ér qī qí jūn yě* — "when the ruler's trustworthiness toward those below is insufficient, those below answer with distrust and deceive their ruler"). Wang Bi makes it a natural law: 信不足焉則有不信此自然之道也 (*xìn bù zú yān zé yǒu bù xìn cǐ zì rán zhī dào yě* — "trust insufficient, then there is distrust: this is the way of what is so of itself").

### Unit 6
> 悠兮其貴言。 (*Yōu xī qí guì yán* — "Unhurried, he values words.")
- **Says:** At ease and remote, the best ruler holds words precious (speaks little).
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 其 (*qí* — "he / his") = the highest ruler of Unit 1
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none (returns to the best ruler)
- **Open:** 貴言 (*guì yán* — "value words"): sparing with words vs. making words weighty. Wang Bi notes Heshang Gong and others read 猶 (*yóu* — "hesitant, cautious") for 悠 (*yōu* — "leisurely, remote").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 無物可以易其言言必有應 (*wú wù kě yǐ yì qí yán, yán bì yǒu yìng* — "nothing can alter his words; his words always meet a response"). Heshang Gong: 太上之君舉事猶貴重於言恐離道失自然 (*tài shàng zhī jūn jǔ shì yóu guì zhòng yú yán, kǒng lí dào shī zì rán* — "the highest ruler, in undertaking affairs, is cautious and weighs words, fearing to leave the Tao and lose what is so of itself").

### Unit 7
> 功成事遂，百姓皆謂：「我自然」。 (*Gōng chéng shì suì, bǎi xìng jiē wèi: "Wǒ zì rán"* — "Work completed, affairs accomplished, the hundred clans all say: 'We are so of ourselves.'")
- **Says:** When the work is done and the task achieved, the people all say, "We did it ourselves / it is just our own way."
- **Act:** asserts (with quoted speech)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 百姓 (*bǎi xìng* — "the hundred clans, the people") speak; 我 (*wǒ* — "I / we") is the people
- **Force:** always/universal (when the best ruler governs)
- **Link:** and (result of the ruler's restraint)
- **Open:** 我自然 (*wǒ zì rán* — "we, so of ourselves"): "we did it by ourselves" vs. "it happened naturally to us".
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 百姓不知君上之德淳厚反以為只自當然也 (*bǎi xìng bù zhī jūn shàng zhī dé chún hòu, fǎn yǐ wéi zhǐ zì dāng rán yě* — "the people do not know the ruler's virtue is pure and deep, and instead think it was simply so of itself"). Wang Bi: 百姓不知其所以然也 (*bǎi xìng bù zhī qí suǒ yǐ rán yě* — "the people do not know how it came to be so").

# Chapter 18

**The chapter as a whole:** Four parallel claims: humaneness and duty appear when the great Tao is abandoned, great artifice when cleverness emerges, filial piety and parental kindness when family relations fall out of harmony, loyal ministers when the state is in disorder. The named virtues show up only as symptoms of their loss.

### Unit 1
> 大道廢，有仁義； (*Dà dào fèi, yǒu rén yì* — "The great Tao abandoned: there is humaneness and duty.")
- **Says:** When the great Tao is set aside, humaneness and duty appear.
- **Act:** if/when … then (implicit; no 則 (*zé* — "then"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** impersonal
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** temporal ("when, then") vs. causal ("because abandoned, therefore"); whether 仁義 (*rén yì* — "humaneness and duty") is condemned or only placed later in sequence.
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 大道廢不用惡逆生乃有仁義可傳道 (*dà dào fèi bù yòng è nì shēng nǎi yǒu rén yì kě chuán dào* — "when the great Tao is abandoned and unused, evil and rebellion arise, and only then are there humaneness and duty to be passed on and spoken of").

### Unit 2
> 智慧出，有大偽； (*Zhì huì chū, yǒu dà wěi* — "Cleverness and wisdom emerge: there is great artifice.")
- **Says:** When cleverness comes out, great falsity appears.
- **Act:** if/when … then (implicit)
- **Who acts / of whom:** impersonal
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** 偽 (*wěi* — "false; man-made") as deceit/hypocrisy vs. artifice, human contrivance. Wang Bi's lemma reads 慧智 (*huì zhì* — "wisdom, cleverness").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 行術用明以察姦偽趣覩形見物知避之故智慧出則大偽生也 (*xíng shù yòng míng yǐ chá jiān wěi, qù dǔ xíng jiàn, wù zhī bì zhī, gù zhì huì chū zé dà wěi shēng yě* — "applying techniques and using sharpness to detect wickedness and deceit, intentions and forms become visible, things know to evade it; so when cleverness emerges, great deceit arises"). Heshang Gong: 智惠之君賤德而貴言 (*zhì huì zhī jūn jiàn dé ér guì yán* — "the clever ruler scorns virtue and prizes words").

### Unit 3
> 六親不和，有孝慈； (*Liù qīn bù hé, yǒu xiào cí* — "The six relations not in harmony: there is filial piety and kindness.")
- **Says:** When family relations are discordant, filial devotion and parental kindness appear.
- **Act:** if/when … then (implicit)
- **Who acts / of whom:** impersonal; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 和 (*hé* — "harmony")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi lists the 六親 (*liù qīn* — "six relations") as 父子兄弟夫婦也 (*fù zǐ xiōng dì fū fù yě* — "father and son, elder and younger brother, husband and wife").

### Unit 4
> 國家昏亂，有忠臣。 (*Guó jiā hūn luàn, yǒu zhōng chén* — "The state dark and in disorder: there are loyal ministers.")
- **Says:** When the state is benighted and chaotic, loyal ministers appear.
- **Act:** if/when … then (implicit)
- **Who acts / of whom:** impersonal
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi on the whole: 甚美之名生於大惡 (*shèn měi zhī míng shēng yú dà è* — "the most beautiful names are born from great evil"), and 若六親自和國家自治則孝慈忠臣不知其所在矣 (*ruò liù qīn zì hé guó jiā zì zhì zé xiào cí zhōng chén bù zhī qí suǒ zài yǐ* — "if the six relations harmonised of themselves and the state governed itself, filial piety, kindness and loyal ministers would not know where they were").

# Chapter 19

**The chapter as a whole:** Cut off sageliness and knowledge, humaneness and duty, cleverness and profit, and the people gain a hundredfold, return to filial piety and kindness, and thieves vanish. Because these three statements alone are not enough, something is given for the people to hold to: show plainness, embrace the uncarved, lessen self-interest, reduce desire.

### Unit 1
> 絕聖棄智，民利百倍； (*Jué shèng qì zhì, mín lì bǎi bèi* — "Cut off sageliness, discard knowledge: the people benefit a hundredfold.")
- **Says:** Get rid of sageliness and cast away knowledge, and the people's benefit is multiplied a hundred times.
- **Act:** if/when … then (or advises/commands, with a promised result)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the cutter is unstated, most likely the ruler; 民 (*mín* — "the people") benefit
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** command vs. conditional. 聖 (*shèng* — "sage, sagacity") as the quality of sageliness vs. "sagely making" (institutions, writing).
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 絶聖制作反初守元 (*jué shèng zhì zuò fǎn chū shǒu yuán* — "cut off sagely making and creating; return to the beginning, keep to the origin"). Wang Bi: 聖智才之善也 (*shèng zhì cái zhī shàn yě* — "sageliness and knowledge are the best of talent").

### Unit 2
> 絕仁棄義，民復孝慈； (*Jué rén qì yì, mín fù xiào cí* — "Cut off humaneness, discard duty: the people return to filial piety and kindness.")
- **Says:** Get rid of humaneness and cast away duty, and the people go back to filial devotion and parental kindness.
- **Act:** if/when … then (or advises/commands)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated, most likely the ruler; the people return
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** 復 (*fù* — "return / again"): return to an earlier state vs. "again have".
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 絶仁之見恩惠棄義之尚華信 (*jué rén zhī jiàn ēn huì, qì yì zhī shàng huá xìn* — "cut off humaneness that displays favours; discard duty that prizes showy pledges").

### Unit 3
> 絕巧棄利，盜賊無有。 (*Jué qiǎo qì lì, dào zéi wú yǒu* — "Cut off cleverness, discard profit: robbers and thieves do not exist.")
- **Says:** Get rid of craft and cast away profit, and there are no robbers or thieves.
- **Act:** if/when … then (or advises/commands)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated, most likely the ruler; 無有 (*wú yǒu* — "there are none") negates the existence of thieves
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** none

### Unit 4
> 此三者以為文不足，故令有所屬：見素抱樸，少私寡欲。 (*Cǐ sān zhě yǐ wéi wén bù zú, gù lìng yǒu suǒ shǔ: xiàn sù bào pǔ, shǎo sī guǎ yù* — "These three, taken as pattern, are not sufficient; therefore let there be something to belong to: show the plain silk, embrace the uncarved wood, lessen the private, reduce desires.")
- **Says:** These three, as 文 (*wén* — "pattern, wording, culture"), are not enough, so something is given for the people to attach to: display plainness, hold to the uncarved, have little self-interest and few desires.
- **Act:** asserts, then advises/commands
- **Who acts / of whom:** 令 (*lìng* — "cause, let") has an unstated causer, likely the ruler or the speaker; those who 有所屬 (*yǒu suǒ shǔ* — "have something they belong to") are the people. 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 足 (*zú* — "suffice")
- **Force:** must/necessary (implied by "not sufficient")
- **Link:** therefore (故 (*gù* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** "as a formulation, these three [sayings] are inadequate" vs. "these three [sageliness, humaneness, cleverness] are 文 (*wén* — "cultural adornment") and inadequate". 見 (*xiàn* — "show") vs. *jiàn* ("see"): "display plainness" vs. "see the plain".
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi takes the first: 直云絶文甚不足 (*zhí yún jué wén shèn bù zú* — "merely saying 'cut off', the wording is very insufficient") … 故令人有所屬屬之於素樸寡欲 (*gù lìng rén yǒu suǒ shǔ, shǔ zhī yú sù pǔ guǎ yù* — "so it makes people have something to attach to: attaching them to plainness, the uncarved and few desires"). Heshang Gong: 文不足以教民 (*wén bù zú yǐ jiào mín* — "pattern is insufficient to teach the people"); he reads 見 (*xiàn* — "show") as "show" and makes 抱朴 (*bào pǔ* — "embrace the uncarved") the ruler showing simplicity 以示下 (*yǐ shì xià* — "to show those below").

# Chapter 20

**The chapter as a whole:** Cut off learning and there is no anxiety: the distinctions learning trains (a polite yes from a careless one, good from bad) are small, though what people fear must still be feared. Then a first-person contrast: the crowd is festive, full, bright, sharp and purposeful, while "I" alone am still, unformed like an unsmiling infant, adrift, seemingly lacking, a fool, dim, dull, obtuse; I alone differ from others, and value the feeding mother.

### Unit 1
> 絕學無憂。 (*Jué xué wú yōu* — "Cut off learning: no anxiety.")
- **Says:** Get rid of learning, and there is no worry.
- **Act:** if/when … then (or advises/commands with result)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; the reader, or the speaker; 無 (*wú* — "there is no") negates the existence of 憂 (*yōu* — "anxiety")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** command ("cut off learning!") vs. conditional ("cut off learning and there is no worry").
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong restricts the learning cut off: 絶學不眞不合道文 (*jué xué bù zhēn bù hé dào wén* — "cut off learning that is not genuine and does not accord with the pattern of the Tao"). Wang Bi links it to "those who do learning daily increase": 學求益所能而進其智者也 (*xué qiú yì suǒ néng ér jìn qí zhì zhě yě* — "learning seeks to add to what one can do and to advance one's knowledge").

### Unit 2
> 唯之與阿，相去幾何？ (*Wéi zhī yǔ ē, xiāng qù jǐ hé?* — "A 'yes, sir' and an 'uh-huh': how far apart are they?")
- **Says:** How much difference is there between a respectful "yes" and a careless "yeah"?
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical: implies very little)
- **Who acts / of whom:** impersonal; 唯 (*wéi* — "a prompt, respectful 'yes'") and 阿 (*ē* — "a slack, casual 'yes'")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (illustrates what learning distinguishes)
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 同為應對而相去幾何 (*tóng wéi yìng duì ér xiāng qù jǐ hé* — "both are answers; how far apart are they?"), and reads it as a complaint that the age 賤質而貴文 (*jiàn zhì ér guì wén* — "scorns substance and prizes ornament").

### Unit 3
> 善之與惡，相去若何？ (*Shàn zhī yǔ è, xiāng qù ruò hé?* — "Good and bad: how far apart are they?")
- **Says:** How far apart, really, are good and bad?
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical: implies little, or less than supposed)
- **Who acts / of whom:** impersonal
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** 惡 (*è* — "bad") vs. *wù*/ugly: the pair as good/bad or fine/ugly. Wang Bi's comment pairs 美惡 (*měi è* — "beautiful and ugly"). Heshang Gong's lemma reads 何若 (*hé ruò* — "how") for 若何 (*ruò hé* — "how").
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong reads court speech: 善者和譽惡者諫爭 (*shàn zhě hé yù, è zhě jiàn zhēng* — "*shàn* is agreeing and praising; *è* is remonstrating and disputing").

### Unit 4
> 人之所畏，不可不畏。 (*Rén zhī suǒ wèi, bù kě bù wèi* — "What people fear, one cannot not fear.")
- **Says:** What others fear, one must also fear.
- **Act:** asserts (a necessity)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; "one", likely the speaker ("I"); double negation 不可不 (*bù kě bù* — "cannot not") = must
- **Force:** must/necessary
- **Link:** but (a limit on the indifference just expressed)
- **Open:** "what people fear, I must fear too" vs. "the one whom people fear (the ruler) must also fear [them]" (人之所畏 (*rén zhī suǒ wèi* — "what/whom people fear")).
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 故人之所畏吾亦畏焉未敢恃之以為用也 (*gù rén zhī suǒ wèi wú yì wèi yān, wèi gǎn shì zhī yǐ wéi yòng yě* — "so what people fear, I too fear; I do not dare rely on [my difference] for use"). Heshang Gong: 人謂道人也人所畏者畏不絶學之君也 (*rén wèi dào rén yě, rén suǒ wèi zhě wèi bù jué xué zhī jūn yě* — "'people' means people of the Tao; what they fear is a ruler who does not cut off learning").

### Unit 5
> 荒兮，其未央哉！ (*Huāng xī, qí wèi yāng zāi!* — "Vast and wild, it has not yet reached its center!")
- **Says:** How vast! It has no end yet.
- **Act:** asserts (exclamation: 哉 (*zāi* — exclamatory particle))
- **Who acts / of whom:** 其 (*qí* — "it") unstated: the gap between the speaker and the world, or the world's wildness; 未 (*wèi* — "not yet") negates 央 (*yāng* — "center, end")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** the "it" is the distance between the speaker and common people, or the endless disorder of worldly striving.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 歎與俗相返之逺也 (*tàn yǔ sú xiāng fǎn zhī yuǎn yě* — "a sigh at how far [I] run opposite to the common"). Heshang Gong: 或言世俗人荒亂欲進學為文未央止也 (*huò yán shì sú rén huāng luàn yù jìn xué wéi wén wèi yāng zhǐ yě* — "some say: worldly people are wild and disordered, wanting to advance in learning and make ornament, never stopping").

### Unit 6
> 眾人熙熙，如享太牢，如春登臺。 (*Zhòng rén xī xī, rú xiǎng tài láo, rú chūn dēng tái* — "The crowd are merry, merry, as if feasting at the great sacrifice, as if climbing a terrace in spring.")
- **Says:** Everyone else is jubilant, as at a feast of the full sacrificial offering, or as when climbing a terrace in springtime.
- **Act:** describes a person (the crowd)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 眾人 (*zhòng rén* — "the many people")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none (begins the contrast)
- **Open:** none. Wang Bi notes one text reads 登春臺 (*dēng chūn tái* — "climb the spring terrace").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 衆人迷於美進惑於榮利 (*zhòng rén mí yú měi jìn huò yú róng lì* — "the crowd are lost in fine advancement, deluded by glory and profit"). Heshang Gong: 熈熈淫放多情欲也 (*xī xī yín fàng duō qíng yù yě* — "*xī xī* is dissolute and full of passions and desires").

### Unit 7
> 我獨泊兮，其未兆； (*Wǒ dú bó xī, qí wèi zhào* — "I alone am still, without a sign yet.")
- **Says:** I alone am placid, showing no sign as yet.
- **Act:** describes a person (self)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 我 (*wǒ* — "I"); 未 (*wèi* — "not yet") negates 兆 (*zhào* — "omen, first sign")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** but (contrast with the crowd)
- **Open:** 泊 (*bó* — "moored, still, placid"): still and calm vs. anchored, unmoving.
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong (lemma 怕 (*bó* — "placid", read so here)): 我獨怕然安靜未有情欲之形兆也 (*wǒ dú bó rán ān jìng wèi yǒu qíng yù zhī xíng zhào yě* — "I alone am placid, calm and still, with no shape or sign of passions and desires yet"). Wang Bi: 言我廓然無形之可名無兆之可舉 (*yán wǒ kuò rán wú xíng zhī kě míng wú zhào zhī kě jǔ* — "it says I am vast, with no form that can be named, no sign that can be pointed out").

### Unit 8
> 如嬰兒之未孩； (*Rú yīng ér zhī wèi hái* — "Like an infant that has not yet smiled.")
- **Says:** Like a newborn who has not yet laughed (or responded).
- **Act:** describes a person (self)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the speaker compared to an infant; 未 (*wèi* — "not yet") negates 孩 (*hái* — "a baby's laugh")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and (continues the simile)
- **Open:** 孩 (*hái* — "baby laugh") as the first smile vs. first social response.
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 如小兒未能荅偶人時也 (*rú xiǎo ér wèi néng dá ǒu rén shí yě* — "like a small child at the time it cannot yet answer people").

### Unit 9
> 傫傫兮，若無所歸。 (*Lěi lěi xī, ruò wú suǒ guī* — "Drooping, drooping, as if with nowhere to return.")
- **Says:** Weary and listless, like one with no place to go home to.
- **Act:** describes a person (self)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the speaker; 無 (*wú* — "there is no") negates 所歸 (*suǒ guī* — "a place to return")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 傫傫 (*lěi lěi* — "worn out, drooping") vs. Heshang Gong's 乘乘 (*chéng chéng* — "drifting, riding along"): dejected vs. unattached.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi (lemma 儽儽 (*lěi lěi* — "weary")): 若無所宅 (*ruò wú suǒ zhái* — "as if having no dwelling"). Heshang Gong: 我乗乗如窮鄙无所歸就 (*wǒ chéng chéng rú qióng bǐ wú suǒ guī jiù* — "I drift along like a poor rustic with nowhere to go and settle").

### Unit 10
> 眾人皆有餘，而我獨若遺。 (*Zhòng rén jiē yǒu yú, ér wǒ dú ruò yí* — "The crowd all have surplus, yet I alone seem to have lost.")
- **Says:** Everyone has more than enough, but I alone seem to be lacking.
- **Act:** describes a person (crowd and self)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 眾人 (*zhòng rén* — "the crowd") vs. 我 (*wǒ* — "I")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** but (而 (*ér* — "yet"))
- **Open:** 遺 (*yí* — "lose, leave behind"): "seem deficient / to have lost something" vs. "seem left behind, abandoned".
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 我獨廓然無為無欲若遺失之也 (*wǒ dú kuò rán wú wéi wú yù ruò yí shī zhī yě* — "I alone am vast, not-doing, without desire, as if having lost it"). Heshang Gong: 我獨如遺棄似於不足也 (*wǒ dú rú yí qì sì yú bù zú yě* — "I alone am as if abandoned, seeming insufficient").

### Unit 11
> 我愚人之心也哉！ (*Wǒ yú rén zhī xīn yě zāi!* — "Mine is the heart of a fool!")
- **Says:** I have the mind of a fool!
- **Act:** describes a person (self; exclamation: 也哉 (*yě zāi* — exclamatory particles))
- **Who acts / of whom:** 我 (*wǒ* — "I")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** self-deprecation taken straight vs. ironic praise of simplicity.
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 不與俗人相隨守一不移如愚人之心也 (*bù yǔ sú rén xiāng suí shǒu yī bù yí rú yú rén zhī xīn yě* — "not following common people, keeping to the one without shifting, like the heart of a fool").

### Unit 12
> 沌沌兮！ (*Dùn dùn xī!* — "Muddled, muddled!")
- **Says:** So undifferentiated!
- **Act:** describes a person (self; exclamation)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the speaker
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 無所别析不可為明 (*wú suǒ bié xī bù kě wéi míng* — "dividing nothing, unable to be clear-cut").

### Unit 13
> 俗人昭昭，我獨昏昏。 (*Sú rén zhāo zhāo, wǒ dú hūn hūn* — "Common people are bright, bright; I alone am dim, dim.")
- **Says:** Ordinary people are brilliant; I alone am dark.
- **Act:** describes a person (contrast)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 俗人 (*sú rén* — "common people") vs. 我 (*wǒ* — "I")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong's lemma reads 我獨若昬 (*wǒ dú ruò hūn* — "I alone seem dim").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 昭昭 (*zhāo zhāo* — "bright, bright") is 耀其光也 (*yào qí guāng yě* — "flaunting their light").

### Unit 14
> 俗人察察，我獨悶悶。 (*Sú rén chá chá, wǒ dú mèn mèn* — "Common people are scrutinising; I alone am dull, dull.")
- **Says:** Ordinary people are sharp and discriminating; I alone am closed and dull.
- **Act:** describes a person (contrast)
- **Who acts / of whom:** common people vs. "I"
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 察察 (*chá chá* — "scrutinising") is 分别别析也 (*fēn bié bié xī yě* — "dividing and splitting apart"). Heshang Gong: 悶悶无所割截 (*mèn mèn wú suǒ gē jié* — "*mèn mèn* is cutting nothing apart").

### Unit 15
> 澹兮，其若海； (*Dàn xī, qí ruò hǎi* — "Tranquil, like the sea.")
- **Says:** Calm and vast, like the sea.
- **Act:** describes a person (self)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 其 (*qí* — "I / it") = the speaker
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none in the base. Wang Bi notes variants 漂乎 (*piāo hū* — "drifting") and 忽兮 (*hū xī* — "vague"); Heshang Gong's lemma is 忽兮若海 (*hū xī ruò hǎi* — "vague, like the sea").
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 我獨忽忽如江海之流莫知其所窮極也 (*wǒ dú hū hū rú jiāng hǎi zhī liú mò zhī qí suǒ qióng jí yě* — "I alone am vague, like the flow of river and sea; no one knows where it ends").

### Unit 16
> 飂兮，若無止。 (*Liào xī, ruò wú zhǐ* — "Blowing high, as if without stopping.")
- **Says:** Like a high wind, as if never coming to rest.
- **Act:** describes a person (self)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the speaker; 無 (*wú* — "without") negates 止 (*zhǐ* — "stop")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 無所繫縶 (*wú suǒ xì zhí* — "tethered to nothing"). Heshang Gong (lemma 漂 (*piāo* — "drift")): 若飛若揚無所止也志意在神域也 (*ruò fēi ruò yáng wú suǒ zhǐ yě, zhì yì zài shén yù yě* — "as if flying, as if soaring, stopping nowhere; the will is in the realm of spirit").

### Unit 17
> 眾人皆有以，而我獨頑似鄙。 (*Zhòng rén jiē yǒu yǐ, ér wǒ dú wán sì bǐ* — "The crowd all have their use, yet I alone am obtuse, like a rustic.")
- **Says:** Everyone has some purpose to serve, but I alone am stubborn and boorish.
- **Act:** describes a person (contrast)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the crowd vs. "I"
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** but (而 (*ér* — "yet"))
- **Open:** 似 (*sì* — "like, seeming") as "like a rustic" vs. "and" (Wang Bi's paraphrase 頑且鄙 (*wán qiě bǐ* — "obtuse and boorish")).
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 以用也皆欲有所施用也 (*yǐ yòng yě, jiē yù yǒu suǒ shī yòng yě* — "*yǐ* means use: all want something to apply themselves to"). Heshang Gong: 以有為也 (*yǐ yǒu wéi yě* — "*yǐ* means taking action"), and 我獨無為 (*wǒ dú wú wéi* — "I alone do not act").

### Unit 18
> 我獨異於人，而貴食母。 (*Wǒ dú yì yú rén, ér guì shí mǔ* — "I alone differ from others, and value the feeding mother.")
- **Says:** I alone am different from others, and I prize being fed by the mother.
- **Act:** describes a person (self)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 我 (*wǒ* — "I") values; 母 (*mǔ* — "mother")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and (而 (*ér* — "and"))
- **Open:** 食母 (*shí mǔ* — "feed-mother"): "the mother who feeds", "being fed from the mother", or *sì mǔ* "the nursing mother, wet nurse".
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 食母生之本也 (*shí mǔ shēng zhī běn yě* — "the feeding mother is the root of life"). Heshang Gong: 食用也母道也我獨貴用道也 (*shí yòng yě, mǔ dào yě, wǒ dú guì yòng dào yě* — "*shí* means use; *mǔ* means the Tao: I alone value using the Tao").

# Chapter 21

**The chapter as a whole:** Great 德 (*dé* — "virtue, potency") follows only the Tao. The Tao as a thing is elusive and indistinct, yet within it are images, things, an essence that is utterly genuine, and something reliable. Its name has never left from antiquity until now, and by it one surveys the beginnings of all; the speaker knows their state "by this".

### Unit 1
> 孔德之容，惟道是從。 (*Kǒng dé zhī róng, wéi dào shì cóng* — "The bearing of great virtue: only the Tao it follows.")
- **Says:** The manner of great 德 (*dé* — "virtue, potency") follows the Tao and nothing else.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 孔德 (*kǒng dé* — "great/empty virtue") or the one who has it; the object 道 (*dào* — "the Tao") is fronted by 惟 … 是 (*wéi … shì* — "only … this")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 孔 (*kǒng* — "great; hollow"): "great virtue" vs. "empty virtue"; 容 (*róng* — "appearance; capacity, containing"): its bearing vs. its capacity to contain.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 孔空也惟以空為徳然後乃能動作從道 (*kǒng kōng yě, wéi yǐ kōng wéi dé rán hòu nǎi néng dòng zuò cóng dào* — "*kǒng* means empty: only by taking emptiness as virtue can one act and follow the Tao"). Heshang Gong: 孔大也有大德之人無所不容 (*kǒng dà yě, yǒu dà dé zhī rén wú suǒ bù róng* — "*kǒng* means great: the person of great virtue contains everything").

### Unit 2
> 道之為物，惟恍惟惚。 (*Dào zhī wéi wù, wéi huǎng wéi hū* — "The Tao as a thing: only elusive, only indistinct.")
- **Says:** As a thing, the Tao is nothing but vague and elusive.
- **Act:** names/defines (describes)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 道 (*dào* — "the Tao")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (turns to the Tao itself)
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 恍惚無形不繋之歎 (*huǎng hū wú xíng bù xì zhī tàn* — "*huǎng hū* is an exclamation at the formless and unbound").

### Unit 3
> 惚兮恍兮，其中有象； (*Hū xī huǎng xī, qí zhōng yǒu xiàng* — "Indistinct, elusive; within it there are images.")
- **Says:** Vague and elusive as it is, inside it there are images.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 其 (*qí* — "its") = the Tao
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (yet: despite the vagueness)
- **Open:** 象 (*xiàng* — "image, figure, likeness"): form-patterns vs. models for things.
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 道唯忽怳无形之中獨為萬物法像 (*dào wéi hū huǎng wú xíng zhī zhōng dú wéi wàn wù fǎ xiàng* — "the Tao is only vague and elusive; within formlessness it alone is the model-image for the myriad things").

### Unit 4
> 恍兮惚兮，其中有物。 (*Huǎng xī hū xī, qí zhōng yǒu wù* — "Elusive, indistinct; within it there are things.")
- **Says:** Elusive and vague, inside it there are things.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the Tao
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** none

### Unit 5
> 窈兮冥兮，其中有精； (*Yǎo xī míng xī, qí zhōng yǒu jīng* — "Deep, dark; within it there is essence.")
- **Says:** Profound and obscure, inside it there is an essence.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the Tao
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** 精 (*jīng* — "refined essence; seed, vital essence"): the finest core vs. a generative vital essence.
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong reads it cosmologically: 其中有精實神明相薄隂陽交㑹也 (*qí zhōng yǒu jīng shí shén míng xiāng bó yīn yáng jiāo huì yě* — "within it is essence-substance: spirit-brightness pressing together, yin and yang meeting"). Wang Bi ties it to things' truth: 萬物由之其可得見以定其真 (*wàn wù yóu zhī qí kě dé jiàn yǐ dìng qí zhēn* — "the myriad things come from it, and it can be seen in fixing their truth").

### Unit 6
> 其精甚真，其中有信。 (*Qí jīng shèn zhēn, qí zhōng yǒu xìn* — "Its essence is very genuine; within it there is trust.")
- **Says:** That essence is utterly real, and inside it there is something reliable.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the Tao's essence
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 信 (*xìn* — "trust; proof, token"): reliability/faithfulness vs. evidence, a verifying sign.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 信信驗也 (*xìn xìn yàn yě* — "*xìn* means verification"). Heshang Gong: 道匿功藏名其信在中也 (*dào nì gōng cáng míng qí xìn zài zhōng yě* — "the Tao hides its achievement and conceals its name; its reliability is within").

### Unit 7
> 自古及今，其名不去，以閱眾甫。 (*Zì gǔ jí jīn, qí míng bù qù, yǐ yuè zhòng fǔ* — "From antiquity to now, its name has not gone, by which to review the many beginnings.")
- **Says:** From ancient times until now its name has never departed, and by it one surveys the origins of all things.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 其名 (*qí míng* — "its name") = the Tao's; the surveyor of 以閱 (*yǐ yuè* — "thereby review") is unstated (one / the speaker); 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 去 (*qù* — "depart")
- **Force:** past continuing into present
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 甫 (*fǔ* — "beginning; father, elder"): "the many beginnings" vs. "the many fathers/progenitors". 閱 (*yuè* — "review, pass in review") vs. Heshang Gong's 禀 (*bǐng* — "endow").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 衆甫物之始也 (*zhòng fǔ wù zhī shǐ yě* — "the many *fǔ* are the beginnings of things"); its name is namelessness: 無名則是其名也 (*wú míng zé shì qí míng yě* — "having no name is its name"). Heshang Gong: 閱禀也甫始也言道禀與萬物始生從道受氣 (*yuè bǐng yě, fǔ shǐ yě, yán dào bǐng yǔ wàn wù shǐ shēng cóng dào shòu qì* — "*yuè* means endow; *fǔ* means beginning: the Tao endows the myriad things; at their first birth they receive breath from the Tao").

### Unit 8
> 吾何以知眾甫之狀哉？ (*Wú hé yǐ zhī zhòng fǔ zhī zhuàng zāi?* — "How do I know the state of the many beginnings?")
- **Says:** How do I know what the origins of all things are like?
- **Act:** asks (real question, answered at once)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 吾 (*wú* — "I"), the speaker
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none. Wang Bi notes other texts read 然 (*rán* — "so-ness") for 狀 (*zhuàng* — "shape, state").

### Unit 9
> 以此。 (*Yǐ cǐ* — "By this.")
- **Says:** By means of this.
- **Act:** asserts (answer)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the speaker
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (answers Unit 8)
- **Open:** 此 (*cǐ* — "this"): what was just said about the Tao, vs. "the present, the things now before us".
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 此上之所云也 (*cǐ shàng zhī suǒ yún yě* — "'this' is what was said above"). Heshang Gong: 此今也以今萬物皆得道精氣而生 (*cǐ jīn yě, yǐ jīn wàn wù jiē dé dào jīng qì ér shēng* — "'this' means now: by the fact that the myriad things now all live by receiving the Tao's essential breath").

# Chapter 22

**The chapter as a whole:** Paradoxes of reversal: bent then whole, crooked then straight, hollow then full, worn then new, little then gain, much then confusion. So the sage embraces the one and is the model for the world: not self-displaying, self-justifying, self-boasting or self-aggrandising, the sage is seen, distinguished, credited and lasting; not contending, no one can contend with them. The old saying "bent then whole" is no empty talk: wholeness truly returns.

### Unit 1
> 曲則全，枉則直，窪則盈，敝則新，少則得，多則惑。 (*Qū zé quán, wǎng zé zhí, wā zé yíng, bì zé xīn, shǎo zé dé, duō zé huò* — "Bent, then whole; crooked, then straight; hollow, then full; worn, then new; little, then gaining; much, then confused.")
- **Says:** What bends stays whole, what is crooked becomes straight, what is hollow fills, what is worn is renewed, having little one gains, having much one is confused.
- **Act:** if/when … then (則 (*zé* — "then", six times))
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; things or persons generally
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** each pair as natural law (what bends survives) vs. as advice (bend and you will be kept whole). 少則得 (*shǎo zé dé* — "little, then get"): "having little, one gains" vs. "seeking little, one obtains".
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi pairs the first four with the "not self-" lines below: 不自見其明則全也 (*bù zì xiàn qí míng zé quán yě* — "not displaying one's own brightness, then one is whole"). Heshang Gong: 曲已從衆不自專則全其身也 (*qū jǐ cóng zhòng bù zì zhuān zé quán qí shēn yě* — "bending oneself to follow the many, not acting on one's own, one keeps one's body whole").

### Unit 2
> 是以聖人抱一為天下式。 (*Shì yǐ shèng rén bào yī wéi tiān xià shì* — "Therefore the sage embraces the one and is the pattern of the world.")
- **Says:** For this reason the sage holds to the one and becomes the model for all under heaven.
- **Act:** asserts (describes a person)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore (是以 (*shì yǐ* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** "embraces the one, [and so] is the world's model" vs. "embraces the one as the world's model". 一 (*yī* — "one"): the extreme of fewness (Wang Bi) or the unity kept in practice (Heshang Gong).
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 一少之極也式猶則之也 (*yī shǎo zhī jí yě, shì yóu zé zhī yě* — "the one is the extreme of fewness; *shì* is like 'taking as rule'"). Heshang Gong: 聖人守一乃知萬事故能為天下法式也 (*shèng rén shǒu yī nǎi zhī wàn shì gù néng wéi tiān xià fǎ shì yě* — "the sage keeps the one and so knows the myriad affairs, therefore can be the model for the world").

### Unit 3
> 不自見，故明； (*Bù zì xiàn, gù míng* — "Not displaying self, therefore bright.")
- **Says:** Not showing themselves off, they are clearly seen (or see clearly).
- **Act:** if/when … then (causal 故 (*gù* — "therefore"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates the self-display
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and (elaborates Unit 2)
- **Open:** 見 (*xiàn* — "show") vs. *jiàn* ("see"); 明 (*míng* — "bright, clear-sighted"): "does not show self, so is illustrious" vs. "does not see only with own eyes, so sees clearly".
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong (though glossing the sound as *xiàn*) reads seeing: 聖人不以其目視千里之外也乃因天下之目以視故能明逹也 (*shèng rén bù yǐ qí mù shì qiān lǐ zhī wài yě, nǎi yīn tiān xià zhī mù yǐ shì, gù néng míng dá yě* — "the sage does not use his own eyes to see beyond a thousand li, but sees through the eyes of the world, therefore sees clearly and far").

### Unit 4
> 不自是，故彰； (*Bù zì shì, gù zhāng* — "Not self-righting, therefore manifest.")
- **Says:** Not insisting they are right, they stand out.
- **Act:** if/when … then (causal)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates self-affirmation
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 聖人不自以為是而非人故能彰顯於世 (*shèng rén bù zì yǐ wéi shì ér fēi rén, gù néng zhāng xiǎn yú shì* — "the sage does not think himself right and others wrong, therefore is distinguished in the world").

### Unit 5
> 不自伐，故有功； (*Bù zì fá, gù yǒu gōng* — "Not self-boasting, therefore has merit.")
- **Says:** Not boasting of themselves, they have achievement.
- **Act:** if/when … then (causal)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates the boasting
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 伐取也 (*fá qǔ yě* — "*fá* means taking"): 不自取其美 (*bù zì qǔ qí měi* — "does not take the credit for himself").

### Unit 6
> 不自矜，故長。 (*Bù zì jīn, gù cháng* — "Not self-aggrandising, therefore long-lasting.")
- **Says:** Not exalting themselves, they endure (or lead).
- **Act:** if/when … then (causal)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates self-exaltation
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** 長 (*cháng* — "long, enduring") vs. *zhǎng* ("be chief, grow").
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong takes *cháng*: 矜大也聖人不自貴大故能久不危 (*jīn dà yě, shèng rén bù zì guì dà, gù néng jiǔ bù wēi* — "*jīn* means making great: the sage does not prize or enlarge himself, therefore lasts long without danger").

### Unit 7
> 夫唯不爭，故天下莫能與之爭。 (*Fú wéi bù zhēng, gù tiān xià mò néng yǔ zhī zhēng* — "Just because not contending, therefore no one under heaven can contend with them.")
- **Says:** Precisely because the sage does not contend, no one in the world is able to contend with them.
- **Act:** if/when … then (causal 夫唯 … 故 (*fú wéi … gù* — "just because … therefore"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates contending; 莫 (*mò* — "no one") is the subject of the second clause
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** because / therefore
- **Open:** none

### Unit 8
> 古之所謂「曲則全」者，豈虛言哉！ (*Gǔ zhī suǒ wèi "qū zé quán" zhě, qǐ xū yán zāi!* — "What the ancients called 'bent, then whole': how could it be empty words!")
- **Says:** The old saying "bend and be whole" is surely not empty talk.
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical: implies it is true)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 古 (*gǔ* — "the ancients") said it
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (returns to the opening)
- **Open:** none

### Unit 9
> 誠全而歸之。 (*Chéng quán ér guī zhī* — "Truly whole, and return to it.")
- **Says:** Truly, wholeness is kept, and [one] returns to it (or it returns to one).
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: the one who bends; 之 (*zhī* — "it, them") unspecified
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (confirms Unit 8)
- **Open:** "truly whole, one returns to it [the origin/wholeness]" vs. "wholeness truly comes back to such a one" vs. "kept whole, one returns [the body] to them".
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 誠實也能行曲從者實其飢體歸之於父母無有傷害也 (*chéng shí yě, néng xíng qū cóng zhě shí qí jī tǐ guī zhī yú fù mǔ wú yǒu shāng hài yě* — "*chéng* means truly: one who can practise bending and yielding truly [keeps whole] their body and returns it to their father and mother without injury"). Wang Bi has no comment.

# Chapter 23

**The chapter as a whole:** To speak little is the way of what is so of itself: even heaven and earth cannot keep a whirlwind or a downpour going all day, much less can people sustain forceful effort. Each takes on what it follows: one who works by the Tao becomes one with the Tao, with 德 (*dé* — "virtue, gain") with that 德 (*dé* — "virtue"), with loss with loss, and each is gladly received by what it joined. Where trust is lacking, there is distrust.

### Unit 1
> 希言自然。 (*Xī yán zì rán* — "Sparse speech: so of itself.")
- **Says:** Speaking little is what is natural (so of itself).
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** impersonal
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** "sparse speech is natural / accords with the self-so" vs. "what is so of itself speaks sparsely". 希 (*xī* — "rare, sparse; faint, inaudible"): sparing speech vs. inaudible speech.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi takes 希 (*xī* — "faint, rare") as inaudible: 聽之不聞名曰希 (*tīng zhī bù wén míng yuē xī* — "listened to and not heard, it is named *xī*"), so 無味不足聽之言乃是自然之至言也 (*wú wèi bù zú tīng zhī yán nǎi shì zì rán zhī zhì yán yě* — "flavourless speech not worth hearing is the utmost speech of the self-so"). Heshang Gong: 希言者是愛言也 (*xī yán zhě shì ài yán yě* — "*xī yán* means being sparing with words").

### Unit 2
> 故飄風不終朝，驟雨不終日。 (*Gù piāo fēng bù zhōng zhāo, zhòu yǔ bù zhōng rì* — "So a whirlwind does not last the morning; a downpour does not last the day.")
- **Says:** Thus a gale does not blow a whole morning and a cloudburst does not fall a whole day.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 飄風 (*piāo fēng* — "whirlwind") and 驟雨 (*zhòu yǔ* — "sudden rain"); 不 (*bù* — "not") negates lasting the span
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** therefore (故 (*gù* — "so"; Wang Bi notes Heshang Gong and others lack it))
- **Open:** none

### Unit 3
> 孰為此者？ (*Shú wèi cǐ zhě?* — "Who makes these?")
- **Says:** Who produces these [storms]?
- **Act:** asks (real question, answered at once)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 孰 (*shú* — "who")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none

### Unit 4
> 天地。 (*Tiān dì* — "Heaven and earth.")
- **Says:** Heaven and earth do.
- **Act:** asserts (answer)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天地 (*tiān dì* — "heaven and earth")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (answers Unit 3)
- **Open:** none

### Unit 5
> 天地尚不能久，而況於人乎？ (*Tiān dì shàng bù néng jiǔ, ér kuàng yú rén hū?* — "If even heaven and earth cannot make it last, how much less people?")
- **Says:** If even heaven and earth cannot keep such things going long, how much less can human beings?
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical: implies people certainly cannot)
- **Who acts / of whom:** heaven and earth, then 人 (*rén* — "people"); 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 能 (*néng* — "be able")
- **Force:** can/possible (negated)
- **Link:** and (而況 (*ér kuàng* — "how much more/less"))
- **Open:** 久 (*jiǔ* — "long"): "cannot make [storms] last long" vs. "cannot themselves last long".
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 天地至神合為飄風暴雨尚不能使終朝至暮何况人欲為暴卒乎 (*tiān dì zhì shén hé wéi piāo fēng bào yǔ shàng bù néng shǐ zhōng zhāo zhì mù, hé kuàng rén yù wéi bào cù hū* — "heaven and earth, most numinous, joining to make whirlwind and cloudburst, still cannot make them last from morning to dusk: how much less a person who wants to act violently and in haste?"). Wang Bi: 言暴疾美興不長也 (*yán bào jí měi xīng bù cháng yě* — "it says the violent and swift, however fine their rise, do not last").

### Unit 6
> 故從事於道者，同於道； (*Gù cóng shì yú dào zhě, tóng yú dào* — "So one who pursues affairs in the Tao is one with the Tao.")
- **Says:** Therefore whoever conducts affairs by the Tao becomes identical with the Tao.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 從事於道者 (*cóng shì yú dào zhě* — "one who pursues affairs in the Tao"), generic
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** therefore (故 (*gù* — "so"))
- **Open:** 同於 (*tóng yú* — "same as, one with"): merges with vs. is matched by. Both lemmas read 從事於道者道者同於道 (*cóng shì yú dào zhě, dào zhě tóng yú dào* — "of those who pursue the Tao, the Tao-people are one with the Tao"), with an extra 道者 (*dào zhě* — "Tao-person"); the base lacks it.
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 從為也人為事當如道安静 (*cóng wéi yě, rén wéi shì dāng rú dào ān jìng* — "*cóng* means to do: in doing things, a person should be like the Tao, calm and still"). Wang Bi: 以無為為君不言為教 (*yǐ wú wéi wéi jūn bù yán wéi jiào* — "taking not-doing as lord and not-speaking as teaching").

### Unit 7
> 德者，同於德； (*Dé zhě, tóng yú dé* — "One of virtue is one with virtue.")
- **Says:** Whoever follows 德 (*dé* — "virtue, potency; gain") becomes one with that 德 (*dé* — "virtue").
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 德者 (*dé zhě* — "the virtue-person / the gainer")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** 德 (*dé* — "virtue") vs. its homophone 得 (*dé* — "gain"): "one with virtue" vs. "one with gaining".
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi reads 得 (*dé* — "gain"): 得少也少則得故曰得也 (*dé shǎo yě, shǎo zé dé, gù yuē dé yě* — "*dé* is having little: little, then gain, so it is called gain"). Heshang Gong: 徳謂好德人也 (*dé wèi hào dé rén yě* — "*dé* means a person who loves virtue").

### Unit 8
> 失者，同於失。 (*Shī zhě, tóng yú shī* — "One of loss is one with loss.")
- **Says:** Whoever goes the way of loss becomes one with loss.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 失者 (*shī zhě* — "the one who loses / errs")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** 失 (*shī* — "lose, fail, err"): losing (the Tao) vs. erring.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 失累多也累多則失 (*shī lěi duō yě, lěi duō zé shī* — "*shī* is piling up much: piling up much, then loss"). Heshang Gong: 失謂任己失人也 (*shī wèi rèn jǐ shī rén yě* — "*shī* means relying on oneself and losing people").

### Unit 9
> 同於道者，道亦樂得之； (*Tóng yú dào zhě, dào yì lè dé zhī* — "One who is one with the Tao, the Tao too is glad to get.")
- **Says:** Whoever is one with the Tao, the Tao likewise gladly gains them.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 道 (*dào* — "the Tao") gladly gets 之 (*zhī* — "them") = the one who is one with it
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 樂得之 (*lè dé zhī* — "glad to get them"): the Tao welcomes them vs. they gladly attain the Tao (less natural in the grammar). Wang Bi notes the Yongle Dadian lacks 樂 (*lè* — "glad") here and in Units 10–11.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 言隨行其所故同而應之 (*yán suí xíng qí suǒ gù tóng ér yìng zhī* — "it says: according to where one goes, so one is matched and responded to").

### Unit 10
> 同於德者，德亦樂得之； (*Tóng yú dé zhě, dé yì lè dé zhī* — "One who is one with virtue, virtue too is glad to get.")
- **Says:** Whoever is one with 德 (*dé* — "virtue"), that 德 (*dé* — "virtue") likewise gladly gains them.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 德 (*dé* — "virtue / gain") gets the one who joined it
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** same 德 / 得 (*dé* — "virtue / gain") doubleness as Unit 7

### Unit 11
> 同於失者，失亦樂得之。 (*Tóng yú shī zhě, shī yì lè dé zhī* — "One who is one with loss, loss too is glad to get.")
- **Says:** Whoever is one with loss, loss likewise gladly gains them.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 失 (*shī* — "loss") gets the one who joined it
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** Heshang Gong's lemma reads 失亦樂失之 (*shī yì lè shī zhī* — "loss too is glad to lose them").

### Unit 12
> 信不足焉，有不信焉。 (*Xìn bù zú yān, yǒu bù xìn yān* — "Where trust is not sufficient, there is no trust.")
- **Says:** When trust(worthiness) falls short, distrust appears.
- **Act:** if/when … then (implicit)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; most likely the ruler's trustworthiness met by distrust from below; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 足 (*zú* — "suffice") and 信 (*xìn* — "trust")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** whose trust, as in Chapter 17 Unit 5.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 忠信不足於下焉有不信焉 (*zhōng xìn bù zú yú xià yān yǒu bù xìn yān* — "when loyalty and trust are insufficient toward those below, there is distrust"). Heshang Gong: 君信不足於下下則應君以不足也 (*jūn xìn bù zú yú xià, xià zé yìng jūn yǐ bù zú yě* — "when the ruler's trust toward those below is insufficient, those below answer the ruler with insufficiency").

# Chapter 24

**The chapter as a whole:** Straining defeats itself: one on tiptoe does not stand, one who strides does not walk; the self-displaying, self-justifying, self-boasting and self-aggrandising lose the very clarity, distinction, merit and endurance they reach for. In the light of the Tao these are leftover food and growths on the body, which things loathe, so one who has the Tao does not dwell in them.

### Unit 1
> 企者不立； (*Qǐ zhě bù lì* — "One on tiptoe does not stand.")
- **Says:** Whoever stands on tiptoe cannot stand (firmly).
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 企者 (*qǐ zhě* — "one who tiptoes"), generic; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates standing
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong and others read 跂 (*qǐ* — "tiptoe").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 物尚進則失安 (*wù shàng jìn zé shī ān* — "when things prize advancing they lose stability"). Heshang Gong: 謂貪權慕名進取功榮也則不可久立身行道也 (*wèi tān quán mù míng jìn qǔ gōng róng yě, zé bù kě jiǔ lì shēn xíng dào yě* — "it means coveting power, craving fame, pushing for achievement and glory: then one cannot long stand and practise the Tao").

### Unit 2
> 跨者不行； (*Kuà zhě bù xíng* — "One who strides does not walk.")
- **Says:** Whoever takes straddling strides cannot walk (on).
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 跨者 (*kuà zhě* — "one who straddles/strides"); 不 (*bù* — "not") negates walking
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** 跨 (*kuà* — "stride wide; straddle, bestride"): overstriding vs. bestriding others.
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 自以為貴而跨於人衆共蔽之使不得行 (*zì yǐ wéi guì ér kuà yú rén, zhòng gòng bì zhī shǐ bù dé xíng* — "thinking oneself noble and bestriding others, the many together block them so they cannot go").

### Unit 3
> 自見者不明； (*Zì xiàn zhě bù míng* — "One who displays self is not bright.")
- **Says:** Whoever shows themselves off is not clearly seen (or does not see clearly).
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 自見者 (*zì xiàn zhě* — "self-displayer"); 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 明 (*míng* — "bright, clear")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 見 (*xiàn* — "show") vs. *jiàn* ("see"), as in Chapter 22 Unit 3.
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong reads both: 人自見其形容以為好自見所行以為應道殊不自知其形醜操行之鄙 (*rén zì jiàn qí xíng róng yǐ wéi hǎo, zì jiàn suǒ xíng yǐ wéi yìng dào, shū bù zì zhī qí xíng chǒu cāo xíng zhī bǐ* — "people look at their own appearance and think it fine, look at their own conduct and think it accords with the Tao, not knowing at all that their form is ugly and their conduct base").

### Unit 4
> 自是者不彰； (*Zì shì zhě bù zhāng* — "One who affirms self is not manifest.")
- **Says:** Whoever insists on being right does not stand out.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 自是者 (*zì shì zhě* — "self-affirmer"); 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 彰 (*zhāng* — "manifest, distinguished")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** none

### Unit 5
> 自伐者無功； (*Zì fá zhě wú gōng* — "One who boasts of self has no merit.")
- **Says:** Whoever boasts has no achievement.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 自伐者 (*zì fá zhě* — "self-boaster"); 無 (*wú* — "there is no") negates the existence of 功 (*gōng* — "merit, achievement")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** none

### Unit 6
> 自矜者不長。 (*Zì jīn zhě bù cháng* — "One who exalts self does not last.")
- **Says:** Whoever is self-important does not endure (or does not lead).
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 自矜者 (*zì jīn zhě* — "self-exalter"); 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 長 (*cháng* — "last")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** 長 (*cháng* — "long-lasting") vs. *zhǎng* ("be chief").
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 好自矜大者不可以長久 (*hào zì jīn dà zhě bù kě yǐ cháng jiǔ* — "one who likes to exalt and enlarge themselves cannot last long").

### Unit 7
> 其在道也，曰餘食贅行，物或惡之，故有道者不處。 (*Qí zài dào yě, yuē yú shí zhuì xíng, wù huò wù zhī, gù yǒu dào zhě bù chǔ* — "As regards the Tao, they are called leftover food and superfluous growths; things perhaps loathe them; therefore one who has the Tao does not dwell [there].")
- **Says:** Measured by the Tao, such behaviours are called leftover food and excess growths; things tend to loathe them, so one who has the Tao does not abide in them.
- **Act:** names/defines (曰 (*yuē* — "are called")), then asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 其 (*qí* — "they") = the behaviours of Units 1–6; 物 (*wù* — "things, beings") loathe; 有道者 (*yǒu dào zhě* — "one who has the Tao") does not abide; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 處 (*chǔ* — "dwell, abide")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** therefore (故 (*gù* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** 贅行 (*zhuì xíng* — "superfluous growth/conduct"): 行 (*xíng* — "go, conduct") as 形 (*xíng* — "form", a wart or tumour) vs. *xìng* ("conduct", greedy or excessive behaviour). 物或 (*wù huò* — "things perhaps / some things"): "things may loathe them" vs. "everyone loathes them". Heshang Gong and others read 於 (*yú* — "in") for 在 (*zài* — "in").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 若卻至之行盛饌之餘也本雖美更可薉也本雖有功而自伐之故更為肬贅者也 (*ruò Què Zhì zhī xíng, shèng zhuàn zhī yú yě; běn suī měi, gèng kě huì yě; běn suī yǒu gōng ér zì fá zhī, gù gèng wéi yóu zhuì zhě yě* — "like the conduct of Que Zhi, the leftovers of a rich feast: though fine at first, it turns foul; though there was merit at first, boasting of it turns it into a wart"). Heshang Gong: 贅貪也 (*zhuì tān yě* — "*zhuì* means greed"), 斂餘禄食為貪行 (*liǎn yú lù shí wéi tān xìng* — "hoarding surplus salary and food as greedy conduct"), and 言有道之人不居其國也 (*yán yǒu dào zhī rén bù jū qí guó yě* — "it says a person with the Tao does not dwell in such a state").

# Chapter 25

**The chapter as a whole:** There is something formed undivided, born before heaven and earth, silent and empty, standing alone and unchanging, going round without peril, fit to be the mother of the world. Not knowing its name, the speaker styles it "Tao" and, forced, names it "great"; great means going, going means far, far means return. The Tao, heaven, earth and the king are the four greats in the realm; people model on earth, earth on heaven, heaven on the Tao, and the Tao on what is so of itself.

### Unit 1
> 有物混成，先天地生。 (*Yǒu wù hùn chéng, xiān tiān dì shēng* — "There is a thing formed in confusion, born before heaven and earth.")
- **Says:** There is something completed in an undifferentiated mass, which arose before heaven and earth.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 物 (*wù* — "a thing"), as yet unnamed
- **Force:** past (its origin) and always (its existence)
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 混成 (*hùn chéng* — "blended-complete"): "formed out of undifferentiated mixture" vs. "undifferentiated, it completes [things]".
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 混然不可得而知而萬物由之以成故曰混成也 (*hùn rán bù kě dé ér zhī ér wàn wù yóu zhī yǐ chéng, gù yuē hùn chéng yě* — "merged, it cannot be known, yet the myriad things are completed through it, so it is called *hùn chéng*"). Heshang Gong: 謂道無形混沌而成萬物乃在天地之前 (*wèi dào wú xíng hùn dùn ér chéng wàn wù nǎi zài tiān dì zhī qián* — "it means the Tao, formless and chaotic, completes the myriad things, and is before heaven and earth").

### Unit 2
> 寂兮寥兮，獨立而不改，周行而不殆，可以為天下母。 (*Jì xī liáo xī, dú lì ér bù gǎi, zhōu xíng ér bù dài, kě yǐ wéi tiān xià mǔ* — "Silent, empty; standing alone and not changing; going round and not imperilled; it can be the mother of the world.")
- **Says:** Soundless and void, it stands alone without changing, moves everywhere without danger, and can be regarded as the mother of all under heaven.
- **Act:** describes (the thing), then asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the thing of Unit 1; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 改 (*gǎi* — "change") and 殆 (*dài* — "danger")
- **Force:** always/universal; can/possible in 可以 (*kě yǐ* — "can")
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 殆 (*dài* — "peril; weary, flag"): "without danger" vs. "without tiring". 周行 (*zhōu xíng* — "go round, go everywhere"): circulating vs. pervading. 可以為 (*kě yǐ wéi* — "can be taken as / can act as"). Wang Bi's lemma lacks 而 (*ér* — "and") after 獨立 (*dú lì* — "stand alone").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 無物之匹故曰獨立也返化終始不失其常故曰不改也 (*wú wù zhī pǐ gù yuē dú lì yě, fǎn huà zhōng shǐ bù shī qí cháng gù yuē bù gǎi yě* — "nothing matches it, so 'stands alone'; reverting and transforming, ending and beginning, it never loses its constancy, so 'does not change'"). Heshang Gong takes 殆 (*dài* — "peril") as danger: 无不貫穿不危殆 (*wú bù guàn chuān bù wēi dài* — "there is nothing it does not pass through, and it is not endangered"), and the mother as nurturer: 道育養萬物精氣如母之養子 (*dào yù yǎng wàn wù jīng qì rú mǔ zhī yǎng zǐ* — "the Tao rears the myriad things with essential breath as a mother rears a child").

### Unit 3
> 吾不知其名，強字之曰道，強為之名曰大。 (*Wú bù zhī qí míng, qiǎng zì zhī yuē dào, qiǎng wèi zhī míng yuē dà* — "I do not know its name; forcing it, I style it 'Tao'; forcing it, I make for it a name and say 'great'.")
- **Says:** I don't know what it is called; for want of better I give it the style-name "Tao", and, straining, call it "great".
- **Act:** names/defines (字之曰 (*zì zhī yuē* — "style it as"); 名曰 (*míng yuē* — "name it as"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** 吾 (*wú* — "I"); 不 (*bù* — "not") negates knowing its name
- **Force:** past/present (the speaker's act)
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 字 (*zì* — "style-name, courtesy name") vs. 名 (*míng* — "given name"): "Tao" is only a style-name, while 大 (*dà* — "great") is the forced personal name. Wang Bi's lemma lacks 強 (*qiǎng* — "force") before 字 (*zì* — "style-name").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 名以定形混成無形不可得而定故曰不知其名也 (*míng yǐ dìng xíng, hùn chéng wú xíng bù kě dé ér dìng, gù yuē bù zhī qí míng yě* — "a name fixes a form; what is formed undivided has no form and cannot be fixed, so 'I do not know its name'"). Heshang Gong: 見萬物皆從道所生故字之曰道也 (*jiàn wàn wù jiē cóng dào suǒ shēng gù zì zhī yuē dào yě* — "seeing that the myriad things are all born from the way, he styles it 'Tao'").

### Unit 4
> 大曰逝，逝曰遠，遠曰反。 (*Dà yuē shì, shì yuē yuǎn, yuǎn yuē fǎn* — "Great is called going; going is called far; far is called return.")
- **Says:** Being great means passing onward; passing onward means going far; going far means coming back.
- **Act:** names/defines (曰 (*yuē* — "is called, means"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** the thing named "great"
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (unfolds the name)
- **Open:** 反 (*fǎn* — "return; reverse, turn against"): coming back vs. reversal. 曰 (*yuē* — "is called") as definition vs. sequence ("great, then going, then far, then back").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 逝行也 (*shì xíng yě* — "*shì* means going"); 不隨於所適其體獨立故曰反也 (*bù suí yú suǒ shì qí tǐ dú lì gù yuē fǎn yě* — "it does not go along with where it goes; its body stands alone, so 'return'"). Heshang Gong: 言其逺不越絶乃復在人身也 (*yán qí yuǎn bù yuè jué nǎi fù zài rén shēn yě* — "it says its farness is not cut off: it is back again in the person's own body").

### Unit 5
> 故道大，天大，地大，王亦大。 (*Gù dào dà, tiān dà, dì dà, wáng yì dà* — "So the Tao is great, heaven is great, earth is great, the king is also great.")
- **Says:** Therefore the Tao is great, heaven is great, earth is great, and the king too is great.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 道 (*dào* — "the Tao"), 天 (*tiān* — "heaven"), 地 (*dì* — "earth"), 王 (*wáng* — "king")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** therefore (故 (*gù* — "so"))
- **Open:** none in the base
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 天地之性人為貴而王是人之主也 (*tiān dì zhī xìng rén wéi guì ér wáng shì rén zhī zhǔ yě* — "of what heaven and earth produce, humans are most precious, and the king is the lord of humans"). Heshang Gong: 王大者無所不制也 (*wáng dà zhě wú suǒ bù zhì yě* — "the king is great in that there is nothing he does not govern").

### Unit 6
> 域中有四大，而王居其一焉。 (*Yù zhōng yǒu sì dà, ér wáng jū qí yī yān* — "Within the realm there are four greats, and the king occupies one of them.")
- **Says:** In the domain there are four great ones, and the king holds one of those places.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 王 (*wáng* — "king") occupies
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (而 (*ér* — "and"))
- **Open:** 域 (*yù* — "region, realm, bounded domain"): the cosmos vs. the unnameable.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 無稱不可得而名曰域也 (*wú chēng bù kě dé ér míng yuē yù yě* — "what has no appellation and cannot be named is called *yù*"). Heshang Gong: 八極之内有四大 (*bā jí zhī nèi yǒu sì dà* — "within the eight extremities there are four greats").

### Unit 7
> 人法地，地法天，天法道，道法自然。 (*Rén fǎ dì, dì fǎ tiān, tiān fǎ dào, dào fǎ zì rán* — "People model on earth, earth models on heaven, heaven models on the Tao, the Tao models on the so-of-itself.")
- **Says:** Humans take earth as their pattern, earth takes heaven, heaven takes the Tao, and the Tao takes what is so of itself.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 人 (*rén* — "people"), earth, heaven, the Tao, each modelling on the next; 法 (*fǎ* — "take as model, follow as law")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 道法自然 (*dào fǎ zì rán* — "the Tao models on the self-so"): the Tao follows its own so-ness (models on nothing beyond itself) vs. the Tao conforms to how each thing is of itself. 人 (*rén* — "people") opens the chain though the king was named just before.
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 道性自然無所法也 (*dào xìng zì rán wú suǒ fǎ yě* — "the Tao's nature is so of itself; there is nothing it models on"). Wang Bi: 道不違自然乃得其性法自然者在方而法方在圓而法圓於自然無所違也 (*dào bù wéi zì rán nǎi dé qí xìng; fǎ zì rán zhě, zài fāng ér fǎ fāng, zài yuán ér fǎ yuán, yú zì rán wú suǒ wéi yě* — "the Tao does not go against the self-so and so attains its nature; modelling on the self-so means: in the square it models the square, in the round it models the round, going against the self-so in nothing"), and 自然者無稱之言窮極之辭也 (*zì rán zhě wú chēng zhī yán qióng jí zhī cí yě* — "'self-so' is a word without appellation, a term for the ultimate").

# Chapter 26

**The chapter as a whole:** The heavy is the root of the light and the still is lord of the restless. So the noble person travels all day without leaving the baggage carts and, even with splendid sights about, stays at ease and aloof. How then can a lord of ten thousand chariots treat his own person lightly before the world? Lightness loses the root; restlessness loses the lord.

### Unit 1
> 重為輕根，靜為躁君。 (*Zhòng wéi qīng gēn, jìng wéi zào jūn* — "Heavy is the root of light; still is the lord of restless.")
- **Says:** Heaviness is the root of lightness, and stillness is the ruler of agitation.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 重 (*zhòng* — "heavy"), 靜 (*jìng* — "still"); 為 (*wéi* — "is, serves as")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 凡物輕不能載重小不能鎮大 (*fán wù qīng bù néng zài zhòng, xiǎo bù néng zhèn dà* — "in general the light cannot carry the heavy, the small cannot hold down the large"). Han Fei: 制在己曰重，不離位曰静 (*zhì zài jǐ yuē zhòng, bù lí wèi yuē jìng* — "control lying in oneself is called heavy; not leaving one's position is called still").

### Unit 2
> 是以君子終日行不離輜重。 (*Shì yǐ jūn zǐ zhōng rì xíng bù lí zī zhòng* — "Therefore the noble person travels all day and does not leave the baggage carts.")
- **Says:** For this reason the noble person, travelling the whole day, never leaves the heavy supply wagons.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 君子 (*jūn zǐ* — "noble person, lord's son"); 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 離 (*lí* — "leave")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore (是以 (*shì yǐ* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** 輜重 (*zī zhòng* — "baggage carts, heavy wagons"): the literal supply train vs. a figure for weight and stillness, or for the state. Wang Bi's and Heshang Gong's lemmas read 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage") for 君子 (*jūn zǐ* — "noble person"); Han Fei reads 君子 (*jūn zǐ* — "noble person").
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 輜靜也聖人終日行道不離其靜與重也 (*zī jìng yě, shèng rén zhōng rì xíng dào bù lí qí jìng yǔ zhòng yě* — "*zī* means stillness: the sage practises the Tao all day and does not leave his stillness and weight"). Han Fei: 邦者，人君之輜重也 (*bāng zhě, rén jūn zhī zī zhòng yě* — "the state is the ruler's baggage train"). Wang Bi: 以重為本故不離 (*yǐ zhòng wéi běn gù bù lí* — "taking the heavy as root, therefore does not leave it").

### Unit 3
> 雖有榮觀，燕處超然。 (*Suī yǒu róng guān, yàn chǔ chāo rán* — "Though there are splendid sights, at ease, dwelling aloof.")
- **Says:** Even with glorious views or palaces at hand, the noble person stays calmly at rest, above them.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the noble person of Unit 2
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** but (雖 (*suī* — "although", concessive))
- **Open:** 榮觀 (*róng guān/guàn* — "glorious sights / splendid towers"). 燕處 (*yàn chǔ* — "dwell at ease") as what the noble person does vs. Heshang Gong's "the women's quarters" that the person keeps away from. 超然 (*chāo rán* — "aloof, rising above"): serene detachment vs. (Han Fei) being cut off.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 不以經心也 (*bù yǐ jīng xīn yě* — "does not let them pass through the heart"). Heshang Gong: 榮觀謂宫闇燕處后妃所居也超然逺避而不處也 (*róng guàn wèi gōng àn, yàn chǔ hòu fēi suǒ jū yě, chāo rán yuǎn bì ér bù chǔ yě* — "*róng guàn* means palace chambers; *yàn chǔ* is where the queen and consorts live; *chāo rán* is keeping far away and not dwelling there"). Han Fei, of King Zhufu who handed over his state while alive: 雖有代、雲中之樂，超然已無趙矣 (*suī yǒu Dài, Yúnzhōng zhī lè, chāo rán yǐ wú Zhào yǐ* — "though he had the pleasures of Dai and Yunzhong, cut off, he no longer had Zhao").

### Unit 4
> 奈何萬乘之主，而以身輕天下？ (*Nài hé wàn shèng zhī zhǔ, ér yǐ shēn qīng tiān xià?* — "How can the lord of ten thousand chariots, with his person, make light of the world?")
- **Says:** How can a ruler of ten thousand chariots treat himself lightly in relation to the world (or carry himself lightly before it)?
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical: implies he should not; a lament)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 萬乘之主 (*wàn shèng zhī zhǔ* — "lord of ten thousand chariots"), a great ruler
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** but (contrast with the noble person)
- **Open:** 以身輕天下 (*yǐ shēn qīng tiān xià* — "with person light world"): "value his own person less than the world" vs. "by his own conduct make light of the world" vs. "be light in his person before the world". Wang Bi's lemma reads 王 (*wáng* — "king") for 主 (*zhǔ* — "lord").
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 王者至尊而以其身行輕躁乎疾時王奢恣輕淫也 (*wáng zhě zhì zūn ér yǐ qí shēn xíng qīng zào hū, jí shí wáng shē zì qīng yín yě* — "the king is most honoured, yet does he carry himself light and restless? A complaint against the extravagant, unbridled, frivolous kings of the time"). Han Fei: 無勢之謂輕 (*wú shì zhī wèi qīng* — "being without power is called light").

### Unit 5
> 輕則失本，躁則失君。 (*Qīng zé shī běn, zào zé shī jūn* — "Light, then lose the root; restless, then lose the lord.")
- **Says:** If light, one loses the root; if restless, one loses the ruler.
- **Act:** if/when … then (則 (*zé* — "then"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; the ruler of Unit 4, or anyone
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 失君 (*shī jūn* — "lose lord"): lose one's lordship/throne vs. lose the inner "lord" (stillness, Unit 1). 本 (*běn* — "root"): Wang Bi notes Heshang Gong reads 臣 (*chén* — "minister") and the Yongle Dadian 根 (*gēn* — "root"); Han Fei reads 臣 (*chén* — "minister").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 失本為喪身也失君為失君位也 (*shī běn wéi sàng shēn yě, shī jūn wéi shī jūn wèi yě* — "losing the root is losing one's life; losing the lord is losing the ruler's position"). Heshang Gong: 王者輕滔則失其臣 (*wáng zhě qīng tāo zé shī qí chén* — "a king who is frivolous and overflowing loses his ministers"). Han Fei: 離位之謂躁 (*lí wèi zhī wèi zào* — "leaving one's position is called restless").

# Chapter 27

**The chapter as a whole:** The skilled leave no trace: good travelling leaves no rut, good speech no flaw, good reckoning needs no tallies, good closing needs no bolt yet will not open, good tying needs no cord yet will not come loose. So the sage is always good at saving people and things and abandons none; this is called 襲明 (*xí míng* — "cloaked / inherited clarity"). The good are teacher to the not-good, the not-good are material to the good; not to value the teacher or love the material is great confusion however clever, and this is called the essential subtlety.

### Unit 1
> 善行無轍跡，善言無瑕謫，善計不用籌策，善閉無關楗而不可開，善結無繩約而不可解。 (*Shàn xíng wú zhé jì, shàn yán wú xiá zhé, shàn jì bù yòng chóu cè, shàn bì wú guān jiàn ér bù kě kāi, shàn jié wú shéng yuē ér bù kě jiě* — "Good travelling has no ruts or tracks; good speaking has no flaw or fault; good counting does not use tallies; good closing has no bar or bolt yet cannot be opened; good tying has no cord or knot yet cannot be undone.")
- **Says:** Skilled travel leaves no wheel ruts or footprints; skilled speech has no blemish to blame; skilled reckoning needs no counting rods; skilled shutting needs no bar or bolt and still cannot be opened; skilled binding needs no rope and still cannot be untied.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; whoever is skilled (善 (*shàn* — "good at, skilled")). Negators: 無 (*wú* — "there is no") negates the existence of ruts, flaws, bolts, cords; 不用 (*bù yòng* — "does not use") negates using; 不可 (*bù kě* — "cannot") negates possibility
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 善 (*shàn* — "good / skilled"): "good travelling" vs. "one skilled at travelling". 行 (*xíng* — "travel") vs. *xìng* ("conduct"). Wang Bi's lemma reads 善數 (*shàn shǔ* — "good counting") for 善計 (*shàn jì* — "good reckoning").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 此五者皆言不造不施因物之性不以形制物也 (*cǐ wǔ zhě jiē yán bù zào bù shī, yīn wù zhī xìng, bù yǐ xíng zhì wù yě* — "these five all say: not making, not imposing, following the nature of things, not controlling things by form"). Heshang Gong reads it as inner practice of the Tao: 善行道者求之於身不下堂不出門故無轍迹 (*shàn xíng dào zhě qiú zhī yú shēn, bù xià táng bù chū mén, gù wú zhé jì* — "one good at practising the Tao seeks it in the self, not leaving the hall or going out the gate, so there are no ruts").

### Unit 2
> 是以聖人常善救人，故無棄人； (*Shì yǐ shèng rén cháng shàn jiù rén, gù wú qì rén* — "Therefore the sage is always good at saving people, so there are no abandoned people.")
- **Says:** For this reason the sage is constantly skilled at rescuing people, and so no one is cast aside.
- **Act:** describes a person, with result
- **Who acts / of whom:** 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage") saves 人 (*rén* — "people"); 無 (*wú* — "there is no") negates the existence of 棄人 (*qì rén* — "discarded people")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore (是以 (*shì yǐ* — "therefore"); 故 (*gù* — "so"))
- **Open:** "no one is discarded" (as a state) vs. "the sage discards no one" (as an act).
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 聖人不立形名以檢於物不造進向以殊棄不肖 (*shèng rén bù lì xíng míng yǐ jiǎn yú wù, bù zào jìn xiàng yǐ shū qì bù xiào* — "the sage does not set up forms and names to check things, does not create standards of advancement to single out and discard the unworthy"). Heshang Gong: 使貴賤各得其所也 (*shǐ guì jiàn gè dé qí suǒ yě* — "letting noble and humble each find their place").

### Unit 3
> 常善救物，故無棄物。 (*Cháng shàn jiù wù, gù wú qì wù* — "Always good at saving things, so there are no abandoned things.")
- **Says:** The sage is constantly skilled at rescuing things, and so nothing is thrown away.
- **Act:** describes a person, with result
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; 無 (*wú* — "there is no") negates the existence of discarded things
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** as Unit 2
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 聖人不賤石而貴玉視之如一 (*shèng rén bù jiàn shí ér guì yù shì zhī rú yī* — "the sage does not scorn stone and prize jade, but sees them as one").

### Unit 4
> 是謂襲明。 (*Shì wèi xí míng* — "This is called cloaked clarity.")
- **Says:** This is named 襲明 (*xí míng* — "doubled / cloaked / inherited brightness").
- **Act:** names/defines (是謂 (*shì wèi* — "this is called"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** 是 (*shì* — "this") = the sage's saving of all
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (names Units 2–3)
- **Open:** 襲 (*xí* — "put on over, cloak; inherit, follow; repeat"): "hidden (cloaked) clarity" vs. "inherited / followed clarity" vs. "doubled clarity".
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 聖人善救人物謂襲明大道也 (*shèng rén shàn jiù rén wù wèi xí míng dà dào yě* — "the sage's skill at saving people and things is called *xí míng* of the great Tao"). Wang Bi has no comment on the term.

### Unit 5
> 故善人者，不善人之師； (*Gù shàn rén zhě, bù shàn rén zhī shī* — "So the good person is the teacher of the not-good person.")
- **Says:** Therefore the good (skilled) person is the teacher of the one who is not.
- **Act:** names/defines (asserts an identity)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 善人 (*shàn rén* — "good / skilled person"); 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 善 (*shàn* — "good")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** therefore (故 (*gù* — "so"))
- **Open:** 善 (*shàn* — "good / skilled"): morally good vs. skilled.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 舉善以師不善故謂之師矣 (*jǔ shàn yǐ shī bù shàn, gù wèi zhī shī yǐ* — "the good is raised up to teach the not-good, so it is called teacher").

### Unit 6
> 不善人者，善人之資。 (*Bù shàn rén zhě, shàn rén zhī zī* — "The not-good person is the resource of the good person.")
- **Says:** The one who is not good is the material (resource) of the good.
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** 不善人 (*bù shàn rén* — "not-good person"); 不 (*bù* — "not") negates good
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** 資 (*zī* — "resource, capital, means; aid"): material to work on vs. means of support (the good live by it).
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 資取也善人以善齊不善以善棄不善也故不善人善人之所取也 (*zī qǔ yě, shàn rén yǐ shàn qí bù shàn, yǐ shàn qì bù shàn yě, gù bù shàn rén shàn rén zhī suǒ qǔ yě* — "*zī* means what is drawn on: the good use goodness to bring the not-good into line, using goodness to put away the not-good; so the not-good are what the good draw on"). Heshang Gong: 資用也 (*zī yòng yě* — "*zī* means use").

### Unit 7
> 不貴其師，不愛其資，雖智大迷，是謂要妙。 (*Bù guì qí shī, bù ài qí zī, suī zhì dà mí, shì wèi yào miào* — "Not valuing one's teacher, not loving one's resource: though clever, greatly lost. This is called the essential subtlety.")
- **Says:** To not prize one's teacher and not cherish one's material is, however clever, great confusion; this is named the essential mystery.
- **Act:** if/when … then (implicit), then names/defines (是謂 (*shì wèi* — "this is called"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; anyone (the good who does not value the not-good, or the not-good who does not value the good). 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 貴 (*guì* — "value") and 愛 (*ài* — "love, cherish")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** what 是 (*shì* — "this") names: the whole teacher-and-resource relation vs. the confusion just described. Han Fei reverses it into strategy: deliberately not valuing or loving as cunning. 雖智 (*suī zhì* — "though clever"); Han Fei reads 知 (*zhì* — "know / wise").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 雖有其智自任其智不因物於其道必失故曰雖智大迷 (*suī yǒu qí zhì zì rèn qí zhì bù yīn wù yú qí dào bì shī, gù yuē suī zhì dà mí* — "though one has cleverness, relying on one's own cleverness and not following things, one will surely lose the way; so 'though clever, greatly lost'"). Heshang Gong: 能通此意是謂知微妙要道也 (*néng tōng cǐ yì shì wèi zhī wēi miào yào dào yě* — "to be able to grasp this meaning is called knowing the subtle, essential Tao"). Han Fei illustrates with King Wen, who gave a jade tablet to the unprincipled Fei Zhong and raised Taigong: 文王舉太公於渭濵者，貴之也；而資費仲玉版者，是愛之也 (*Wén Wáng jǔ Tàigōng yú Wèi bīn zhě, guì zhī yě; ér zī Fèi Zhòng yù bǎn zhě, shì ài zhī yě* — "King Wen raising Taigong from the banks of the Wei was valuing him; supplying Fei Zhong with the jade tablet was loving him").

# Chapter 28

**The chapter as a whole:** Three parallel stanzas: know the male (the cock) and keep the female (the hen), know the white and keep the black, know glory and keep disgrace, and one becomes the world's ravine, model, and valley; constant 德 (*dé* — "virtue, potency") then does not leave, does not err, is sufficient, and one returns to the infant, the limitless, the uncarved. When the uncarved is broken up it becomes vessels; the sage, using it, becomes chief of officials; so the greatest cutting does not cut.

### Unit 1
> 知其雄，守其雌，為天下谿。 (*Zhī qí xióng, shǒu qí cí, wéi tiān xià xī* — "Know its cock, keep its hen, be the ravine of the world.")
- **Says:** Knowing the male, holding to the female, one becomes the ravine of all under heaven.
- **Act:** advises/commands (or if/when … then)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; the reader / the sage. 其 (*qí* — "its / one's") unspecified; 雄 (*xióng* — "cock, male bird") and 雌 (*cí* — "hen, female bird")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** imperative ("know … keep … be") vs. description ("one who knows … keeps … becomes"). 其 (*qí* — "its / one's"): "one's own" maleness/femaleness vs. "the" male and female in general.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 雄先之屬雌後之屬也 (*xióng xiān zhī shǔ, cí hòu zhī shǔ yě* — "the cock is of the kind that goes first; the hen of the kind that goes after"); 谿不求物而物自歸之 (*xī bù qiú wù ér wù zì guī zhī* — "the ravine does not seek things, yet things come to it of themselves"). Heshang Gong: 雄以喻尊雌以喻卑 (*xióng yǐ yù zūn, cí yǐ yù bēi* — "the cock figures the honoured, the hen the lowly"), and 去之强梁就雌之柔和如是則天下歸之如水流入深谿也 (*qù zhī qiáng liáng jiù cí zhī róu hé, rú shì zé tiān xià guī zhī rú shuǐ liú rù shēn xī yě* — "leave the hard and forceful and go to the hen's softness and harmony; then the world comes home to one as water flows into a deep ravine").

### Unit 2
> 為天下谿，常德不離，復歸於嬰兒。 (*Wéi tiān xià xī, cháng dé bù lí, fù guī yú yīng ér* — "Being the ravine of the world, constant virtue does not leave; return again to the infant.")
- **Says:** Being the world's ravine, constant 德 (*dé* — "virtue, potency") does not depart, and one goes back to being an infant.
- **Act:** if/when … then (implicit)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the one who is the ravine; 常德 (*cháng dé* — "constant virtue"); 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 離 (*lí* — "depart")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (chain from Unit 1)
- **Open:** 常 (*cháng* — "constant / always"): "constant virtue does not leave" vs. "virtue always stays". 復歸 (*fù guī* — "return again"): return to infancy as a state vs. to the infant as an image.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 嬰兒不用智而合自然之智 (*yīng ér bù yòng zhì ér hé zì rán zhī zhì* — "the infant uses no cleverness yet accords with the knowing of the self-so"). Heshang Gong: 常復歸志於孾兒惷然而無所知也 (*cháng fù guī zhì yú yīng ér chǔn rán ér wú suǒ zhī yě* — "always return one's will to the infant, simple and knowing nothing").

### Unit 3
> 知其白，守其黑，為天下式。 (*Zhī qí bái, shǒu qí hēi, wéi tiān xià shì* — "Know its white, keep its black, be the model of the world.")
- **Says:** Knowing the white, holding to the black, one becomes the pattern for all under heaven.
- **Act:** advises/commands (or if/when … then)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; the reader / the sage
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel stanza)
- **Open:** 式 (*shì* — "model, standard, pattern"). Imperative vs. description, as Unit 1.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 式模則也 (*shì mó zé yě* — "*shì* means a model and rule"). Heshang Gong: 白以喻昭昭黒以喻黙黙 (*bái yǐ yù zhāo zhāo, hēi yǐ yù mò mò* — "white figures brightness, black figures silence").

### Unit 4
> 為天下式，常德不忒，復歸於無極。 (*Wéi tiān xià shì, cháng dé bù tè, fù guī yú wú jí* — "Being the model of the world, constant virtue does not err; return again to the limitless.")
- **Says:** Being the world's model, constant 德 (*dé* — "virtue") does not go astray, and one goes back to the boundless.
- **Act:** if/when … then (implicit)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the one who is the model; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 忒 (*tè* — "err, deviate"); 無 (*wú* — "without") negates 極 (*jí* — "limit")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 無極 (*wú jí* — "no limit"): the boundless vs. the inexhaustible.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 忒差也 (*tè chā yě* — "*tè* means to deviate"); 無極 (*wú jí* — "the limitless") is 不可窮也 (*bù kě qióng yě* — "what cannot be exhausted"). Heshang Gong: 長生久壽歸身於無窮極也 (*cháng shēng jiǔ shòu guī shēn yú wú qióng jí yě* — "long life and lasting years: returning the body to the limitless").

### Unit 5
> 知其榮，守其辱，為天下谷。 (*Zhī qí róng, shǒu qí rǔ, wéi tiān xià gǔ* — "Know its glory, keep its disgrace, be the valley of the world.")
- **Says:** Knowing honour, holding to disgrace, one becomes the valley of all under heaven.
- **Act:** advises/commands (or if/when … then)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; the reader / the sage
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel stanza)
- **Open:** 辱 (*rǔ* — "disgrace, humiliation; soiled"): social disgrace vs. lowliness/dirt.
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 榮以喻尊貴辱以喻汚濁 (*róng yǐ yù zūn guì, rǔ yǐ yù wū zhuó* — "glory figures honour and rank; disgrace figures filth and mud").

### Unit 6
> 為天下谷，常德乃足，復歸於樸。 (*Wéi tiān xià gǔ, cháng dé nǎi zú, fù guī yú pǔ* — "Being the valley of the world, constant virtue is then sufficient; return again to the uncarved wood.")
- **Says:** Being the world's valley, constant 德 (*dé* — "virtue") is then complete, and one goes back to the uncarved block.
- **Act:** if/when … then (乃 (*nǎi* — "then"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** the one who is the valley
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 足 (*zú* — "sufficient, full; stop, rest"): "virtue is complete" vs. "virtue comes to rest [in one]".
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 足止也人能為天下谷徳乃止於巳 (*zú zhǐ yě, rén néng wéi tiān xià gǔ dé nǎi zhǐ yú jǐ* — "*zú* means to rest: if a person can be the valley of the world, virtue then rests in them"). Wang Bi on the three stanzas: 此三者言常反終後乃徳全其所處也 (*cǐ sān zhě yán cháng fǎn zhōng hòu nǎi dé quán qí suǒ chǔ yě* — "these three say: always turn back, end up behind, and then virtue is whole where it abides").

### Unit 7
> 樸散則為器，聖人用之，則為官長，故大制不割。 (*Pǔ sàn zé wéi qì, shèng rén yòng zhī, zé wéi guān zhǎng, gù dà zhì bù gē* — "The uncarved, dispersed, then becomes vessels; the sage uses it, then becomes chief of officials; so the great cutting does not cut.")
- **Says:** When the uncarved block is broken up it becomes implements; when the sage uses it (or them), the sage becomes (or makes) the head of officials; therefore the great carving (or governing) does not cut apart.
- **Act:** if/when … then (則 (*zé* — "then", twice)), then asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 樸 (*pǔ* — "uncarved wood") disperses; 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage") uses 之 (*zhī* — "it / them"); the subject of 為官長 (*wéi guān zhǎng* — "become/make chief of officials") is unstated; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 割 (*gē* — "cut")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** therefore (故 (*gù* — "so"))
- **Open:** 之 (*zhī* — "it / them"): the vessels vs. the uncarved block itself. 則為官長 (*zé wéi guān zhǎng* — "then become/make chief of officials"): "the sage becomes chief of officials" vs. "the sage makes them into officials and chiefs". 大制 (*dà zhì* — "great cutting / great ruling"): tailoring/carving vs. government.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 樸真也真散則百行出殊𩔖生若器也聖人因其分散故為之立官長 (*pǔ zhēn yě, zhēn sàn zé bǎi xíng chū shū lèi shēng ruò qì yě; shèng rén yīn qí fēn sàn gù wèi zhī lì guān zhǎng* — "the uncarved is the genuine; when the genuine disperses, the hundred conducts emerge and distinct kinds arise, like vessels; the sage follows their dispersal and so sets up officials and chiefs for them"), and 大制者以天下之心為心故無割也 (*dà zhì zhě yǐ tiān xià zhī xīn wéi xīn gù wú gē yě* — "the great ruling takes the heart of the world as its heart, so there is no cutting"). Heshang Gong: 聖人升用則為百官之元長也 (*shèng rén shēng yòng zé wéi bǎi guān zhī yuán zhǎng yě* — "when the sage is raised and employed, he becomes the prime chief of the hundred officials"), and 以大道制御天下无所傷割 (*yǐ dà dào zhì yù tiān xià wú suǒ shāng gē* — "rules the world by the great Tao, injuring and cutting nothing").
