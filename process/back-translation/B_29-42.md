# Chapter 29

**The chapter as a whole:** Anyone who sets out to take the world and work on it will not succeed. The world is a numinous vessel that cannot be worked on or held: working on it ruins it, holding it loses it. Things differ, some leading and some following, some strong and some weak, so the sage gets rid of the extreme, the extravagant and the excessive.

### Unit 1
> 將欲取天下而為之，吾見其不得已。 (*jiāng yù qǔ tiān xià ér wéi zhī, wú jiàn qí bù dé yǐ* — "about to want to take all-under-heaven and act on it, I see that he will not get it")
- **Says:** When someone sets out to take the world and act on it, the speaker sees that this person will not get it.
- **Act:** if/when … then (framed as the speaker's observation)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the would-be taker is unstated, most likely a ruler or contender; 吾 (*wú* — "I") is the speaker; 之 (*zhī* — "it") is the world
- **Force:** will/future (將 *jiāng* — "about to")
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 不得已 (*bù dé yǐ* — "not get / no choice"), with 不 (*bù* — "not") negating getting. (a) 已 (*yǐ* — final particle, "that's all"): "will not get it." (b) The fixed phrase "cannot help it / has no choice," as in chapters 30 and 31: "I see he is driven to it." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong takes (a), reading 不得天道人心 (*bù dé tiān dào rén xīn* — "does not get heaven's way and people's hearts"), and glosses 為 (*wéi* — "to act on") as 欲以有為治民 (*yù yǐ yǒu wéi zhì mín* — "wants to govern the people through purposive action").

### Unit 2
> 天下神器，不可為也，不可執也。 (*tiān xià shén qì, bù kě wéi yě, bù kě zhí yě* — "all-under-heaven is a numinous vessel; it cannot be acted on, it cannot be held")
- **Says:** The world is a numinous vessel; it cannot be acted on and cannot be held.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the world is what is acted on; whoever would act on it is unstated, anyone and a ruler especially
- **Force:** always/universal; 不可 (*bù kě* — "cannot / may not"), 不 (*bù* — "not") negating possibility or permission
- **Link:** because (explains Unit 1, without a connective)
- **Open:** 神器 (*shén qì* — "spirit vessel"). **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 神無形無方也器合成也 (*shén wú xíng wú fāng yě, qì hé chéng yě* — "shén is without form and without direction; qì is something put together"), so the world is a formless thing put together. Heshang Gong: 器物也人乃天下之神物也 (*qì wù yě, rén nǎi tiān xià zhī shén wù yě* — "qì means 'thing'; people are the numinous things of the world"), so the vessel is the people. 不可 (*bù kě* — "cannot") can be "cannot" or "must not." Wang Bi's lemma lacks the second clause 不可執也 (*bù kě zhí yě* — "cannot be held"), though his comment has 不可執 (*bù kě zhí* — "cannot be held").

### Unit 3
> 為者敗之，執者失之。 (*wéi zhě bài zhī, zhí zhě shī zhī* — "one who acts on it ruins it; one who holds it loses it")
- **Says:** Whoever acts on it ruins it; whoever holds it loses it.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 為者 (*wéi zhě* — "one who acts"), 執者 (*zhí zhě* — "one who holds"); 之 (*zhī* — "it") is the world
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (states the consequence)
- **Open:** none

### Unit 4
> 故物或行或隨； (*gù wù huò xíng huò suí* — "so, of things, some go ahead, some follow")
- **Says:** So among things, some go ahead and some follow.
- **Act:** asserts (describes)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 物 (*wù* — "things, beings")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore/so (故 *gù* — "therefore"), though how it follows from the units before is loose
- **Open:** 或…或 (*huò … huò* — "some … some"): "some … others" (variety among things) or "now … now" (one thing alternating). **Commentators:** Heshang Gong reads it politically: 上所行下必隨之也 (*shàng suǒ xíng xià bì suí zhī yě* — "what superiors do, inferiors must follow"). Wang Bi reads it of things in general: 凡此諸或言物事逆順反覆 (*fán cǐ zhū huò yán wù shì nì shùn fǎn fù* — "all these 'some' speak of things and affairs going against or along, reversing and overturning").

### Unit 5
> 或歔或吹； (*huò xū huò chuī* — "some breathe out softly, some blow")
- **Says:** Some breathe out gently, some blow hard.
- **Act:** asserts (describes)
- **Who acts / of whom:** things, as in Unit 4
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 歔 (*xū* — "to breathe out, sigh"). **Commentators:** Heshang Gong's text reads 呴 (*xǔ* — "to breathe warmly") and glosses 呴温也吹寒也 (*xǔ wēn yě, chuī hán yě* — "xǔ is warming, chuī is chilling"), adding 有所温必有所寒也 (*yǒu suǒ wēn bì yǒu suǒ hán yě* — "where something is warmed, something must be chilled").

### Unit 6
> 或強或羸； (*huò qiáng huò léi* — "some strong, some lean")
- **Says:** Some are strong, some are weak.
- **Act:** asserts (describes)
- **Who acts / of whom:** things
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none

### Unit 7
> 或挫或隳。 (*huò cuò huò huī* — "some snap, some collapse")
- **Says:** Some break, some fall into ruin.
- **Act:** asserts (describes)
- **Who acts / of whom:** things
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** In the base, 挫 (*cuò* — "to snap, blunt") and 隳 (*huī* — "to collapse, ruin") are both negative, so this pair loses the contrast the other pairs have. They can be active ("some crush, some topple") or passive ("some are broken, some are toppled"). **Commentators:** Heshang Gong's text reads 載 (*zài* — "to carry, be secure") for 挫 (*cuò* — "to snap"), glossed 載安也隳危也 (*zài ān yě, huī wēi yě* — "zài is security, huī is danger"): "some are secure, some are imperilled."

### Unit 8
> 是以聖人去甚，去奢，去泰。 (*shì yǐ shèng rén qù shèn, qù shē, qù tài* — "therefore the sage removes the extreme, removes the extravagant, removes the excessive")
- **Says:** Therefore the sage does away with the extreme, the extravagant and the excessive.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore (是以 *shì yǐ* — "for this reason")
- **Open:** 去 (*qù* — "to remove, leave, keep away from") can mean removing these from oneself or from the realm. **Commentators:** Heshang Gong gives each term an object: 甚謂貪淫聲色 (*shèn wèi tān yín shēng sè* — "'the extreme' means greed and indulgence in music and sex"), 奢謂服飾飲食 (*shē wèi fú shì yǐn shí* — "'the extravagant' means clothing, ornament, food and drink"), 泰謂宫室臺榭 (*tài wèi gōng shì tái xiè* — "'the excessive' means palaces, halls, terraces and pavilions"). Wang Bi keeps it inward: 除其所以迷去其所以惑 (*chú qí suǒ yǐ mí, qù qí suǒ yǐ huò* — "removes what leads astray, discards what deludes").

# Chapter 30

**The chapter as a whole:** Someone who helps a ruler by the Tao does not use arms to dominate the world. Warfare comes back on the one who wages it, and it leaves brambles and bad years. The skilled one gets a result and stops, without pride and without seeking to dominate. Forced vigor ages, which is not-Tao, and not-Tao ends early.

### Unit 1
> 以道佐人主者，不以兵強天下。 (*yǐ dào zuǒ rén zhǔ zhě, bù yǐ bīng qiáng tiān xià* — "one who assists a ruler of people by means of the Tao does not use weapons to strong-arm all-under-heaven")
- **Says:** Whoever assists a ruler by means of the Tao does not use arms to force the world.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 以道佐人主者 (*yǐ dào zuǒ rén zhǔ zhě* — "one who assists a ruler by the Tao"), most naturally a minister; 天下 (*tiān xià* — "all-under-heaven") is what is forced
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 不 (*bù* — "not") negates using arms. 強 (*qiáng* — "strong"), used as a verb, can mean "to dominate the world" or "to be strong in the world." **Commentators:** Wang Bi takes the helper as a minister and argues from lesser to greater: 以道佐人主尚不可以兵强於天下况人主躬於道者乎 (*yǐ dào zuǒ rén zhǔ shàng bù kě yǐ bīng qiáng yú tiān xià, kuàng rén zhǔ gōng yú dào zhě hū* — "even one who assists a ruler by the Tao may not use arms to be strong over the world, how much less a ruler who embodies the Tao himself?"). Heshang Gong reads it reflexively: 謂人主能以道自輔佐也 (*wèi rén zhǔ néng yǐ dào zì fǔ zuǒ yě* — "it means the ruler is able to assist himself by the Tao").

### Unit 2
> 其事好還。 (*qí shì hào huán* — "its affairs tend to return")
- **Says:** Such affairs tend to come back.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 其 (*qí* — "its, his"): the business of using arms, or the affairs of the Tao-guided helper
- **Force:** habitual (好 *hào* — "to be prone to")
- **Link:** because (implied: the reason not to use arms)
- **Open:** 還 (*huán* — "to return, turn back"). (a) Warfare recoils on whoever wages it. **Commentators:** (b) Wang Bi: 有道者務欲還反無為 (*yǒu dào zhě wù yù huán fǎn wú wéi* — "one who has the Tao strives to return to non-action"). (c) Heshang Gong: 其舉事好還自責不怨於人也 (*qí jǔ shì hào huán zì zé bù yuàn yú rén yě* — "in undertaking affairs he tends to turn back and blame himself, not resent others").

### Unit 3
> 師之所處，荊棘生焉。 (*shī zhī suǒ chǔ, jīng jí shēng yān* — "where an army stays, thorns and brambles grow there")
- **Says:** Wherever an army camps, thorns and brambles grow.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 師 (*shī* — "army")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and (elaborates)
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong spells out the cause: 農事廢田不修 (*nóng shì fèi tián bù xiū* — "farm work is abandoned, the fields go untended").

### Unit 4
> 大軍之後，必有凶年。 (*dà jūn zhī hòu, bì yǒu xiōng nián* — "after great armies there must be ill years")
- **Says:** After a great army, bad years are sure to follow.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 大軍 (*dà jūn* — "great armies")
- **Force:** must/necessary (必 *bì* — "surely")
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none

### Unit 5
> 善有果而已，不敢以取強。 (*shàn yǒu guǒ ér yǐ, bù gǎn yǐ qǔ qiáng* — "the skilled one has a result and stops; does not dare to use it to take strength")
- **Says:** The skilled one gets a result and stops there, and does not presume to use it to seize dominance.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 善 (*shàn* — "the skilled / the good"), unstated beyond that; both commentators make it one skilled at using troops
- **Force:** habitual; 不敢 (*bù gǎn* — "does not dare"), with 不 (*bù* — "not") negating presumption
- **Link:** none (turns from warning to the right way)
- **Open:** 果 (*guǒ* — "fruit, result, resolute"). **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 果猶濟也 (*guǒ yóu jì yě* — "guǒ is like 'relieving'"), and 善用師者趣以濟難而已矣 (*shàn yòng shī zhě qù yǐ jì nán ér yǐ yǐ* — "one good at using troops aims only to relieve the crisis"). Heshang Gong, whose text reads 善者果而已 (*shàn zhě guǒ ér yǐ* — "the skilled one is resolute and stops"), glosses 善兵者當果敢而已不休 (*shàn bīng zhě dāng guǒ gǎn ér yǐ bù xiū* — "one good at war should be resolute and then stop, not keep on"). So 果 (*guǒ* — "result / resolute") is "achieve the result" (Wang Bi) or "be resolute" (Heshang Gong). 取強 (*qǔ qiáng* — "take strength"): Heshang Gong reads it as 取强大之名 (*qǔ qiáng dà zhī míng* — "take the name of strong and great").

### Unit 6
> 果而勿矜，果而勿伐，果而勿驕。 (*guǒ ér wù jīn, guǒ ér wù fá, guǒ ér wù jiāo* — "get the result and do not pride yourself, get the result and do not boast, get the result and do not be arrogant")
- **Says:** Get the result without pride, without boasting, without arrogance.
- **Act:** advises/commands (勿 *wù* — "do not," a prohibition)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: the skilled one, or anyone who must use force
- **Force:** must/necessary (prohibitive)
- **Link:** and (unfolds 果 *guǒ* — "result")
- **Open:** 勿 (*wù* — "do not") is normally prohibitive; it can also be read as continuing the description of Unit 5 ("gets the result yet does not boast"). 果 (*guǒ* — "result / resolute") is "result" (Wang Bi) or "resolute" (Heshang Gong), as in Unit 5.

### Unit 7
> 果而不得已，果而勿強。 (*guǒ ér bù dé yǐ, guǒ ér wù qiáng* — "get the result as something you could not avoid; get the result and do not be strong")
- **Says:** Get the result only because there was no choice; get it without making yourself strong by it.
- **Act:** advises/commands
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated, as in Unit 6
- **Force:** must/necessary
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 不得已 (*bù dé yǐ* — "cannot stop / no choice"), with 不 (*bù* — "not") negating the possibility of stopping; 勿 (*wù* — "do not") is a prohibition. **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 時故不得已當復用者 (*shí gù bù dé yǐ dāng fù yòng zhě* — "when the times leave no choice and one must use [troops] again"), then 不遂用果以為强也 (*bù suì yòng guǒ yǐ wéi qiáng yě* — "one does not go on to use the result to make oneself strong"). Heshang Gong instead reads 勿強 (*wù qiáng* — "do not be strong") as 勿以為强兵堅甲以侵凌人也 (*wù yǐ wéi qiáng bīng jiān jiǎ yǐ qīn líng rén yě* — "do not take it as license for strong troops and hard armor to invade and bully others").

### Unit 8
> 物壯則老，是謂不道，不道早已。 (*wù zhuàng zé lǎo, shì wèi bù dào, bù dào zǎo yǐ* — "things at their peak then age; this is called not-Tao; not-Tao ends early")
- **Says:** When things reach their peak they grow old; that is called not-Tao, and what is not-Tao comes to an early end.
- **Act:** if/when … then (則 *zé* — "then"); names/defines (是謂 *shì wèi* — "this is called"); asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 物 (*wù* — "things")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 不道 (*bù dào* — "not the Tao"): 不 (*bù* — "not") negates the Tao itself, or the following of it. Wang Bi notes one text has 非 (*fēi* — "is not"). 壯 (*zhuàng* — "robust, at full vigor") can be forced, violent strength or natural ripeness. **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 壯武力暴興 (*zhuàng wǔ lì bào xīng* — "zhuàng is martial force rising violently"), a figure for dominating by arms. Heshang Gong: 草木壯極則枯落 (*cǎo mù zhuàng jí zé kū luò* — "when plants and trees reach full vigor they wither and fall"), and 不行道者早死 (*bù xíng dào zhě zǎo sǐ* — "one who does not walk the Tao dies early"). Also open: whether 是 (*shì* — "this") points to the peak alone or to the whole sequence of peaking and then ageing.

# Chapter 31

**The chapter as a whole:** Fine weapons are ill-omened tools that beings hate, so one who has the Tao does not stay near them. The noble person honors the left in peace and the right in war, and uses weapons only when there is no choice, calmly. To glory in victory is to delight in killing, and such a person will not get their way in the world. War belongs on the side of mourning: its generals stand as at a funeral, its dead are wept over, and victory is kept with funeral rites.

### Unit 1
> 夫佳兵者，不祥之器，物或惡之，故有道者不處。 (*fú jiā bīng zhě, bù xiáng zhī qì, wù huò wù zhī, gù yǒu dào zhě bù chǔ* — "now, fine weapons are implements of ill omen; things perhaps hate them; so one who has the Tao does not abide with them")
- **Says:** Fine weapons are ill-omened tools, which beings hate, so one who has the Tao does not stay with them.
- **Act:** asserts; describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 物 (*wù* — "things, beings") hate them; 有道者 (*yǒu dào zhě* — "one who has the Tao") does not stay with them
- **Force:** always/universal; habitual
- **Link:** none (夫 *fú* — "now," opening a topic); inside the unit, therefore (故 *gù* — "therefore")
- **Open:** 不祥 (*bù xiáng* — "not auspicious"): 不 (*bù* — "not") negates a quality. 不處 (*bù chǔ* — "does not dwell"): 不 (*bù* — "not") negates an action. 佳兵 (*jiā bīng* — "fine weapons") can also be "excellent warfare," since 兵 (*bīng* — "weapons, troops") is weapons, troops, or war. 物或惡之 (*wù huò wù zhī* — "things perhaps / some things hate them"): **Commentators:** Heshang Gong makes it universal: 萬物無有不惡之 (*wàn wù wú yǒu bù wù zhī* — "among the myriad things there are none that do not hate them"). Heshang Gong also gives a place for 不處 (*bù chǔ* — "does not dwell"): 不處其國 (*bù chǔ qí guó* — "does not dwell in such a state"). Wang Bi's editor notes 一本無者字 (*yī běn wú zhě zì* — "one edition lacks the word 者 *zhě*"), and Heshang Gong's lemma also lacks it.

### Unit 2
> 君子居則貴左，用兵則貴右。 (*jūn zǐ jū zé guì zuǒ, yòng bīng zé guì yòu* — "the noble person at home then values the left; using weapons then values the right")
- **Says:** At home the noble person gives honor to the left; at war, to the right.
- **Act:** if/when … then (則 *zé* — "then," twice)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 君子 (*jūn zǐ* — "the noble person")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong glosses the sides: 貴柔弱也 (*guì róu ruò yě* — "values the soft and weak") and 貴剛強也 (*guì gāng qiáng yě* — "values the hard and strong").

### Unit 3
> 兵者不祥之器，非君子之器，不得已而用之，恬淡為上。 (*bīng zhě bù xiáng zhī qì, fēi jūn zǐ zhī qì, bù dé yǐ ér yòng zhī, tián dàn wéi shàng* — "weapons are implements of ill omen, not the implements of the noble person; using them when one cannot help it, calm and bland is highest")
- **Says:** Weapons are ill-omened tools and not the noble person's tools. One uses them when there is no choice, and calm detachment is best.
- **Act:** asserts; advises
- **Who acts / of whom:** the user is unstated, most likely the noble person
- **Force:** must/necessary (only under compulsion)
- **Link:** and (takes up Unit 1 again)
- **Open:** 不 (*bù* — "not") negates a quality in 不祥 (*bù xiáng* — "not auspicious") and the possibility of stopping in 不得已 (*bù dé yǐ* — "no choice"); 非 (*fēi* — "is not") denies an identity. 恬淡為上 (*tián dàn wéi shàng* — "calm and bland is best") can describe the inner manner of using weapons, or the aim. **Commentators:** Heshang Gong takes the aim: 不貪土地利人財寳 (*bù tān tǔ dì lì rén cái bǎo* — "not coveting land, not profiting from others' wealth and treasure"). Wang Bi's editor notes that from 兵者不祥之器 (*bīng zhě bù xiáng zhī qì* — "weapons are ill-omened tools") down to 言以喪禮處之 (*yán yǐ sàng lǐ chǔ zhī* — "that is, it is treated by mourning rites"), 似有注語雜入 (*sì yǒu zhù yǔ zá rù* — "commentary seems to have been mixed in"), though all editions carry it as text.

### Unit 4
> 勝而不美，而美之者，是樂殺人。 (*shèng ér bù měi, ér měi zhī zhě, shì lè shā rén* — "win and do not beautify it; one who beautifies it, this is delighting in killing people")
- **Says:** Victory is not to be glorified; to glorify it is to delight in killing people.
- **Act:** advises (or asserts); names/defines (是 *shì* — "this is")
- **Who acts / of whom:** the victor, unstated; 美之者 (*měi zhī zhě* — "one who glorifies it")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 不 (*bù* — "not") negates glorifying. 勝而不美 (*shèng ér bù měi* — "win and not beautiful") is either a statement ("victory is not a fine thing") or an instruction ("win, but do not glorify it"). **Commentators:** Heshang Gong reads 美 (*měi* — "fine") as gain: 雖得勝而不以為利己也 (*suī dé shèng ér bù yǐ wéi lì jǐ yě* — "though he wins, he does not take it as profit for himself").

### Unit 5
> 夫樂殺人者，則不可得志於天下矣。 (*fú lè shā rén zhě, zé bù kě dé zhì yú tiān xià yǐ* — "one who delights in killing people, then cannot get their will in all-under-heaven")
- **Says:** One who delights in killing people cannot achieve their aims in the world.
- **Act:** if/when … then (則 *zé* — "then")
- **Who acts / of whom:** 樂殺人者 (*lè shā rén zhě* — "one who delights in killing people")
- **Force:** can/possible, negated: 不可 (*bù kě* — "cannot"), with 不 (*bù* — "not") negating possibility
- **Link:** none (夫 *fú* — "now")
- **Open:** a prediction ("will not succeed") or a norm ("must not be allowed to succeed"). **Commentators:** Heshang Gong takes the norm: 此不可使得志於天下 (*cǐ bù kě shǐ dé zhì yú tiān xià* — "such a one must not be allowed to get his will in the world").

### Unit 6
> 吉事尚左，凶事尚右。 (*jí shì shàng zuǒ, xiōng shì shàng yòu* — "in auspicious affairs the left is honored, in ill-omened affairs the right")
- **Says:** In auspicious affairs the left is the place of honor; in inauspicious ones, the right.
- **Act:** asserts (a ritual custom)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: custom
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none

### Unit 7
> 偏將軍居左，上將軍居右，言以喪禮處之。 (*piān jiāng jūn jū zuǒ, shàng jiāng jūn jū yòu, yán yǐ sàng lǐ chǔ zhī* — "the deputy general stands on the left, the supreme general on the right; that is, it is handled by the rites of mourning")
- **Says:** The deputy general takes the left and the commander-in-chief the right, which means war is handled by the rites of mourning.
- **Act:** describes; explains (言 *yán* — "that is to say")
- **Who acts / of whom:** 偏將軍 (*piān jiāng jūn* — "deputy general"), 上將軍 (*shàng jiāng jūn* — "supreme general"); 之 (*zhī* — "it") is war, or the army
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore/so (applies Unit 6)
- **Open:** 言 (*yán* — "that is to say") may mark a gloss that slipped into the text, as Wang Bi's editor suspects (see Unit 3). Heshang Gong explains the seating: 上將軍尊而居右者言其主殺也 (*shàng jiāng jūn zūn ér jū yòu zhě yán qí zhǔ shā yě* — "the supreme general, the honored one, sits on the right because he is in charge of killing").

### Unit 8
> 殺人之眾，以悲哀泣之，戰勝以喪禮處之。 (*shā rén zhī zhòng, yǐ bēi āi qì zhī, zhàn shèng yǐ sàng lǐ chǔ zhī* — "the multitude of people killed: weep for them with grief and sorrow; a victory in battle: handle it with mourning rites")
- **Says:** The many killed are to be wept over in grief, and a victory in battle is to be kept with funeral rites.
- **Act:** advises (or describes a custom)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: the victor, the noble person
- **Force:** habitual; must (normative)
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 殺人之眾 (*shā rén zhī zhòng* — "killed-people's multitude"): "the multitude of people killed" (the dead as object) or "when many people have been killed" (the killing as event). Heshang Gong reads the weeping as self-reproach: 傷已德薄不能以道化人而害无辜之民 (*shāng yǐ dé bó bù néng yǐ dào huà rén ér hài wú gū zhī mín* — "grieving that one's own virtue is thin, unable to transform people by the Tao, so that innocent people are harmed").

# Chapter 32

**The chapter as a whole:** The Tao is always nameless. The uncarved block, small as it is, can be made a subject by no one. If rulers keep it, things come of themselves, heaven and earth join and send down sweet dew, and the people come into balance without commands. Once cutting begins there are names, and once there are names one must know where to stop, which keeps one from danger. The Tao in the world is like streams and valleys in relation to the rivers and the sea.

### Unit 1
> 道常無名。 (*dào cháng wú míng* — "the Tao is constantly without name")
- **Says:** The Tao is always nameless.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 道 (*dào* — "the Tao")
- **Force:** always/universal (常 *cháng* — "constant")
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 無 (*wú* — "there is no") negates the existence of a name. (a) "Constantly nameless." (b) "Has no constant name." The line can also be punctuated 道常無名樸 (*dào cháng wú míng pǔ* — "the Tao is constantly the nameless uncarved block"). **Commentators:** Wang Bi takes (a): 以無名為常故曰道常無名也 (*yǐ wú míng wéi cháng gù yuē dào cháng wú míng yě* — "it takes namelessness as its constant, hence 'the Tao is constantly nameless'"). Heshang Gong takes (b): 故無常名也 (*gù wú cháng míng yě* — "so it has no constant name").

### Unit 2
> 樸雖小，天下莫能臣也。 (*pǔ suī xiǎo, tiān xià mò néng chén yě* — "the uncarved block, though small, no one under heaven can make it a subject")
- **Says:** Small as the uncarved block is, no one in the world can make it serve.
- **Act:** asserts (with a concession: 雖 *suī* — "although")
- **Who acts / of whom:** 樸 (*pǔ* — "the uncarved block") cannot be made a subject; 天下 (*tiān xià* — "all-under-heaven") is the would-be master
- **Force:** always/universal; can (negated)
- **Link:** and (the block restates the nameless Tao)
- **Open:** 莫 (*mò* — "no one") negates the agent: no one can. 臣 (*chén* — "minister") is a verb here: "to make a subject of." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong's text reads 天下不敢臣 (*tiān xià bù gǎn chén* — "the world does not dare make it a subject"), with 不 (*bù* — "not"); Wang Bi's editor notes it as 莫敢臣 (*mò gǎn chén* — "none dare make it a subject"), turning ability into daring. Heshang Gong identifies the block with the Tao: 道朴雖小 (*dào pǔ suī xiǎo* — "the Tao-block, though small").

### Unit 3
> 侯王若能守之，萬物將自賓。 (*hóu wáng ruò néng shǒu zhī, wàn wù jiāng zì bīn* — "if lords and kings can keep it, the myriad things will of themselves become guests")
- **Says:** If lords and kings can keep it, all things will come to them of their own accord.
- **Act:** if/when … then (若 *ruò* — "if")
- **Who acts / of whom:** 侯王 (*hóu wáng* — "lords and kings") keep it; 之 (*zhī* — "it") is the block or the Tao; 萬物 (*wàn wù* — "the myriad things") come of themselves
- **Force:** will/future (將 *jiāng* — "will")
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 賓 (*bīn* — "guest; to come as a guest, to pay allegiance"): coming as an honored guest, or submitting. Heshang Gong takes submission: 萬物將自賔服從於德也 (*wàn wù jiāng zì bīn fú cóng yú dé yě* — "the myriad things will submit of themselves and follow its virtue").

### Unit 4
> 天地相合，以降甘露，民莫之令而自均。 (*tiān dì xiāng hé, yǐ jiàng gān lù, mín mò zhī lìng ér zì jūn* — "heaven and earth join together and so send down sweet dew; the people, no one commanding them, are even of themselves")
- **Says:** Heaven and earth unite and send down sweet dew, and the people come into balance on their own without anyone ordering them.
- **Act:** asserts (describes the result)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天地 (*tiān dì* — "heaven and earth"); 民 (*mín* — "the people")
- **Force:** will/future (continues the condition of Unit 3)
- **Link:** and (a further result)
- **Open:** 莫 (*mò* — "no one") negates the agent of commanding. 民莫之令而自均 (*mín mò zhī lìng ér zì jūn* — "people, none commanding, of itself even") can be read two ways. (a) "The people, with no one ordering them, become even of themselves." (b) "No one among the people orders it, yet it [the dew] falls evenly." **Commentators:** Wang Bi takes (a): 民不令而自均 (*mín bù lìng ér zì jūn* — "the people, uncommanded, even themselves out"). Heshang Gong widens it to all things: 萬物莫有教令之者皆自均調若一也 (*wàn wù mò yǒu jiào lìng zhī zhě jiē zì jūn tiáo ruò yī yě* — "with no one instructing them, the myriad things all come into even accord, as one").

### Unit 5
> 始制有名，名亦既有，夫亦將知止，知止可以不殆。 (*shǐ zhì yǒu míng, míng yì jì yǒu, fū yì jiāng zhī zhǐ, zhī zhǐ kě yǐ bù dài* — "beginning to cut, there are names; names once existing, one also will know to stop; knowing to stop, one can be without danger")
- **Says:** When cutting begins there are names. Once names exist, one should also know where to stop, and knowing where to stop keeps one out of danger.
- **Act:** asserts; advises (implicitly)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; 夫 (*fū* — "that one / one") is either a pronoun for whoever holds names (likely the ruler) or a particle
- **Force:** will/future (將 *jiāng* — "will"); can (可以 *kě yǐ* — "can")
- **Link:** none (a new turn, from the nameless to names)
- **Open:** 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 殆 (*dài* — "danger"). 制 (*zhì* — "to cut, to tailor; to institute"). 亦將知止 (*yì jiāng zhī zhǐ* — "also will know to stop"): "will know to stop" or "must know to stop." **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 始制謂樸散始為官長之時也 (*shǐ zhì wèi pǔ sàn shǐ wéi guān zhǎng zhī shí yě* — "'beginning to cut' means the time when the block disperses and offices and chiefs are first set up"), and he reads 知止所以不殆 (*zhī zhǐ suǒ yǐ bù dài* — "knowing to stop is the way not to be endangered"). Heshang Gong makes 始 (*shǐ* — "the beginning") the subject: 始道也有名萬物也道無名能制於有名 (*shǐ dào yě, yǒu míng wàn wù yě, dào wú míng néng zhì yú yǒu míng* — "'the beginning' is the Tao; 'what has names' is the myriad things; the nameless Tao can rule the named"). His text then reads 天亦將知之 (*tiān yì jiāng zhī zhī* — "heaven too will know it").

### Unit 6
> 譬道之在天下，猶川谷之於江海。 (*pì dào zhī zài tiān xià, yóu chuān gǔ zhī yú jiāng hǎi* — "compare the Tao's being in all-under-heaven: like streams and valleys in relation to the rivers and sea")
- **Says:** The Tao's presence in the world is like the relation of streams and valleys to the rivers and the sea.
- **Act:** asserts (a simile)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 道 (*dào* — "the Tao"); 川谷 (*chuān gǔ* — "streams and valleys"); 江海 (*jiāng hǎi* — "rivers and sea")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** Which side the Tao stands on. (a) The Tao is to the world as the rivers and sea are to the streams: everything flows to it. (b) Following the word order, the Tao in the world is like the streams, which flow on toward the sea. **Commentators:** Wang Bi takes (a): 川谷之求江與海非江海召之 (*chuān gǔ zhī qiú jiāng yǔ hǎi fēi jiāng hǎi zhào zhī* — "streams and valleys seek the river and the sea; it is not that the river and sea summon them"), so things return without being called. Heshang Gong reads mutual flow: 如川谷與江海相流通也 (*rú chuān gǔ yǔ jiāng hǎi xiāng liú tōng yě* — "as streams and valleys flow through with the rivers and the sea").

# Chapter 33

**The chapter as a whole:** A string of definitions sets outward capacities against inward ones. Knowing others is cleverness and knowing oneself is clarity; overcoming others is force and overcoming oneself is strength. Contentment is wealth, persistence is will, keeping one's place is lasting, and dying without perishing is long life.

### Unit 1
> 知人者智，自知者明。 (*zhī rén zhě zhì, zì zhī zhě míng* — "one who knows others is clever; one who knows oneself is clear")
- **Says:** Knowing others is cleverness; knowing oneself is clarity.
- **Act:** names/defines (A 者 *zhě* — "one who," B)
- **Who acts / of whom:** anyone
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** whether 智 (*zhì* — "clever, knowing") is plain praise or a lesser good set beneath 明 (*míng* — "clear, illumined"). **Commentators:** Wang Bi ranks them: 未若自知者超智之上也 (*wèi ruò zì zhī zhě chāo zhì zhī shàng yě* — "it does not match the one who knows himself, who goes beyond cleverness").

### Unit 2
> 勝人者有力，自勝者強。 (*shèng rén zhě yǒu lì, zì shèng zhě qiáng* — "one who overcomes others has force; one who overcomes oneself is strong")
- **Says:** Overcoming others takes force; overcoming oneself is strength.
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** anyone
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong specifies the self overcome: 自勝巳情欲 (*zì shèng jǐ qíng yù* — "overcomes his own passions and desires").

### Unit 3
> 知足者富。 (*zhī zú zhě fù* — "one who knows enough is rich")
- **Says:** Whoever knows what is enough is rich.
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** anyone
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none

### Unit 4
> 強行者有志。 (*qiáng xíng zhě yǒu zhì* — "one who forces on in action has will")
- **Says:** Whoever presses on has will.
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** anyone
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 強行 (*qiáng xíng* — "act with force / strive"); 志 (*zhì* — "will, aim, ambition"). (a) Praise: "one who perseveres has resolve." (b) Read against "knows enough" in Unit 3: "one who forces his way has only ambition." **Commentators:** both read (a). Wang Bi: 勤能行之其志必獲 (*qín néng xíng zhī qí zhì bì huò* — "one diligent enough to carry it out surely attains his aim"). Heshang Gong: 強力行善 (*qiáng lì xíng shàn* — "strives with force to do good").

### Unit 5
> 不失其所者久。 (*bù shī qí suǒ zhě jiǔ* — "one who does not lose their place lasts")
- **Says:** Whoever does not lose their place endures.
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** anyone
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 不 (*bù* — "not") negates losing. 其所 (*qí suǒ* — "its place") is one's proper station, or what one rests on. **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 量力而行不失其所 (*liàng lì ér xíng bù shī qí suǒ* — "acting within one's strength, one does not lose one's place"). Heshang Gong: 不失其所受天之精氣 (*bù shī qí suǒ shòu tiān zhī jīng qì* — "does not lose the vital essence received from heaven").

### Unit 6
> 死而不亡者壽。 (*sǐ ér bù wáng zhě shòu* — "one who dies yet does not perish is long-lived")
- **Says:** Whoever dies without perishing is long-lived.
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** anyone
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 亡 (*wáng* — "perish, be gone"). (a) The body dies but something lasts. (b) A practical long life. **Commentators:** Wang Bi takes (a): 身沒而道猶存 (*shēn mò ér dào yóu cún* — "the body is gone, yet the Tao still remains"). Heshang Gong takes (b): 目不妄視耳不妄聽口不妄言則无怨惡於天下故長壽 (*mù bù wàng shì, ěr bù wàng tīng, kǒu bù wàng yán, zé wú yuàn wù yú tiān xià, gù cháng shòu* — "eyes not looking wildly, ears not listening wildly, mouth not speaking wildly, there is no resentment toward one in the world, hence long life").

# Chapter 34

**The chapter as a whole:** The great Tao floods out in every direction. All things depend on it to live and it turns none away; it finishes its work and claims nothing, and it clothes and feeds all things without making itself their master. Being without desire, it can be called small; being the place all things return to without mastering them, it can be called great. Because it never makes itself great, it can complete its greatness.

### Unit 1
> 大道汎兮，其可左右。 (*dà dào fàn xī, qí kě zuǒ yòu* — "the great Tao overflows, ah! it can go left and right")
- **Says:** The great Tao flows out everywhere and can go left or right.
- **Act:** asserts (exclamatory: 兮 *xī* — "ah")
- **Who acts / of whom:** 大道 (*dà dào* — "the great Tao")
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 汎 (*fàn* — "overflowing, drifting, floating"). 其可左右 (*qí kě zuǒ yòu* — "it can left-right"): (a) it can reach to left and right, it goes everywhere; (b) it can be turned to left or right, it is pliable. **Commentators:** Wang Bi takes (a): 無所不適 (*wú suǒ bù shì* — "there is nowhere it does not go"). Heshang Gong is closer to (b): 无所不宜 (*wú suǒ bù yí* — "there is nowhere it does not suit").

### Unit 2
> 萬物恃之以生而不辭，功成而不名有。 (*wàn wù shì zhī yǐ shēng ér bù cí, gōng chéng ér bù míng yǒu* — "the myriad things rely on it to live, and it does not decline; its work done, it does not name having")
- **Says:** All things depend on it to live and it turns none away; when its work is done it claims nothing as its own.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 萬物 (*wàn wù* — "the myriad things") rely on 之 (*zhī* — "it," the Tao); the Tao is the subject of 不辭 (*bù cí* — "does not decline") and 不名有 (*bù míng yǒu* — "does not name having")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 不 (*bù* — "not") negates actions, twice. 辭 (*cí* — "to decline; to speak; to take leave"): (a) it does not refuse them; (b) it says nothing about it. 不名有 (*bù míng yǒu* — "not name having"): it does not call the work its own, or it does not take the name of possessing. **Commentators:** Heshang Gong takes (a): 道不辭謝而逆止也 (*dào bù cí xiè ér nì zhǐ yě* — "the Tao does not decline them and turn them back"), and 有道不名其有功也 (*yǒu dào bù míng qí yǒu gōng yě* — "the Tao does not claim the name of having merit"). Wang Bi's lemma reads 而生 (*ér shēng* — "and live") for 以生 (*yǐ shēng* — "to live").

### Unit 3
> 衣養萬物而不為主，常無欲，可名於小； (*yī yǎng wàn wù ér bù wéi zhǔ, cháng wú yù, kě míng yú xiǎo* — "it clothes and nourishes the myriad things and does not act as master; constantly without desire, it can be named among the small")
- **Says:** It clothes and feeds all things without making itself their master; always without desire, it can be called small.
- **Act:** asserts; names/defines (可名 *kě míng* — "can be named")
- **Who acts / of whom:** the Tao; 萬物 (*wàn wù* — "the myriad things") are clothed and fed
- **Force:** can/possible; always (常 *cháng* — "constantly")
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 不 (*bù* — "not") negates acting as master; 無 (*wú* — "without") negates having desire. 不為主 (*bù wéi zhǔ* — "not act as master"): "does not act as master" or "does not become master." Heshang Gong's text reads 愛養 (*ài yǎng* — "loves and nourishes") for 衣養 (*yī yǎng* — "clothes and nourishes"). **Commentators:** on why it is "small": Wang Bi: 若道無施於物故名於小矣 (*ruò dào wú shī yú wù gù míng yú xiǎo yǐ* — "it is as though the Tao bestows nothing on things, so it is named small"). Heshang Gong: 道匿德藏名恒然無為似若微小也 (*dào nì dé cáng míng héng rán wú wéi sì ruò wēi xiǎo yě* — "the Tao hides its virtue and conceals its name, constantly not acting, so it seems tiny").

### Unit 4
> 萬物歸焉而不為主，可名為大。 (*wàn wù guī yān ér bù wéi zhǔ, kě míng wéi dà* — "the myriad things return to it and it does not act as master; it can be named great")
- **Says:** All things return to it and it does not make itself their master; it can be called great.
- **Act:** asserts; names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** 萬物 (*wàn wù* — "the myriad things") return to it (焉 *yān* — "to it, there"); the Tao does not master them
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** and (set against "small")
- **Open:** 不 (*bù* — "not") negates acting as master. Wang Bi's editor notes a Yongle text with 不知主 (*bù zhī zhǔ* — "does not know itself as master").

### Unit 5
> 以其終不自為大，故能成其大。 (*yǐ qí zhōng bù zì wéi dà, gù néng chéng qí dà* — "because it never in the end makes itself great, therefore it can complete its greatness")
- **Says:** Because it never makes itself great, it can complete its greatness.
- **Act:** asserts (because … therefore)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 其 (*qí* — "it"), the Tao in the base text
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** because … therefore (以 *yǐ* — "because"; 故 *gù* — "therefore")
- **Open:** 不 (*bù* — "not") negates making oneself great. The subject differs by text. **Commentators:** Heshang Gong's text reads 是以聖人終不為大 (*shì yǐ shèng rén zhōng bù wéi dà* — "therefore the sage in the end does not act great"), which makes the sage the subject: 聖人法道匿德藏名不為滿大 (*shèng rén fǎ dào nì dé cáng míng bù wéi mǎn dà* — "the sage takes the Tao as model, hides virtue, conceals name, and does not make himself full and great"). Wang Bi's editor reports a Yongle text that adds 是以聖人能成其大也 (*shì yǐ shèng rén néng chéng qí dà yě* — "therefore the sage can complete his greatness").

# Chapter 35

**The chapter as a whole:** Whoever holds the great image draws the world to them, and those who come are not harmed but find peace. Music and food stop the passing traveler, while the Tao, put into words, is bland and has no taste. It is not enough to see and not enough to hear, yet using it never exhausts it.

### Unit 1
> 執大象，天下往。 (*zhí dà xiàng, tiān xià wǎng* — "hold the great image, and all-under-heaven goes")
- **Says:** When the great image is held, the world goes to whoever holds it.
- **Act:** if/when … then (implicit); possibly a command ("hold the great image")
- **Who acts / of whom:** the holder is unstated: the ruler or the sage; 天下 (*tiān xià* — "all-under-heaven") goes
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 大象 (*dà xiàng* — "great image, great form"). 往 (*wǎng* — "to go"): (a) the world goes to the holder; (b) holding the great image, one goes throughout the world. **Commentators:** both take (a). Heshang Gong: 象道也聖人守大道則天下萬民移心歸往之也 (*xiàng dào yě, shèng rén shǒu dà dào zé tiān xià wàn mín yí xīn guī wǎng zhī yě* — "'the image' is the Tao; when the sage keeps the great Tao, the myriad people of the world turn their hearts and go to him"). Wang Bi: 大象天象之母也 … 主若執之則天下徃也 (*dà xiàng tiān xiàng zhī mǔ yě … zhǔ ruò zhí zhī zé tiān xià wǎng yě* — "the great image is the mother of heaven's images … if the ruler holds it, the world goes to him").

### Unit 2
> 往而不害，安平太。 (*wǎng ér bù hài, ān píng tài* — "going and not harmed: safe, level, grand")
- **Says:** Those who go are not harmed: there is safety, peace and great calm.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** those who go (the world)
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 不 (*bù* — "not") negates harm. 不害 (*bù hài* — "not harm"): "are not harmed" or "do no harm." 安平太 (*ān píng tài* — "safe, level, grand"): (a) a list, "safety, peace, great calm"; (b) 安 (*ān* — "safe; thereupon") as "thereupon": "then comes level calm." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong's text reads 安太平 (*ān tài píng* — "safety and great peace"), glossed 國安家寧而致太平矣 (*guó ān jiā níng ér zhì tài píng yǐ* — "the state is secure, families at rest, and great peace arrives"). Wang Bi: 萬物得徃而不害妨也 (*wàn wù dé wǎng ér bù hài fáng yě* — "the myriad things can go and are not harmed or hindered").

### Unit 3
> 樂與餌，過客止。 (*yuè yǔ ěr, guò kè zhǐ* — "music and food: the passing guest stops")
- **Says:** Music and food make the passing traveler stop.
- **Act:** asserts (describes)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 過客 (*guò kè* — "passing guest")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none (sets up the contrast in Unit 4)
- **Open:** 樂 (*yuè* — "music" / *lè* — "delight"). **Commentators:** Wang Bi takes music and food literally, as what pleases people for a moment: 樂與餌應時感恱人心也 (*yuè yǔ ěr yìng shí gǎn yuè rén xīn yě* — "music and food answer the moment and please people's hearts"). Heshang Gong reads an allegory: 餌美也過客一也 (*ěr měi yě, guò kè yī yě* — "ěr is 'beauty'; the passing guest is the One"), and 人能樂美於道則一留止也 (*rén néng lè měi yú dào zé yī liú zhǐ yě* — "if a person can delight in the beauty of the Tao, the One will stay").

### Unit 4
> 道之出口，淡乎其無味。 (*dào zhī chū kǒu, dàn hū qí wú wèi* — "the Tao's coming out of the mouth: bland, how it has no flavor!")
- **Says:** When the Tao comes out of the mouth it is bland and has no taste at all.
- **Act:** asserts (exclamatory: 乎 *hū* — "how!")
- **Who acts / of whom:** 道 (*dào* — "the Tao")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** but (against Unit 3)
- **Open:** 無 (*wú* — "without") negates the existence of flavor. 道之出口 (*dào zhī chū kǒu* — "the Tao's exiting the mouth"): the Tao put into words, or the Tao as a taste in the mouth. **Commentators:** Wang Bi takes words: 道之出言淡然無味 (*dào zhī chū yán dàn rán wú wèi* — "the Tao's issuing in words is bland and tasteless"). Heshang Gong takes taste: 道出入於口淡淡非如五味 (*dào chū rù yú kǒu dàn dàn fēi rú wǔ wèi* — "the Tao going in and out of the mouth is bland, unlike the five flavors").

### Unit 5
> 視之不足見，聽之不足聞，用之不可既。 (*shì zhī bù zú jiàn, tīng zhī bù zú wén, yòng zhī bù kě jì* — "look at it, not enough to see; listen to it, not enough to hear; use it, it cannot be used up")
- **Says:** Look for it and there is not enough to see; listen for it and not enough to hear; use it and it cannot be used up.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: anyone; 之 (*zhī* — "it") is the Tao
- **Force:** can/possible (negated)
- **Link:** and; but (the last clause turns)
- **Open:** 不足 (*bù zú* — "not sufficient to"), twice, and 不可 (*bù kě* — "cannot"), with 不 (*bù* — "not") negating possibility. 不足見 (*bù zú jiàn* — "not enough to see"): "too faint to be seen" or "not worth seeing." **Commentators:** Wang Bi takes the second: 不足以恱其目 (*bù zú yǐ yuè qí mù* — "not enough to please the eye"). His text reads 用之不足既 (*yòng zhī bù zú jì* — "using it is not enough to use it up"). Heshang Gong takes formlessness: 道無形非若五色 … 可得見也 (*dào wú xíng fēi ruò wǔ sè … kě dé jiàn yě* — "the Tao has no form; it is not like the five colors, which can be seen").

# Chapter 36

**The chapter as a whole:** Four paired sayings: whatever is to shrink must first be stretched, and likewise weakening, discarding and taking are preceded by strengthening, raising and giving. This is called subtle clarity, and the soft and weak overcome the hard and strong. A fish must not leave the deep, and the state's sharp instruments must not be shown to people.

### Unit 1
> 將欲歙之，必固張之； (*jiāng yù xì zhī, bì gù zhāng zhī* — "about to want to shrink it, one must surely first stretch it")
- **Says:** Whatever is to be shrunk must first be stretched.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: one who wants an outcome; 之 (*zhī* — "it") is unspecified, a thing or an opponent
- **Force:** must/necessary (必 *bì* — "must")
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** (a) A strategy: deliberately swell an opponent so that he collapses. (b) A description of reversal: what is about to contract has first expanded, with 將欲 (*jiāng yù* — "about to want") read as "is about to" rather than "wants to." 固 (*gù* — "certainly; first"). **Commentators:** Heshang Gong and Han Fei take (a). Heshang Gong: 先開張之者欲極其奢淫 (*xiān kāi zhāng zhī zhě yù jí qí shē yín* — "first spreading him open is to drive his extravagance to its limit"). Han Fei tells of the king of Yue letting Wu win, and of 張之於江、濟 (*zhāng zhī yú jiāng, jì* — "stretching it at the Jiang and the Ji rivers") before Wu was brought down. Wang Bi makes it a way of removing the violent by following things' nature: 因物之性令其自戮 (*yīn wù zhī xìng lìng qí zì lù* — "following the nature of things, letting them destroy themselves").

### Unit 2
> 將欲弱之，必固強之； (*jiāng yù ruò zhī, bì gù qiáng zhī* — "about to want to weaken it, one must surely first strengthen it")
- **Says:** Whatever is to be weakened must first be strengthened.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** as in Unit 1
- **Force:** must/necessary
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** as in Unit 1. Heshang Gong: 先強大之者欲使遇禍患 (*xiān qiáng dà zhī zhě yù shǐ yù huò huàn* — "first making him strong and great is to make him meet disaster").

### Unit 3
> 將欲廢之，必固興之； (*jiāng yù fèi zhī, bì gù xīng zhī* — "about to want to discard it, one must surely first raise it")
- **Says:** Whatever is to be cast down must first be raised up.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** as in Unit 1
- **Force:** must/necessary
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** as in Unit 1

### Unit 4
> 將欲奪之，必固與之。 (*jiāng yù duó zhī, bì gù yǔ zhī* — "about to want to seize it, one must surely first give to it")
- **Says:** Whatever is to be taken must first be given.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** as in Unit 1
- **Force:** must/necessary
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** as in Unit 1. Han Fei's text reads 將欲取之，必固與之 (*jiāng yù qǔ zhī, bì gù yǔ zhī* — "if you would take it, you must first give to it") and illustrates it with gifts sent ahead of an attack.

### Unit 5
> 是謂微明。 (*shì wèi wēi míng* — "this is called subtle clarity")
- **Says:** This is called subtle clarity.
- **Act:** names/defines (是謂 *shì wèi* — "this is called")
- **Who acts / of whom:** 是 (*shì* — "this") is the four sayings
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (sums up)
- **Open:** 微明 (*wēi míng* — "subtle + bright"): "subtle insight," "hidden light," or "subtle in method, clear in result." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong takes the last: 其道微其效明也 (*qí dào wēi qí xiào míng yě* — "its way is subtle, its effect is plain"). Han Fei: 起事於無形 (*qǐ shì yú wú xíng* — "starting affairs from the formless").

### Unit 6
> 柔弱勝剛強。 (*róu ruò shèng gāng qiáng* — "the soft and weak overcome the hard and strong")
- **Says:** The soft and weak overcome the hard and strong.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 柔弱 (*róu ruò* — "the soft and weak") and 剛強 (*gāng qiáng* — "the hard and strong")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (or because: the principle behind the four sayings)
- **Open:** none. Wang Bi's editor notes a Yongle text with 柔勝剛弱勝强 (*róu shèng gāng, ruò shèng qiáng* — "soft overcomes hard, weak overcomes strong").

### Unit 7
> 魚不可脫於淵，國之利器不可以示人。 (*yú bù kě tuō yú yuān, guó zhī lì qì bù kě yǐ shì rén* — "fish cannot leave the deep pool; the state's sharp implements cannot be shown to people")
- **Says:** A fish must not leave the deep water; the state's sharp instruments must not be shown to people.
- **Act:** advises (or asserts)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 魚 (*yú* — "fish"); 國之利器 (*guó zhī lì qì* — "the state's sharp implements"); 人 (*rén* — "people")
- **Force:** must/necessary, or can (negated)
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 不可 (*bù kě* — "cannot / must not"), twice, with 不 (*bù* — "not") negating possibility or permission. What the 利器 (*lì qì* — "sharp/advantageous implements") are. **Commentators:** Han Fei: 賞罰者，邦之利器也 (*shǎng fá zhě, bāng zhī lì qì yě* — "rewards and punishments are the state's sharp instruments"), and the deep pool is 勢重者，人君之淵也 (*shì zhòng zhě, rén jūn zhī yuān yě* — "the weight of power is the ruler's deep pool"). Heshang Gong: 利器權道也 (*lì qì quán dào yě* — "the sharp instruments are the methods of power"), not to be shown to ministers. Wang Bi reverses the emphasis: the instrument is following things' nature unseen, and 示人者任刑也 (*shì rén zhě rèn xíng yě* — "'showing them to people' means relying on punishment"), which fails.

# Chapter 37

**The chapter as a whole:** The Tao always does nothing, and yet nothing is left undone. If rulers keep to it, things transform of themselves. If desire stirs as they transform, the speaker will settle it with the nameless uncarved block, which brings an end to desire. Without desire there is stillness, and the world sets itself right.

### Unit 1
> 道常無為而無不為。 (*dào cháng wú wéi ér wú bù wéi* — "the Tao constantly does nothing and yet there is nothing it does not do")
- **Says:** The Tao always does nothing, and yet there is nothing it leaves undone.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 道 (*dào* — "the Tao")
- **Force:** always (常 *cháng* — "constantly")
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 無 (*wú* — "no") negates action; 無不 (*wú bù* — "nothing … not") is a double negative, "nothing left undone." **Commentators:** Wang Bi reads the second half as what things do through it: 萬物無不由為以治以成之也 (*wàn wù wú bù yóu wéi yǐ zhì yǐ chéng zhī yě* — "of the myriad things there are none that are not ordered and completed through it"). Heshang Gong: 道以無為為常也 (*dào yǐ wú wéi wéi cháng yě* — "the Tao takes non-action as its constant").

### Unit 2
> 侯王若能守之，萬物將自化。 (*hóu wáng ruò néng shǒu zhī, wàn wù jiāng zì huà* — "if lords and kings can keep it, the myriad things will transform of themselves")
- **Says:** If lords and kings can keep to it, all things will transform of their own accord.
- **Act:** if/when … then (若 *ruò* — "if")
- **Who acts / of whom:** 侯王 (*hóu wáng* — "lords and kings"); 萬物 (*wàn wù* — "the myriad things")
- **Force:** will/future (將 *jiāng* — "will")
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 自化 (*zì huà* — "transform themselves / be transformed of themselves"). **Commentators:** Heshang Gong adds a model: 萬物將自化效於己也 (*wàn wù jiāng zì huà xiào yú jǐ yě* — "the myriad things will transform of themselves, imitating him").

### Unit 3
> 化而欲作，吾將鎮之以無名之樸。 (*huà ér yù zuò, wú jiāng zhèn zhī yǐ wú míng zhī pǔ* — "transforming, if desire arises, I will press it down with the nameless uncarved block")
- **Says:** If desire stirs while they are transforming, the speaker will settle it with the nameless uncarved block.
- **Act:** if/when … then; declares an intention
- **Who acts / of whom:** 吾 (*wú* — "I"); 之 (*zhī* — "it / them"): the desire, or the stirring things
- **Force:** will/future (將 *jiāng* — "will")
- **Link:** but (a complication)
- **Open:** 欲作 (*yù zuò* — "desire arise / want to act"): (a) "desire arises," with 欲 (*yù* — "desire") as subject; (b) "they wish to act / rise up," with 欲 (*yù* — "want to") as auxiliary. 鎮 (*zhèn* — "to weigh down, settle, pacify"): suppress, or steady. 無 (*wú* — "without") negates having a name. Who 吾 (*wú* — "I") is: the speaker, or the ruler speaking. **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 作欲成也 (*zuò yù chéng yě* — "zuò means desire coming to fruition"). Heshang Gong makes the ruler the agent: 吾身也 (*wú shēn yě* — "'I' means the self"), and 侯王當身鎮撫以道德 (*hóu wáng dāng shēn zhèn fǔ yǐ dào dé* — "the lord or king should himself settle and soothe them with the Tao and virtue").

### Unit 4
> 無名之樸，夫亦將無欲。 (*wú míng zhī pǔ, fū yì jiāng wú yù* — "the nameless uncarved block: it, too, will be without desire")
- **Says:** The nameless uncarved block will itself bring desirelessness.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 夫 (*fū* — "that / it / they"): the block itself, or the things and people it settles
- **Force:** will/future
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 無 (*wú* — "without") negates desire. Who is without desire: the block (it has none), or those it settles (they come to have none). **Commentators:** Wang Bi's editor notes that Heshang Gong's and other texts lack 夫 (*fū* — "it") and read 不欲 (*bù yù* — "not desire"). Heshang Gong makes the people the subject: 民亦將不欲 (*mín yì jiāng bù yù* — "the people too will not desire"). Wang Bi: 無欲競也 (*wú yù jìng yě* — "without desire to compete").

### Unit 5
> 不欲以靜，天下將自正。 (*bù yù yǐ jìng, tiān xià jiāng zì zhèng* — "not desiring, thereby still, all-under-heaven will right itself")
- **Says:** Without desire, and so still, the world will set itself right.
- **Act:** if/when … then (implicit)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the one without desire is unstated (the ruler, the people, or the block); 天下 (*tiān xià* — "all-under-heaven") rights itself
- **Force:** will/future
- **Link:** therefore/so
- **Open:** 不 (*bù* — "not") negates desiring. 以 (*yǐ* — "by, thereby"): "and thereby (become) still" or "by means of stillness." 自正 (*zì zhèng* — "rights itself") against 自定 (*zì dìng* — "settles itself") in both Wang Bi's and Heshang Gong's lemmas; Heshang Gong combines them: 天下將自正定也 (*tiān xià jiāng zì zhèng dìng yě* — "the world will right and settle itself").

# Chapter 38

**The chapter as a whole:** The highest virtue does not hold on to virtue and so has it; the lower virtue clings to it and so lacks it. A ranked descent runs from virtue to humaneness, duty and ritual. Each acts with more purpose than the last, until ritual, unanswered, forces people into line. Ritual is the thinning of loyalty and trust and the start of disorder, and foreknowledge is the Tao's flower and the start of folly. So the great person keeps to the thick and the fruit, not the thin and the flower.

### Unit 1
> 上德不德，是以有德； (*shàng dé bù dé, shì yǐ yǒu dé* — "high virtue does not 'virtue,' therefore it has virtue")
- **Says:** The highest virtue does not treat itself as virtue, and so it has virtue.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 上德 (*shàng dé* — "high virtue"), the quality or a person or ruler who has it
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none; inside the unit, therefore (是以 *shì yǐ* — "for this reason")
- **Open:** 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 德 (*dé* — "virtue, potency") used as a verb. 不德 (*bù dé* — "not virtue"): (a) does not claim or show itself as virtue; (b) does not grasp after gain, punning 德 (*dé* — "virtue") with 得 (*dé* — "to get"). **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 不徳其徳 (*bù dé qí dé* — "does not 'virtue' its virtue"). Heshang Gong: 上德謂太古無名號之君 (*shàng dé wèi tài gǔ wú míng hào zhī jūn* — "high virtue means the rulers of high antiquity who had no names or titles"), and 不以德教民 (*bù yǐ dé jiào mín* — "they did not teach the people by virtue"), so that 其德不見 (*qí dé bù jiàn* — "their virtue was not seen"). Han Fei: 德者，得身也 (*dé zhě, dé shēn yě* — "virtue is getting [keeping] the self").

### Unit 2
> 下德不失德，是以無德。 (*xià dé bù shī dé, shì yǐ wú dé* — "low virtue does not lose virtue, therefore it has no virtue")
- **Says:** The lower virtue never lets go of virtue, and so it has no virtue.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 下德 (*xià dé* — "low virtue")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (the contrast); inside the unit, therefore (是以 *shì yǐ*)
- **Open:** 不 (*bù* — "not") negates losing; 無 (*wú* — "has no") negates having. 不失德 (*bù shī dé* — "not lose virtue"): clings anxiously to virtue, or never fails in visible virtue. **Commentators:** Heshang Gong takes the second: 其德可見其功可稱也 (*qí dé kě jiàn qí gōng kě chēng yě* — "its virtue can be seen, its merit can be praised").

### Unit 3
> 上德無為而無以為； (*shàng dé wú wéi ér wú yǐ wéi* — "high virtue does nothing and has nothing by which/for which it acts")
- **Says:** The highest virtue does not act, and has nothing it acts for.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 上德 (*shàng dé* — "high virtue")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 無 (*wú* — "no"), twice. 無以為 (*wú yǐ wéi* — "nothing by/for which to act"): (a) "has no purpose to act for"; (b) "has nothing by which it acts." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 言無以名號為 (*yán wú yǐ míng hào wéi* — "it does not act for name or title"). Wang Bi: 無以為者無所徧為也 (*wú yǐ wéi zhě wú suǒ piān wéi yě* — "'wú yǐ wéi' means it does nothing one-sidedly"). Han Fei's text reads 上德無爲而無不爲也 (*shàng dé wú wéi ér wú bù wéi yě* — "high virtue does nothing, and yet there is nothing it does not do").

### Unit 4
> 下德為之而有以為。 (*xià dé wéi zhī ér yǒu yǐ wéi* — "low virtue acts and has something for which it acts")
- **Says:** The lower virtue acts, and has a purpose it acts for.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 下德 (*xià dé* — "low virtue")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (the contrast)
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong: 言以為己取名號也 (*yán yǐ wèi jǐ qǔ míng hào yě* — "it means acting to win names and titles for oneself").

### Unit 5
> 上仁為之而無以為； (*shàng rén wéi zhī ér wú yǐ wéi* — "high humaneness acts and has nothing for which it acts")
- **Says:** The highest humaneness acts, but has no purpose it acts for.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 上仁 (*shàng rén* — "high humaneness")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 無 (*wú* — "no") negates having a purpose. **Commentators:** Han Fei: 生心之所不能已也，非求其報也 (*shēng xīn zhī suǒ bù néng yǐ yě, fēi qiú qí bào yě* — "it springs from what the heart cannot help; it does not seek a return").

### Unit 6
> 上義為之而有以為。 (*shàng yì wéi zhī ér yǒu yǐ wéi* — "high duty acts and has something for which it acts")
- **Says:** The highest duty acts, and has a purpose it acts for.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 上義 (*shàng yì* — "high duty, rightness")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none. Han Fei: 義者，謂其宜也，宜而爲之 (*yì zhě, wèi qí yí yě, yí ér wéi zhī* — "duty means what is fitting; it is done because it is fitting").

### Unit 7
> 上禮為之而莫之應，則攘臂而扔之。 (*shàng lǐ wéi zhī ér mò zhī yìng, zé rǎng bì ér rēng zhī* — "high ritual acts and no one answers it, then it bares its arm and yanks them")
- **Says:** The highest ritual acts, and when no one responds, it rolls up its sleeves and drags them into line.
- **Act:** if/when … then (則 *zé* — "then")
- **Who acts / of whom:** 上禮 (*shàng lǐ* — "high ritual") acts; the unresponsive are 之 (*zhī* — "them")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 莫 (*mò* — "no one") negates the agent of responding. 扔 (*rēng* — "to pull, drag, throw"); Wang Bi's editor notes 各本俱作仍 (*gè běn jù zuò réng* — "all editions read 仍 *réng*, 'to follow, persist'"). **Commentators:** Heshang Gong reads mutual strife: 上下忿爭故攘臂相仍引 (*shàng xià fèn zhēng gù rǎng bì xiāng réng yǐn* — "superiors and inferiors quarrel in anger, so they bare their arms and pull at one another"). Han Fei reads the sage persisting in ritual even when it is not answered: 聖人之復恭敬盡手足之禮也不衰 (*shèng rén zhī fù gōng jìng jìn shǒu zú zhī lǐ yě bù shuāi* — "the sage's renewed reverence, carrying out the rites with hands and feet, does not slacken").

### Unit 8
> 故失道而後德，失德而後仁，失仁而後義，失義而後禮。 (*gù shī dào ér hòu dé, shī dé ér hòu rén, shī rén ér hòu yì, shī yì ér hòu lǐ* — "so, the Tao lost, afterwards virtue; virtue lost, afterwards humaneness; humaneness lost, afterwards duty; duty lost, afterwards ritual")
- **Says:** So when the Tao is lost, virtue follows; when virtue is lost, humaneness; when humaneness is lost, duty; when duty is lost, ritual.
- **Act:** asserts (a sequence)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: the world, or a people and its rulers
- **Force:** past/habitual
- **Link:** therefore/so (故 *gù* — "therefore")
- **Open:** **Commentators:** Han Fei's text reads 失道而後失德 (*shī dào ér hòu shī dé* — "the Tao lost, then virtue lost") at every step: a chain of cumulative losses, with each term derived from the one above (德者，道之功 *dé zhě, dào zhī gōng* — "virtue is the Tao's achievement"). The base gives a substitution: each term appears once the one above it is lost.

### Unit 9
> 夫禮者，忠信之薄，而亂之首。 (*fū lǐ zhě, zhōng xìn zhī bó, ér luàn zhī shǒu* — "ritual is the thinness of loyalty and trust, and the head of disorder")
- **Says:** Ritual is the thinning of loyalty and trust and the beginning of disorder.
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** 禮 (*lǐ* — "ritual")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (夫 *fū* — "now")
- **Open:** 首 (*shǒu* — "head"): "beginning" or "chief." Han Fei's text ends with 乎 (*hū* — question particle), making it rhetorical.

### Unit 10
> 前識者，道之華，而愚之始。 (*qián shí zhě, dào zhī huá, ér yú zhī shǐ* — "fore-knowing is the flower of the Tao and the beginning of folly")
- **Says:** Foreknowledge is the flower of the Tao and the beginning of folly.
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** 前識 (*qián shí* — "fore-knowing"), or 前識者 (*qián shí zhě* — "those who fore-know")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** What 前識 (*qián shí* — "fore-knowing") is. **Commentators:** Han Fei: 先物行先理動之謂前識 (*xiān wù xíng xiān lǐ dòng zhī wèi qián shí* — "going ahead of things, moving ahead of principle, is called fore-knowing"), 無緣而妄意度也 (*wú yuán ér wàng yì duó yě* — "groundless, reckless guessing"). Heshang Gong: 不知而言知為前識 (*bù zhī ér yán zhī wéi qián shí* — "to say one knows when one does not is fore-knowing"). Wang Bi: 前人而識也 (*qián rén ér shí yě* — "knowing ahead of others"). 華 (*huá* — "flower") is a showy surface, a blossom without fruit. Han Fei's text reads 愚之首 (*yú zhī shǒu* — "the head of folly").

# Chapter 39

**The chapter as a whole:** Long ago, heaven, earth, the spirits, the valley, all things and the rulers each got the One and through it became clear, settled, potent, full, alive, and the world's standard. Without what makes them so, each would crack, burst, fade, run dry, die out or fall. So the noble is rooted in the lowly and the high in the low, which is why rulers call themselves by humble names. Praise sought is praise lost, and one should not want to shine like jade or clatter like stone.

### Unit 1

> 天無以清，將恐裂 (*tiān wú yǐ qīng, jiāng kǒng liè* — "heaven lacking that by which it is clear would, one fears, split")

Heshang Gong takes (b): 不可但欲清明無巳時將恐分裂 (*bù kě dàn yù qīng míng wú yǐ shí jiāng kǒng fēn liè* — "it cannot just want to be clear with no time of stopping, or it will split").

- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: one, most likely the ruler
- **Force:** always/universal

Wang Bi: 玉石琭琭珞珞體盡於形故不欲也 (*yù shí lù lù luò luò tǐ jìn yú xíng gù bù yù yě* — "jade gleaming and stone rough have their whole being in their form, so he wants neither").

# Chapter 40

**The chapter as a whole:** Two short statements. Return is how the Tao moves and weakness is how it works. All things are born from what is, and what is is born from what is not.

### Unit 1
> 反者道之動 (*fǎn zhě dào zhī dòng* — "returning is the movement of the Tao")
- **Says:** Return, or reversal, is how the Tao moves. (The base has no punctuation after 動 *dòng* — "movement.")
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** 道 (*dào* — "the Tao")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 反 (*fǎn* — "to return; to reverse; the opposite"). (a) Turning into the opposite. (b) Returning to the root. **Commentators:** Wang Bi takes (a): 髙以下為基貴以賤為本有以無為用此其反也 (*gāo yǐ xià wéi jī, guì yǐ jiàn wéi běn, yǒu yǐ wú wéi yòng, cǐ qí fǎn yě* — "the high takes the low as foundation, the noble the lowly as root, being takes non-being as its use: this is its reversal"). Heshang Gong takes (b): 反本也 (*fǎn běn yě* — "fǎn is the root / returning to the root").

### Unit 2
> 弱者道之用。 (*ruò zhě dào zhī yòng* — "weakness is the use of the Tao")
- **Says:** Weakness is how the Tao works.
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** 道 (*dào* — "the Tao")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 用 (*yòng* — "use, function"): "what the Tao uses," or "how the Tao functions." Heshang Gong takes the first: 柔弱者道之所常用 (*róu ruò zhě dào zhī suǒ cháng yòng* — "the soft and weak is what the Tao constantly uses").

### Unit 3
> 天下萬物生於有，有生於無。 (*tiān xià wàn wù shēng yú yǒu, yǒu shēng yú wú* — "the myriad things of all-under-heaven are born from there-being; there-being is born from there-not-being")
- **Says:** All things in the world are born from what is, and what is is born from what is not.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 萬物 (*wàn wù* — "the myriad things"); 有 (*yǒu* — "being, what is")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 無 (*wú* — "non-being, what is not") is a noun here, not a negator. **Commentators:** Heshang Gong makes it concrete: 天地有形位故言生於有 (*tiān dì yǒu xíng wèi gù yán shēng yú yǒu* — "heaven and earth have form and place, so it says 'born from being'"), and 道无形故言生於无 (*dào wú xíng gù yán shēng yú wú* — "the Tao has no form, so it says 'born from non-being'"). Wang Bi keeps it abstract: 有之所始以無為本 (*yǒu zhī suǒ shǐ yǐ wú wéi běn* — "where being begins, it takes non-being as its root").

# Chapter 41

**The chapter as a whole:** Hearing the Tao, the higher scholar practices it, the middling one half-keeps it, and the lower one laughs, and without that laughter it would not be the Tao. Established sayings set out how each great quality looks like its opposite, and how the greatest forms are without corners, late, faint or formless. The Tao is hidden and nameless, and only the Tao is good at giving and completing.

### Unit 1
> 上士聞道，勤而行之； (*shàng shì wén dào, qín ér xíng zhī* — "the higher scholar hears the Tao and diligently practices it")
- **Says:** When the higher scholar hears the Tao, they put it into practice diligently.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 上士 (*shàng shì* — "higher scholar, officer"); 之 (*zhī* — "it") is the Tao
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none

### Unit 2
> 中士聞道，若存若亡； (*zhōng shì wén dào, ruò cún ruò wáng* — "the middling scholar hears the Tao: as if keeping it, as if losing it")
- **Says:** When the middling scholar hears the Tao, they half keep it and half lose it.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 中士 (*zhōng shì* — "middling scholar")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 若存若亡 (*ruò cún ruò wáng* — "as if present, as if gone"): (a) they keep it, then lose it, wavering; (b) to them the Tao seems to exist and not to exist, that is, they half believe it. **Commentators:** Heshang Gong takes (a): 欣然而存之退見財色榮譽或於情欲而復亡之也 (*xīn rán ér cún zhī, tuì jiàn cái sè róng yù, huò yú qíng yù ér fù wáng zhī yě* — "gladly they keep it, but when they withdraw and see wealth, beauty, glory and fame, they are led astray by desire and lose it again").

- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none

### Unit 4
> 不笑不足以為道。 (*bù xiào bù zú yǐ wéi dào* — "not laughed at, it would not be enough to be the Tao")
- **Says:** If it were not laughed at, it would not deserve to be the Tao.
- **Act:** if/when … then (implicit)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the laughers (the lower scholars); the subject of 為道 (*wéi dào* — "to be the Tao") is "it"
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (draws the point)
- **Open:** 不 (*bù* — "not") negates laughing; 不足 (*bù zú* — "not enough") negates fitness. Heshang Gong: 不為下士所笑不足以名為道 (*bù wéi xià shì suǒ xiào bù zú yǐ míng wéi dào* — "if it were not laughed at by the lower scholar, it would not deserve the name Tao").

- **Says:** So the established sayings put it this way:
- **Act:** names/defines (introduces a quotation)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 建言 (*jiàn yán* — "established sayings")
- **Force:** past/habitual
- **Link:** therefore/so (故 *gù*)
- **Open:** 建言 (*jiàn yán* — "established sayings") may be the title of a collection of sayings, or just "set sayings." **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 建猶立也 (*jiàn yóu lì yě* — "jiàn is like 'to establish'"). Heshang Gong: 建設也設言以有道當如下句 (*jiàn shè yě, shè yán yǐ yǒu dào dāng rú xià jù* — "jiàn means 'to set up'; it sets out sayings to show that one who has the Tao should be like the lines below").

### Unit 6
> 明道若昧，進道若退，夷道若纇。 (*míng dào ruò mèi, jìn dào ruò tuì, yí dào ruò lèi* — "the bright way seems dim, the advancing way seems retreating, the level way seems knotted")
- **Says:** The bright way looks dark, the way forward looks like retreat, and the level way looks rough.
- **Act:** asserts (quoted sayings)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 道 (*dào* — "way, path"), three times
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (the quotation begins)
- **Open:** Paths or people. **Commentators:** Heshang Gong reads people: 明道之人若闇昧 (*míng dào zhī rén ruò àn mèi* — "a person bright in the Tao seems dim and dark"). 纇 (*lèi* — "a knot in silk, a flaw"): Wang Bi glosses 纇㘨也 (*lèi tì yě* — "lèi means uneven, knotted"); Heshang Gong's text reads 𩔖 (*lèi* — "kind, category"), glossed 不自别殊若多比𩔖也 (*bù zì bié shū ruò duō bǐ lèi yě* — "does not set himself apart, as if he were one of the many").

### Unit 7
> 上德若谷，大白若辱，廣德若不足，建德若偷，質真若渝。 (*shàng dé ruò gǔ, dà bái ruò rǔ, guǎng dé ruò bù zú, jiàn dé ruò tōu, zhì zhēn ruò yú* — "high virtue seems a valley, great white seems soiled, broad virtue seems insufficient, established virtue seems slack, plain genuineness seems changeable")
- **Says:** The highest virtue looks like a valley, great whiteness looks soiled, broad virtue looks lacking, established virtue looks lax, and plain genuineness looks fickle.
- **Act:** asserts (quoted sayings)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the qualities named, or the people who have them
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 不 (*bù* — "not") in 不足 (*bù zú* — "insufficient") negates sufficiency. 辱 (*rǔ* — "disgrace, soiled"); Wang Bi: 知其白守其黑 (*zhī qí bái shǒu qí hēi* — "know its white, keep its black"). 偷 (*tōu* — "furtive, slack, to steal"). **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 偷匹也 (*tōu pǐ yě* — "tōu means 'ordinary'"), 因物自然不立不施故若偷匹 (*yīn wù zì rán bù lì bù shī gù ruò tōu pǐ* — "it follows things as they are, sets nothing up, bestows nothing, so it seems ordinary"). Heshang Gong: 若可偷引使空虚也 (*ruò kě tōu yǐn shǐ kōng xū yě* — "as if it could be stolen and drawn off, leaving it empty"). 渝 (*yú* — "to change, fade"); Wang Bi's editor notes 一作媮 (*yī zuò tōu* — "one text has 媮 *tōu*, 'slack'"). Heshang Gong: 若五色有渝淺不明 (*ruò wǔ sè yǒu yú qiǎn bù míng* — "like the five colors faded, pale and dim").

### Unit 8
> 大方無隅，大器晚成，大音希聲，大象無形。 (*dà fāng wú yú, dà qì wǎn chéng, dà yīn xī shēng, dà xiàng wú xíng* — "the great square has no corners, the great vessel is late completed, the great tone is faint of sound, the great image has no form")
- **Says:** The great square has no corners, the great vessel is finished late, the great music is faint in sound, and the great image has no shape.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the great things named, or, for Heshang Gong, great people
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 無 (*wú* — "no"), twice, negating existence. 希聲 (*xī shēng* — "rare / faint sound"): inaudible, or seldom heard. **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 聽之不聞名曰希 (*tīng zhī bù wén míng yuē xī* — "listened for and not heard is called xī"). Heshang Gong: 大音猶雷霆待時而動 (*dà yīn yóu léi tíng dài shí ér dòng* — "the great sound is like thunder, waiting for its time to move"), so it is rare rather than silent. Heshang Gong reads persons throughout: 大方正之人无委曲亷隅 (*dà fāng zhèng zhī rén wú wěi qū lián yú* — "a person of great squareness has no crooked bends or sharp corners"). Both read 晚成 (*wǎn chéng* — "late completed") as late; Han Fei illustrates it with King Zhuang of Chu waiting three years before acting.

### Unit 9
> 道隱無名。 (*dào yǐn wú míng* — "the Tao is hidden, without name")
- **Says:** The Tao is hidden and nameless.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 道 (*dào* — "the Tao")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 無 (*wú* — "without") negates having a name. Heshang Gong: 道潛隱使人无能指名也 (*dào qián yǐn shǐ rén wú néng zhǐ míng yě* — "the Tao lies hidden, so that no one can point it out and name it").

### Unit 10
> 夫唯道，善貸且成。 (*fú wéi dào, shàn dài qiě chéng* — "now, only the Tao is good at lending and also completing")
- **Says:** Only the Tao is good at giving and at bringing to completion.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 道 (*dào* — "the Tao"); what it lends to and completes is unstated (things, people)
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** 夫唯 (*fú wéi* — "now, only")
- **Open:** 貸 (*dài* — "to lend, bestow"); 善 (*shàn* — "good at"). **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 一貸之則足以永終其徳 (*yī dài zhī zé zú yǐ yǒng zhōng qí dé* — "once it lends to them, that is enough to fulfil their virtue for good"). Heshang Gong: 道善稟貸人精氣且成就之也 (*dào shàn bǐng dài rén jīng qì qiě chéng jiù zhī yě* — "the Tao is good at bestowing vital essence on people and also bringing them to completion").

# Chapter 42

**The chapter as a whole:** The Tao gives rise to one, one to two, two to three, and three to all things. All things carry yin and embrace yang, and the blending of qi makes their harmony. People hate the humble names that kings and dukes take as titles, so loss can be gain and gain can be loss. What others teach, the speaker teaches too: the violent do not die their own death, and that will be the father of the speaker's teaching.

### Unit 1
> 道生一，一生二，二生三，三生萬物。 (*dào shēng yī, yī shēng èr, èr shēng sān, sān shēng wàn wù* — "the Tao gives birth to one, one gives birth to two, two gives birth to three, three gives birth to the myriad things")
- **Says:** The Tao produces one, one produces two, two produces three, and three produces all things.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 道 (*dào* — "the Tao"), then each number in turn
- **Force:** always/universal (or past, as a cosmogony)
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** What one, two and three are. **Commentators:** Heshang Gong reads them physically: 一生隂與陽也 (*yī shēng yīn yǔ yáng yě* — "one gives birth to yin and yang"), and 隂陽生和氣濁三氣分為天地人也 (*yīn yáng shēng hé qì zhuó sān qì fēn wéi tiān dì rén yě* — "yin and yang produce harmonious and turbid qi; the three qi divide into heaven, earth and humankind"). Wang Bi reads them as how naming counts: 由無乃一一可謂無已謂之一豈得無言乎有言有一非二如何 (*yóu wú nǎi yī, yī kě wèi wú, yǐ wèi zhī yī, qǐ dé wú yán hū? yǒu yán yǒu yī, fēi èr rú hé* — "from non-being comes one; one may be called non-being, but once it is called 'one,' how can there be no word? With the word and the one, what is that if not two?").

### Unit 2
> 萬物負陰而抱陽，沖氣以為和。 (*wàn wù fù yīn ér bào yáng, chōng qì yǐ wéi hé* — "the myriad things carry yin on the back and embrace yang; surging qi makes harmony")
- **Says:** All things carry yin on their backs and hold yang in their arms, and the blending of qi makes their harmony.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 萬物 (*wàn wù* — "the myriad things")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 沖 (*chōng* — "to surge, pour, blend; empty"). 沖氣 (*chōng qì* — "surging/blending qi") is "surging qi," "blending qi" or "empty qi." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 萬物中皆有元氣得以和柔 (*wàn wù zhōng jiē yǒu yuán qì dé yǐ hé róu* — "within all things there is original qi, by which they are harmonious and soft"), likened to the hollow in plants 與氣通 (*yǔ qì tōng* — "through which qi passes"). For 負陰而抱陽 (*fù yīn ér bào yáng* — "carry yin, embrace yang") he has 萬物无不負隂而向陽 (*wàn wù wú bù fù yīn ér xiàng yáng* — "there is nothing that does not turn its back to the yin and face the yang"), that is, back to the shade and face to the sun.

### Unit 3
> 人之所惡，唯孤、寡、不穀，而王公以為稱。 (*rén zhī suǒ wù, wéi gū, guǎ, bù gǔ, ér wáng gōng yǐ wéi chēng* — "what people hate is only 'orphan,' 'widowed,' 'not-grain,' yet kings and dukes take them as titles")
- **Says:** What people hate is to be "orphan," "widowed" or "unworthy," yet kings and dukes take these as their titles.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 人 (*rén* — "people"); 王公 (*wáng gōng* — "kings and dukes")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none (inside the unit, but: 而 *ér* — "yet")
- **Open:** 不 (*bù* — "not") in 不穀 (*bù gǔ* — "not grain, unworthy") negates a quality. Heshang Gong's text reads 不轂 (*bù gǔ* — "not a hub"), as in chapter 39, and explains: 不祥之名而王公以為稱者處謙卑 (*bù xiáng zhī míng ér wáng gōng yǐ wéi chēng zhě chǔ qiān bēi* — "names of ill omen, which kings and dukes take as titles so as to dwell in humility").

### Unit 4
> 故物或損之而益，或益之而損。 (*gù wù huò sǔn zhī ér yì, huò yì zhī ér sǔn* — "so of things, some are diminished and gain, some are increased and lose")
- **Says:** So with things, some gain by being diminished and some lose by being increased.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 物 (*wù* — "things"); whoever diminishes or increases them is unstated
- **Force:** habitual / can
- **Link:** therefore/so (故 *gù*)
- **Open:** 或 (*huò* — "some / sometimes"). Heshang Gong: 夫増髙者崩貪富者致患 (*fú zēng gāo zhě bēng, tān fù zhě zhì huàn* — "what is built higher collapses; one greedy for wealth brings on disaster").

### Unit 5
> 人之所教，我亦教之。 (*rén zhī suǒ jiào, wǒ yì jiào zhī* — "what people teach, I too teach it")
- **Says:** What others teach, the speaker also teaches.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 人 (*rén* — "people, others"); 我 (*wǒ* — "I"), the speaker
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 之 (*zhī* — "it / them"): (a) the same lesson, "I teach it too"; (b) the people, "I teach them (otherwise)." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong takes (b): the crowd teaches 去弱為強 (*qù ruò wéi qiáng* — "leave weakness to become strong"), while 我教衆人使去強為弱 (*wǒ jiào zhòng rén shǐ qù qiáng wéi ruò* — "I teach the crowd to leave strength and become weak"). Wang Bi's editor reports variants, including 人之所以教我亦我之所以教人 (*rén zhī suǒ yǐ jiào wǒ yì wǒ zhī suǒ yǐ jiào rén* — "what people teach me with is also what I teach people with").

### Unit 6
> 強梁者不得其死，吾將以為教父。 (*qiáng liáng zhě bù dé qí sǐ, wú jiāng yǐ wéi jiào fù* — "the strong and overbearing do not get their death; I will take this as the father of teaching")
- **Says:** The violent do not die a natural death, and the speaker will take this as the father of their teaching.
- **Act:** asserts; declares an intention
- **Who acts / of whom:** 強梁者 (*qiáng liáng zhě* — "the violent, overbearing"); 吾 (*wú* — "I")
- **Force:** habitual; will/future (將 *jiāng* — "will")
- **Link:** none (states the lesson)
- **Open:** 不 (*bù* — "not") negates obtaining. 不得其死 (*bù dé qí sǐ* — "do not get their death") means dying before one's time; Heshang Gong: 不得以壽命死也 (*bù dé yǐ shòu mìng sǐ yě* — "do not die at the end of their allotted span"). 教父 (*jiào fù* — "teaching-father"): **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 父始也 (*fù shǐ yě* — "fù means 'beginning'"), so it is the starting point of the teaching. Literally it is "father," the chief or model. Wang Bi makes the violent person the thing taken: 故得其違教之徒適可以為教父也 (*gù dé qí wéi jiào zhī tú shì kě yǐ wéi jiào fù yě* — "so getting someone who defies the teaching is just right to serve as the teaching's father"), the violent as an object lesson rather than the saying.
