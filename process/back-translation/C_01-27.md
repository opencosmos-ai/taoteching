## Summary

**Major**
1. **Ch 13 · CLOSED · the closing couplet.** Heshang Gong reads the 貴 (*guì* — to prize) / 寄 (*jì* — to lodge) line as a warning and the 愛 (*ài* — to love) / 託 (*tuō* — to entrust) line as praise. The English makes both lines one positive claim. The logged note quotes Heshang Gong in support of its reading and leaves out his gloss that reads the first line the other way.
2. **Ch 13 · ACT · 貴大患若身 (*guì dà huàn ruò shēn* — "prize great trouble as the body").** The English turns this into a command to the reader, *"Prize great trouble as your own body."* Both commentators read it as a description: Wang Bi of people taken in by favor, Heshang Gong of fear that trouble will reach the body. Not logged.
3. **Ch 22 · WHO · 枉則直 (*wǎng zé zhí* — "bent, then straight").** *"Be wronged, and be made straight"* hands both acts to other people. Both commentators put the bending on the self, and Heshang Gong has the straightness arrive of itself. The chapter's own note maps this line to *"they do not insist they are right"*, which the English then breaks. Not logged.

**Minor**
4. **Ch 1 · ACT · 以觀 (*yǐ guān* — "thereby observe").** Both commentators read 可以觀 (*kě yǐ guān* — "can observe"), a capacity. The English gives a bare imperative, *"observe"*.
5. **Ch 1 · CLAIM · 徼 (*jiào*) → *"boundaries"*.** Both commentators gloss it as the end things return to (歸終, *guī zhōng*; 歸, *guī*), which is a time sense. *Boundaries* is spatial. No record of the choice.
6. **Ch 2 · OPAQUE · 音聲相和 (*yīn shēng xiāng hé*).** *"Sound and tone"* reads as two synonyms in a series built of opposites. Heshang Gong reads the pair as call and answer. Reader A could not see the contrast.
7. **Ch 3 · WHO · the dangling *"By not exalting…, the people…"*.** English grammar makes the people the ones who refrain. The Chinese doer is the ruler.
8. **Ch 14 · CLAIM / LINK · 不可致詰 (*bù kě zhì jié* — "cannot be questioned to the end") → *"defy comprehension"*.** The Chinese says questioning cannot get to the bottom of them. The English says they cannot be understood, and the *Therefore* that follows loses its logic.
9. **Ch 17 · CLOSED · 太上 (*tài shàng* — "the very highest") → *"the greatest leaders"*.** Heshang Gong reads "the rulers of high antiquity", which makes the ladder a history of decline. The English gives only a grade.
10. **Ch 17 / 23 · CLAIM · 有不信 (*yǒu bù xìn* — "there is untrust") → *"there is no trust"*. Logged.** Reader A heard a tautology and could not connect the line to its chapter. Both commentators describe something that appears in response, not trust going to zero.
11. **Ch 19 · CLOSED · 見素 (*xiàn sù* / *jiàn sù* — "show / see the undyed silk") → *"see"*.** Heshang Gong marks the sound *xiàn* (show) and has the ruler showing plainness to those below. Not recorded. Ch 3 reads the same graph as *display*.
12. **Ch 21 · CLOSED · 孔德 (*kǒng dé*) → *"the greatest integrity"*.** Wang Bi, our base commentator, reads 孔 as *empty*. Heshang Gong reads it as *great*, and he also reads 容 (*róng*) as *contain*. Neither split is recorded. Accuracy-audit finding 2's proposed fix, *bearing*, would close the second split.
13. **Ch 23 · CLOSED (LOCK) · 德者同於德 (*dé zhě tóng yú dé*).** Wang Bi reads 德 (*dé* — integrity) as 得 (*dé* — gain), which gives a gain/loss pair. Under *integrity / loss*, Reader A could not tell what *"one of loss"* means. Not recorded.

## Entries

### Ch 13 · A 11–12 / B 6–7 · CLOSED · major
- **Chinese:** 故貴以身為天下，若可寄天下；愛以身為天下，若可託天下 (*gù guì yǐ shēn wéi tiān xià, ruò kě jì tiān xià; ài yǐ shēn wéi tiān xià, ruò kě tuō tiān xià* — "so one who prizes the body as / for the world can be lodged with the world; one who loves the body as / for the world can be entrusted with the world")
- **English:** "So one who prizes their body as a way to serve the world can be trusted with the world. / One who loves their body as a way to serve the world can be trusted with it."
- **Reader A heard:** two parallel promises. Both prizing and loving the body for the world's sake qualify a person to be given the world.
- **Reader B read:** the commentators split on polarity. Heshang Gong makes the first line a warning and the second praise. Wang Bi makes both praise, on the ground that the person cannot be bought.
- **The divergence:** Heshang Gong's two glosses are opposites, and they turn on 貴/愛 and 寄/託.
  - On the first line: 言人君貴其身而賤人，欲為天下主者，則可寄立，不可以久也 (*yán rén jūn guì qí shēn ér jiàn rén … zé kě jì lì, bù kě yǐ jiǔ yě* — "it says a ruler who prizes his own body and holds others cheap, wanting to be lord of the world, can be set up only as a lodger, not for long").
  - On the second line: 能愛其身，非為己也 (*néng ài qí shēn, fēi wèi jǐ yě* — "can love his body, and not for his own sake") … 長无咎也 (*cháng wú jiù yě* — "and be long without fault").
  - The chapter's note (`notes/translation.md` → *Ch 13 · 貴以身為天下*) cites that second gloss as support for Reading B across the whole couplet. It never mentions that Heshang Gong reads the first line as the opposite.
  - The 寄/託 merger (*trusted with* for both) is logged on Wang Bi's authority, who swaps the two verbs. But Heshang Gong's contrast rests on exactly that difference: 寄立，不可以久 ("lodged, not for long") against 託…長无咎 ("entrusted… long without fault").
  - The English therefore takes Heshang Gong's construal of the line, *for the world*, and Wang Bi's polarity, positive in both lines. Neither commentator reads the couplet that way, and nothing on the record says so.
- **Logged?** Partly. Reading B is Shalom's call. The 寄/託 merger is logged on Wang Bi (`chapters/013.md` Notes; `notes/translation.md` → *Ch 13*). Heshang Gong's warning reading of the 貴 line is recorded nowhere, and the note cites him one-sidedly.
- **Direction:** At minimum, correct the note so it records that Heshang Gong reads the 貴/寄 line as a warning. Then decide whether the verse keeps one claim for both lines, or keeps a difference between 寄 and 託 that leaves his contrast open.

### Ch 13 · A 2 / B 1 · ACT · major
- **Chinese:** 貴大患若身 (*guì dà huàn ruò shēn* — "prize great trouble as the body")
- **English:** "Prize great trouble as your own body."
- **Reader A heard:** advice. *Value great trouble as you value your own body.*
- **Reader B read:** a paired maxim. Its subject is people in general, and the next units explain it as a saying.
- **The divergence:** Neither commentator reads an instruction.
  - Wang Bi: 大患，榮寵之屬也…人迷之於榮寵，返之於身，故曰大患若身也 (*dà huàn, róng chǒng zhī shǔ yě … rén mí zhī yú róng chǒng, fǎn zhī yú shēn, gù yuē dà huàn ruò shēn yě* — "great trouble is of the class of glory and favor … people are deluded by glory and favor, and it rebounds onto the body; therefore it says 'great trouble as the body'"). He describes the deluded, and does not counsel the reader.
  - Heshang Gong: 貴，畏也；若，至也。畏大患至身，故皆驚 (*guì, wèi yě; ruò, zhì yě. wèi dà huàn zhì shēn, gù jiē jīng* — "貴 means to fear; 若 means to arrive: fearing great trouble reaching the body, so all are alarmed"). This is description.
  - The English imperative tells the reader to prize trouble, and it sets up the closing couplet's positive *prizes their body* as the same advice. Bare Chinese grammar allows the imperative, but the commentators do not take it.
- **Logged?** No. The note settles 貴 → *prize* and says nothing about mood.
- **Direction:** Make it a statement of the saying, not a command, e.g. *"Great trouble is prized like one's own body."* The *What does it mean…?* frame then asks about a saying, as the Chinese does.

### Ch 22 · A 2 / B 1 · WHO · major
- **Chinese:** 枉則直 (*wǎng zé zhí* — "bent, then straight")
- **English:** "Be wronged, and be made straight."
- **Reader A heard:** you are treated unjustly by unnamed wrongers, and then vindicated or corrected by unnamed straighteners.
- **Reader B read:** what is crooked becomes straight. It can be a natural law or advice, with no outside agent either way.
- **The divergence:** 枉 (*wǎng*) is *bent, crooked*. The English *wronged* gives it the legal sense of injustice, done to you by others. The passive *be made straight* supplies another outside agent. Both commentators put the act on the self.
  - Heshang Gong: 枉屈己而申人，久久自得直也 (*wǎng qū jǐ ér shēn rén, jiǔ jiǔ zì dé zhí yě* — "枉 is bending oneself and letting others stretch out; in time one becomes straight of oneself").
  - Wang Bi: 不自是則其是彰也 (*bù zì shì zé qí shì zhāng yě* — "not affirming oneself right, one's rightness shows").
  - The chapter's own note (`notes/translation.md` → *Ch 22 · The four 不自X lines*) maps this line to 不自是，故彰 (*bù zì shì, gù zhāng* — "they do not insist they are right, and so their rightness shows"). The English stanza 3 then renders that line as the sage's own restraint. The English line 1 breaks the mapping the notes rely on.
- **Logged?** No. 枉 appears nowhere in the notes except in that mapping table.
- **Direction:** Keep the self as the doer and the result of its own accord, e.g. *"Be bent, and grow straight."* That restores the physical image the series runs on (bend, hollow, worn) and the 不自是 echo.

### Ch 1 · A 5–6 / B 5–6 · ACT · minor
- **Chinese:** 故常無欲，以觀其妙；常有欲，以觀其徼 (*gù cháng wú yù, yǐ guān qí miào; cháng yǒu yù, yǐ guān qí jiào* — "so, ever desireless, thereby observe its subtlety; ever desiring, thereby observe its outcome")
- **English:** "Ever desireless, observe its subtlety; / Ever desiring, observe its boundaries."
- **Reader A heard:** two commands to the reader. A noted that a statement reading ("one who is desireless observes") is possible.
- **Reader B read:** an assertion with an advisory edge. The observer is unstated and the force is *can*.
- **The divergence:** Both commentators read capacity, not command.
  - Wang Bi: 常無欲空虛，可以觀其始物之妙 (*cháng wú yù kōng xū, kě yǐ guān qí shǐ wù zhī miào* — "ever desireless and empty, one **can** observe the subtlety by which it begins things"). He uses 可以觀 (*kě yǐ guān* — "can observe") again for 徼.
  - Heshang Gong: 人常能無欲，則可以觀道之要 (*rén cháng néng wú yù, zé kě yǐ guān dào zhī yào* — "if a person can always be desireless, then they **can** observe the Tao's essential").
  - The English bare imperative states a method. The Chinese states what the condition makes possible. (The dropped 故 (*gù* — so) is already in the accuracy audit's 故 tally.)
- **Logged?** No. `glossary/chang-常.md` discusses the four 常 (*cháng* — ever-present) but not the mood.
- **Direction:** Carry the capacity, e.g. *"Ever desireless, one can observe its subtlety; / ever desiring, its …"*. Or record the imperative as a choice.

### Ch 1 · A 6 / B 6 · CLAIM · minor
- **Chinese:** 以觀其徼 (*yǐ guān qí jiào* — "thereby observe its 徼")
- **English:** "observe its boundaries."
- **Reader A heard:** edges, limits, or outward manifestations, which are spatial.
- **Reader B read:** its boundary in the sense of *where it ends up*.
- **The divergence:** Both commentators gloss 徼 (*jiào*) as an endpoint in time, the place things return to.
  - Wang Bi: 徼，歸終也 (*jiào, guī zhōng yě* — "徼 means returning to the end"), and he pairs it with 妙 (*miào* — subtle) as end against beginning: 始物之妙 (*shǐ wù zhī miào* — "the subtlety that begins things") against 終物之徼 (*zhōng wù zhī jiào* — "the 徼 that completes things").
  - Heshang Gong: 徼，歸也 (*jiào, guī yě* — "徼 means where things go").
  - *Boundaries* is the graph's literal frontier sense and is defensible. But it turns the couplet's beginning-and-end axis into inside-and-edge, and the reader cannot recover the commentators' pairing.
- **Logged?** No. 徼 has no glossary entry and no note. The source table's Literal column reads *manifestations*.
- **Direction:** Weigh *outcome* / *where it ends* against *boundaries*, and record the choice either way.

### Ch 2 · A 3 / B 3 · OPAQUE · minor
- **Chinese:** 音聲相和 (*yīn shēng xiāng hé* — "tone and voice harmonize each other")
- **English:** "Sound and tone harmonize each other,"
- **Reader A heard:** *"sound and tone" are near-synonyms to an English reader and the contrast is unclear.*
- **Reader B read:** musical tone against plain sound, or note against echo. It is one of six pairs of opposites.
- **The divergence:** The series is six opposed pairs. Heshang Gong reads this one as lead and response: 上唱下必和也 (*shàng chàng xià bì hé yě* — "the one above sings, the one below must answer"). In English *sound* and *tone* are nearly one thing, so this is the one member where a reader cannot see the opposition. The English also reverses the Chinese order: 音 (*yīn* — tone) comes first.
- **Logged?** No. The source table's Literal column reads *sound and silence*, which renders neither character.
- **Direction:** Find a pair an English reader hears as two, such as *note and echo* or *call and answer*, and log it.

### Ch 3 · A 1–3 / B 1–3 · WHO · minor
- **Chinese:** 不尚賢，使民不爭 (*bù shàng xián, shǐ mín bù zhēng* — "not exalting the worthy makes the people not contend"), and the two lines that follow it
- **English:** "By not exalting the exceptional, the people have no cause to contend." (and *"By not prizing… the people…"*, *"By not displaying…, the people's hearts…"*)
- **Reader A heard:** *"grammatically the people are the ones 'not exalting'; most readers will take the ruler."*
- **Reader B read:** the unstated doer is the ruler (Heshang Gong: 人君 (*rén jūn* — the ruler)). The people are acted upon.
- **The divergence:** All three openings are dangling modifiers. English grammar attaches *By not exalting* to the subject of the main clause, *the people*. The Chinese has two agents: the one who refrains, and the people who do not contend. The ch 3 note logs the causal *cause* (it is in the accuracy audit's smaller table) but not the attachment.
- **Logged?** No.
- **Direction:** Give the refraining its own clause, e.g. *"Exalt no one as exceptional, and the people do not contend."* That would also lose the *cause*.

### Ch 14 · A 4–5 / B 4 · CLAIM / LINK · minor
- **Chinese:** 此三者不可致詰，故混而為一 (*cǐ sān zhě bù kě zhì jié, gù hùn ér wéi yī* — "these three cannot be questioned to the end, so they mix into one")
- **English:** "These three defy comprehension. / Therefore, they mix into one."
- **Reader A heard:** *these three cannot be understood*. And *"the causal link is not explained."*
- **Reader B read:** they cannot be investigated to the bottom, and therefore they merge.
- **The divergence:** 致詰 (*zhì jié*) is pressing a question to its end. The logic is that inquiry cannot tell the three apart, so they are one. *Defy comprehension* is a broader claim, that they cannot be understood at all, and it leaves *Therefore* hanging. Heshang Gong denies the broader claim outright: 當受之以靜，求之以神，不可詰問而得之也 (*dāng shòu zhī yǐ jìng, qiú zhī yǐ shén, bù kě jié wèn ér dé zhī yě* — "one should receive it with stillness and seek it with the spirit; it cannot be got by interrogation"). So it can be had, just not by questioning.
- **Logged?** No. The source table's Literal column reads *cannot be fully probed/investigated*, which is right.
- **Direction:** *"These three cannot be questioned to the end. / So they mix into one."*

### Ch 17 / Ch 23 · A 17.5, 23.14 / B 17.5, 23.12 · CLAIM · minor (logged)
- **Chinese:** 信不足焉，有不信焉 (*xìn bù zú yān, yǒu bù xìn yān* — "where trust is not enough, there is untrust")
- **English:** "Where trust runs short, there is no trust."
- **Reader A heard:** *"reads as near-tautology"* in ch 17. In ch 23: *"connection to the preceding lines unclear."*
- **Reader B read:** *when trust(worthiness) falls short, distrust appears.*
- **The divergence:** 有不信 (*yǒu bù xìn*) asserts that something is present: 有 (*yǒu* — there is) untrust. The commentators read it that way, and the logged note itself translates both of them that way.

### Ch 21 · A 1 / B 1 · CLOSED · minor
- **Chinese:** 孔德之容，惟道是從 (*kǒng dé zhī róng, wéi dào shì cóng* — "the bearing of great/empty integrity follows only the Tao")
- **English:** "The greatest integrity follows only the Tao."
- **Reader A heard:** the highest integrity follows nothing but the Tao.
- **Reader B read:** 孔 (*kǒng*) as *great* or *hollow*, and 容 (*róng*) as *bearing* or *capacity to contain*. Both are open.
- **The divergence:** The commentators split on both words.
  - Wang Bi, our base commentator: 孔，空也。惟以空為德，然後乃能動作從道 (*kǒng, kōng yě. wéi yǐ kōng wéi dé, rán hòu nǎi néng dòng zuò cóng dào* — "孔 means empty: only by taking emptiness as integrity can one act and follow the Tao").
  - Heshang Gong: 孔，大也。有大德之人，無所不容 (*kǒng, dà yě. yǒu dà dé zhī rén, wú suǒ bù róng* — "孔 means great: the person of great integrity contains everything"). He reads 容 as *contain*, not *bearing*.
  - The English takes Heshang Gong's *great*, raises it to a superlative, and drops 容. The drop is accuracy-audit finding 2. That finding's proposal, *"The bearing of the greatest integrity,"* would fix the omission by closing the 容 fork against Heshang Gong. It would still leave 孔 settled against Wang Bi without a record.
- **Logged?** No. `glossary/de-德.md` quotes the line as *great*.
- **Direction:** When finding 2 lands, record both forks, 孔 (*empty / great*) and 容 (*bearing / containing*), and choose with both in view.

### Ch 23 · A 9–13 / B 7–11 · CLOSED (LOCK) · minor
- **Chinese:** 德者同於德 … 同於德者，德亦樂得之 (*dé zhě tóng yú dé … tóng yú dé zhě, dé yì lè dé zhī* — "one of integrity is the same as integrity … those the same as integrity, integrity too gladly gets them")
- **English:** "One of integrity is the same as integrity." … "Those the same as integrity, integrity too gladly gets them."
- **Reader A heard:** for the third term, *"'one of loss' as someone who has lost (the Tao), who fails, or who is in loss; whether loss is negative or a deliberate letting-go"*. The series' logic was unclear.
- **Reader B read:** 德 (*dé* — integrity) against its homophone 得 (*dé* — gain), and Wang Bi reads *gain*.
- **The divergence:** Wang Bi's lemma prints 徳 (*dé* — integrity), and his comment reads 得 (*dé* — gain) throughout: 得，少也。少則得，故曰得也 (*dé, shǎo yě. shǎo zé dé, gù yuē dé yě* — "得 is having little: little, then gain, so it says 得"). That answers ch 22's 少則得，多則惑 (*shǎo zé dé, duō zé huò* — "little, then gain; much, then confusion"). It pairs with 失 (*shī* — loss): 失，累多也 (*shī, lěi duō yě* — "失 is piling up much"). On his reading the series is Tao, gain, loss, and the third term needs no explaining. Heshang Gong reads 德謂好德人也 (*dé wèi hào dé rén yě* — "德 means a person who loves integrity"), which the English follows under the 德 lock. The lock is not at fault. What is missing is the record that the base commentator reads the middle term differently, which is the reading under which *loss* makes sense.
- **Logged?** No. `glossary/de-德.md` notes the 德/得 pun in general, not Wang Bi's reading here.
- **Direction:** A note in ch 23 recording Wang Bi's 得. The verse can stay on the lock.
