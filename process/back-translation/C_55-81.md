# Comparer — chapters 55–81

*Reader A (English only): `bt/A_out/A_55-81.md`. Reader B (Chinese and commentaries only): `bt/B_out/B_43-55.md` (ch 55), `B_56-68.md`, `B_69-81.md`. Each finding below was checked against the chapter's `## Source` and `## Translation`, the vendored commentaries in `sources/commentaries/`, the chapter's `## Notes`, `notes/*.md`, the glossary, `process/form-audit.md`, `process/accuracy-audit.md` and `WORKLIST.md`. Nothing in the repository was edited.*

**Count:** 1 critical · 3 major · 4 minor · 3 logged calls the cold reader paid for (minor, listed for evidence).

## Summary

1. **critical** · Ch 60 · CLAIM · 非 (*fēi* — "it is not that") is rendered *"not only,"* twice. The English says the ghosts lose their potency and then that the same potency does no harm, which contradicts itself. Both commentators read the lines as corrections: the ghosts keep their potency, and it does not harm people.
2. **major** · Ch 57 · ACT · the opening three maxims become three commands joined by *and*, so the English endorses all three. Wang Bi reads the first two as what fails, and Heshang Gong reads all three as descriptions of who gets what, not as advice.
3. **major** · Ch 58 · CLAIM · 其民缺缺 (*qí mín quē quē* — "its people are lacking / broken") is rendered *"the people turn cunning."* Neither commentator reads craft into the word, and nothing records the choice.
4. **major** · Ch 62 · CLAIM + OPENED · 有罪以免 (*yǒu zuì yǐ miǎn* — "having an offense, by it you are spared") is rendered *"commit a crime, and you are spared,"* where the chapter's own source table and notes read *"have a crime against you."* The line beside it, 求以得 (*qiú yǐ dé* — "seeking, by it you find"), is rendered *"seek, and you find,"* which drops 以 (*yǐ* — by means of it) and lands on Matthew 7:7.
5. **minor** · Ch 61 · ACT · 夫兩者各得所欲 (*fū liǎng zhě gè dé suǒ yù* — "now, for the two each to get what they want") is rendered as the flat statement *"Both get what they want."* It reads as a fact, where Wang Bi reads the condition the closing counsel rests on.
6. **minor** · Ch 63 · FORCE · 故能成其大 (*gù néng chéng qí dà* — "and so can complete their greatness") drops 能 (*néng* — can). Ch 34 drops it the same way.
7. **minor** · Ch 65 · CLAIM · 將以愚之 (*jiāng yǐ yú zhī* — "meant to make them guileless") becomes *"to **return** them to being guileless."* The English adds a lost original state that neither the text nor the commentators give.
8. **minor** · Ch 73 · LINK · 疏而不失 (*shū ér bù shī* — "wide-meshed, yet loses nothing") is rendered *"Its mesh is wide, **and** nothing slips through,"* so the concession becomes an addition.
9. **logged · OPAQUE** · Ch 75 · *"Only those who don't use living for doing"*: Reader A could not parse it.
10. **logged · LOCK** · Ch 74 · 孰敢 (*shú gǎn* — "who would dare?") is rendered *"Who would still push?"* Reader A found *push* vague here.
11. **logged · LOCK** · Ch 55 · 心使氣曰強 (*xīn shǐ qì yuē qiáng* — "the heart driving the breath is called forcing") is rendered *"that is called strong."* Reader A held it open, so this is evidence the lock holds, not a fault.

## The findings

### Ch 60 · A 2–3 / B 2–5 · CLAIM · critical
- **Chinese:** 以道莅天下，其鬼不神；非其鬼不神，其神不傷人；非其神不傷人，聖人亦不傷人 (*yǐ dào lì tiān xià, qí guǐ bù shén; fēi qí guǐ bù shén, qí shén bù shāng rén; fēi qí shén bù shāng rén, shèng rén yì bù shāng rén* — "oversee the world with the Tao and its ghosts are not numinous; **it is not that** the ghosts are not numinous, it is that their numinous power does not harm people; **it is not that** their numinous power does not harm people, it is that the sage, too, does not harm people")
- **English:** "Oversee the world with the Tao, and the ghosts lose their potency, / not only do ghosts lose their potency, their potency causes no harm to people, / not only does their potency do no harm, the sage, too, does no harm to people."
- **Reader A heard:** something "logically odd": the ghosts lose their potency, and then the potency they lost is said to do no harm. Reader A guessed that the second clause might be correcting the first.
- **Reader B read:** two corrections. The ghosts do have numinous power, but it does not injure people. Then the cause is moved to the sage, or the sage is added beside the ghosts.
- **The divergence:** The English reads 非 (*fēi* — "it is not that") as additive *not only*, so a chain of corrections becomes a chain of piled-up claims. Since 其神 (*qí shén* — "their potency") stays the ghosts' own potency, the second line asserts that the potency lost in the first line is still there and harmless. Both commentators read the corrective sense.
  - Heshang Gong is explicit: 其□非无精神也 (*qí [guǐ] fēi wú jīng shén yě* — "it is not that the [ghosts] are without spirit-essence"; the □ is a glyph the transcribers could not encode, and context makes it 鬼 (*guǐ* — ghost)). He then puts the cause in the sage: 以聖人在位不傷害人，故□不敢干之也 (*yǐ shèng rén zài wèi bù shāng hài rén, gù [guǐ] bù gǎn gān zhī yě* — "because the sage in office does not harm people, the [ghosts] dare not intrude on them").
  - Wang Bi glosses 神不害自然也 (*shén bù hài zì rán yě* — "the numinous does not harm what is so of itself"). He never says the ghosts lose it.
  - Some modern readings do take 非 (*fēi* — it is not that) as 非唯 (*fēi wéi* — "not only"), but they make the second 神 (*shén*) a different class of being, the spirits. The English keeps the ghosts' own potency, and that combination is the one that contradicts itself.
  - Under both commentators the chapter says that under the Tao the numinous keeps its power and harms no one, because nothing provokes it, or because the sage does not. The English says the ghosts are disarmed, and lists the sage as one more harmless party.
- **Logged?** No. The chapter has no notes. The source table's literal column already reads *"Not only…"*, and the form audit records the chain as *Kept* with that wording (`process/form-audit.md` → *Ch 60*). Nobody has argued the reading.
- **Direction:** Render 非 (*fēi* — it is not that) as a correction, for example *"It is not that the ghosts lose their potency; their potency does no harm to people. / It is not that their potency does no harm; the sage, too, does no harm to people."* The first line's *lose* would then need checking against 不神 (*bù shén* — "are not numinous"). The chain stays in position, so the form audit's verdict survives.

### Ch 57 · A 1 / B 1 · ACT · major
- **Chinese:** 以正治國，以奇用兵，以無事取天下 (*yǐ zhèng zhì guó, yǐ qí yòng bīng, yǐ wú shì qǔ tiān xià* — "by the straight a state is governed; by the crooked troops are used; by not meddling the world is won")
- **English:** "Govern the state with uprightness, / wage war with surprise, / and win the world by not meddling."
- **Reader A heard:** three commands to a ruler, all endorsed, the third added to the first two by *and*.
- **Reader B read:** three parallel maxims with no connective. Reader B marked them genuinely open between all three endorsed and the first two set up as a foil for the third.
- **The divergence:** The English picks the imperative mood and adds *and*, which turns the series into one programme: do all three. Neither commentator reads it that way.
  - Wang Bi reads the first two as what goes wrong: 以道治國則國平，以正治國則竒正起也 (*yǐ dào zhì guó zé guó píng, yǐ zhèng zhì guó zé qí zhèng qǐ yě* — "govern a state by the Tao and it is at peace; govern it by the straight and the crooked and the straight both arise"). He adds: 以正治國則不足以取天下而以竒用兵也 (*yǐ zhèng zhì guó zé bù zú yǐ qǔ tiān xià ér yǐ qí yòng bīng yě* — "governing by the straight is not enough to win the world, and so one ends up using troops by the crooked").
  - Heshang Gong reads 以 (*yǐ*) as 至 (*zhì* — to arrive), so the three are descriptions of whom nature appoints, not advice: 天使正身之人使至有國也 (*tiān shǐ zhèng shēn zhī rén shǐ zhì yǒu guó yě* — "nature lets a person of upright self come to possess a state"). He does not endorse 奇 (*qí* — the crooked), which he glosses 竒詐也 (*qí zhà yě* — "竒, a variant form of 奇, means deceit").
  - The project's own reading of ch 58 agrees with Wang Bi. `notes/translation.md` → *Ch 57–58 · 正 / 奇* (*zhèng / qí* — straight / crooked) says ch 58 is *"deliberately unmaking 57: there the ruler governs with 正 (zhèng — straight) and wages war with 奇 (qí — crooked) … certain of the boundary."* An English that commands the first two leaves ch 58 nothing to unmake.
- **Logged?** No. `process/form-audit.md` T3-17 takes *"a series of three imperatives"* as given and does not decide it. **This finding does not touch how 正 / 奇 (*zhèng / qí* — straight / crooked) are rendered, which is deferred (T4-2).** It concerns only the mood and the added *and*, so it should ride with T4-2 when that opens rather than be settled here.
- **Direction:** Make the first two maxims statements, not orders, and drop the *and* so the third stands against them, e.g. *"A state is governed by …, war is waged by …; the world is won by not meddling."* The words for 正 / 奇 (*zhèng / qí* — straight / crooked) wait for T4-2.

### Ch 58 · A 2 / B 2 · CLAIM · major
- **Chinese:** 其政察察，其民缺缺 (*qí zhèng chá chá, qí mín quē quē* — "when its governing is sharp-eyed, its people are lacking")
- **English:** "When the government is sharp and prying, the people turn cunning."
- **Reader A heard:** the people become crafty. Prying government produces scheming subjects.
- **Reader B read:** the people become lacking, broken, deficient.
- **The divergence:** 缺 (*quē*) is *lacking, broken*, and the doubled 缺缺 (*quē quē* — "lacking, lacking") answers 淳淳 (*chún chún* — "simple, honest") as damage, not guile. The English makes the people the culprits. The Chinese makes them the casualties.
  - Wang Bi: 殊𩔖分析，民懐争競，故曰其民缺缺 (*shū lèi fēn xī, mín huái zhēng jìng, gù yuē qí mín quē quē* — "kinds are set apart and divided, the people harbor contention, so it says the people are lacking"). This means fractured, not crafty.
  - Heshang Gong: 政教急，民不聊生，故𡙇𡙇日以踈薄 (*zhèng jiào jí, mín bù liáo shēng, gù quē quē rì yǐ shū bó* — "governing is harsh, the people cannot make a living, so they are 'lacking', daily more estranged and thin"; his text writes 𡙇𡙇 (*quē quē* — "lacking"), a variant form of 缺缺 (*quē quē* — "lacking")). This means impoverished.
  - *Cunning* is also already spent on 巧 (*qiǎo* — skill) at ch 57 and on 知 / 智 (*zhī / zhì* — knowing / cleverness) at ch 3. `notes/translation.md` → *Ch 65 · 智 (zhì — cleverness) → "guile"* counts *"ch 58 once more"* among its uses without asking which character it renders there.
- **Logged?** No choice is recorded. The source table's literal reads *"lacking/crafty."* `process/form-audit.md` §6 / T2-67 has already proposed *"the people are wanting,"* noting that *"turn cunning, which no character says, goes."* That is a form proposal. This comparison independently confirms the content cost, from both commentators.
- **Direction:** *"the people are wanting"* (the form audit's line) or *"…broken."*

### Ch 62 · A 7 / B 7 · CLAIM + OPENED · major
- **Chinese:** 不曰：求以得，有罪以免邪？ (*bù yuē: qiú yǐ dé, yǒu zuì yǐ miǎn xié?* — "is it not said: seeking, by it you find; having an offense, by it you are spared?")
- **English:** "Did they not say: seek, and you find; / commit a crime, and you are spared?"
- **Reader A heard:** a Gospel echo in *seek, and you find*, and a promise that offenders go unpunished.
- **Reader B read:** by means of this Tao, a seeker obtains and an offender escapes. The second half is open: either the guilty are pardoned, or, with Heshang Gong, a person escapes arbitrary punishment.
- **The divergence:** The English makes two moves, and they compound.
  - **First, 有罪 (*yǒu zuì* — "to have an offense / to be charged") became *commit a crime*.** That asserts guilt, and it closes off Heshang Gong's reading, which is the one the chapter's notes build on: 有罪謂遭亂世闇君妄行刑誅，修道則可以解死 (*yǒu zuì wèi zāo luàn shì àn jūn wàng xíng xíng zhū, xiū dào zé kě yǐ jiě sǐ* — "'having an offense' means meeting a chaotic age and a benighted ruler who inflicts punishment and execution at random; cultivating the Tao, one can be released from death"). The verse and its own apparatus disagree. The source table's literal column, `notes/translation.md` → *Ch 62 · 奧 (ào — the inner corner) → "the sanctuary"*, and `notes/reading.md` → *Ch 62* all quote the line as *"have a crime against you, and you are spared"*, and the sanctuary argument rests on that legal sense.
  - **Second, 以 (*yǐ* — "by it") is dropped from both halves.** Wang Bi makes it the hinge: 以求則得求，以免則得免 (*yǐ qiú zé dé qiú, yǐ miǎn zé dé miǎn* — "use it to seek and you get what you seek; use it to escape and you escape"). Without it, *seek, and you find* is an unconditional promise in the cadence of Matthew 7:7. That fails the hymn test. Together with *commit a crime … spared* it rebuilds the reading `notes/translation.md` → *善 / 不善* (*shàn / bù shàn* — masterful / unmasterful) says the chapter was rendered to avoid: *"a doctrine of grace … a gospel assembled out of three separate slips."*
- **Logged?** Partly. The legal reading of 有罪 (*yǒu zuì* — to have an offense) is logged, and the verse contradicts it. Dropping 以 (*yǐ* — by means of it) and the Gospel cadence are not discussed anywhere.
- **Direction:** Bring the verse back to its own notes (*"have a crime against you, and you are spared"*) and give 以 (*yǐ* — by means of it) a word, e.g. *"seek by it, and you find; / have a crime against you, and by it you are spared."*

### Ch 61 · A 9–10 / B 7 · ACT · minor
- **Chinese:** 夫兩者各得所欲，大者宜為下 (*fū liǎng zhě gè dé suǒ yù, dà zhě yí wéi xià* — "now, for the two each to get what they want, the large one ought to be underneath")
- **English:** "Both get what they want. / The large one ought to be underneath."
- **Reader A heard:** a statement of fact, that both already get what they want, followed by an unconnected *ought*.
- **Reader B read:** most naturally a purpose or condition: if both are to get what they want, the large one must go low.
- **The divergence:** Wang Bi supplies the link the English drops: 故曰各得其所欲則大者宜為下也 (*gù yuē gè dé qí suǒ yù zé dà zhě yí wéi xià yě* — "so it says: if each is to get what it wants, then the large one ought to be underneath"). Heshang Gong does not have the large and small state *get* it either: 大國小國各欲得其所 (*dà guó xiǎo guó gè yù dé qí suǒ* — "the large state and the small state each *want* to get their place"). The English closes the chapter on a fact plus a non-sequitur, where the Chinese closes on the condition that grounds the counsel.
- **Logged?** No. The notes on 為下 (*wéi xià* — be underneath) and 宜 (*yí* — fitting) do not discuss the clause.
- **Direction:** *"For both to get what they want, / the large one ought to be underneath."*

### Ch 63 · A 7 / B 6 · FORCE · minor
- **Chinese:** 是以聖人終不為大，故能成其大 (*shì yǐ shèng rén zhōng bù wéi dà, gù néng chéng qí dà* — "so the sage never does the great, and so **can** complete their greatness")
- **English:** "So the sage never does the great, / and so completes their greatness."
- **Reader A heard:** a fact: the sage achieves greatness.
- **Reader B read:** a capacity: the sage is able to complete it.
- **The divergence:** 能 (*néng* — can) is dropped, so *can* becomes *does*. That is the same shift the accuracy audit's finding 9 records at ch 77. Ch 34's 故能成其大 (*gù néng chéng qí dà* — "and so can complete its greatness") reads *"and so completes its greatness,"* which drops it the same way. The form audit pairs the two lines as a formula, so fix both or neither.
- **Logged?** No.
- **Direction:** *"and so can complete their greatness"*, here and at ch 34.

### Ch 65 · A 2 / B 1 · CLAIM · minor
- **Chinese:** 非以明民，將以愚之 (*fēi yǐ míng mín, jiāng yǐ yú zhī* — "did not use it to make the people clever, but meant to make them guileless")
- **English:** "They used it to return them to being guileless."
- **Reader A heard:** the people are brought *back* to an artlessness they once had.
- **Reader B read:** the aim was to make them simple. Nothing says they had been simple before.
- **The divergence:** *Return* adds a fall and a restoration. The Chinese has only 將以 (*jiāng yǐ* — "meant to, would use it to") and the causative 愚 (*yú* — make guileless). Neither commentator says *return*. Wang Bi: 愚謂無知守真 (*yú wèi wú zhī shǒu zhēn* — "'guileless' means without cunning, **keeping** to the true"). Heshang Gong: 使朴質不詐偽 (*shǐ pǔ zhì bù zhà wěi* — "make them plain and not false").
- **Logged?** No. The notes on 愚 (*yú* — guileless) → *guileless* and 明 (*míng* — clear-seeing) → *clever* do not mention *return*.
- **Direction:** *"They used it to make them guileless."*

### Ch 73 · A 11 / B 6 · LINK · minor
- **Chinese:** 天網恢恢，疏而不失 (*tiān wǎng huī huī, shū ér bù shī* — "nature's net is vast; wide-meshed, yet it loses nothing")
- **English:** "Nature's net is vast. / Its mesh is wide, and nothing slips through."
- **Reader A heard:** *and*, not *yet*. Reader A noted that the paradox is *"left implicit."*
- **Reader B read:** a concession: coarse-meshed, yet nothing gets lost.
- **The divergence:** 而 (*ér*) here is adversative, and the paradox is the point. The chapter's own series renders the same 而 (*ér* — and / yet) as *yet* four times two lines earlier (*"does not contend, yet is masterful at overcoming"* …). The last member is the only one that goes flat.
- **Logged?** No. `notes/translation.md` → *Ch 73 · 繟然* (*chǎn rán* — unhurried) discusses the net's meaning, not its connective.
- **Direction:** *"Its mesh is wide, yet nothing slips through."*

#### Ch 74 · A 3 / B 2 · LOCK · logged

- **English:** "Who would still push?"
- **Reader A heard:** *"'push' is vague"*. Reader A could not tell whether it meant transgress or kill.
- **Reader B read:** a real double reading: dare to deviate (Heshang Gong: 誰敢有犯者 (*shuí gǎn yǒu fàn zhě* — "who would dare offend")) or dare to do the killing, which leads into the next line.

#### Ch 55 · A 9 / B 8 · LOCK · logged
- **Chinese:** 心使氣曰強 (*xīn shǐ qì yuē qiáng* — "the heart driving the breath is called forcing")
- **English:** "When the heart drives the vital energy, that is called strong."
- **Reader A heard:** an open term, *"neutral or positive … or (given the surrounding lines) a negative term meaning forced, overbearing."*

- **Logged?** Yes: `glossary/qiang-強.md`, which asks whether the one beat of misheard approval *"is a flaw or the point."* The cold reader heard the beat and resolved it from context. **This is evidence for the lock, not against it.**

## Readers' errors

Places where a reader misread, or flagged something the two languages share, so the comparison was void there:

- **Reader A, ch 77 Unit 8**, paraphrased *"Who has more than enough"* as *"asks who **can** give their surplus."* The reader restored the 能 (*néng* — can) the English drops, which hid accuracy-audit finding 9 from the comparison.
- **Reader A, ch 63 Unit 10**, paraphrased *"the sage still finds it difficult"* as *"treats things as difficult,"* which is the Chinese 猶難之 (*yóu nán zhī* — "still treats it as hard") rather than the English's *finds*. A reader who takes *finds* as *experiences* gets a sage who struggles. This one is not reported, because the reader did not.
- **Reader A, ch 64 Units 5 / 10**, flagged a contradiction: *handle* is commanded, then condemned. The Chinese has the same 為 (*wéi* — to handle) in both, 為之於未有 (*wéi zhī yú wèi yǒu* — "handle it before it exists") and 為者敗之 (*wéi zhě bài zhī* — "those who handle it ruin it"). The tension is the text's, not the translation's.
- **Reader A, ch 56 Unit 8**, flagged a tension: *cannot be honored* sits beside *the most honored*. The Chinese has 貴 (*guì* — prize, honor) in both places, and Reader B flags the same tension. Void.
- **Reader B**: no misreading found in 55–81 that voided a comparison. Reader B's glosses of 見 (*xiàn* — display) at ch 72 and ch 77 correct the packet's pinyin *jiàn* (see), and they are right.
