# Chapter 43

**The chapter as a whole:** The softest thing in the world runs freely through the hardest, and what has no substance gets into what has no gap. From this the speaker ("I") concludes that non-doing has benefit. The wordless teaching and the benefit of non-doing are matched or attained by few in the world.

### Unit 1
> 天下之至柔，馳騁天下之至堅。 (*tiān xià zhī zhì róu, chí chěng tiān xià zhī zhì jiān* — "the utmost-soft of under-heaven gallops over / through the utmost-hard of under-heaven")
- **Says:** The softest thing in the world races 馳騁 (*chí chěng* — "to gallop, to ride at will") over or through the hardest thing in the world.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the "utmost soft" 至柔 (*zhì róu* — "the softest") acts on the "utmost hard" 至堅 (*zhì jiān* — "the hardest"); neither is named.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 馳騁 (*chí chěng* — "gallop / drive") can be read as "rides roughshod over, masters" or as "runs freely through, penetrates." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong names the pair: 至柔者水，至堅金石 (*zhì róu zhě shuǐ, zhì jiān jīn shí* — "the utmost soft is water; the utmost hard is metal and stone"), 水能貫堅入剛 (*shuǐ néng guàn jiān rù gāng* — "water can pierce the hard and enter the rigid"), so penetration. Wang Bi gives two: 氣無所不入，水無所不出於經 (*qì wú suǒ bù rù, shuǐ wú suǒ bù chū yú jīng* — "breath / qi has nowhere it does not enter; water has nowhere it does not pass through").

### Unit 2
> 無有入無間，吾是以知無為之有益。 (*wú yǒu rù wú jiàn, wú shì yǐ zhī wú wéi zhī yǒu yì* — "no-having enters no-gap; I by this know that non-doing has benefit")
- **Says:** What has no substance enters where there is no gap; by this I know that non-doing 無為 (*wú wéi* — "not doing / non-action") is beneficial. Negation: 無 (*wú* — "without") three times, each negating existence or action: 無有 (*wú yǒu* — "no having / nothing"), 無間 (*wú jiàn* — "no gap"), 無為 (*wú wéi* — "no doing").
- **Act:** asserts (an observation, then an inference drawn from it)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 無有 (*wú yǒu* — "that which has nothing / is without being") enters 無間 (*wú jiàn* — "that which has no crack"); the knower is 吾 (*wú* — "I"), the speaker.
- **Force:** always/universal (first clause); the inference is stated as present knowledge
- **Link:** 是以 (*shì yǐ* — "by this / therefore") inside the unit draws the conclusion from Unit 1 and the first clause; to Unit 1 itself: and (a second instance)
- **Open:** 無有 (*wú yǒu* — "no-having") is either "the substanceless" in general or a name for the Tao. **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 無有謂道也，道無形質 (*wú yǒu wèi dào yě, dào wú xíng zhì* — "'no-having' means the Tao; the Tao has no form or substance"). Wang Bi keeps it general: 虛無柔弱無所不通 (*xū wú róu ruò wú suǒ bù tōng* — "the empty, the soft and the weak have nowhere they do not pass through"). Wang Bi also records the Huainanzi reading 出於無有入於無間 (*chū yú wú yǒu rù yú wú jiàn* — "comes out of no-having and enters no-gap").

### Unit 3
> 不言之教，無為之益，天下希及之。 (*bù yán zhī jiào, wú wéi zhī yì, tiān xià xī jí zhī* — "the teaching of not speaking, the benefit of non-doing: under-heaven rarely reaches it")
- **Says:** The teaching that uses no words and the benefit that comes from non-doing are rarely reached (or matched) in the world. Negation: 不 (*bù* — "not") negates the act of speaking; 無 (*wú* — "without") negates doing.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天下 (*tiān xià* — "under heaven / the world": its people) is the subject of 希及 (*xī jí* — "rarely reach"); 之 (*zhī* — "it") is the teaching and the benefit.
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and (extends the conclusion of Unit 2)
- **Open:** 希及之 (*xī jí zhī* — "rarely reach it") is either "few people attain it" or "few things come up to it, it is matched by little in the world." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong reads 天下 (*tiān xià* — "under heaven") as 人主 (*rén zhǔ* — "rulers of men"): 希能有及道無為之治身治國 (*xī néng yǒu jí dào wú wéi zhī zhì shēn zhì guó* — "few can attain the Tao's non-doing in governing the body and governing the state").

# Chapter 44

**The chapter as a whole:** Three questions weigh reputation against one's own person, one's person against goods, and gaining against losing. Hence excessive attachment costs greatly and much hoarding loses heavily. Knowing what is enough and knowing when to stop keeps one from disgrace and danger and lets one last long.

### Unit 1
> 名與身孰親？ (*míng yǔ shēn shú qīn* — "name and body: which is closer?")
- **Says:** Of reputation 名 (*míng* — "name, fame") and one's own person 身 (*shēn* — "body, self"), which is nearer and dearer?
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical: implies the self is closer than fame)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; addressed to anyone, the reader
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none. **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 尚名好高其身必疏 (*shàng míng hào gāo qí shēn bì shū* — "who prizes name and loves height will surely be estranged from his own person").

### Unit 2
> 身與貨孰多？ (*shēn yǔ huò shú duō* — "body and goods: which is more?")
- **Says:** Of one's own person and goods 貨 (*huò* — "goods, wealth"), which counts for more?
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical: implies the self outweighs wealth)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; anyone
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 多 (*duō* — "much, more") is "more valuable" in the plain question. **Commentators:** Wang Bi reads it as quantity of self diminished: 貪貨無厭其身必少 (*tān huò wú yàn qí shēn bì shǎo* — "greedy for goods without satiety, one's person surely becomes less").

### Unit 3
> 得與亡孰病？ (*dé yǔ wáng shú bìng* — "getting and losing: which is the sickness?")
- **Says:** Of gaining and losing, which is the harm?
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical: implies gaining is the real harm, against expectation)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; anyone
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 亡 (*wáng* — "lose, perish") may be losing in general or losing oneself. **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 得多利而亡其身，何者為病也 (*dé duō lì ér wáng qí shēn, hé zhě wéi bìng yě* — "gaining much profit yet losing one's own person: which is the sickness?"). Heshang Gong: 好得利則病於行也 (*hào dé lì zé bìng yú xíng yě* — "loving to gain profit, one is sick in conduct").

### Unit 4
> 是故甚愛必大費，多藏必厚亡。 (*shì gù shèn ài bì dà fèi, duō cáng bì hòu wáng* — "therefore extreme love must greatly spend; much storing must heavily lose")
- **Says:** Excessive attachment necessarily costs greatly; hoarding much necessarily leads to heavy loss.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; anyone who loves too much or hoards
- **Force:** must/necessary 必 (*bì* — "surely, necessarily")
- **Link:** therefore 是故 (*shì gù* — "for this reason")
- **Open:** 甚愛 (*shèn ài* — "loving too much") has no stated object: fame, goods, oneself, or anything. **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 甚愛色費精神，甚愛財遇禍患 (*shèn ài sè fèi jīng shén, shèn ài cái yù huò huàn* — "loving sex too much spends the vital spirit; loving wealth too much meets disaster"). Wang Bi: 甚愛不與物通 (*shèn ài bù yǔ wù tōng* — "loving too much, one does not share with things"), 求之者多，攻之者衆 (*qiú zhī zhě duō, gōng zhī zhě zhòng* — "those who seek it are many, those who attack it are a crowd").

### Unit 5
> 知足不辱，知止不殆，可以長久。 (*zhī zú bù rǔ, zhī zhǐ bù dài, kě yǐ cháng jiǔ* — "knowing enough, not disgraced; knowing stopping, not endangered; can thereby be long and lasting")
- **Says:** Whoever knows what is enough is not disgraced; whoever knows when to stop is not endangered; such a one can last a long time. Negation: 不 (*bù* — "not") twice, negating the states of being disgraced 辱 (*rǔ* — "disgrace") and being endangered 殆 (*dài* — "danger, peril").
- **Act:** if/when … then (implied: if one knows enough, then no disgrace)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; one in general
- **Force:** always/universal; 可以 (*kě yǐ* — "can, is able to") gives can/possible for the last clause
- **Link:** none stated; contrasts with Unit 4
- **Open:** none

# Chapter 45

**The chapter as a whole:** The greatest completion looks lacking, the greatest fullness looks empty, and both are inexhaustible in use; the greatest straightness, skill and eloquence look bent, clumsy and tongue-tied. Agitation overcomes cold and stillness overcomes heat. Clear stillness is the norm, or the rectifier, of the world.

### Unit 1
> 大成若缺，其用不弊。 (*dà chéng ruò quē, qí yòng bù bì* — "great completion seems lacking; its use does not wear out")
- **Says:** The greatest completion seems incomplete or broken, yet its usefulness never wears out. Negation: 不 (*bù* — "not") negates wearing out 弊 (*bì* — "worn out, exhausted").
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 大成 (*dà chéng* — "great completion"): a thing or a person, unstated
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** whether 大成 (*dà chéng* — "great completion") is an abstract quality or a person. **Commentators:** Heshang Gong reads a person: 道德大成之君 (*dào dé dà chéng zhī jūn* — "the lord whose Way and virtue are greatly complete"), who 滅名藏譽 (*miè míng cáng yù* — "extinguishes his name and hides his praise"), and his lemma writes the second character in a variant form of 缺 (*quē* — "lacking"). Wang Bi reads a quality: 隨物而成，不為一象，故若缺也 (*suí wù ér chéng, bù wéi yī xiàng, gù ruò quē yě* — "it is completed by following things and does not make itself one image; so it seems lacking"). Wang Bi's lemma has a lost character where the base has 弊 (*bì* — "worn out").

### Unit 2
> 大盈若沖，其用不窮。 (*dà yíng ruò chōng, qí yòng bù qióng* — "great fullness seems empty; its use is not exhausted")
- **Says:** The greatest fullness seems empty, yet its use is never used up. Negation: 不 (*bù* — "not") negates being exhausted 窮 (*qióng* — "to come to an end").
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 大盈 (*dà yíng* — "great fullness"), unstated thing or person
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel to Unit 1)
- **Open:** 沖 (*chōng* — "empty, hollow; also 'pour, surge'") is "empty like a vessel." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong again reads a person, the 沖 (*chōng* — "empty") as humility: 貴不敢驕也，富不敢奢也 (*guì bù gǎn jiāo yě, fù bù gǎn shē yě* — "noble, he does not dare be arrogant; rich, he does not dare be extravagant"). Wang Bi: 隨物而與，無所愛矜，故若沖也 (*suí wù ér yǔ, wú suǒ ài jīn, gù ruò chōng yě* — "it gives according to things, holding nothing back with pride; so it seems empty").

### Unit 3
> 大直若屈，大巧若拙，大辯若訥。 (*dà zhí ruò qū, dà qiǎo ruò zhuō, dà biàn ruò nè* — "great straightness seems bent; great skill seems clumsy; great eloquence seems tongue-tied")
- **Says:** The greatest straightness looks bent, the greatest skill looks clumsy, the greatest eloquence looks halting.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the qualities themselves; a possessor unstated
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 辯 (*biàn* — "eloquence; argument, disputation") may be fine speech or skill in debate. **Commentators:** Heshang Gong makes all three a person's restraint: 不與俗人爭 (*bù yǔ sú rén zhēng* — "he does not contend with common people"), 不敢見其能 (*bù gǎn xiàn qí néng* — "he does not dare show his ability"). Wang Bi makes them ways of following things: 大巧因自然以成器 (*dà qiǎo yīn zì rán yǐ chéng qì* — "great skill goes along with the so-of-itself to complete the vessel").

### Unit 4
> 躁勝寒，靜勝熱。 (*zào shèng hán, jìng shèng rè* — "agitation overcomes cold; stillness overcomes heat")
- **Says:** Restless movement overcomes cold; stillness overcomes heat.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 躁 (*zào* — "agitation, restless movement") and 靜 (*jìng* — "stillness"), as forces
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 勝 (*shèng* — "overcome, beat") is plainly "overcomes." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong reads 勝 (*shèng* — "overcome") as 極 (*jí* — "reach the extreme"): 勝極也 (*shèng jí yě* — "勝 (*shèng* — 'overcome') means at the extreme"); agitation at its extreme turns to cold, 萬物盛大極則寒 (*wàn wù shèng dà jí zé hán* — "the myriad things flourish, and at the extreme comes cold"), and stillness at its extreme turns to heat, 熱者生之源 (*rè zhě shēng zhī yuán* — "heat is the source of life"). So for him the unit describes reversal, with the moral 人不當剛躁 (*rén bù dāng gāng zào* — "people should not be hard and agitated"). Wang Bi keeps "overcome" but limits agitation: 躁罷然後勝寒，靜無為以勝熱 (*zào bà rán hòu shèng hán, jìng wú wéi yǐ shèng rè* — "agitation, worn out, only then overcomes cold; stillness, doing nothing, thereby overcomes heat").

### Unit 5
> 清靜為天下正。 (*qīng jìng wéi tiān xià zhèng* — "clear stillness is / becomes the straight of under-heaven")
- **Says:** Clarity and stillness are the norm of the world, or make one the one who sets the world right.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 清靜 (*qīng jìng* — "clear and still"): a quality, or one who is clear and still; unstated
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none stated; follows from Unit 4 (stillness)
- **Open:** 為 (*wéi* — "is / becomes / acts as") with 正 (*zhèng* — "straight, correct; the standard; the one who rectifies, the head") gives "is the standard for the world" or "becomes the rectifier / head of the world." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 能清靜則為天下長，持正則無終已時也 (*néng qīng jìng zé wéi tiān xià zhǎng, chí zhèng zé wú zhōng yǐ shí yě* — "if one can be clear and still, one becomes the elder of the world; holding to the correct, there is no time of ending"), a person who leads. Wang Bi: 靜則全物之真 (*jìng zé quán wù zhī zhēn* — "stillness keeps whole the genuineness of things"), 惟清靜乃得如上諸大 (*wéi qīng jìng nǎi dé rú shàng zhū dà* — "only clear stillness attains all the 'greats' above").

# Chapter 46

**The chapter as a whole:** When the world has the Way, running horses are turned back to manure the fields; when it lacks the Way, war horses breed on the borders. No calamity is greater than not knowing what is enough, no fault greater than wanting to get. So the enough of knowing enough is lasting enough.

### Unit 1
> 天下有道，卻走馬以糞。 (*tiān xià yǒu dào, què zǒu mǎ yǐ fèn* — "under-heaven has the Way: turns back running horses to manure")
- **Says:** When the world has the Way, galloping (war or courier) horses are sent back to work manuring the fields.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天下 (*tiān xià* — "the world") has the Way; the one who 卻 (*què* — "drives back, withdraws") the horses is unstated, most likely the ruler / the state
- **Force:** habitual (whenever the condition holds)
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 糞 (*fèn* — "dung; to fertilize") as "to fertilize the fields." **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 卻走馬以治田糞也 (*què zǒu mǎ yǐ zhì tián fèn yě* — "turns back the running horses to work and manure the fields"). Heshang Gong reads 天下 (*tiān xià* — "under heaven") as 人主 (*rén zhǔ* — "the ruler"): 謂人主有道也 (*wèi rén zhǔ yǒu dào yě* — "it means the ruler has the Way"), and adds a body reading: 治身者卻陽精以糞其身 (*zhì shēn zhě què yáng jīng yǐ fèn qí shēn* — "one who governs the body turns back the yang essence to fertilize the body"). Han Fei: 遽傳不用 (*jù chuán bù yòng* — "the express couriers are not used").

### Unit 2
> 天下無道，戎馬生於郊。 (*tiān xià wú dào, róng mǎ shēng yú jiāo* — "under-heaven lacks the Way: war horses are born in the outskirts")
- **Says:** When the world lacks the Way, war horses are born (or live) in the border fields. Negation: 無 (*wú* — "lacks") negates possession of the Way.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天下 (*tiān xià* — "the world"); 戎馬 (*róng mǎ* — "war horses")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** but (contrast with Unit 1)
- **Open:** 生 (*shēng* — "be born / live") and 郊 (*jiāo* — "outskirts, border land") divide. **Commentators:** Han Fei 解老 (*jiě lǎo* — "Explaining Laozi"): war horses run short, so 牸馬出 (*zì mǎ chū* — "the mares come out", i.e., breeding mares go to war), and 郊者，言其近也 (*jiāo zhě, yán qí jìn yě* — "'outskirts' speaks of nearness"): even foaling mares are used, close at hand. Heshang Gong: 戎馬生於郊境之上久不還也 (*róng mǎ shēng yú jiāo jìng zhī shàng jiǔ bù huán yě* — "war horses live on the frontier and long do not return"), taking 生 (*shēng* — "be born") as "dwell." Han Fei 喻老 (*yù lǎo* — "Illustrating Laozi") also: 甲胄生蟣虱 (*jiǎ zhòu shēng jǐ shī* — "armor and helmets breed lice"), soldiers never coming home.

### Unit 3
> 禍莫大於不知足； (*huò mò dà yú bù zhī zú* — "calamity: none greater than not knowing enough")
- **Says:** No calamity is greater than not knowing what is enough. Negation: 莫 (*mò* — "none, nothing") negates any greater calamity; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates knowing what suffices.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the one who does not know enough: unstated; anyone, likely the ruler in context
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none stated; it gives the cause behind Unit 2
- **Open:** none. Note: Wang Bi records that the Heshang Gong text has before this line 罪莫大於可欲 (*zuì mò dà yú kě yù* — "no crime greater than the desirable / than what can be desired"); Heshang Gong glosses it 好淫色也 (*hào yín sè yě* — "loving lust"). The base lacks it.

### Unit 4
> 咎莫大於欲得。 (*jiù mò dà yú yù dé* — "fault: none greater than wanting to get")
- **Says:** No fault (or misfortune) is greater than the desire to acquire. Negation: 莫 (*mò* — "none") negates any greater fault.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; anyone
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** 咎 (*jiù* — "blame, fault; misfortune, harm") is either moral fault or the harm one brings on oneself. Wang Bi notes Han Fei's text has a different character for 大 (*dà* — "great") here.

### Unit 5
> 故知足之足，常足矣。 (*gù zhī zú zhī zú, cháng zú yǐ* — "so the enough of knowing enough is constant enough")
- **Says:** Therefore the sufficiency that comes from knowing what is enough is a lasting sufficiency.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; anyone who knows enough
- **Force:** always/universal 常 (*cháng* — "always, constant")
- **Link:** therefore 故 (*gù* — "so")
- **Open:** 常 (*cháng* — "constant; always") may be "always enough" (never lacking) or "the constant enough," sufficiency of the enduring kind.

# Chapter 47

**The chapter as a whole:** Without going out the door one knows the world; without looking out the window one sees heaven's way. The farther one goes out, the less one knows. So the sage knows without travelling, names without seeing, completes without doing.

### Unit 1
> 不出戶，知天下； (*bù chū hù, zhī tiān xià* — "not going out the door, knows under-heaven")
- **Says:** Without going out of one's door, one knows the world. Negation: 不 (*bù* — "not") negates going out.
- **Act:** asserts (with a condition: even without going out)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; "one," then specified in Unit 4 as the sage
- **Force:** can/possible (Han Fei's text has 可以 (*kě yǐ* — "can"): 不出於戶，可以知天下 (*bù chū yú hù, kě yǐ zhī tiān xià* — "not going out of the door, one can know the world")); the base states it plainly
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none. **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 以己身知人身，以己家知人家 (*yǐ jǐ shēn zhī rén shēn, yǐ jǐ jiā zhī rén jiā* — "by one's own person one knows other persons, by one's own house other houses"). Han Fei reads the door and window as the senses: 空竅者，神明之戶牖也 (*kōng qiào zhě, shén míng zhī hù yǒu yě* — "the hollow openings are the doors and windows of the spirit").

### Unit 2
> 不闚牖，見天道。 (*bù kuī yǒu, jiàn tiān dào* — "not peeping through the window, sees heaven's way")
- **Says:** Without peering out of the window, one sees the way of heaven. Negation: 不 (*bù* — "not") negates peeping.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; "one"
- **Force:** can/possible (as Unit 1)
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 天道 (*tiān dào* — "heaven's way") as the pattern of the heavens / nature, or the Way generally. **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 天道與人道同 (*tiān dào yǔ rén dào tóng* — "heaven's way and the human way are the same"); 人君清淨天氣自正 (*rén jūn qīng jìng tiān qì zì zhèng* — "when the ruler is clear and still, heaven's breath rights itself").

### Unit 3
> 其出彌遠，其知彌少。 (*qí chū mí yuǎn, qí zhī mí shǎo* — "one's going out ever farther, one's knowing ever less")
- **Says:** The farther one goes out, the less one knows.
- **Act:** asserts (a correlation: the more X, the more Y)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 其 (*qí* — "one's / their"), unstated; anyone
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none stated; contrast with Units 1–2
- **Open:** none. **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 無在於一而求之於衆也 (*wú zài yú yī ér qiú zhī yú zhòng yě* — "nothing is in the one, and one seeks it in the many"), 出愈遠愈迷 (*chū yù yuǎn yù mí* — "the farther one goes out, the more lost").

### Unit 4
> 是以聖人不行而知，不見而名，不為而成。 (*shì yǐ shèng rén bù xíng ér zhī, bù jiàn ér míng, bù wéi ér chéng* — "therefore the sage, not going, knows; not seeing, names; not doing, completes")
- **Says:** So the sage knows without travelling, names without seeing, and accomplishes without acting. Negation: 不 (*bù* — "not") three times, negating going, seeing, doing.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage") knows, names, completes; what is completed is unstated
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore 是以 (*shì yǐ* — "for this reason")
- **Open:** (a) 名 (*míng* — "to name") as "can name / identify things rightly." **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 是非之理可得而名也 (*shì fēi zhī lǐ kě dé ér míng yě* — "the pattern of right and wrong can be named"). (b) 成 (*chéng* — "complete") may have the sage as doer ("the sage completes things") or things as subject ("things get done"). **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 明物之性，因之而已，故雖不為而使之成矣 (*míng wù zhī xìng, yīn zhī ér yǐ, gù suī bù wéi ér shǐ zhī chéng yǐ* — "clear on the nature of things, the sage only goes along with them; so though not doing, the sage causes them to be completed"); Heshang Gong (lemma 無為而成 (*wú wéi ér chéng* — "without doing, completes")): 萬物自化成也 (*wàn wù zì huà chéng yě* — "the myriad things transform and complete themselves"). Wang Bi also notes one text has 至 (*zhì* — "arrives") for 知 (*zhī* — "knows").

# Chapter 48

**The chapter as a whole:** Pursuing learning adds something every day; pursuing the Way takes something away every day, and the subtraction goes on until it reaches non-doing, where nothing is left undone. Taking the world always works through having no business; once one has business, one is not fit to take the world.

### Unit 1
> 為學日益，為道日損。 (*wéi xué rì yì, wéi dào rì sǔn* — "doing learning, daily increase; doing the Way, daily decrease")
- **Says:** One who pursues learning gains more each day; one who pursues the Way loses more each day.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; anyone who pursues 為 (*wéi* — "do, engage in") learning or the Way
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** what increases or decreases is unstated: knowledge and skill, or desires and ornament. **Commentators:** Wang Bi: learning 務欲進其所能，益其所習 (*wù yù jìn qí suǒ néng, yì qí suǒ xí* — "strives to advance what one can do and add to what one practices"); the Way 務欲反虛無 (*wù yù fǎn xū wú* — "strives to return to emptiness and nothing"). Heshang Gong: 學 (*xué* — "learning") is 政教禮樂之學 (*zhèng jiào lǐ yuè zhī xué* — "the learning of government, instruction, ritual and music"), and what grows is 情欲文飾 (*qíng yù wén shì* — "passions, desires and ornament").

### Unit 2
> 損之又損，以至於無為。 (*sǔn zhī yòu sǔn, yǐ zhì yú wú wéi* — "decrease it and again decrease, thereby arriving at non-doing")
- **Says:** Decrease and decrease again, until you arrive at non-doing. Negation: 無 (*wú* — "without") negates doing.
- **Act:** asserts (a process); can be read as advises
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; the one pursuing the Way; 之 (*zhī* — "it") is whatever is being reduced
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and (continues 為道日損 (*wéi dào rì sǔn* — "doing the Way, daily decrease"))
- **Open:** none

### Unit 3
> 無為而無不為。 (*wú wéi ér wú bù wéi* — "no doing, and yet nothing not done")
- **Says:** Doing nothing, yet nothing is left undone. Negation: 無 (*wú* — "without") negates doing; 無不 (*wú bù* — "there is nothing that is not") is a double negation: there is nothing not done.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; the one who has reached non-doing
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and 而 (*ér* — "and yet" inside the unit); to Unit 2: and (states what non-doing is)
- **Open:** whether 無不為 (*wú bù wéi* — "nothing not done") means the agent does everything or everything gets done. **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 有為則有所失，故無為乃無所不為也 (*yǒu wéi zé yǒu suǒ shī, gù wú wéi nǎi wú suǒ bù wéi yě* — "with doing there is something lost; so non-doing is then nothing left undone"). Heshang Gong: 德與道合則無所不施無所不為也 (*dé yǔ dào hé zé wú suǒ bù shī wú suǒ bù wéi yě* — "when virtue joins the Way, there is nothing it does not bestow and nothing it does not do").

### Unit 4
> 取天下常以無事，及其有事，不足以取天下。 (*qǔ tiān xià cháng yǐ wú shì, jí qí yǒu shì, bù zú yǐ qǔ tiān xià* — "taking under-heaven, always by no business; when it comes to having business, not enough to take under-heaven")
- **Says:** One takes the world always by having no business (no undertakings); once one has business, one is not equal to taking the world. Negation: 無 (*wú* — "without") negates having affairs; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates being sufficient.
- **Act:** asserts, then if/when … then 及 (*jí* — "when it comes to")
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; whoever would take the world, most likely the ruler
- **Force:** always/universal 常 (*cháng* — "always")
- **Link:** none stated; applies non-doing to rule
- **Open:** 取 (*qǔ* — "take, seize, win") may be winning the world or governing it. **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 取，治也 (*qǔ, zhì yě* — "取 (*qǔ* — 'take') means to govern"), 治天下常當以無事，不當煩勞也 (*zhì tiān xià cháng dāng yǐ wú shì, bù dāng fán láo yě* — "governing the world should always be by having no business, not by vexing labor"). Wang Bi: 動常因也 (*dòng cháng yīn yě* — "in moving, always go along"), and 有事 (*yǒu shì* — "having business") is 自己造也 (*zì jǐ zào yě* — "making things up oneself").

# Chapter 49

**The chapter as a whole:** The sage has no fixed heart-mind of their own and takes the people's heart-mind as theirs. The speaker treats the good and the not-good alike as good, the trustworthy and untrustworthy alike as trustworthy, and so virtue (or what is gained) is good and trustworthy. In the world the sage draws in and blends their heart-mind for the world's sake; the people all strain their ears and eyes, and the sage treats them all as children.

### Unit 1
> 聖人無常心，以百姓心為心。 (*shèng rén wú cháng xīn, yǐ bǎi xìng xīn wéi xīn* — "the sage has no constant heart; takes the hundred surnames' heart as heart")
- **Says:** The sage has no fixed heart-mind and makes the heart-mind of the common people their own. Negation: 無 (*wú* — "has no") negates possession of a constant heart-mind 常心 (*cháng xīn* — "constant / fixed heart-mind").
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage"); 百姓 (*bǎi xìng* — "the hundred surnames, the common people")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 無常心 (*wú cháng xīn* — "no constant heart") may mean "no fixed opinions of their own" or "no heart-mind that is constantly their own." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 聖人重改更，貴因循，若自無心 (*shèng rén zhòng gǎi gēng, guì yīn xún, ruò zì wú xīn* — "the sage is wary of change and prizes going along, as if having no heart of their own"). Wang Bi: 動常因也 (*dòng cháng yīn yě* — "in moving, always going along").

### Unit 2
> 善者，吾善之； (*shàn zhě, wú shàn zhī* — "the good ones: I good them")
- **Says:** Those who are good, I treat as good.
- **Act:** asserts (in the first person)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 吾 (*wú* — "I"), the speaker, most likely the sage speaking (following Unit 1); 善者 (*shàn zhě* — "those who are good")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none stated; elaborates Unit 1
- **Open:** 善之 (*shàn zhī* — "good them") is "treat them as good / am good to them." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 百姓為善，聖人因而善之 (*bǎi xìng wéi shàn, shèng rén yīn ér shàn zhī* — "the people do good, and the sage, going along, treats them as good").

### Unit 3
> 不善者，吾亦善之； (*bù shàn zhě, wú yì shàn zhī* — "the not-good ones: I also good them")
- **Says:** Those who are not good, I also treat as good. Negation: 不 (*bù* — "not") negates being good (an identity / quality of the people).
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 吾 (*wú* — "I"); 不善者 (*bù shàn zhě* — "those who are not good")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and 亦 (*yì* — "also")
- **Open:** "treat them as good" (acceptance) or "make them good" (transform). **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 聖人化之使善也 (*shèng rén huà zhī shǐ shàn yě* — "the sage transforms them and makes them good"). Wang Bi: 各因其用則善不失也 (*gè yīn qí yòng zé shàn bù shī yě* — "if each is used according to their use, the good is not lost").

### Unit 4
> 德善。 (*dé shàn* — "virtue good")
- **Says:** Virtue is good, or goodness is gained.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** therefore (implied: the result of Units 2–3)
- **Open:** 德 (*dé* — "virtue, power") is either the word itself ("virtue is goodness / this virtue is good") or a loan for 得 (*dé* — "to get"), giving "goodness is attained." **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 無棄人也 (*wú qì rén yě* — "no one is cast away"), and notes one text adds 矣 (*yǐ* — final particle of completion). Heshang Gong: 百姓德化，聖人為善 (*bǎi xìng dé huà, shèng rén wéi shàn* — "the people are transformed by virtue; the sage acts as good"), keeping 德 (*dé* — "virtue").

### Unit 5
> 信者，吾信之； (*xìn zhě, wú xìn zhī* — "the trustworthy ones: I trust them")
- **Says:** Those who are trustworthy, I trust.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 吾 (*wú* — "I"); 信者 (*xìn zhě* — "those who are trustworthy / truthful")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and (parallel to Unit 2)
- **Open:** none

### Unit 6
> 不信者，吾亦信之； (*bù xìn zhě, wú yì xìn zhī* — "the untrustworthy ones: I also trust them")
- **Says:** Those who are not trustworthy, I also trust. Negation: 不 (*bù* — "not") negates being trustworthy.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 吾 (*wú* — "I"); 不信者 (*bù xìn zhě* — "those not trustworthy")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and 亦 (*yì* — "also")
- **Open:** "trust them" or "make them trustworthy." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 聖人化之使信也 (*shèng rén huà zhī shǐ xìn yě* — "the sage transforms them and makes them trustworthy").

### Unit 7
> 德信。 (*dé xìn* — "virtue trustworthy")
- **Says:** Virtue is trustworthy, or trustworthiness is gained.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** therefore (implied)
- **Open:** as Unit 4: 德 (*dé* — "virtue") or 得 (*dé* — "get"). Wang Bi notes one text adds 矣 (*yǐ* — final particle).

### Unit 8
> 聖人在天下，歙歙焉，為天下渾其心，百姓皆注其耳目，聖人皆孩之。 (*shèng rén zài tiān xià, xī xī yān, wéi tiān xià hún qí xīn, bǎi xìng jiē zhù qí ěr mù, shèng rén jiē hái zhī* — "the sage, being in under-heaven, drawn-in, drawn-in; for under-heaven blurs their heart; the hundred surnames all fix their ears and eyes; the sage children them all")
- **Says:** The sage, in the world, is withdrawn and drawn in, and for the world's sake blends (muddies) their heart-mind; the people all turn their ears and eyes intently; the sage treats them all as children.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage") draws in, blends, treats as children; 百姓 (*bǎi xìng* — "the people") fix their ears and eyes
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none stated; returns from the "I" to the sage of Unit 1
- **Open:** (a) 歙歙 (*xī xī* — "drawing in, contracting, inhaling") is the sage's reserve; Heshang Gong's text reads 怵怵 (*chù chù* — "anxious, fearful"): 常恐怖富貴不敢驕奢 (*cháng kǒng bù fù guì bù gǎn jiāo shē* — "always fearful; rich and noble, not daring to be arrogant or extravagant"); the Shiwen records 惵惵 (*dié dié* — "fearful") (reported by Wang Bi's editor). (b) 渾其心 (*hún qí xīn* — "blur / merge its heart"): whose heart, the sage's own or the world's? **Commentators:** both make it the sage's own: Wang Bi 為天下渾心焉，意無所適莫也 (*wéi tiān xià hún xīn yān, yì wú suǒ shì mò yě* — "for the world the sage blends the heart, the intent having no favoring and no disfavoring"); Heshang Gong 渾濁其心若愚闇不通也 (*hún zhuó qí xīn ruò yú àn bù tōng yě* — "muddies the heart as if dull and dark, not penetrating"). (c) 注其耳目 (*zhù qí ěr mù* — "fix / pour their ears and eyes"): onto the sage, or each using their own senses. **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 注，用也，百姓皆用其耳目為聖人視聽也 (*zhù, yòng yě, bǎi xìng jiē yòng qí ěr mù wèi shèng rén shì tīng yě* — "注 (*zhù* — 'fix, pour') means use: the people all use their ears and eyes to see and hear for the sage"); Wang Bi: 各用聰明 (*gè yòng cōng míng* — "each uses their own hearing and sight"). (d) 孩之 (*hái zhī* — "child them"): "treat them as infants (cherish)" or "make them childlike." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 愛念百姓如孩嬰赤子 (*ài niàn bǎi xìng rú hái yīng chì zǐ* — "loves and cares for the people like infants and newborns"); Wang Bi: 皆使和而無欲如嬰兒也 (*jiē shǐ hé ér wú yù rú yīng ér yě* — "makes them all harmonious and desireless like infants"); the Shiwen says Wang Bi's text read 咳 (*hái* — "an infant's laugh / to smile at as at an infant"). Wang Bi's editor notes that many texts add 之 (*zhī* — possessive particle) after 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage"), without change of sense.

# Chapter 50

**The chapter as a whole:** Coming out is life and going in is death. Three in ten (or thirteen) are followers of life, as many of death, and as many of those who live move into the ground of death, because they live life too thickly. One good at holding on to life meets no rhinoceros or tiger and takes no wound in war, because in them there is no ground of death.

### Unit 1
> 出生入死。 (*chū shēng rù sǐ* — "go out, live; go in, die")
- **Says:** Coming out is life; going in is death (or: one comes out into life and goes into death).
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; any living being
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** the grammar allows "coming out is birth, going in is death," "leaving life, one enters death," or "out of life, into death." **Commentators:** Han Fei: 人始於生而卒於死。始之謂出，卒之謂入 (*rén shǐ yú shēng ér zú yú sǐ. shǐ zhī wèi chū, zú zhī wèi rù* — "people begin in life and end in death; the beginning is called coming out, the ending is called going in"). Wang Bi: 出生地入死地 (*chū shēng dì rù sǐ dì* — "leaving the ground of life, entering the ground of death"). Heshang Gong: 情欲出 (*qíng yù chū* — "passions go out") means life, 情欲入於胸臆 (*qíng yù rù yú xiōng yì* — "passions enter the chest") means death.

### Unit 2
> 生之徒，十有三； (*shēng zhī tú, shí yǒu sān* — "life's companions: ten have three")
- **Says:** The followers of life are three in ten (or thirteen).
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 生之徒 (*shēng zhī tú* — "the companions / followers / class of life")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 十有三 (*shí yǒu sān* — "ten and three / of ten, three") is "three out of ten" or "thirteen." **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 十有三猶云十分有三分 (*shí yǒu sān yóu yún shí fēn yǒu sān fēn* — "'ten have three' is like saying of ten parts, three parts"). Han Fei: the thirteen are 四肢與九竅 (*sì zhī yǔ jiǔ qiào* — "the four limbs and nine orifices"), and 屬之謂徒 (*shǔ zhī wèi tú* — "belonging to it is called 徒 (*tú* — 'companion')"). Heshang Gong likewise: 九竅四關 (*jiǔ qiào sì guān* — "nine orifices and four joints").

### Unit 3
> 死之徒，十有三； (*sǐ zhī tú, shí yǒu sān* — "death's companions: ten have three")
- **Says:** The followers of death are three in ten (or thirteen).
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 死之徒 (*sǐ zhī tú* — "companions of death")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** as Unit 2. Han Fei: at death the same thirteen 皆還而屬之於死 (*jiē huán ér shǔ zhī yú sǐ* — "all turn back and belong to death").

### Unit 4
> 人之生，動之於死地，亦十有三。 (*rén zhī shēng, dòng zhī yú sǐ dì, yì shí yǒu sān* — "people's living: moving go to the death-ground, also ten have three")
- **Says:** Of people in their living, those who move into the ground of death are also three in ten (or thirteen).
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 人 (*rén* — "people"), who 動 (*dòng* — "move, stir")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and 亦 (*yì* — "also")
- **Open:** 之 (*zhī*) after 動 (*dòng* — "move") may be the verb "go to" ("move and go to the death-ground") or an object pronoun. Wang Bi notes Han Fei's text: 生而動，動皆之死地 (*shēng ér dòng, dòng jiē zhī sǐ dì* — "living, they move; moving, all go to the death-ground"); Han Fei explains 動盡則損也 (*dòng jìn zé sǔn yě* — "moving to exhaustion is depletion"). Heshang Gong: 人之求生，動作反之十三死也 (*rén zhī qiú shēng, dòng zuò fǎn zhī shí sān sǐ yě* — "people seek life, yet their actions turn them back to the thirteen deaths").

### Unit 5
> 夫何故？以其生生之厚。 (*fú hé gù? yǐ qí shēng shēng zhī hòu* — "now, what cause? because of the thickness of their living-living")
- **Says:** Why is this? Because they live life too thickly (pursue life too intensely).
- **Act:** asks (real question, answered at once)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 其 (*qí* — "their"): those in Unit 4 who move to death, or people in general
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** because (the answer explains Unit 4); 夫 (*fú* — "now, as for") opens the question
- **Open:** 生生 (*shēng shēng* — "living life / giving life to life") as "the pursuit of living" or "nourishing life too richly." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 以其求生活之事太厚 (*yǐ qí qiú shēng huó zhī shì tài hòu* — "because the business of seeking a living is too heavy"). Wang Bi: 民生生之厚，更之無生之地焉 (*mín shēng shēng zhī hòu, gèng zhī wú shēng zhī dì yān* — "people live life thickly and instead go to the ground of no life").

### Unit 6
> 蓋聞善攝生者，陸行不遇兕虎，入軍不被甲兵； (*gài wén shàn shè shēng zhě, lù xíng bù yù sì hǔ, rù jūn bù pèi jiǎ bīng* — "it is heard: one good at holding life, going by land does not meet rhinoceros or tiger; entering an army does not wear armor and weapons")
- **Says:** I have heard that one skilled at holding on to life, travelling by land, does not encounter rhinoceros or tiger, and entering battle is not equipped with (or not hit by) armor and weapons. Negation: 不 (*bù* — "not") twice, negating meeting and wearing / suffering.
- **Act:** asserts (as reported hearsay: 蓋聞 (*gài wén* — "it is said / I have heard"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** 善攝生者 (*shàn shè shēng zhě* — "one good at holding / conserving life"); the hearer is unstated, the speaker
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none stated; turns from the many to the one who is skilled
- **Open:** (a) 攝 (*shè* — "hold, grasp, conserve"); Heshang Gong: 攝，養也 (*shè, yǎng yě* — "攝 (*shè* — 'hold') means nourish"). (b) 被 (*pèi / bèi* — "wear, put on" / "suffer, be struck by"): "does not put on armor" or "is not struck by weapons." The variants divide: Heshang Gong's lemma 不避甲兵 (*bù bì jiǎ bīng* — "does not avoid armor and weapons"), glossed 不好戰以殺人 (*bù hào zhàn yǐ shā rén* — "does not love war in order to kill people"); Han Fei quotes 不備甲兵 (*bù bèi jiǎ bīng* — "does not equip armor and weapons"), explaining 無忿爭之心，則無所用救害之備 (*wú fèn zhēng zhī xīn, zé wú suǒ yòng jiù hài zhī bèi* — "having no heart for angry contention, one has no use for defenses against harm").

### Unit 7
> 兕無所投其角，虎無所用其爪，兵無所容其刃。 (*sì wú suǒ tóu qí jiǎo, hǔ wú suǒ yòng qí zhǎo, bīng wú suǒ róng qí rèn* — "the rhinoceros has nowhere to thrust its horn, the tiger nowhere to use its claws, the weapon nowhere to lodge its blade")
- **Says:** The rhinoceros finds no place to thrust its horn, the tiger no place to use its claws, the weapon no place to lodge its edge. Negation: 無所 (*wú suǒ* — "has no place where") three times: 無 (*wú* — "there is no") negates the existence of a place.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 兕 (*sì* — "rhinoceros / wild buffalo"), 虎 (*hǔ* — "tiger"), 兵 (*bīng* — "weapon"); the one skilled at holding life is the implied target
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (elaborates Unit 6)
- **Open:** none. Wang Bi's lemma has 措 (*cuò* — "place, put") and Han Fei 錯 (*cuò* — "place") for 用 (*yòng* — "use"), same sense.

### Unit 8
> 夫何故？以其無死地。 (*fú hé gù? yǐ qí wú sǐ dì* — "now, what cause? because they have no death-ground")
- **Says:** Why is this? Because in them there is no ground of death. Negation: 無 (*wú* — "has no") negates the existence of a death-ground.
- **Act:** asks (real question, answered at once)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 其 (*qí* — "their / its"): the one skilled at holding life
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** because (explains Units 6–7)
- **Open:** "they have no place of death in them" or "they do not stand where death is." **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 善攝生者無以生為生，故無死地也 (*shàn shè shēng zhě wú yǐ shēng wéi shēng, gù wú sǐ dì yě* — "one good at holding life does not treat life as life; so there is no ground of death"). Heshang Gong: 以其不犯十三之死地 (*yǐ qí bù fàn shí sān zhī sǐ dì* — "because they do not violate the thirteen grounds of death"), and adds 神明營護之 (*shén míng yíng hù zhī* — "the spirits guard them"). Han Fei: 不設備而必無害，天地之道理也 (*bù shè bèi ér bì wú hài, tiān dì zhī dào lǐ yě* — "setting no defense and surely suffering no harm is the pattern of the way of heaven and earth").

# Chapter 51

**The chapter as a whole:** The Tao gives things life, de 德 (*dé* — "virtue, power") rears them, matter shapes them, circumstance completes them; so all things honor the Tao and prize de, an honor no one decrees, which is always so of itself. The Tao and de bear, rear, grow, nurture, settle, mature, feed and shelter things, yet bear without possessing, act without relying, lead without ruling. This is called the dark de.

### Unit 1
> 道生之，德畜之，物形之，勢成之。 (*dào shēng zhī, dé xù zhī, wù xíng zhī, shì chéng zhī* — "the Tao births them, de rears them, things shape them, circumstance completes them")
- **Says:** The Tao gives birth to them, de rears them, matter (or things) gives them form, and circumstance (or force) brings them to completion.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 道 (*dào* — "the Tao"), 德 (*dé* — "virtue, power"), 物 (*wù* — "things, matter"), 勢 (*shì* — "momentum, circumstance, force of position") act; 之 (*zhī* — "them") is the myriad things, named in Unit 2
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** (a) 物形之 (*wù xíng zhī* — "things shape them") is "matter gives them shape" or "they take shape as things." **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 何由而形？物也 (*hé yóu ér xíng? wù yě* — "by what are they shaped? by things"); Heshang Gong: 一為萬物設形象也 (*yī wèi wàn wù shè xíng xiàng yě* — "the One sets up form and image for the myriad things"). (b) 勢 (*shì* — "force, circumstance"): Heshang Gong 寒暑之勢 (*hán shǔ zhī shì* — "the force of cold and heat"); Wang Bi 唯勢也，故能無物而不成 (*wéi shì yě, gù néng wú wù ér bù chéng* — "only by circumstance can there be nothing not completed"). Heshang Gong also equates de with the One: 德一也 (*dé yī yě* — "de is the One").

### Unit 2
> 是以萬物莫不尊道而貴德。 (*shì yǐ wàn wù mò bù zūn dào ér guì dé* — "therefore of the myriad things none does not honor the Tao and prize de")
- **Says:** So all things, without exception, honor the Tao and value de. Negation: 莫不 (*mò bù* — "none … not"), a double negation: 莫 (*mò* — "none") and 不 (*bù* — "not") together give "all, without exception."
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 萬物 (*wàn wù* — "the myriad things") honor the Tao and prize de
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** therefore 是以 (*shì yǐ* — "for this reason")
- **Open:** none

### Unit 3
> 道之尊，德之貴，夫莫之命而常自然。 (*dào zhī zūn, dé zhī guì, fú mò zhī mìng ér cháng zì rán* — "the Tao's being honored, de's being prized: now, no one commands it, and it is always so-of-itself")
- **Says:** The honor of the Tao and the value of de are not conferred by anyone's command; they are always so of themselves. Negation: 莫 (*mò* — "no one") negates any commander: nobody decrees it.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 莫 (*mò* — "no one") is the would-be commander; 之 (*zhī* — "it / them") is the honor and value (or, on the second reading, things)
- **Force:** always/universal 常 (*cháng* — "always")
- **Link:** none stated; explains Unit 2; 夫 (*fú* — "now, indeed") marks the comment
- **Open:** 莫之命 (*mò zhī mìng* — "no one commands it") may be "no one orders this honor / confers it" (the honor arises unbidden) or "the Tao and de command nothing, and things are so of themselves." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong takes the second: 道一不命召萬物而常自然應之如影響 (*dào yī bù mìng zhào wàn wù ér cháng zì rán yìng zhī rú yǐng xiǎng* — "the Tao and the One do not command or summon the myriad things, yet the things always respond of themselves, like shadow and echo"). Wang Bi's text note: 命並作爵 (*mìng bìng zuò jué* — "命 (*mìng* — 'command') is also written 爵 (*jué* — 'rank, to ennoble')"), giving "no one ennobles it."

### Unit 4
> 故道生之，德畜之； (*gù dào shēng zhī, dé xù zhī* — "so the Tao births them, de rears them")
- **Says:** Thus the Tao gives them birth and de rears them.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 道 (*dào* — "the Tao"), 德 (*dé* — "de"); 之 (*zhī* — "them"): things
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** therefore 故 (*gù* — "so"), restating Unit 1
- **Open:** none

### Unit 5
> 長之育之；亭之毒之；養之覆之。 (*zhǎng zhī yù zhī; tíng zhī dú zhī; yǎng zhī fù zhī* — "grows them, raises them; settles them, matures them; feeds them, covers them")
- **Says:** It grows them and raises them, settles and ripens them, nourishes and shelters them.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: the Tao, or de, or both continuing from Unit 4; 之 (*zhī* — "them"): things
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** (a) the doer: Heshang Gong makes it the Tao: 道之於萬物非但生之而已，乃復長養成熟覆育 (*dào zhī yú wàn wù fēi dàn shēng zhī ér yǐ, nǎi fù zhǎng yǎng chéng shú fù yù* — "the Tao does not only give birth to the myriad things; it also grows, nourishes, completes, ripens, shelters and rears them"). (b) 亭之毒之 (*tíng zhī dú zhī* — "settle them, 'poison' them"): 毒 (*dú*) is ordinarily "poison"; here it must be a sense like "make firm / mature." Heshang Gong's lemma reads 成之熟之 (*chéng zhī shú zhī* — "completes them, ripens them"). Wang Bi glosses the whole sequence: 謂成其實，各得其庇蔭，不傷其體矣 (*wèi chéng qí shí, gè dé qí bì yìn, bù shāng qí tǐ yǐ* — "it means completing their fruit, each getting its shelter and shade, their bodies unharmed").

### Unit 6
> 生而不有，為而不恃，長而不宰。 (*shēng ér bù yǒu, wéi ér bù shì, zhǎng ér bù zǎi* — "births but does not have, does but does not rely, grows but does not rule")
- **Says:** It gives birth but does not possess, acts but does not depend on (or claim) the result, grows things (or leads them) but does not lord over them. Negation: 不 (*bù* — "not") three times, negating possessing, relying on, ruling.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; the Tao (and de) as in Units 4–5
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and 而 (*ér* — "but" inside each clause)
- **Open:** (a) 長 (*zhǎng* — "to grow, to rear; to be elder / chief over") as "raises them" or "stands at their head." (b) 宰 (*zǎi* — "rule, steward; carve, slaughter"). **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 不宰割以為利也 (*bù zǎi gē yǐ wéi lì yě* — "does not carve them up for profit"); 為而不恃 (*wéi ér bù shì* — "acts without relying") as 不恃望其報也 (*bù shì wàng qí bào yě* — "does not count on and expect its reward").

### Unit 7
> 是謂玄德。 (*shì wèi xuán dé* — "this is called dark de")
- **Says:** This is called the dark (deep, hidden) de.
- **Act:** names/defines 是謂 (*shì wèi* — "this is called")
- **Who acts / of whom:** 是 (*shì* — "this"): the conduct of Unit 6
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none stated; names what precedes
- **Open:** 玄 (*xuán* — "dark, deep, mysterious; dark-dyed") as "hidden from sight" or "profound." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 恩德玄闇不可得見 (*ēn dé xuán àn bù kě dé jiàn* — "its kindness is dark and hidden and cannot be seen"); Wang Bi: 有德而不知其主也，出乎幽冥 (*yǒu dé ér bù zhī qí zhǔ yě, chū hū yōu míng* — "there is de, yet its master is not known; it comes out of the dim and dark").

# Chapter 52

**The chapter as a whole:** The world has a beginning that serves as its mother; having the mother one knows the children, and knowing the children one should return to keep the mother, so one is safe to the end of life. Block the openings and shut the doors and one never toils; open them and add to one's affairs and one is past saving. Seeing the small is clarity and keeping the soft is strength; using the light and returning to clarity leaves no harm for oneself, and this is called entering (or inheriting) the constant.

### Unit 1
> 天下有始，以為天下母。 (*tiān xià yǒu shǐ, yǐ wéi tiān xià mǔ* — "under-heaven has a beginning, taken as under-heaven's mother")
- **Says:** The world has a beginning, and that beginning serves as (or is taken as) the mother of the world.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天下 (*tiān xià* — "the world"); 始 (*shǐ* — "beginning"); 母 (*mǔ* — "mother")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 以為 (*yǐ wéi* — "take as / serve as") may be "which acts as mother" or "which one may take as mother." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 始有道也 (*shǐ yǒu dào yě* — "the beginning: there is the Tao"), 道為天下萬物之母 (*dào wéi tiān xià wàn wù zhī mǔ* — "the Tao is the mother of the myriad things of the world").

### Unit 2
> 既得其母，以知其子； (*jì dé qí mǔ, yǐ zhī qí zǐ* — "having got the mother, thereby know the children")
- **Says:** Once one has the mother, one can thereby know her children.
- **Act:** if/when … then 既 (*jì* — "once, having")
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; "one," the reader; 其 (*qí* — "its / her") refers to the world's mother
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 子 (*zǐ* — "child, children") as the myriad things or as a single "child." **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 母本也，子末也 (*mǔ běn yě, zǐ mò yě* — "the mother is the root; the child is the branch-tip"). Heshang Gong (lemma 既知其母，復知其子 (*jì zhī qí mǔ, fù zhī qí zǐ* — "having known the mother, again know the child")): 子，一也 (*zǐ, yī yě* — "the child is the One").

### Unit 3
> 既知其子，復守其母，没身不殆。 (*jì zhī qí zǐ, fù shǒu qí mǔ, mò shēn bù dài* — "having known the children, return to guard the mother; to the body's end, no danger")
- **Says:** Once one knows the children, one goes back to hold to the mother, and to the end of one's life one is not in danger. Negation: 不 (*bù* — "not") negates being in danger 殆 (*dài* — "peril").
- **Act:** if/when … then; the middle clause reads as advice
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; "one"
- **Force:** habitual; with 没身 (*mò shēn* — "to the end of one's life") a lifetime span
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 復 (*fù* — "again; return to") as "go back to" or simply "also." Wang Bi: 不舍本以逐末也 (*bù shě běn yǐ zhú mò yě* — "do not abandon the root to chase the branches").

### Unit 4
> 塞其兌，閉其門，終身不勤。 (*sè qí duì, bì qí mén, zhōng shēn bù qín* — "block its openings, shut its gates: to the end of life, no toil")
- **Says:** Block the openings, shut the doors, and to the end of life one does not toil (or is not used up). Negation: 不 (*bù* — "not") negates toiling.
- **Act:** advises/commands, with if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; "one," the reader; 其 (*qí* — "its / one's") most likely one's own openings
- **Force:** habitual; lifetime span
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** (a) 兌 (*duì* — "opening, hole; also the name of a trigram"). **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 兌，目也 (*duì, mù yě* — "兌 (*duì* — 'opening') means the eyes") and 門，口也 (*mén, kǒu yě* — "the gate means the mouth"); Wang Bi: 兌，事欲之所由生 (*duì, shì yù zhī suǒ yóu shēng* — "the opening is where affairs and desires arise"). (b) 勤 (*qín* — "toil, labor; be exhausted"): "never labors" or "is never worn out."

### Unit 5
> 開其兌，濟其事，終身不救。 (*kāi qí duì, jì qí shì, zhōng shēn bù jiù* — "open its openings, further its affairs: to the end of life, no rescue")
- **Says:** Open the openings and push one's affairs forward, and to the end of life one cannot be saved. Negation: 不 (*bù* — "not") negates being rescued.
- **Act:** if/when … then (a warning)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; "one"
- **Force:** habitual; lifetime span
- **Link:** but (contrast with Unit 4)
- **Open:** 濟 (*jì* — "help, further, complete; cross") as "accomplish one's affairs" or "add to one's affairs." **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 濟，益也，益情欲之事 (*jì, yì yě, yì qíng yù zhī shì* — "濟 (*jì* — 'further') means increase: increase the business of the passions"); Wang Bi: 不閉其原而濟其事 (*bù bì qí yuán ér jì qí shì* — "not shutting the source, yet furthering its affairs").

### Unit 6
> 見小曰明，守柔曰強。 (*jiàn xiǎo yuē míng, shǒu róu yuē qiáng* — "seeing the small is called clarity; guarding the soft is called strength")
- **Says:** Seeing what is small is called clarity; holding to softness is called strength.
- **Act:** names/defines 曰 (*yuē* — "is called")
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; "one" who sees and holds
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none. **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 見大不明，見小乃明；守強不強，守柔乃強也 (*jiàn dà bù míng, jiàn xiǎo nǎi míng; shǒu qiáng bù qiáng, shǒu róu nǎi qiáng yě* — "seeing the large is not clarity, seeing the small is clarity; holding the strong is not strength, holding the soft is strength"). Heshang Gong: the small is 萌芽未動，禍亂未見 (*méng yá wèi dòng, huò luàn wèi xiàn* — "the sprout not yet stirring, disorder not yet showing").

### Unit 7
> 用其光，復歸其明，無遺身殃； (*yòng qí guāng, fù guī qí míng, wú yí shēn yāng* — "use its light, return again to its clarity, leave no calamity to the body")
- **Says:** Use the light, return to the clarity, and leave no calamity for oneself. Negation: 無 (*wú* — "no, do not") negates leaving behind 遺 (*yí* — "leave, bequeath") calamity; it can be read as "there is no" or as a prohibition ("do not leave").
- **Act:** advises/commands (the first two clauses), with the result in the third
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; "one"; 其 (*qí* — "its / one's") unclear: one's own light, or the light of the Tao
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none stated; continues from 明 (*míng* — "clarity") in Unit 6
- **Open:** 光 (*guāng* — "light, radiance") vs 明 (*míng* — "clarity, brightness"): **Commentators:** Heshang Gong reads light as outward sight and clarity as turned inward: 用其目光於外 (*yòng qí mù guāng yú wài* — "use the light of one's eyes outwardly"), 復當反其光明於內 (*fù dāng fǎn qí guāng míng yú nèi* — "then turn the light back inward"). Wang Bi reads it of governing: 用其光 (*yòng qí guāng* — "use its light") is 顯道以去民迷 (*xiǎn dào yǐ qù mín mí* — "manifest the Tao to remove the people's confusion"), and 復歸其明 (*fù guī qí míng* — "return to its clarity") is 不明察也 (*bù míng chá yě* — "not being sharply inquisitive").

### Unit 8
> 是謂襲常。 (*shì wèi xí cháng* — "this is called entering / inheriting the constant")
- **Says:** This is called entering into (or wearing, or carrying on) the constant.
- **Act:** names/defines 是謂 (*shì wèi* — "this is called")
- **Who acts / of whom:** 是 (*shì* — "this"): the conduct of Unit 7
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none stated; names what precedes
- **Open:** 襲 (*xí* — "to put on a garment over; to follow on, inherit; to enter by stealth") gives "cloaking oneself in the constant," "inheriting the constant," or "stealthily entering the constant." Wang Bi's and Heshang Gong's texts read 習常 (*xí cháng* — "practicing the constant"); Wang Bi's editor notes 習各本作襲 (*xí gè běn zuò xí* — "for 習 (*xí* — 'practice'), the various texts have 襲 (*xí* — 'inherit / cloak')"). Wang Bi: 道之常也 (*dào zhī cháng yě* — "the constancy of the Tao"); Heshang Gong: 人能行此，是謂習修常道 (*rén néng xíng cǐ, shì wèi xí xiū cháng dào* — "if a person can do this, it is called practicing and cultivating the constant Tao").

# Chapter 53

**The chapter as a whole:** If the speaker had even a little knowledge, walking the great Way, the one fear would be 施 (*shī* — "acting / swerving"). The great Way is very level, yet people like byways. The court is spotless while the fields are overgrown and the granaries empty, and the rulers wear fine silk, carry sharp swords, gorge on food and hoard wealth: this is the boast of robbers, not the Way.

### Unit 1
> 使我介然有知，行於大道，唯施是畏。 (*shǐ wǒ jiè rán yǒu zhī, xíng yú dà dào, wéi shī shì wèi* — "if I were to have a little knowledge, walking on the great Way, only 施 (*shī* — "acting / swerving") is what I fear")
- **Says:** Supposing I had even a slight (or firm) knowledge and walked the great Way, the one thing I would fear is 施 (*shī* — "acting / deviating").
- **Act:** if/when … then 使 (*shǐ* — "suppose / if"), hypothetical
- **Who acts / of whom:** 我 (*wǒ* — "I"), the speaker
- **Force:** wish / hypothetical
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** (a) 介然 (*jiè rán* — "slightly, a small bit; or firmly, steadfastly"). **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 介，大也 (*jiè, dà yě* — "介 (*jiè* — 'small, firm') means great"), and he reads the line as Laozi's lament 疾時王不行大道 (*jí shí wáng bù xíng dà dào* — "grieving that the kings of his day did not walk the great Way"). (b) 施 (*shī* — "to act, to bestow, to apply; also a loan for 迤 (*yǐ* — 'to swerve, slant')"). **Commentators:** both read "acting": Wang Bi 唯施為之是畏也 (*wéi shī wéi zhī shì wèi yě* — "only taking action is what I fear"); Heshang Gong 獨畏有所施為失道意 (*dú wèi yǒu suǒ shī wéi shī dào yì* — "I alone fear that taking some action would lose the Way's intent"). The "swerve / byway" sense is supported by the contrast with 徑 (*jìng* — "byway") in Unit 2.

### Unit 2
> 大道甚夷，而人好徑。 (*dà dào shèn yí, ér rén hào jìng* — "the great Way is very level, yet people love byways")
- **Says:** The great Way is very level and easy, yet people prefer shortcuts and byways.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 人 (*rén* — "people"); the commentaries' lemmas read 民 (*mín* — "the common people")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** but 而 (*ér* — "yet")
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong: 夷，平易也 (*yí, píng yì yě* — "夷 (*yí* — 'level') means level and easy"), 徑，邪不平正也 (*jìng, xié bù píng zhèng yě* — "徑 (*jìng* — 'path') means crooked, not level and straight").

### Unit 3
> 朝甚除，田甚蕪，倉甚虛； (*cháo shèn chú, tián shèn wú, cāng shèn xū* — "the court is very swept, the fields very weedy, the granaries very empty")
- **Says:** The court is immaculately kept, the fields are badly overgrown, the granaries are utterly empty.
- **Act:** describes (a state of affairs)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 朝 (*cháo* — "court, palace"), 田 (*tián* — "fields"), 倉 (*cāng* — "granaries")
- **Force:** habitual (a present scene)
- **Link:** none stated; illustrates the byways of Unit 2
- **Open:** whether the three clauses are merely juxtaposed or causal. **Commentators:** Wang Bi makes it causal: 朝甚除則田甚蕪倉甚虛，設一而衆害生也 (*cháo shèn chú zé tián shèn wú cāng shèn xū, shè yī ér zhòng hài shēng yě* — "when the court is very well kept, then the fields are very weedy and the granaries very empty: set up one thing and many harms arise"). 除 (*chú* — "clear, sweep; remove"): Wang Bi 除，潔好也 (*chú, jié hǎo yě* — "除 (*chú* — 'clear away') means clean and fine"); Heshang Gong 高臺榭宮室修 (*gāo tái xiè gōng shì xiū* — "high terraces and pavilions, palaces well kept").

### Unit 4
> 服文綵，帶利劍，厭飲食，財貨有餘； (*fú wén cǎi, dài lì jiàn, yàn yǐn shí, cái huò yǒu yú* — "wear patterned silks, belt on sharp swords, are glutted with drink and food, wealth and goods in surplus")
- **Says:** They wear embroidered silks, carry sharp swords at the belt, are sated with drink and food, and have wealth and goods to spare.
- **Act:** describes a person (persons)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; those of the court, the rulers (Heshang Gong: 人君 (*rén jūn* — "the ruler of men"))
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 厭 (*yàn* — "be sated; be glutted; also loathe") as "sated" or "fed to disgust." Heshang Gong: 多嗜欲無足時 (*duō shì yù wú zú shí* — "many cravings, never a time of enough").

### Unit 5
> 是謂盜夸。 (*shì wèi dào kuā* — "this is called robber-boasting")
- **Says:** This is called the robbers' flaunting (or: the boast of a brigand chief).
- **Act:** names/defines 是謂 (*shì wèi* — "this is called")
- **Who acts / of whom:** 是 (*shì* — "this"): the conduct of Units 3–4
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none stated; names what precedes
- **Open:** 夸 (*kuā* — "boast, extravagance"); Wang Bi notes Han Fei's text reads 竽 (*yú* — "a reed pipe," i.e., the leading instrument, the robbers' leader). **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 凡物不以其道得之則皆邪也，邪則盜也 (*fán wù bù yǐ qí dào dé zhī zé jiē xié yě, xié zé dào yě* — "whatever is got not by its proper way is crooked, and crooked is robbery"), 夸而不以其道得之，竊位也 (*kuā ér bù yǐ qí dào dé zhī, qiè wèi yě* — "boasting of what is not got by its way is stealing position"). Heshang Gong: 劫盜以為服飾 (*jié dào yǐ wéi fú shì* — "robbing to make clothes and ornaments").

### Unit 6
> 非道也哉！ (*fēi dào yě zāi* — "not the Way, indeed!")
- **Says:** This is surely not the Way! Negation: 非 (*fēi* — "is not") negates identity: this is not the Way.
- **Act:** asserts (exclamation: 哉 (*zāi* — exclamatory particle))
- **Who acts / of whom:** the conduct described
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none stated; verdict on Unit 5
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong: 哉者痛傷之辭 (*zāi zhě tòng shāng zhī cí* — "哉 (*zāi* — exclamatory particle) is a word of grief and pain").

# Chapter 54

**The chapter as a whole:** What is well planted is not uprooted and what is well held does not slip away, so the descendants' sacrifices never cease. Cultivated in the person, the household, the village, the state and the world, it makes the de of each genuine, abundant, lasting, rich and universal. So each level is observed by its own measure, and that is how the speaker knows the world is as it is.

### Unit 1
> 善建者不拔，善抱者不脫，子孫以祭祀不輟。 (*shàn jiàn zhě bù bá, shàn bào zhě bù tuō, zǐ sūn yǐ jì sì bù chuò* — "the well-planted is not uprooted, the well-embraced does not slip away; sons and grandsons thereby sacrifice without ceasing")
- **Says:** What is well established cannot be uprooted, what is well held cannot slip away, and through it the descendants' ancestral sacrifices never cease. Negation: 不 (*bù* — "not") three times, negating being uprooted, slipping loose, ceasing.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 善建者 (*shàn jiàn zhě* — "the well-established / one good at establishing"), 善抱者 (*shàn bào zhě* — "the well-held / one good at holding"); 子孫 (*zǐ sūn* — "sons and grandsons, descendants")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** 者 (*zhě*) makes either the person ("one who is good at establishing is not uprooted") or the thing ("what is well established is not uprooted"). **Commentators:** Heshang Gong reads persons: 善以道立身立國者，不可得引而拔也 (*shàn yǐ dào lì shēn lì guó zhě, bù kě dé yǐn ér bá yě* — "one good at establishing self and state by the Way cannot be pulled up"); Wang Bi: 固其根而後營其末 (*gù qí gēn ér hòu yíng qí mò* — "make the root firm, then tend the branches"). Han Fei reads the uprooting as being drawn by desirable things: 雖見所好之物不能引，不能引之謂不拔 (*suī jiàn suǒ hào zhī wù bù néng yǐn, bù néng yǐn zhī wèi bù bá* — "even seeing things one likes, one cannot be drawn; not being drawn is called not being uprooted"). Han Fei's text: 子孫以其祭祀世世不輟 (*zǐ sūn yǐ qí jì sì shì shì bù chuò* — "descendants, by it, sacrifice generation after generation without ceasing").

### Unit 2
> 修之於身，其德乃真； (*xiū zhī yú shēn, qí dé nǎi zhēn* — "cultivate it in the person, its de is then genuine")
- **Says:** Cultivate it in one's own person, and its (or one's) de becomes genuine.
- **Act:** if/when … then (implied conditional; 乃 (*nǎi* — "then, thereupon"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; "one"; 之 (*zhī* — "it") is unstated: what is well established and held, most likely the Tao (Heshang Gong: 修道 (*xiū dào* — "cultivate the Tao"))
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** none stated; turns to how it is done
- **Open:** 其德 (*qí dé* — "its de / one's de"): the de of the cultivated thing or of the person. Han Fei: 真者，慎之固也 (*zhēn zhě, shèn zhī gù yě* — "genuine means firm in carefulness"), and 身以積精為德 (*shēn yǐ jī jīng wéi dé* — "the person's de is its stored essence").

### Unit 3
> 修之於家，其德乃餘； (*xiū zhī yú jiā, qí dé nǎi yú* — "cultivate it in the household, its de is then abundant")
- **Says:** Cultivate it in the household, and its de becomes ample, more than enough.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; "one"
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong's and Han Fei's texts: 其德有餘 (*qí dé yǒu yú* — "its de has surplus"). Han Fei: 家以資財為德 (*jiā yǐ zī cái wéi dé* — "the household's de is its wealth").

### Unit 4
> 修之於鄉，其德乃長； (*xiū zhī yú xiāng, qí dé nǎi cháng* — "cultivate it in the village, its de is then long-lasting")
- **Says:** Cultivate it in the village, and its de becomes long-lasting (or grows).
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; "one"
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 長 (*cháng / zhǎng* — "long, lasting" / "to grow, be elder") gives "endures" or "grows / reaches far." Heshang Gong: 乃無不覆及也 (*nǎi wú bù fù jí yě* — "then there is nothing it does not cover and reach"), the reach reading; Han Fei: 家之有餘者益衆 (*jiā zhī yǒu yú zhě yì zhòng* — "the households with surplus grow more numerous").

### Unit 5
> 修之於邦，其德乃豐； (*xiū zhī yú bāng, qí dé nǎi fēng* — "cultivate it in the state, its de is then abundant")
- **Says:** Cultivate it in the state, and its de becomes rich and plentiful.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; "one," most likely the one who governs the state
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none. Wang Bi's and Heshang Gong's texts read 國 (*guó* — "state") for 邦 (*bāng* — "state").

### Unit 6
> 修之於天下，其德乃普。 (*xiū zhī yú tiān xià, qí dé nǎi pǔ* — "cultivate it in under-heaven, its de is then universal")
- **Says:** Cultivate it in the whole world, and its de becomes all-pervading.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; Heshang Gong: 人主 (*rén zhǔ* — "the ruler")
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none

### Unit 7
> 故以身觀身，以家觀家，以鄉觀鄉，以邦觀邦，以天下觀天下。 (*gù yǐ shēn guān shēn, yǐ jiā guān jiā, yǐ xiāng guān xiāng, yǐ bāng guān bāng, yǐ tiān xià guān tiān xià* — "so by the person observe the person, by the household the household, by the village the village, by the state the state, by under-heaven under-heaven")
- **Says:** So one looks at a person by way of the person, a household by the household, a village by the village, a state by the state, the world by the world.
- **Act:** advises/commands (or asserts a method)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; "one" who observes
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore 故 (*gù* — "so")
- **Open:** whether each level is to be judged by its own standard, or others observed by one's own (cultivated) self. **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 以修道之身觀不修道之身，孰亡孰存也 (*yǐ xiū dào zhī shēn guān bù xiū dào zhī shēn, shú wáng shú cún yě* — "by the person who cultivates the Tao observe the person who does not: which perishes, which survives"). Wang Bi: 彼皆然也 (*bǐ jiē rán yě* — "the others are all like this"), and for the world, 以天下百姓心觀天下之道也 (*yǐ tiān xià bǎi xìng xīn guān tiān xià zhī dào yě* — "by the hearts of the world's people observe the Way of the world"). Han Fei: each level is a measure 適觀息耗 (*shì guān xī hào* — "to observe growth and decline").

### Unit 8
> 吾何以知天下然哉？以此。 (*wú hé yǐ zhī tiān xià rán zāi? yǐ cǐ* — "by what do I know under-heaven is so? by this")
- **Says:** How do I know the world is like this? By this.
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical, answered at once: implies the method of Unit 7)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 吾 (*wú* — "I"), the speaker
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none stated; closes the argument
- **Open:** 此 (*cǐ* — "this") as the method of observation in Unit 7, or as what lies near at hand, oneself. **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 察己以知之，不求於外也 (*chá jǐ yǐ zhī zhī, bù qiú yú wài yě* — "one examines oneself to know it, and does not seek outside"), linking it to 不出戶以知天下 (*bù chū hù yǐ zhī tiān xià* — "knowing the world without going out the door"). Heshang Gong: 以此五事觀而知之也 (*yǐ cǐ wǔ shì guān ér zhī zhī yě* — "by observing these five matters one knows it").

# Chapter 55

**The chapter as a whole:** One who holds de in fullness is like a newborn: stinging and clawing creatures leave it alone, its bones are soft yet its grip firm, its member rises without knowledge of sex, and it cries all day without going hoarse, all from fullness of essence and harmony. Knowing harmony is constancy, knowing constancy is clarity; but adding to life is ominous, and the heart-mind driving the breath is forcing. Things at their peak then age; this is called not the Way, and what is not the Way ends early.

### Unit 1
> 含德之厚，比於赤子。 (*hán dé zhī hòu, bǐ yú chì zǐ* — "holding de in its thickness: compare to the red child")
- **Says:** One who holds de in its fullness is comparable to a newborn infant.
- **Act:** asserts (a comparison)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 含德之厚 (*hán dé zhī hòu* — "the thickness of holding de / one who holds de thickly"); 赤子 (*chì zǐ* — "red child, newborn")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong adds 神明保祐含德之人，若父母之於赤子也 (*shén míng bǎo yòu hán dé zhī rén, ruò fù mǔ zhī yú chì zǐ yě* — "the spirits protect the person who holds de, as parents do a newborn").

### Unit 2
> 毒蟲不螫，猛獸不據，攫鳥不搏。 (*dú chóng bù shì, měng shòu bù jù, jué niǎo bù bó* — "poisonous creatures do not sting, fierce beasts do not seize, clawing birds do not strike")
- **Says:** Venomous creatures do not sting it, fierce beasts do not pounce on it, birds of prey do not strike it. Negation: 不 (*bù* — "not") three times, negating stinging, seizing, striking.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 毒蟲 (*dú chóng* — "poisonous creatures"), 猛獸 (*měng shòu* — "fierce beasts"), 攫鳥 (*jué niǎo* — "seizing birds, raptors"); the object, unstated, is the infant / the one full of de
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (elaborates Unit 1)
- **Open:** none. Wang Bi's lemma: 蜂蠆虺蛇不螫 (*fēng chài huǐ shé bù shì* — "wasps, scorpions, vipers and snakes do not sting"). Wang Bi: 赤子無求無欲，不犯衆物 (*chì zǐ wú qiú wú yù, bù fàn zhòng wù* — "the newborn seeks nothing and wants nothing, and does not offend the things around it").

### Unit 3
> 骨弱筋柔而握固。 (*gǔ ruò jīn róu ér wò gù* — "bones weak, sinews soft, yet grip firm")
- **Says:** Its bones are weak and its sinews soft, yet its grip is firm.
- **Act:** describes a person (the infant)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the infant, unstated
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and; 而 (*ér* — "yet") inside the unit
- **Open:** none. Wang Bi: 以柔弱之故，故握能周固 (*yǐ róu ruò zhī gù, gù wò néng zhōu gù* — "because it is soft and weak, its grip can be all-round firm").

### Unit 4
> 未知牝牡之合而朘作，精之至也。 (*wèi zhī pìn mǔ zhī hé ér zuī zuò, jīng zhī zhì yě* — "not yet knowing the union of female and male, yet the member rises: the utmost of essence")
- **Says:** It does not yet know the joining of female and male, yet its penis stands up: this is essence at its height. Negation: 未 (*wèi* — "not yet") negates knowing: not yet.
- **Act:** asserts (fact, then explanation)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the infant, unstated; 朘 (*zuī* — "an infant boy's penis")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** the base's 朘 (*zuī* — "infant's member") is not in Wang Bi's text. **Commentators:** Wang Bi's lemma reads 全作 (*quán zuò* — "wholeness arises"), glossed 作，長也，無物以損其身，故能全長也 (*zuò, zhǎng yě, wú wù yǐ sǔn qí shēn, gù néng quán zhǎng yě* — "作 (*zuò* — 'arise') means grow: nothing harms its body, so it can grow whole"). Heshang Gong reads a variant character for the member 陰 (*yīn* — "genitals"): 赤子未知男女之合會而陰作怒者，由精氣多之所致也 (*chì zǐ wèi zhī nán nǚ zhī hé huì ér yīn zuò nù zhě, yóu jīng qì duō zhī suǒ zhì yě* — "that the newborn, not yet knowing the union of man and woman, has its member roused is caused by abundant essence"). 精 (*jīng* — "essence, vital seminal essence").

### Unit 5
> 終日號而不嗄，和之至也。 (*zhōng rì háo ér bù shà, hé zhī zhì yě* — "all day howls yet is not hoarse: the utmost of harmony")
- **Says:** It wails all day and does not grow hoarse: this is harmony at its height. Negation: 不 (*bù* — "not") negates becoming hoarse.
- **Act:** asserts (fact, then explanation)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the infant, unstated
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong's text reads 啞 (*yǎ* — "mute, hoarse") and, per Wang Bi's editor, adds 嗌 (*yì* — "throat"). Heshang Gong: 和氣多之所致 (*hé qì duō zhī suǒ zhì* — "caused by abundant harmonious breath"); Wang Bi: 無爭欲之心 (*wú zhēng yù zhī xīn* — "it has no heart of contention or desire").

### Unit 6
> 知和曰常，知常曰明。 (*zhī hé yuē cháng, zhī cháng yuē míng* — "knowing harmony is called constant; knowing the constant is called clarity")
- **Says:** Knowing harmony is called constancy; knowing the constant is called clarity.
- **Act:** names/defines 曰 (*yuē* — "is called")
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; "one" who knows
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none stated; draws from 和 (*hé* — "harmony") in Unit 5
- **Open:** none. Wang Bi: 物以和為常，故知和則得常也 (*wù yǐ hé wéi cháng, gù zhī hé zé dé cháng yě* — "things take harmony as their constant; so knowing harmony one gains the constant").

### Unit 7
> 益生曰祥。 (*yì shēng yuē xiáng* — "increasing life is called 祥 (*xiáng* — 'omen')")
- **Says:** Adding to life is called an omen (ominous / auspicious), or "growth."
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated; anyone who adds to life
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none stated; turns from approval to warning
- **Open:** 祥 (*xiáng* — "omen; good omen, auspicious; portent of ill") divides sharply. **Commentators:** Wang Bi reads it as ill: 生不可益，益之則夭也 (*shēng bù kě yì, yì zhī zé yāo yě* — "life cannot be increased; increase it and one dies young"). Heshang Gong reads it as growth: 祥，長也，言益生欲自生日以長大 (*xiáng, zhǎng yě, yán yì shēng yù zì shēng rì yǐ zhǎng dà* — "祥 (*xiáng* — 'omen') means to grow: it says that one who increases life, wanting to make oneself live, grows bigger day by day"), his context making this a fault. The plain word alone would read "auspicious," which the context of Units 8–9 contradicts.

### Unit 8
> 心使氣曰強。 (*xīn shǐ qì yuē qiáng* — "the heart directing the breath is called strong / forcing")
- **Says:** The heart-mind driving the breath (qi) is called forcing (or strength).
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** 心 (*xīn* — "heart-mind") directs 使 (*shǐ* — "employ, make, command") 氣 (*qì* — "breath, vital energy")
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel to Unit 7)
- **Open:** 強 (*qiáng* — "strong; forcing, stiff, violent") is praise in Chapter 52's 守柔曰強 (*shǒu róu yuē qiáng* — "holding the soft is called strength"); here the context makes it blame. **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 心宜無有，使氣則強 (*xīn yí wú yǒu, shǐ qì zé qiáng* — "the heart-mind should hold nothing; if it drives the breath, that is forcing"). Heshang Gong: 和氣去於中，故形體日以剛強也 (*hé qì qù yú zhōng, gù xíng tǐ rì yǐ gāng qiáng yě* — "the harmonious breath leaves the center, so the body grows hard and rigid day by day").

### Unit 9
> 物壯則老，謂之不道，不道早已。 (*wù zhuàng zé lǎo, wèi zhī bù dào, bù dào zǎo yǐ* — "things at full strength then age; call it not-the-Way; not-the-Way ends early")
- **Says:** When things reach their peak of vigor they grow old; this is called not the Way, and what is not the Way comes to an early end. Negation: 不 (*bù* — "not") twice, negating 道 (*dào* — "the Way") used as verb or predicate: "not following the Way / not the Way."
- **Act:** if/when … then 則 (*zé* — "then"), then names/defines 謂之 (*wèi zhī* — "it is called"), then asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 物 (*wù* — "things"); 之 (*zhī* — "it") is the peaking-and-aging
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none stated; generalizes from Units 7–8
- **Open:** 已 (*yǐ* — "stop, end") as "ends / dies." Heshang Gong's text: 物壯將老 (*wù zhuàng jiāng lǎo* — "things at their peak are about to age"); 不得道者早已死也 (*bù dé dào zhě zǎo yǐ sǐ yě* — "those who do not attain the Way die early").
