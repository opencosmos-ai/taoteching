# Chapter 1

**The chapter as a whole:** A way that can be spoken (or walked) and a name that can be named are not the constant ones. It then sets two terms side by side: the nameless (or "non-being") as the beginning of sky and earth, and the named (or "being") as the mother of the myriad things, and two kinds of looking, without desire and with desire, which see the subtle and the outcome. The two come from one source under different names, both called "dark", and the dark of the dark is the gate of every subtlety.

### Unit 1
> 道可道，非常道； (*dào kě dào, fēi cháng dào* — "a way that can be way-ed [spoken / walked] is not the constant way")
- **Says:** a dao that can be "dao-ed" (told, or followed as a path) is not the constant dao.
- **Act:** asserts / names/defines (by exclusion)
- **Who acts / of whom:** of the Tao itself; the one who would speak or walk it is unstated ("anyone").
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (opening)
- **Open:** (1) The second 道 (*dào* — verb) is "to speak / to tell" (Wang Bi: 可道之道……指事造形 (*kě dào zhī dào … zhǐ shì zào xíng* — "the dao that can be spoken … points to affairs and makes forms"); Han Fei's lemma 道之可道，非常道也 (*dào zhī kě dào, fēi cháng dào yě* — "a dao's being speakable: that is not the constant dao") reads the same) or "to walk / follow as a way". (2) Grammar also allows the pause 道，可道，非常道 (*dào, kě dào, fēi cháng dào* — "the way can be told, but not [as] the constant way"). Negator 非 (*fēi* — "is not"): denies identity.
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong makes the speakable dao a specific worldly one: 經術政教之道 (*jīng shù zhèng jiào zhī dào* — "the way of the classics, techniques, government and teaching"), against 自然長生之道 (*zì rán cháng shēng zhī dào* — "the way of the self-so and long life"). Wang Bi and Han Fei treat it as anything speakable at all.

### Unit 2
> 名可名，非常名。 (*míng kě míng, fēi cháng míng* — "a name that can be named is not the constant name")
- **Says:** a name that can be applied as a name is not the constant name.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** of names; the namer unstated.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (strict parallel to Unit 1)
- **Open:** Same pause alternative as Unit 1. Negator 非 (*fēi* — "is not"): identity. Heshang Gong again narrows the nameable name to 富貴尊榮高世之名 (*fù guì zūn róng gāo shì zhī míng* — "the name of wealth, rank, honour, glory, standing above the age").

### Unit 3
> 無名，天地之始； (*wú míng, tiān dì zhī shǐ* — "nameless: the beginning of sky and earth")
- **Says:** the nameless is the beginning of sky and earth.
- **Act:** names/defines (nominal predication, no verb)
- **Who acts / of whom:** "the nameless" (of the Tao, by context); sky and earth.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (new pair begins)
- **Open:** (1) 無名 (*wú míng* — "no name / the nameless"), negator 無 (*wú* — "lacks"): absence of a name. (2) Grammar equally allows 無，名天地之始 (*wú, míng tiān dì zhī shǐ* — "non-being names the beginning of sky and earth"), making 無 (*wú* — "non-being") and 有 (*yǒu* — "being") the terms.
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 無名者謂道 (*wú míng zhě wèi dào* — "'the nameless' means the Tao"). Wang Bi reads two phases of one Tao: 未形無名之時，則為萬物之始 (*wèi xíng wú míng zhī shí, zé wéi wàn wù zhī shǐ* — "at the time when it has no shape and no name, it is the beginning of the myriad things"); note his comment says "myriad things", not the base's "sky and earth".

### Unit 4
> 有名，萬物之母。 (*yǒu míng, wàn wù zhī mǔ* — "named: the mother of the myriad things")
- **Says:** the named is the mother of the myriad things.
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** "the named"; the myriad things as its offspring.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel to Unit 3)
- **Open:** Same pause alternative: 有，名萬物之母 (*yǒu, míng wàn wù zhī mǔ* — "being names the mother of the myriad things").
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 有名謂天地 (*yǒu míng wèi tiān dì* — "'the named' means sky and earth"), which 含氣生萬物 (*hán qì shēng wàn wù* — "hold breath-energy and give birth to the myriad things"). Wang Bi: the same Tao, once it has form and name, 長之育之亭之毒之，為其母也 (*zhǎng zhī yù zhī tíng zhī dú zhī, wéi qí mǔ yě* — "grows them, rears them, shapes them, matures them, and is their mother").

### Unit 5
> 故常無欲，以觀其妙； (*gù cháng wú yù, yǐ guān qí miào* — "so, constantly without desire, by this see its subtlety")
- **Says:** therefore, being constantly without desire, one thereby observes its subtlety.
- **Act:** asserts (with an advisory edge)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated observer, most likely "one / a person" (Heshang Gong: 人 (*rén* — "a person")); 其 (*qí* — "its") most likely the Tao's, or things' beginnings.
- **Force:** habitual / can/possible
- **Link:** therefore/so (故 (*gù* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** (1) Pause 常無欲，以觀 (*cháng wú yù, yǐ guān* — "constantly desireless, thereby observe") vs 常無，欲以觀 (*cháng wú, yù yǐ guān* — "in constant non-being, one wishes thereby to observe"); both grammatical. Negator 無 (*wú* — "without"): absence of desire. (2) 妙 (*miào*): Wang Bi 妙者微之極也 (*miào zhě wēi zhī jí yě* — "miao is the extreme of the minute"); Heshang Gong 妙，要也 (*miào, yào yě* — "miao means the essential").
- **Commentators:** both read 無欲 (*wú yù* — "without desire") as a unit; Wang Bi: 常無欲空虛，可以觀其始物之妙 (*cháng wú yù kōng xū, kě yǐ guān qí shǐ wù zhī miào* — "constantly desireless and empty, one can observe the subtlety by which it begins things").

### Unit 6
> 常有欲，以觀其徼。 (*cháng yǒu yù, yǐ guān qí jiào* — "constantly with desire, by this see its boundary / outcome")
- **Says:** being constantly with desire, one thereby observes its boundary (where it ends up).
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated observer; 其 (*qí* — "its") as in Unit 5.
- **Force:** habitual / can/possible
- **Link:** and (parallel to Unit 5)
- **Open:** (1) Same pause alternative: 常有，欲以觀 (*cháng yǒu, yù yǐ guān* — "in constant being, one wishes thereby to observe"). (2) Whether desire here is a legitimate second mode of seeing (Wang Bi) or the lesser one (Heshang Gong).
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 徼，歸終也 (*jiào, guī zhōng yě* — "jiao means returning to the end"), so desire sees 終物之徼 (*zhōng wù zhī jiào* — "the outcome in which it completes things"). Heshang Gong: 徼，歸也 (*jiào, guī yě* — "jiao means where things go"), and 常有欲之人，可以觀世俗之所歸趣也 (*cháng yǒu yù zhī rén, kě yǐ guān shì sú zhī suǒ guī qù yě* — "a person who constantly has desire can see where worldly custom heads").

### Unit 7
> 此兩者，同出而異名，同謂之玄。 (*cǐ liǎng zhě, tóng chū ér yì míng, tóng wèi zhī xuán* — "these two come out together and differ in name; together they are called dark")
- **Says:** these two emerge from the same place but have different names; both alike are called "dark".
- **Act:** asserts + names/defines (謂之 (*wèi zhī* — "is called"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** "these two" — referent unstated.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (summing up), with 而 (*ér* — "and yet") inside
- **Open:** What "the two" are: grammar allows 無 / 有 (*wú / yǒu* — "non-being / being"), the nameless / the named, or 妙 / 徼 (*miào / jiào* — "subtlety / outcome").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 兩者，始與母也 (*liǎng zhě, shǐ yǔ mǔ yě* — "the two are the beginning and the mother"), and 玄者冥也，默然無有也 (*xuán zhě míng yě, mò rán wú yǒu yě* — "dark means the obscure, silent and without anything"). Heshang Gong: 兩者謂有欲無欲也 (*liǎng zhě wèi yǒu yù wú yù yě* — "the two means having desire and not having desire"), 同出人心 (*tóng chū rén xīn* — "both come from the human heart"), and 玄，天也 (*xuán, tiān yě* — "dark means heaven").

### Unit 8
> 玄之又玄，眾妙之門。 (*xuán zhī yòu xuán, zhòng miào zhī mén* — "dark and again dark: the gate of all subtleties")
- **Says:** the dark, and darker still, is the gate of the many subtleties.
- **Act:** names/defines (nominal, no verb)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the "dark" of Unit 7.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (intensifies Unit 7)
- **Open:** 玄之又玄 (*xuán zhī yòu xuán*) as "dark upon dark" (nominal) or "darken it and darken it again" (之 (*zhī* — "it") as object of a verbal 玄 (*xuán* — "to darken")).
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi takes it as refusing to fix even the name "dark": 不可以定乎一玄而已 (*bù kě yǐ dìng hū yī xuán ér yǐ* — "it cannot be settled at a single 'dark' and no more"). Heshang Gong: 天中復有天也 (*tiān zhōng fù yǒu tiān yě* — "within heaven there is heaven again").

# Chapter 2

**The chapter as a whole:** When everyone recognizes the beautiful as beautiful and the good as good, ugliness and the not-good are already there, because opposites arise and are defined only through each other. So the sage works by not-doing and teaches without words: things arise and the sage does not start them or own them, acts without presuming, and does not dwell in the achievement, and for that very reason the achievement does not leave.

### Unit 1
> 天下皆知美之為美，斯惡已。 (*tiān xià jiē zhī měi zhī wéi měi, sī è yǐ* — "all under heaven know the beautiful as beautiful; this is ugliness already")
- **Says:** when everyone in the world recognizes the beautiful as being beautiful, that is (already) the ugly.
- **Act:** if/when … then (斯 (*sī* — "this / then") marks the consequence)
- **Who acts / of whom:** 天下 (*tiān xià* — "all under heaven"): everyone.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (opening)
- **Open:** (1) 斯惡已 (*sī è yǐ*): "then ugliness [comes to exist]" (the pair is born together) vs "this is already bad" (the knowing itself is the harm). (2) 美之為美 (*měi zhī wéi měi*): "the beautiful as beautiful" vs "what makes beauty beautiful".
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi reads a relative pair: 美惡猶喜怒也 … 不可得而偏舉也 (*měi è yóu xǐ nù yě … bù kě dé ér piān jǔ yě* — "beautiful and ugly are like joy and anger … neither can be raised alone"). Heshang Gong reads a moral harm: the line is 自揚己美使顯彰也 (*zì yáng jǐ měi shǐ xiǎn zhāng yě* — "advertising one's own beauty to make it conspicuous"), and 斯惡已 means 有危亡也 (*yǒu wēi wáng yě* — "there is danger and ruin").

### Unit 2
> 皆知善之為善，斯不善已。 (*jiē zhī shàn zhī wéi shàn, sī bù shàn yǐ* — "all know the good as good; this is not-good already")
- **Says:** when all recognize the good as good, that is (already) the not-good.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** everyone (carried from Unit 1).
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** As Unit 1. Negator 不 (*bù* — "not"): negates the quality 善 (*shàn* — "good / skilled"). Heshang Gong: knowing the good = 有功名也 (*yǒu gōng míng yě* — "having merit and fame"), and the not-good = 人所爭也 (*rén suǒ zhēng yě* — "what people fight over").

### Unit 3
> 故有無相生，難易相成，長短相形，高下相盈，音聲相和，前後相隨。 (*gù yǒu wú xiāng shēng, nán yì xiāng chéng, cháng duǎn xiāng xíng, gāo xià xiāng yíng, yīn shēng xiāng hé, qián hòu xiāng suí* — "so being and non-being give rise to each other, hard and easy complete each other, long and short shape each other, high and low fill each other, tone and voice harmonize each other, front and back follow each other")
- **Says:** therefore each member of six pairs arises, is completed, shaped, filled, harmonized or followed only by way of its opposite.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the paired opposites act on each other (相 (*xiāng* — "mutually")).
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** therefore/so (故 (*gù* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** (1) 有無 (*yǒu wú*) as "being and non-being" or "having and lacking". (2) 相盈 (*xiāng yíng* — "fill / complete each other"); Wang Bi's lemma has 相較 (*xiāng jiào* — "measure against each other") for 相形 and both commentators have 相傾 (*xiāng qīng* — "lean toward each other") for 相盈. (3) 音聲 (*yīn shēng*): musical tone vs plain sound, or note and echo.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 此六者皆陳自然不可偏舉之明數也 (*cǐ liù zhě jiē chén zì rán bù kě piān jǔ zhī míng shù yě* — "these six all set out the plain pattern of the self-so, in which nothing can be raised alone"). Heshang Gong moralizes each pair, e.g. 見有而為無也 (*jiàn yǒu ér wéi wú yě* — "seeing something, one treats it as nothing").

### Unit 4
> 是以聖人處無為之事，行不言之教。 (*shì yǐ shèng rén chǔ wú wéi zhī shì, xíng bù yán zhī jiào* — "therefore the sage abides in the business of not-doing and practises the teaching of not-speaking")
- **Says:** for this reason the sage dwells in affairs of non-action and carries out a teaching without words.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage.
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore/so (是以 (*shì yǐ* — "for this reason"))
- **Open:** 處 (*chǔ*) as "abide in" or "handle / manage". Negators: 無 (*wú* — "no / without") negates 為 (*wéi* — "doing"), as absence; 不 (*bù* — "not") negates 言 (*yán* — "speaking"), an action. Heshang Gong: 以道治也 (*yǐ dào zhì yě* — "governing by the Tao"); 以身師導之也 (*yǐ shēn shī dǎo zhī yě* — "leading them with one's own person as teacher").

### Unit 5
> 萬物作而弗始，生而弗有，為而弗恃，功成而弗居。 (*wàn wù zuò ér fú shǐ, shēng ér fú yǒu, wéi ér fú shì, gōng chéng ér fú jū* — "the myriad things arise and [one] does not start them; produces and does not own; acts and does not presume; the work is done and [one] does not dwell in it")
- **Says:** the myriad things arise and [the doer] does not initiate them; brings them to life and does not possess them; acts and does not rely on it; the work is accomplished and does not dwell in it.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** 萬物 (*wàn wù* — "the myriad things") arise; the subject of the four 弗 (*fú* — "does not [do it]") verbs is unstated: by context the sage of Unit 4; the commentators also read the Tao.
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and (elaborates Unit 4)
- **Open:** (1) Negator 弗 (*fú* — "does not … it"): negates an action with an implied object, four times. (2) 弗始 (*fú shǐ*): "does not start them" vs "does not claim to be their beginning / take the lead". (3) 恃 (*shì*): "rely on" vs "presume on / claim credit". (4) The base's 弗始 (*fú shǐ* — "does not initiate") differs from both commentators' lemma 不辭 (*bù cí* — "does not decline / does not speak").
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong makes the producer cosmic: 元氣生萬物而不有 (*yuán qì shēng wàn wù ér bù yǒu* — "primal breath produces the myriad things and does not own them"); 道所施為不恃望其報也 (*dào suǒ shī wéi bù shì wàng qí bào yě* — "what the Tao bestows and does, it does not count on a return"). Wang Bi keeps it with the sage: 智慧自備，為則偽也 (*zhì huì zì bèi, wéi zé wěi yě* — "wisdom is complete of itself; to act would be falsifying").

### Unit 6
> 夫唯弗居，是以不去。 (*fú wéi fú jū, shì yǐ bù qù* — "just because [one] does not dwell in it, for this reason it does not leave")
- **Says:** precisely because [the doer] does not dwell in it, it does not go away.
- **Act:** if/when … then (夫唯 … 是以 (*fú wéi … shì yǐ* — "just because … therefore"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage does not dwell; what does not leave is unstated: the achievement, or the sage's own place.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** because (the paradox of Unit 5's last clause)
- **Open:** Negators 弗 (*fú* — "does not [dwell in] it") and 不 (*bù* — "not") on 去 (*qù* — "leave / be removed"). What stays: the merit (Wang Bi: 使功在己則功不可久也 (*shǐ gōng zài jǐ zé gōng bù kě jiǔ yě* — "if one makes the merit rest in oneself, the merit cannot last")) or blessing that stays with the person (Heshang Gong: 福德常在，不去其身也 (*fú dé cháng zài, bù qù qí shēn yě* — "good fortune and virtue always remain and do not leave the person")).

# Chapter 3

**The chapter as a whole:** A governing policy: by not exalting the worthy, not prizing rare goods and not displaying the desirable, the people are kept from contention, theft and disorder. The sage's government empties hearts and fills bellies, weakens wills and strengthens bones, keeps the people without (cunning) knowledge and desire, and stops the clever from daring to act; doing non-doing, nothing goes ungoverned.

### Unit 1
> 不尚賢，使民不爭； (*bù shàng xián, shǐ mín bù zhēng* — "not exalting the worthy makes the people not contend")
- **Says:** not exalting the worthy causes the people not to compete.
- **Act:** if/when … then (implicit policy advice)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated governor, most likely the ruler (Heshang Gong: 人君 (*rén jūn* — "the ruler")); the people are done-to.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (opening)
- **Open:** (1) Negators 不 (*bù* — "not"): one negates the ruler's action (exalting), one the people's (contending). (2) 使 (*shǐ*): result ("makes") or purpose ("so as to make"). (3) 賢 (*xián*): Wang Bi 賢猶能也 (*xián yóu néng yě* — "'worthy' is like 'the able'"); Heshang Gong 世俗之賢 (*shì sú zhī xián* — "the worthy as the world counts them"): glib talkers.

### Unit 2
> 不貴難得之貨，使民不為盜； (*bù guì nán dé zhī huò, shǐ mín bù wéi dào* — "not prizing hard-to-get goods makes the people not become thieves")
- **Says:** not valuing rare goods keeps the people from turning to theft.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** the ruler (unstated); the people.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** none. Negators 不 (*bù* — "not") twice: actions.

### Unit 3
> 不見可欲，使民心不亂。 (*bù jiàn kě yù, shǐ mín xīn bù luàn* — "not showing / seeing the desirable keeps the people's hearts from disorder")
- **Says:** not displaying what could be desired keeps the people's hearts undisturbed.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** the ruler (unstated); the people's hearts.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** 見 (*jiàn* — "see", or *xiàn* — "show"): "not displaying the desirable [to the people]" vs "[the ruler] not looking at the desirable". Heshang Gong reads the ruler's own abstinence: 放鄭聲，遠美人 (*fàng zhèng shēng, yuǎn měi rén* — "banish the music of Zheng, keep beautiful women at a distance"). Heshang Gong's lemma lacks 民 (*mín* — "people"); Wang Bi's note says most editions lack it.

### Unit 4
> 是以聖人之治，虛其心，實其腹，弱其志，強其骨。 (*shì yǐ shèng rén zhī zhì, xū qí xīn, shí qí fù, ruò qí zhì, qiáng qí gǔ* — "therefore the sage's governing empties their hearts, fills their bellies, weakens their wills, strengthens their bones")
- **Says:** so the sage's way of ordering empties their hearts and fills their bellies, softens their ambitions and strengthens their bones.
- **Act:** describes a person (method of rule)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; 其 (*qí* — "their") most naturally the people's.
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore/so (是以 (*shì yǐ* — "for this reason"))
- **Open:** 其 (*qí*): the people's (context of Units 1–3) or the sage's own (self-cultivation).
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong reads both at once: 聖人治國與治身同也 (*shèng rén zhì guó yǔ zhì shēn tóng yě* — "the sage governs the state the same way as governing the body"), and 虛其心 is 除嗜欲，去亂煩 (*chú shì yù, qù luàn fán* — "removing cravings, getting rid of confusion"). Wang Bi: 心懷智而腹懷食 (*xīn huái zhì ér fù huái shí* — "the heart holds cleverness and the belly holds food").

### Unit 5
> 常使民無知無欲。 (*cháng shǐ mín wú zhī wú yù* — "constantly makes the people without knowing and without desiring")
- **Says:** [the sage] always keeps the people free of knowing and desiring.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage (carried); the people.
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 無知 (*wú zhī*): "no knowledge" vs "no cunning / no scheming". Negator 無 (*wú* — "without") twice: absence. Wang Bi: 守其真也 (*shǒu qí zhēn yě* — "they keep to their genuineness"); Heshang Gong: 反朴守淳 (*fǎn pǔ shǒu chún* — "return to the uncarved and keep the pure").

### Unit 6
> 使夫智者不敢為也。 (*shǐ fū zhì zhě bù gǎn wéi yě* — "makes those clever ones not dare to act")
- **Says:** [the sage] keeps the clever from daring to act.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; "those who are clever" (夫 (*fū* — "those"), demonstrative).
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** Negator 不 (*bù* — "not") on 敢 (*gǎn* — "dare"). 智者 (*zhì zhě*): "the cunning" whom the ruler restrains (Wang Bi: 智者謂知為也 (*zhì zhě wèi zhī wéi yě* — "'the clever' means those who know how to contrive")), or "the wise", whom Heshang Gong (lemma 知者 (*zhì zhě* — "knowers")) describes approvingly: 思慮深，不輕言 (*sī lǜ shēn, bù qīng yán* — "deep in thought, they do not speak lightly").

### Unit 7
> 為無為，則無不治。 (*wéi wú wéi, zé wú bù zhì* — "do not-doing, then nothing is not ordered")
- **Says:** if one does non-doing, nothing is left ungoverned.
- **Act:** if/when … then (則 (*zé* — "then"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: the sage / ruler of Unit 4.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (summation)
- **Open:** none. Negators: 無 (*wú* — "no") on 為 (*wéi* — "doing"); 無不 (*wú bù* — "nothing not"): double negation = everything. Heshang Gong: 不造作，動因循 (*bù zào zuò, dòng yīn xún* — "not fabricating; moving by following along").

# Chapter 4

**The chapter as a whole:** The Tao is empty like a vessel, and using it never fills it up; it is deep, seeming the ancestor of all things. A four-part verse of blunting, untying, softening and mingling follows; then the Tao is called deep and still, as if barely present. The speaker does not know whose child it is: it seems to come before the Di.

### Unit 1
> 道沖，而用之或不盈。 (*dào chōng, ér yòng zhī huò bù yíng* — "the Tao is empty, and using it, it perhaps is never full")
- **Says:** the Tao is hollow, and when used it is (perhaps / never) filled up.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the Tao; the user unstated.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (opening); 而 (*ér* — "and / yet") inside
- **Open:** (1) 沖 (*chōng*): "empty (as a vessel)" (Wang Bi: 沖而用之又復不盈 (*chōng ér yòng zhī yòu fù bù yíng* — "empty and used, yet again not filled")) vs Heshang Gong 沖，中也 (*chōng, zhōng yě* — "chong means the middle"). (2) 或 (*huò*): "perhaps / somehow" vs Heshang Gong 或，常也 (*huò, cháng yě* — "huo means constantly"). (3) 不盈 (*bù yíng*), negator 不 (*bù* — "not"): "never gets full (never overflows)" vs "never needs refilling (never runs out)".

### Unit 2
> 淵兮，似萬物之宗。 (*yuān xī, sì wàn wù zhī zōng* — "deep, ah, it seems the ancestor of the myriad things")
- **Says:** it is abyss-deep, and seems to be the ancestral source of all things.
- **Act:** asserts (hedged: 似 (*sì* — "seems"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** the Tao.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none. 兮 (*xī* — exclamatory particle); Heshang Gong's lemma 淵乎 (*yuān hū* — "deep, oh"). 宗 (*zōng* — "ancestor / lineage head / source"): Heshang Gong 宗祖 (*zōng zǔ* — "ancestral forebear").

### Unit 3
> 挫其銳，解其紛，和其光，同其塵。 (*cuò qí ruì, jiě qí fēn, hé qí guāng, tóng qí chén* — "blunt its sharpness, untie its tangles, soften its light, be one with its dust")
- **Says:** it blunts the sharp, unties the tangled, tempers the bright, and merges with the dust.
- **Act:** describes (of the Tao) or advises/commands (to a person): grammar does not decide
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated. 其 (*qí* — "its / their / one's"): of the Tao, of things, or of one's own self.
- **Force:** always/universal (descriptive) or must (prescriptive)
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** Description of the Tao vs imperative to a person.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi reads it of the Tao: 銳挫而無損，紛解而不勞 (*ruì cuò ér wú sǔn, fēn jiě ér bù láo* — "the sharpness is blunted and nothing is lost; the tangles are untied without labour"). Heshang Gong reads it as instruction: 人欲銳精進取功名，當挫止之 (*rén yù ruì jīng jìn qǔ gōng míng, dāng cuò zhǐ zhī* — "when a person wants to press sharply forward for merit and fame, one should blunt and stop it"); 同其塵 is 常與眾庶同垢塵 (*cháng yǔ zhòng shù tóng gòu chén* — "always share the grime and dust of the common crowd").

### Unit 4
> 湛兮，似或存。 (*zhàn xī, sì huò cún* — "deep-still, ah, it seems perhaps to exist")
- **Says:** it is clear and deep (or submerged), and seems as if it may exist.
- **Act:** asserts (double hedge: 似 (*sì* — "seems") + 或 (*huò* — "perhaps"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** the Tao.
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** and (parallel to Unit 2)
- **Open:** 存 (*cún*): "exists / is present" vs "endures". Wang Bi notes a variant 若 (*ruò* — "as if") for 或; Heshang Gong reads 似若存 and turns it to people: 言當湛然安靜，故能長存不亡 (*yán dāng zhàn rán ān jìng, gù néng cháng cún bù wáng* — "it says one should be deep-still and calm, and so can long endure and not perish").

### Unit 5
> 吾不知誰之子，象帝之先。 (*wú bù zhī shuí zhī zǐ, xiàng dì zhī xiān* — "I do not know whose child it is; it images what came before the Di")
- **Says:** the speaker does not know whose offspring it is; it appears to precede the Di.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** 吾 (*wú* — "I"), the text's speaker (Heshang Gong: 老子言 (*lǎo zǐ yán* — "Laozi says")); "it" = the Tao.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** (1) Negator 不 (*bù* — "not") on knowing. 誰 (*shuí* — "who") is an embedded question, not a question asked. (2) 象 (*xiàng*): "seems / resembles" vs "image / figure [of what is] before the Di". (3) 帝 (*dì*): Wang Bi 帝，天帝也 (*dì, tiān dì yě* — "Di is the Heavenly Thearch"); Heshang Gong: 道自在天帝之前 (*dào zì zài tiān dì zhī qián* — "the Tao exists of itself before the Heavenly Thearch").

# Chapter 5

**The chapter as a whole:** Sky and earth are not humane and treat the myriad things as straw dogs; the sage, likewise not humane, treats the people the same way. The space between sky and earth is like a bellows: empty yet not exhausted, the more it moves the more it yields. Much speech soon runs out; better to keep to the center.

### Unit 1
> 天地不仁，以萬物為芻狗； (*tiān dì bù rén, yǐ wàn wù wéi chú gǒu* — "sky and earth are not humane; they take the myriad things as straw dogs")
- **Says:** sky and earth are not humane; they treat all things as straw dogs.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** sky and earth; the myriad things.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (opening)
- **Open:** (1) Negator 不 (*bù* — "not"): negates the quality 仁 (*rén* — "humane / kind"): callous, or simply not partial (Wang Bi: 任自然，無為無造 (*rèn zì rán, wú wéi wú zào* — "they leave things to the self-so, doing nothing and making nothing")). (2) 芻狗 (*chú gǒu*): "straw dogs" (ritual effigies, a well-attested sense) vs "grass and dogs".
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi splits it: 地不為獸生芻而獸食芻，不為人生狗而人食狗 (*dì bù wèi shòu shēng chú ér shòu shí chú, bù wèi rén shēng gǒu ér rén shí gǒu* — "earth does not grow grass for beasts, yet beasts eat grass; it does not produce dogs for people, yet people eat dogs"). Heshang Gong: 視之如芻草狗畜，不貴望其報也 (*shì zhī rú chú cǎo gǒu chù, bù guì wàng qí bào yě* — "they regard them like straw-grass and dog-livestock, and do not prize them or expect a return").

### Unit 2
> 聖人不仁，以百姓為芻狗。 (*shèng rén bù rén, yǐ bǎi xìng wéi chú gǒu* — "the sage is not humane; they take the hundred clans as straw dogs")
- **Says:** the sage is not humane and treats the people as straw dogs.
- **Act:** asserts / describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; 百姓 (*bǎi xìng* — "the hundred surnames": the common people).
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel; the sage matches sky and earth)
- **Open:** As Unit 1. Wang Bi: 聖人與天地合其德 (*shèng rén yǔ tiān dì hé qí dé* — "the sage joins sky and earth in their virtue"); Heshang Gong: 不責望其禮意 (*bù zé wàng qí lǐ yì* — "does not demand their courtesy or gratitude").

### Unit 3
> 天地之間，其猶橐籥乎？ (*tiān dì zhī jiān, qí yóu tuó yuè hū* — "the space between sky and earth — is it not like a bellows?")
- **Says:** the space between sky and earth is surely like a bellows.
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical: implies "it is like a bellows")
- **Who acts / of whom:** the space between sky and earth.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (new image)
- **Open:** 其 … 乎 (*qí … hū* — "surely … ?"), a rhetorical question. 橐籥 (*tuó yuè*): one thing (a bellows with its tube) or two (Wang Bi: 橐，排橐也；籥，樂籥也 (*tuó, pái tuó yě; yuè, yuè yuè yě* — "tuo is the blowing-bag; yue is the musical pipe")).

### Unit 4
> 虛而不屈，動而愈出。 (*xū ér bù qū, dòng ér yù chū* — "empty yet not exhausted; moved, the more comes out")
- **Says:** empty, it does not give out; worked, it puts out all the more.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the bellows / the space between sky and earth.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (explains the bellows)
- **Open:** Negator 不 (*bù* — "not"): negates 屈 (*qū* — here "to be used up"); Wang Bi notes the variant 掘 (*jué* — "to exhaust"). No other division.

### Unit 5
> 多言數窮，不如守中。 (*duō yán shù qióng, bù rú shǒu zhōng* — "much speech is soon exhausted; better to keep to the center")
- **Says:** many words quickly run dry; it is not as good as holding to the middle.
- **Act:** advises (comparative maxim)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: one / the ruler.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (turn from image to lesson)
- **Open:** (1) 數 (*shuò* — "soon / often") vs *shù* ("reckoning / fate": "much speech meets its fated end"); Heshang Gong's note records both, Wang Bi's reading as *shù* 理數 (*lǐ shù* — "pattern-number"). (2) 中 (*zhōng*): "center / mean" vs "the inside (the empty within, like the bellows)". (3) 不如 (*bù rú* — "not as good as"): comparative negation.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi ties it to the bellows: 橐籥而守數中則無窮盡 (*tuó yuè ér shǒu shù zhōng zé wú qióng jìn* — "if, like the bellows, one keeps to the empty center, there is no running out"). Heshang Gong: 守德於中 (*shǒu dé yú zhōng* — "keep virtue within").

# Chapter 6

**The chapter as a whole:** The valley spirit does not die, and is named the dark female; the dark female's gate is named the root of sky and earth. Faint and unbroken, it seems barely there, and using it does not wear it out.

### Unit 1
> 谷神不死，是謂玄牝。 (*gǔ shén bù sǐ, shì wèi xuán pìn* — "the valley spirit does not die; this is called the dark female")
- **Says:** the spirit of the valley does not die; it is called the dark female.
- **Act:** asserts + names/defines (是謂 (*shì wèi* — "this is called"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** the valley spirit.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (opening)
- **Open:** (1) Negator 不 (*bù* — "not") on 死 (*sǐ* — "die"). (2) 谷神 (*gǔ shén*): "the valley's spirit" (Wang Bi: 谷中央無谷也 (*gǔ zhōng yāng wú gǔ yě* — "the no-valley at the center of the valley")) vs Heshang Gong's verb 谷，養也 (*gǔ, yǎng yě* — "gu means to nourish"): 人能養神則不死也 (*rén néng yǎng shén zé bù sǐ yě* — "if a person can nourish the spirits, they do not die").
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong reads the dark female as the body: 玄，天也，於人為鼻；牝，地也，於人為口 (*xuán, tiān yě, yú rén wéi bí; pìn, dì yě, yú rén wéi kǒu* — "dark is heaven, in a person the nose; female is earth, in a person the mouth").

### Unit 2
> 玄牝之門，是謂天地根。 (*xuán pìn zhī mén, shì wèi tiān dì gēn* — "the dark female's gate: this is called the root of sky and earth")
- **Says:** the gate of the dark female is called the root of sky and earth.
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** the dark female's gate.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (chained naming)
- **Open:** none. Wang Bi: 門，玄牝之所由也 (*mén, xuán pìn zhī suǒ yóu yě* — "the gate is that through which the dark female proceeds"); Heshang Gong: 鼻口之門 (*bí kǒu zhī mén* — "the gate of nose and mouth").

### Unit 3
> 綿綿若存，用之不勤。 (*mián mián ruò cún, yòng zhī bù qín* — "unbroken as a thread, as if present; used, it does not wear out")
- **Says:** continuous and faint, it seems to be there; using it does not exhaust it.
- **Act:** asserts (or advises, per Heshang Gong)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the dark female / its gate; the user unstated.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** (1) Negator 不 (*bù* — "not") on 勤 (*qín*): "is not exhausted" vs "takes no toil". (2) 若存 (*ruò cún* — "as if present"): hedged existence. (3) Description (Wang Bi: 用而不勞也 (*yòng ér bù láo yě* — "used and not wearied")) vs instruction for breathing (Heshang Gong: 用氣常寬舒，不當急疾懃勞也 (*yòng qì cháng kuān shū, bù dāng jí jí qín láo yě* — "use the breath always loosely and easily; one should not rush or labour it")).

# Chapter 7

**The chapter as a whole:** Sky and earth last because they do not live for themselves. So the sage puts their own person last and ends up first, sets it aside and keeps it. Is it not because they have no private aims? This is how they fulfil what is their own.

### Unit 1
> 天長地久。 (*tiān cháng dì jiǔ* — "sky is long-lasting, earth endures")
- **Says:** sky lasts long and earth endures.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** sky, earth.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (opening)
- **Open:** none

### Unit 2
> 天地所以能長且久者，以其不自生，故能長生。 (*tiān dì suǒ yǐ néng cháng qiě jiǔ zhě, yǐ qí bù zì shēng, gù néng cháng shēng* — "that by which sky and earth can be long and lasting is that they do not live for themselves, so they can live long")
- **Says:** sky and earth can last because they do not live for (or of) themselves; therefore they live long.
- **Act:** asserts (explanation: 所以 … 者，以 … (*suǒ yǐ … zhě, yǐ …* — "the reason … is …"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** sky and earth.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** because (explains Unit 1); 故 (*gù* — "therefore") inside
- **Open:** Negator 不 (*bù* — "not") on 自生 (*zì shēng*): "do not live for themselves" (Wang Bi: 自生則與物爭 (*zì shēng zé yǔ wù zhēng* — "if they lived for themselves they would contend with things")) vs "do not give birth to themselves (are not self-generated)" vs Heshang Gong 以其不求生 (*yǐ qí bù qiú shēng* — "because they do not seek life").

### Unit 3
> 是以聖人後其身而身先，外其身而身存。 (*shì yǐ shèng rén hòu qí shēn ér shēn xiān, wài qí shēn ér shēn cún* — "therefore the sage puts their person behind and the person is ahead, puts their person outside and the person survives")
- **Says:** so the sage places their own self last and that self comes first; treats it as outside and it is preserved.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage; their own 身 (*shēn* — "body / self").
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore/so (是以 (*shì yǐ* — "for this reason"))
- **Open:** 外其身 (*wài qí shēn*): "disregards one's self" vs "puts one's self outside (at risk)". Heshang Gong reads social precedence: 先人而後己 (*xiān rén ér hòu jǐ* — "putting others first and oneself after"); the people then 敬之先以為長 (*jìng zhī xiān yǐ wéi zhǎng* — "respect him and put him first as their elder").

### Unit 4
> 非以其無私邪？ (*fēi yǐ qí wú sī xié* — "is it not because they have no private self?")
- **Says:** surely it is because they have no selfish aims.
- **Act:** asks (rhetorical: implies "yes, it is because of their selflessness")
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** because (explains Unit 3)
- **Open:** Negators: 非 (*fēi* — "is it not") denies identity (the question form); 無 (*wú* — "without") negates existence of 私 (*sī* — "the private / self-interest"). 邪 (*xié / yé* — question particle). Heshang Gong: 公正無私 (*gōng zhèng wú sī* — "impartial and upright, without selfishness").

### Unit 5
> 故能成其私。 (*gù néng chéng qí sī* — "therefore they can complete their private [self]")
- **Says:** therefore they are able to accomplish their own private ends (or self).
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage.
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** therefore/so (故 (*gù* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** Whether 成其私 (*chéng qí sī*) names a goal the selflessness serves (strategic) or a result that simply follows. Wang Bi takes 私 as the self that comes first and survives: 身先身存，故曰能成其私也 (*shēn xiān shēn cún, gù yuē néng chéng qí sī yě* — "the person comes first and the person survives; therefore it says they can complete their private self"). Heshang Gong: 聖人無私而己自厚 (*shèng rén wú sī ér jǐ zì hòu* — "the sage has no selfishness, and is enriched of themselves").

# Chapter 8

**The chapter as a whole:** The highest good is like water, which benefits all things without contending and settles in the low places people dislike, and so is near the Tao. A list of seven "good in …" pairs follows, for dwelling, heart, giving, speech, governing, work and movement. Because it does not contend, it incurs no fault.

### Unit 1
> 上善若水。 (*shàng shàn ruò shuǐ* — "the highest good is like water")
- **Says:** the highest goodness (or skill) is like water.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** "the highest good": a quality, or the person who has it.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (opening)
- **Open:** 上善 (*shàng shàn*): an abstract "highest good" vs "the person of highest good" (Heshang Gong: 上善之人，如水之性 (*shàng shàn zhī rén, rú shuǐ zhī xìng* — "the person of highest good is like the nature of water")). 善 (*shàn*): "good" vs "skilled".

### Unit 2
> 水善利萬物而不爭，處眾人之所惡，故幾於道。 (*shuǐ shàn lì wàn wù ér bù zhēng, chǔ zhòng rén zhī suǒ wù, gù jī yú dào* — "water is good at benefiting the myriad things and does not contend, dwells where the many dislike, and so is near the Tao")
- **Says:** water is good at benefiting all things and does not compete; it occupies what the crowd dislikes; therefore it is close to the Tao.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** water; the myriad things; the crowd.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** because (explains Unit 1); 而 (*ér* — "and yet") and 故 (*gù* — "therefore") inside
- **Open:** (1) Negator 不 (*bù* — "not") on 爭 (*zhēng* — "contend"). (2) 幾 (*jī* — "near / almost"): Wang Bi: 道無水有，故曰幾也 (*dào wú shuǐ yǒu, gù yuē jī yě* — "the Tao is nothing, water is something; therefore it says 'near'"). (3) What the crowd dislikes: Wang Bi 人惡卑也 (*rén wù bēi yě* — "people dislike the low").

### Unit 3
> 居善地，心善淵，與善仁，言善信，政善治，事善能，動善時。 (*jū shàn dì, xīn shàn yuān, yǔ shàn rén, yán shàn xìn, zhèng shàn zhì, shì shàn néng, dòng shàn shí* — "in dwelling, good at ground; in heart, good at depth; in giving, good at humaneness; in speech, good at trust; in governing, good at order; in affairs, good at ability; in moving, good at timing")
- **Says:** seven pairs: in dwelling, the good is the place (the ground); in the heart, depth; in giving, humaneness; in speech, trustworthiness; in governing, order; in work, competence; in movement, timeliness.
- **Act:** asserts (list)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: water (continuing Unit 2) or the person of highest good.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (list)
- **Open:** (1) Syntax of X善Y (*X shàn Y*): "in X, good at Y", "in X, values Y as good", or "the excellence of X is Y". (2) Subject: water vs a person. (3) 與 (*yǔ*): "giving" vs "associating with others". Wang Bi's lemma has 正 (*zhèng* — "upright") for 政 (*zhèng* — "governing"), noted as interchangeable.
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong reads every pair of water, e.g. 居善地 as 水性善喜於地 (*shuǐ xìng shàn xǐ yú dì* — "water's nature delights in the ground") and 動善時 as 夏散冬凝 (*xià sàn dōng níng* — "dispersing in summer, freezing in winter"). Wang Bi reads people: 言人皆應於治道也 (*yán rén jiē yìng yú zhì dào yě* — "it says people all respond to the way of order"), with a variant 水 (*shuǐ* — "water") for 人 (*rén* — "people").

### Unit 4
> 夫唯不爭，故無尤。 (*fú wéi bù zhēng, gù wú yóu* — "just because it does not contend, therefore there is no fault")
- **Says:** precisely because it does not contend, it incurs no blame.
- **Act:** if/when … then (夫唯 … 故 (*fú wéi … gù* — "just because … therefore"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** water / the person of highest good.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** because (sums up)
- **Open:** Negators 不 (*bù* — "not") on contending; 無 (*wú* — "no") on 尤 (*yóu*): "no blame from others" (Heshang Gong: 天下無有怨尤水者也 (*tiān xià wú yǒu yuàn yóu shuǐ zhě yě* — "no one under heaven resents or blames water")) vs "no fault / mistake".

# Chapter 9

**The chapter as a whole:** Filling a thing to the brim is worse than stopping; honing an edge keeps it from lasting. A hall full of gold and jade cannot be guarded, and arrogance in wealth brings its own disaster. To withdraw once the work is done is heaven's way.

### Unit 1
> 持而盈之，不如其已； (*chí ér yíng zhī, bù rú qí yǐ* — "holding it and filling it is not as good as stopping")
- **Says:** to hold something and fill it full is worse than stopping.
- **Act:** advises (comparative maxim)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: one / anyone.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (opening)
- **Open:** (1) 不如 (*bù rú* — "not as good as"), comparative negation. (2) 持而盈之 (*chí ér yíng zhī*): "keep (a vessel) full" vs "cling to something and add to it". Heshang Gong: 持滿必傾 (*chí mǎn bì qīng* — "hold it full and it will tip"). Wang Bi reads it of virtue: 持謂不失德也 (*chí wèi bù shī dé yě* — "'holding' means not losing virtue").

### Unit 2
> 揣而銳之，不可長保。 (*chuǎi ér ruì zhī, bù kě cháng bǎo* — "hammering it and sharpening it, it cannot be long kept")
- **Says:** working an edge and making it sharp, it cannot be kept long.
- **Act:** asserts (warning)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated one; the blade or point.
- **Force:** can/possible (negated: cannot)
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** (1) Negator 不 (*bù* — "not") on 可 (*kě* — "can"). (2) 揣 (*chuǎi*): "hammer / temper" vs "measure / probe" (Heshang Gong 揣，治也 (*chuǎi, zhì yě* — "chuai means to work")). Wang Bi's lemma 梲 (*ruì*, per his note — variant graph for "sharpen"); his comment: 既揣末令尖，又銳之令利 (*jì chuǎi mò lìng jiān, yòu ruì zhī lìng lì* — "having worked the tip to a point, one also sharpens it to make it keen").

### Unit 3
> 金玉滿堂，莫之能守； (*jīn yù mǎn táng, mò zhī néng shǒu* — "gold and jade fill the hall; no one can guard them")
- **Says:** when gold and jade fill the hall, no one can keep them.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** no one; the treasure.
- **Force:** can/possible (negated)
- **Link:** none (new pair)
- **Open:** none. Negator 莫 (*mò* — "no one"); 之 (*zhī* — "them") is the fronted object.

### Unit 4
> 富貴而驕，自遺其咎。 (*fù guì ér jiāo, zì yí qí jiù* — "rich and noble and arrogant, one leaves oneself one's own disaster")
- **Says:** being rich, high-ranked and arrogant, one brings disaster on oneself.
- **Act:** asserts (warning)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated one; 自 (*zì* — "oneself").
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** none of substance. 遺 (*yí* — "leave / bequeath"; Heshang Gong's note reads *wèi* — "give to"). 咎 (*jiù* — "blame / calamity").

### Unit 5
> 功遂身退，天之道。 (*gōng suì shēn tuì, tiān zhī dào* — "the work done, the person withdraws: heaven's way")
- **Says:** when the work is accomplished, the person steps back; this is the way of heaven.
- **Act:** names/defines (nominal predication)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated person who withdraws; heaven.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (conclusion)
- **Open:** Wang Bi notes that Heshang Gong and other editions read 功成名遂身退 (*gōng chéng míng suì shēn tuì* — "work completed, name achieved, the person withdraws"). Wang Bi: 四時更運，功成則移 (*sì shí gēng yùn, gōng chéng zé yí* — "the four seasons rotate; when the work is done, they move on").

# Chapter 10

**The chapter as a whole:** Six challenge-questions to "you": can you hold the one without parting, soften the breath like an infant, clear the dark seeing without flaw, love the people and govern without cunning, take the female part as heaven's gate opens and closes, and see clearly in every direction without doing? Then a closing description: giving birth and rearing without possessing, acting without presuming, leading without ruling, which is called dark virtue.

### Unit 1
> 載營魄抱一，能無離乎？ (*zài yíng pò bào yī, néng wú lí hū* — "carrying the soul, embracing the one, can you be without parting?")
- **Says:** settling the bodily soul and embracing the one, can [you] keep them from separating?
- **Act:** asks (a challenge put to the reader: implies one should be able to)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: "you / one".
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** none (opening)
- **Open:** (1) 載 (*zài*): verb "carry / dwell in" (Wang Bi 載猶處也 (*zài yóu chǔ yě* — "zai is like 'to dwell'")) vs a sentence-opening particle. (2) 營魄 (*yíng pò*): Wang Bi 人之常居處也 (*rén zhī cháng jū chǔ yě* — "a person's constant dwelling"); Heshang Gong 營魄，魂魄也 (*yíng pò, hún pò yě* — "yingpo means the hun and po souls"). (3) 一 (*yī* — "one"): Wang Bi 人之真也 (*rén zhī zhēn yě* — "a person's genuineness"); Heshang Gong 道始所生，太和之精氣 (*dào shǐ suǒ shēng, tài hé zhī jīng qì* — "what the Tao first produces, the essential breath of great harmony"). Negator 無 (*wú* — "without") on 離 (*lí* — "parting"). 乎 (*hū* — question particle).

### Unit 2
> 專氣致柔，能如嬰兒乎？ (*zhuān qì zhì róu, néng rú yīng ér hū* — "concentrating breath to reach softness, can you be like an infant?")
- **Says:** focusing the breath and attaining softness, can [you] be like a newborn?
- **Act:** asks (challenge)
- **Who acts / of whom:** "you / one".
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** and (series)
- **Open:** 專 (*zhuān*): "concentrate / guard" (Heshang Gong 專守精氣 (*zhuān shǒu jīng qì* — "concentrate on and guard the essential breath")) vs Wang Bi 專，任也 (*zhuān, rèn yě* — "zhuan means to give free rein to"): 任自然之氣 (*rèn zì rán zhī qì* — "giving free rein to the self-so breath"). Wang Bi's lemma lacks 如 (*rú* — "like").

### Unit 3
> 滌除玄覽，能無疵乎？ (*dí chú xuán lǎn, néng wú cī hū* — "washing clean the dark seeing, can you be without flaw?")
- **Says:** cleansing the dark vision (or mirror), can [you] leave no blemish on it?
- **Act:** asks (challenge)
- **Who acts / of whom:** "you / one".
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** (1) 覽 (*lǎn*): "seeing / survey" vs "mirror" (a current reading that fits 疵 (*cī* — "blemish")). (2) Negator 無 (*wú* — "without") on flaw.
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 心居玄冥之處，覽知萬事，故謂之玄覽 (*xīn jū xuán míng zhī chù, lǎn zhī wàn shì, gù wèi zhī xuán lǎn* — "the heart dwells in the dark and obscure place and surveys and knows the myriad affairs; therefore it is called the dark survey"). Wang Bi: 能滌除邪飾，至於極覽 (*néng dí chú xié shì, zhì yú jí lǎn* — "able to wash away false ornament and reach the utmost seeing").

### Unit 4
> 愛民治國，能無知乎？ (*ài mín zhì guó, néng wú zhī hū* — "loving the people and governing the state, can you be without knowing?")
- **Says:** loving the people and ordering the state, can [you] do it without (cunning) knowledge?
- **Act:** asks (challenge)
- **Who acts / of whom:** "you", here plainly a ruler.
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** 無知 (*wú zhī*), negator 無 (*wú* — "without"): "without using cleverness" (Wang Bi: 治國無以智，猶棄智也 (*zhì guó wú yǐ zhì, yóu qì zhì yě* — "governing the state without cleverness is like discarding cleverness")) vs Heshang Gong 布施惠德，無令下知也 (*bù shī huì dé, wú lìng xià zhī yě* — "bestowing kindness without letting those below know").

### Unit 5
> 天門開闔，能為雌乎？ (*tiān mén kāi hé, néng wéi cí hū* — "as heaven's gate opens and closes, can you be the hen?")
- **Says:** as the gate of heaven opens and shuts, can [you] play the female?
- **Act:** asks (challenge)
- **Who acts / of whom:** "you / one".
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** (1) 天門 (*tiān mén* — "heaven's gate"): Wang Bi 天下之所由從也 (*tiān xià zhī suǒ yóu cóng yě* — "that by which all under heaven proceeds"), its opening and closing being 治亂之際 (*zhì luàn zhī jì* — "the junctures of order and disorder"); Heshang Gong the pole-star palace for the state and 鼻孔 (*bí kǒng* — "the nostrils") for the body. (2) No negator in the base; both commentators' lemmas read 能無雌 (*néng wú cí* — "can you be without the female?"), though Wang Bi's comment reads 能為雌乎 (*néng wéi cí hū* — "can you be the female?"), and Heshang Gong's comment too says 當如雌牝安靜柔弱 (*dāng rú cí pìn ān jìng róu ruò* — "one should be like the female: at peace, calm, soft and yielding").

### Unit 6
> 明白四達，能無為乎？ (*míng bái sì dá, néng wú wéi hū* — "bright and clear, reaching the four directions, can you be without doing?")
- **Says:** with clarity reaching everywhere, can [you] refrain from acting?
- **Act:** asks (challenge)
- **Who acts / of whom:** "you / one".
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** Negator 無 (*wú* — "without") on 為 (*wéi* — "doing"). Heshang Gong's lemma reads 能無知 (*néng wú zhī* — "can you be without knowing?"), i.e. Units 4 and 6 swap; Wang Bi has 能無以為乎 (*néng wú yǐ wéi hū* — "can you be without using doing?"). 明白 (*míng bái*): one's own clarity (Wang Bi: 至明四達 (*zhì míng sì dá* — "utmost clarity reaching in four directions")) vs the Tao's light filling the world (Heshang Gong).

### Unit 7
> 生之畜之，生而不有，為而不恃，長而不宰，是謂玄德。 (*shēng zhī xù zhī, shēng ér bù yǒu, wéi ér bù shì, zhǎng ér bù zǎi, shì wèi xuán dé* — "births them, rears them; births and does not own, acts and does not presume, raises and does not rule: this is called dark virtue")
- **Says:** to give life to things and nurture them, to produce without possessing, act without relying, raise without ruling: this is called dark virtue.
- **Act:** describes + names/defines (是謂 (*shì wèi* — "this is called"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: the Tao (Heshang Gong: 道生萬物而畜養之 (*dào shēng wàn wù ér xù yǎng zhī* — "the Tao produces the myriad things and rears them")) or the "you" addressed above (Wang Bi: 不塞其原也 (*bù sè qí yuán yě* — "not blocking their source")); 之 (*zhī* — "them"): things / people.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (shift from questions to statement)
- **Open:** (1) Negator 不 (*bù* — "not") three times, on owning, presuming, ruling. (2) 長 (*zhǎng*): "rear / raise" vs "be elder / head over". 宰 (*zǎi*): "rule / steward" vs Heshang Gong's 不宰割以為器用 (*bù zǎi gē yǐ wéi qì yòng* — "does not carve them up to make tools"). (3) 德 (*dé* — "virtue / potency").

# Chapter 11

**The chapter as a whole:** Three images: in a wheel's hub, a clay pot and a room with cut doors and windows, the use comes from the empty part. Hence what is there provides the benefit, and what is not there provides the use.

### Unit 1
> 三十輻共一轂，當其無，有車之用。 (*sān shí fú gòng yī gǔ, dāng qí wú, yǒu chē zhī yòng* — "thirty spokes share one hub; at its nothing, there is the cart's use")
- **Says:** thirty spokes share one hub; at its empty part lies the cart's usefulness.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the spokes, the hub, its emptiness.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (opening)
- **Open:** (1) Pause 當其無，有車之用 (*dāng qí wú, yǒu chē zhī yòng* — "at its nothing, there is the cart's use") vs 當其無有，車之用 (*dāng qí wú yǒu, chē zhī yòng* — "at its nothing-and-something [lies] the cart's use"); both grammatical. (2) 其 (*qí* — "its"): the hub's (Heshang Gong: 轂中有孔 (*gǔ zhōng yǒu kǒng* — "in the hub there is a hole")). 無 (*wú* — "nothing / empty"), here a noun, not a negator of a verb.
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 轂所以能統三十輻者，無也 (*gǔ suǒ yǐ néng tǒng sān shí fú zhě, wú yě* — "that by which the hub can unite thirty spokes is nothing"). Heshang Gong adds bodily and state readings: emptying the five organs so the spirit returns.

### Unit 2
> 埏埴以為器，當其無，有器之用。 (*yán zhí yǐ wéi qì, dāng qí wú, yǒu qì zhī yòng* — "kneading clay to make a vessel; at its nothing, there is the vessel's use")
- **Says:** clay is worked into a vessel; at its emptiness lies the vessel's use.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated potter; the vessel.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** Same pause alternative as Unit 1. Heshang Gong: 埏，和也；埴，土也 (*yán, hé yě; zhí, tǔ yě* — "yan means to mix; zhi means clay"); 器中空虛，故得有所盛受 (*qì zhōng kōng xū, gù dé yǒu suǒ shèng shòu* — "the vessel is empty within, so it can hold things").

### Unit 3
> 鑿戶牖以為室，當其無，有室之用。 (*záo hù yǒu yǐ wéi shì, dāng qí wú, yǒu shì zhī yòng* — "cutting doors and windows to make a room; at its nothing, there is the room's use")
- **Says:** doors and windows are cut to make a room; at its emptiness lies the room's use.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated builder; the room.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** Same pause alternative. Heshang Gong takes the emptiness as both the openings (人得以出入觀視 (*rén dé yǐ chū rù guān shì* — "people can go in and out and look")) and the room's interior (人得以居處 (*rén dé yǐ jū chǔ* — "people can live in it")).

### Unit 4
> 故有之以為利，無之以為用。 (*gù yǒu zhī yǐ wéi lì, wú zhī yǐ wéi yòng* — "so its being-there makes the benefit, its not-being-there makes the use")
- **Says:** therefore what is present serves as the advantage, and what is absent serves as the use.
- **Act:** asserts (general conclusion)
- **Who acts / of whom:** "something" and "nothing" in each thing.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** therefore/so (故 (*gù* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** 有之 / 無之 (*yǒu zhī / wú zhī*): "the something of it / the nothing of it" (之 (*zhī*) as a particle) vs "having it / lacking it" (之 as object). 利 (*lì* — "benefit / advantage") and 用 (*yòng* — "use / function") as distinct or near-synonyms. Wang Bi: 言無者，有之所以為利，皆賴無以為用也 (*yán wú zhě, yǒu zhī suǒ yǐ wéi lì, jiē lài wú yǐ wéi yòng yě* — "it says that 'nothing' is what lets 'something' be a benefit: all rely on nothing for their use").

# Chapter 12

**The chapter as a whole:** The five colours blind, the five tones deafen, the five flavours spoil the palate, galloping and hunting madden the heart, and rare goods harm one's conduct (or block one's going). So the sage serves the belly, not the eye: they discard that and take this.

### Unit 1
> 五色令人目盲； (*wǔ sè lìng rén mù máng* — "the five colours make a person's eyes blind")
- **Says:** the five colours make people's eyes go blind.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the five colours; people.

- **Link:** none (opening)
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong: 貪淫好色則傷精失明也 (*tān yín hào sè zé shāng jīng shī míng yě* — "craving and indulging in colour harms the essence and loses sight").

### Unit 2
> 五音令人耳聾； (*wǔ yīn lìng rén ěr lóng* — "the five tones make a person's ears deaf")

- **Open:** none

### Unit 4
> 馳騁畋獵，令人心發狂； (*chí chěng tián liè, lìng rén xīn fā kuáng* — "galloping and hunting make a person's heart go wild")

### Unit 5
> 難得之貨，令人行妨。 (*nán dé zhī huò, lìng rén xíng fáng* — "hard-to-get goods make a person's conduct harmed / going obstructed")

### Unit 6
> 是以聖人為腹不為目，故去彼取此。 (*shì yǐ shèng rén wèi fù bù wèi mù, gù qù bǐ qǔ cǐ* — "therefore the sage is for the belly, not for the eye; so they discard that and take this")
- **Says:** so the sage acts for the belly and not for the eye; therefore they reject the one and keep the other.
- **Act:** describes a person
- **Who acts / of whom:** the sage.
- **Force:** habitual
- **Link:** therefore/so (是以 (*shì yǐ* — "for this reason"), then 故 (*gù* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** Negator 不 (*bù* — "not") on 為 (*wèi* — "for the sake of"). "That" = the eye (outward stimulation), "this" = the belly. Wang Bi: 為腹者以物養己，為目者以物役己 (*wèi fù zhě yǐ wù yǎng jǐ, wèi mù zhě yǐ wù yì jǐ* — "serving the belly uses things to nourish oneself; serving the eye lets things enslave oneself").

# Chapter 13

**The chapter as a whole:** The chapter states two sayings, that favour and disgrace are alarming and that one should value great trouble as one's body, then explains each. Favour is lowly, so gaining and losing it are equally alarming; I have great trouble because I have a body, and without one what trouble could I have? So one who values (or loves) their body as they would the world can be entrusted with the world.

### Unit 1
> 寵辱若驚，貴大患若身。 (*chǒng rǔ ruò jīng, guì dà huàn ruò shēn* — "favour and disgrace: as if alarmed; value great trouble as the body")
- **Says:** favour and disgrace are both met as if with alarm; great trouble is valued like (or as) one's own body.
- **Act:** asserts (paired maxims; the next units treat them as sayings to be explained)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: people in general.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (opening)
- **Open:** (1) 若驚 (*ruò jīng*): "are as alarming" vs "one is as if startled by them". (2) 貴大患若身 (*guì dà huàn ruò shēn*): "value great trouble as you value your body" vs "great trouble is valued like the body (the body is the great trouble)".
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong: 貴，畏也；若，至也 (*guì, wèi yě; ruò, zhì yě* — "gui means to fear; ruo means to arrive"): "fear great trouble reaching the body"; and 身寵亦驚，身辱亦驚 (*shēn chǒng yì jīng, shēn rǔ yì jīng* — "favoured, one is alarmed; disgraced, one is alarmed"). Wang Bi: 寵必有辱，榮必有患 (*chǒng bì yǒu rǔ, róng bì yǒu huàn* — "favour always brings disgrace, glory always brings trouble"), and 大患，榮寵之屬也 (*dà huàn, róng chǒng zhī shǔ yě* — "great trouble is of the class of glory and favour").

### Unit 2
> 何謂寵辱若驚？ (*hé wèi chǒng rǔ ruò jīng* — "what is meant by 'favour and disgrace: as if alarmed'?")
- **Says:** what does "favour and disgrace are as alarming" mean?
- **Act:** asks (real question, posed in order to be answered)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the speaker, of the saying in Unit 1.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (opens explanation)
- **Open:** none. 何謂 (*hé wèi* — "what is meant by"). Heshang Gong's lemma is shorter: 何謂寵辱 (*hé wèi chǒng rǔ* — "what is meant by favour and disgrace?").

### Unit 3
> 寵為下，得之若驚，失之若驚，是謂寵辱若驚。 (*chǒng wéi xià, dé zhī ruò jīng, shī zhī ruò jīng, shì wèi chǒng rǔ ruò jīng* — "favour is lowly: getting it, as if alarmed; losing it, as if alarmed; this is what is called 'favour and disgrace: as if alarmed'")
- **Says:** favour is a lowly thing; one is alarmed at gaining it and alarmed at losing it; that is what the saying means.
- **Act:** names/defines (是謂 (*shì wèi* — "this is called"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated one who gains or loses favour; 之 (*zhī* — "it") = favour.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** because (answers Unit 2)
- **Open:** 寵為下 (*chǒng wéi xià*): "favour is lowly" vs "favour makes one an inferior" vs "being below, [on receiving favour] …". Heshang Gong reads 辱為下 (*rǔ wéi xià* — "disgrace is the low"), glossed 辱為下賤 (*rǔ wéi xià jiàn* — "disgrace is lowly and base"). Wang Bi reads 為下 (*wéi xià* — "being below") as the condition: 為下得寵辱榮患若驚 (*wéi xià dé chǒng rǔ róng huàn ruò jīng* — "one who is below, meeting favour, disgrace, glory or trouble, is as if alarmed").

### Unit 4
> 何謂貴大患若身？ (*hé wèi guì dà huàn ruò shēn* — "what is meant by 'value great trouble as the body'?")
- **Says:** what does "value great trouble as one's body" mean?
- **Act:** asks (real question, posed in order to be answered)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the speaker, of the second saying.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel to Unit 2)
- **Open:** none. Heshang Gong: 復還自問何故畏人若身 (*fù huán zì wèn hé gù wèi rén ruò shēn* — "again he asks himself: why fear [it] as [it reaches] a person's body?"), consistent with his reading of 貴 (*guì*) as "fear".

### Unit 5
> 吾所以有大患者，為吾有身，及吾無身，吾有何患？ (*wú suǒ yǐ yǒu dà huàn zhě, wèi wú yǒu shēn, jí wú wú shēn, wú yǒu hé huàn* — "the reason I have great trouble is that I have a body; when I have no body, what trouble do I have?")
- **Says:** I have great trouble because I have a body; once I have no body, what trouble could I have?
- **Act:** asserts, then asks (rhetorical: implies "none")
- **Who acts / of whom:** 吾 (*wú* — "I"), the speaker, standing for anyone.
- **Force:** always/universal; the last clause hypothetical
- **Link:** because (answers Unit 4)
- **Open:** (1) 及吾無身 (*jí wú wú shēn*), negator 無 (*wú* — "have no") on having a body: "when I come to have no body (at death)", "if I had no body", or "when I cease to treat the body as mine". 何 (*hé* — "what"). (2) 身 (*shēn*): "body" vs "self".
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 歸之自然也 (*guī zhī zì rán yě* — "returning it to the self-so"). Heshang Gong: 使吾無有身體，得道自然，輕舉昇雲 (*shǐ wú wú yǒu shēn tǐ, dé dào zì rán, qīng jǔ shēng yún* — "if I had no bodily form, attaining the Tao and the self-so, I would rise lightly into the clouds").

### Unit 6
> 故貴以身為天下，若可寄天下； (*gù guì yǐ shēn wéi tiān xià, ruò kě jì tiān xià* — "so one who values their body as / in acting for all under heaven, then can be lodged with all under heaven")
- **Says:** therefore one who values their own person as (or in caring for) the world can be entrusted with the world.
- **Act:** if/when … then (若 (*ruò*) read as "then / thus")
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: one / a ruler; the world entrusted to them.
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** therefore/so (故 (*gù* — "therefore"))
- **Open:** (1) 貴以身為天下 (*guì yǐ shēn wéi tiān xià*): "values the body as [if it were] the world", "values the body more than governing the world" (為 (*wèi*) "for"), or "values using one's own person in managing the world". (2) 若 (*ruò*): "then / so" vs "as if"; Wang Bi notes variants 則可寄 (*zé kě jì* — "then can lodge") and Heshang Gong's 則可寄於天下 (*zé kě jì yú tiān xià* — "then can be lodged with the world"). (3) 寄 (*jì* — "lodge, entrust temporarily").
- **Commentators:** Heshang Gong reads this unit as a warning: 言人君貴其身而賤人……則可寄立，不可以久也 (*yán rén jūn guì qí shēn ér jiàn rén … zé kě jì lì, bù kě yǐ jiǔ yě* — "it says a ruler who values himself and holds others cheap … can be set up only provisionally, not for long"). Wang Bi reads it as praise: 無以易其身，故曰貴也 (*wú yǐ yì qí shēn, gù yuē guì yě* — "nothing can be exchanged for one's person; therefore it says 'value'").

### Unit 7
> 愛以身為天下，若可託天下。 (*ài yǐ shēn wéi tiān xià, ruò kě tuō tiān xià* — "one who loves their body as / in acting for all under heaven, then can be entrusted with all under heaven")
- **Says:** one who cherishes their own person as (or in caring for) the world can have the world entrusted to them.
- **Act:** if/when … then
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: one / a ruler.
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** and (parallel to Unit 6)
- **Open:** As Unit 6; 託 (*tuō* — "entrust, rely on"). Heshang Gong's lemma reads 乃可以託於天下 (*nǎi kě yǐ tuō yú tiān xià* — "only then can be entrusted with the world"), and reads this unit as the positive case: 乃可以託其身於萬民之上，長無咎也 (*nǎi kě yǐ tuō qí shēn yú wàn mín zhī shàng, cháng wú jiù yě* — "only then can he entrust his person above the myriad people and long be without blame"). Wang Bi: 無物可以損其身，故曰愛也 (*wú wù kě yǐ sǔn qí shēn, gù yuē ài yě* — "nothing can harm one's person; therefore it says 'love'"); his comments swap 寄 (*jì* — "lodge") and 託 (*tuō* — "entrust") between the two units.

# Chapter 14

**The chapter as a whole:** Something that cannot be seen, heard or grasped gets three names; the three cannot be pursued to the end and so blend into one. Neither bright above nor dark below, endless and unnameable, it returns to no-thing: the shape of the shapeless, the image of no-thing, the elusive. You meet it and see no front, follow it and see no back. Holding to the ancient way, one manages what is present, and knowing the ancient beginning is called the thread of the Tao.

### Unit 1
> 視之不見，名曰夷； (*shì zhī bù jiàn, míng yuē yí* — "looked at, not seen: it is named 'level / invisible'")
- **Says:** what you look at and do not see is named "yi" (the level, the colourless).
- **Act:** names/defines (名曰 (*míng yuē* — "is named"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated looker ("one"); 之 (*zhī* — "it"), unnamed (Heshang Gong: 一 (*yī* — "the one")).
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (opening)
- **Open:** Negator 不 (*bù* — "not") on seeing. 夷 (*yí* — "level / smooth / faint"): Heshang Gong 無色曰夷 (*wú sè yuē yí* — "colourless is called yi").

### Unit 2
> 聽之不聞，名曰希； (*tīng zhī bù wén, míng yuē xī* — "listened to, not heard: it is named 'faint / rare'")
- **Says:** what you listen to and do not hear is named "xi" (the rarefied, the soundless).
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** as Unit 1.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** none. Negator 不 (*bù* — "not") on hearing. Heshang Gong: 無聲曰希 (*wú shēng yuē xī* — "soundless is called xi").

### Unit 3
> 搏之不得，名曰微。 (*bó zhī bù dé, míng yuē wēi* — "grasped at, not got: it is named 'minute'")
- **Says:** what you grab at and do not catch is named "wei" (the minute, the subtle).
- **Act:** names/defines
- **Who acts / of whom:** as Unit 1.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and (parallel)
- **Open:** none. Negator 不 (*bù* — "not") on getting. 搏 (*bó* — "seize / strike"); Heshang Gong: 無形曰微 (*wú xíng yuē wēi* — "formless is called wei").

### Unit 4
> 此三者不可致詰，故混而為一。 (*cǐ sān zhě bù kě zhì jié, gù hùn ér wéi yī* — "these three cannot be questioned to the end, so they blend and become one")
- **Says:** these three cannot be investigated to the bottom; therefore they merge into one.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the three (夷 / 希 / 微 (*yí / xī / wēi* — "level / faint / minute")).
- **Force:** can/possible (negated)
- **Link:** therefore/so (故 (*gù* — "therefore") inside); none to Unit 3
- **Open:** (1) Negator 不 (*bù* — "not") on 可 (*kě* — "can"). (2) 混而為一 (*hùn ér wéi yī*): "they merge and become one" vs "[one] blends them and takes them as one" (Heshang Gong: 混，合也 (*hùn, hé yě* — "hun means to combine"); 合於三名之而為一 (*hé yú sān míng zhī ér wéi yī* — "combining the three, one names it as one")).

### Unit 5
> 其上不皦，其下不昧。 (*qí shàng bù jiǎo, qí xià bù mèi* — "its upper part is not bright, its lower part is not dim")
- **Says:** above, it is not bright; below, it is not dark.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the "one" of Unit 4.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none
- **Open:** Negators 不 (*bù* — "not") twice, on qualities. 其上 / 其下 (*qí shàng / qí xià*): "its top / its bottom" vs "above it / below it" (Heshang Gong: 一在天上不皦皦光明 (*yī zài tiān shàng bù jiǎo jiǎo guāng míng* — "the one, up in the sky, is not dazzlingly bright")).

### Unit 6
> 繩繩兮不可名，復歸於無物。 (*shéng shéng xī bù kě míng, fù guī yú wú wù* — "endless as a cord, it cannot be named; it returns again to no-thing")
- **Says:** stretching on and on, it cannot be named, and goes back to being no thing.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** the one.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** (1) Negators 不 (*bù* — "not") on 可名 (*kě míng* — "can be named"); 無 (*wú* — "no") in 無物 (*wú wù* — "no-thing"), negating existence of a thing. (2) 繩繩 (*shéng shéng*): "continuous" (Heshang Gong: 動行無窮極也 (*dòng xíng wú qióng jí yě* — "moving without end")). (3) Heshang Gong turns the return into an instruction: 復當歸之於無質 (*fù dāng guī zhī yú wú zhì* — "again one should return it to no-substance"). Wang Bi's lemma lacks 兮 (*xī* — exclamatory particle).

### Unit 7
> 是謂無狀之狀，無物之象，是謂惚恍。 (*shì wèi wú zhuàng zhī zhuàng, wú wù zhī xiàng, shì wèi hū huǎng* — "this is called the shape of no shape, the image of no thing; this is called the vague and elusive")
- **Says:** this is called the shape of what has no shape, the image of what is no thing; this is called the indistinct and evanescent.
- **Act:** names/defines (是謂 (*shì wèi* — "this is called") twice)
- **Who acts / of whom:** the one.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** none (summary naming)
- **Open:** (1) 無狀之狀 (*wú zhuàng zhī zhuàng*): "the shape of the shapeless" vs "a shapeless shape". (2) 惚恍 (*hū huǎng*): Heshang Gong 若存若亡 (*ruò cún ruò wáng* — "as if present, as if gone"); Wang Bi 不可得而定也 (*bù kě dé ér dìng yě* — "cannot be fixed").
- **Commentators:** Wang Bi: 欲言無耶，而物由以成；欲言有耶，而不見其形 (*yù yán wú yé, ér wù yóu yǐ chéng; yù yán yǒu yé, ér bù jiàn qí xíng* — "you want to call it nothing, yet things come to be through it; you want to call it something, yet its form is not seen"). Han Fei reads 象 (*xiàng*) through its sense "elephant": 人希見生象也，而得死象之骨，案其圖以想其生也，故諸人之所以意想者皆謂之象也 (*rén xī jiàn shēng xiàng yě, ér dé sǐ xiàng zhī gǔ, àn qí tú yǐ xiǎng qí shēng yě, gù zhū rén zhī suǒ yǐ yì xiǎng zhě jiē wèi zhī xiàng yě* — "people rarely see a living elephant, but they get the bones of a dead one and, following its outline, picture it alive; therefore whatever people picture in thought is called an 'image'").

### Unit 8
> 迎之不見其首，隨之不見其後。 (*yíng zhī bù jiàn qí shǒu, suí zhī bù jiàn qí hòu* — "meeting it, you do not see its head; following it, you do not see its back")
- **Says:** going to meet it, one sees no front; following it, one sees no rear.
- **Act:** asserts
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated "one / you"; "it" = the one.
- **Force:** always/universal
- **Link:** and
- **Open:** none. Negators 不 (*bù* — "not") twice, on seeing. Heshang Gong: 一無端末 (*yī wú duān mò* — "the one has no beginning or end").

### Unit 9
> 執古之道，以御今之有。 (*zhí gǔ zhī dào, yǐ yù jīn zhī yǒu* — "holding the ancient way, by it manage what is present today")
- **Says:** hold to the way of old in order to manage the things that exist now.
- **Act:** advises (or describes a practice; grammar allows both)
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated: one / the ruler (Heshang Gong: 聖人 (*shèng rén* — "the sage")).
- **Force:** habitual / must
- **Link:** none (turn to application)
- **Open:** (1) Imperative vs description. (2) 古之道 (*gǔ zhī dào*): "the ancient Tao" vs "the way of antiquity (the old practice)". (3) 御 (*yù*): "drive / steer / manage". Wang Bi's lemma adds 有有其事 (*yǒu yǒu qí shì* — "'what is present' means having its affairs").

### Unit 10
> 能知古始，是謂道紀。 (*néng zhī gǔ shǐ, shì wèi dào jì* — "to be able to know the ancient beginning: this is called the thread of the Tao")
- **Says:** being able to know the ancient beginning is called the guiding thread of the Tao.
- **Act:** names/defines (是謂 (*shì wèi* — "this is called"))
- **Who acts / of whom:** unstated knower.
- **Force:** can/possible
- **Link:** and (completes Unit 9)
- **Open:** (1) 紀 (*jì*): "the end-thread of a skein" → "guideline / main principle" (Heshang Gong: 道綱紀 (*dào gāng jì* — "the Tao's main rope and guideline")). (2) 古始 (*gǔ shǐ*): "the ancient beginning" vs "antiquity and origins". Heshang Gong's lemma reads 以知古始 (*yǐ zhī gǔ shǐ* — "thereby know the ancient beginning"), linking it causally to Unit 9; Wang Bi: 雖在今可以知古始也 (*suī zài jīn kě yǐ zhī gǔ shǐ yě* — "though in the present, one can know the ancient beginning").
