## 廣 (Guǎng) — Broad

*Say "gwahng," dipping. Three times, in two chapters, and both times the breadth looks like its opposite or costs something to have.*

**Locked: 廣 → "broad," and "breadth" where it stands as a noun, in all three lines.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-11-15, setting aside his own call of 2026-09-07, which read "boundless" at chapter 41.)* *(Not "boundless," which takes the walls off a hall that has a roof, and is the infinite that the 極 (jí — the far end) entry forbids as "limitless." Not "reach far," which turns width into distance: "far" is 遠's (yuǎn — far). Not "vast," which is 恢恢's (huī huī — vast) at chapter 73. Not "expansive," which is a mood.)*

### The graph: a great hall, under a roof

> 廣，殿之大屋也。从广黃聲。
> *"廣 is the great hall of a palace. From 广 (yǎn — a roof), with 黃 (huáng — yellow) for sound."* — the *Shuowen Jiezi* (the Han dictionary)

**Breadth begins as a building's.** 广 is the roof, the part that marks a character as a building: it heads 府 (*fǔ* — a storehouse), 庫 (*kù* — an armoury), 廟 (*miào* — an ancestral temple). A 廣 is the widest span one roof can cover, the great hall of a palace. Character dictionaries list the graph from early Western Zhou bronzes, and give no oracle-bone form.

**Where the picture stops.** 黃 (*huáng*) gives the sound, and nothing in the word is yellow. And the hall has a roof. Its breadth is great, and it is not endless. The book's emptiness is bounded in the same way: `xu-虛.md` finds every 虛 (*xū* — empty) a container with its contents gone, *"walls still standing, nobody home."*

### Three lines, and breadth looks like lack, or is bought

> *Broad integrity seems not enough.*
> *Sturdy integrity seems slack.*
> *Substantial truth seems to waver.*
>
> 廣德若不足，
> 建德若偷，
> 質真若渝。
>
> — chapter 41

> *Tenderness, and so you can be brave.*
> *Frugality, and so you can be broad.*
> *Never pushing to be first in the world, and so you can become chief of the vessels.*
>
> 慈故能勇；
> 儉故能廣；
> 不敢為天下先，故能成器長。
>
> — chapter 67

> *Now, to abandon tenderness and grab at courage,*
> *to abandon frugality and grab at breadth,*
> *to abandon being last and grab at being first,*
> *that is death.*
>
> 今捨慈且勇，
> 捨儉且廣，
> 捨後且先，
> 死矣！
>
> — chapter 67

**The book never offers breadth for free.** In chapter 41, the broad seems to have too little. In chapter 67, breadth is the price frugality pays for, and grabbing it without paying is death. The word names a size every time. It never names a reach, a distance or an infinity.

### Chapter 41: a hall too broad to fill looks empty

> 廣德不盈，廓然無形，不可滿也。
> *"Broad integrity does not fill. Open and formless, it cannot be filled."* — Wang Bi

> 德行廣大之人若愚須不足也。
> *"A person whose conduct of integrity is broad and great seems foolish, as though they had too little."* — Heshang Gong

**Wang Bi's paradox is a matter of capacity.** What is broad cannot be filled, and so it seems not enough. 不盈 (*bù yíng* — not full) is the valley's and the vessel's word in this book: chapter 4's 用之或不盈 (*yòng zhī huò bù yíng* — "used, it never fills") and chapter 15's 保此道者不欲盈 (*bǎo cǐ dào zhě bù yù yíng* — "those who keep the Tao safe like this do not want to be full"). The Shuowen makes 盈 a full vessel (`gu-谷.md`).

**The saying stands beside one that builds.** The next line is 建德若偷 (*jiàn dé ruò tōu* — "sturdy integrity seems slack"), and 建 (*jiàn*) is to erect. `chapters/041.md` sets the two side by side: the span of a hall, and the setting-up of a structure. Each saying pairs a thing with the look of its own lack. *Broad* keeps the hall. *Boundless* gave the line a reason for the paradox, but it had to remove the walls to do it, and the building went with them.

### Chapter 67: breadth is what frugality buys, and it lands on others

All three of the oldest readers make 67's breadth something that spreads beyond the frugal one. For Han Fei the state is broad because the people are many: 民衆則國廣 (*mín zhòng zé guó guǎng* — "when the people are many, the state is broad"). For Wang Bi the world never runs short: 天下不匱，故能廣也 (*tiān xià bù kuì, gù néng guǎng yě* — "the world does not run short, and so it can be broad"). For Heshang Gong the people have enough to live on: 民日用廣矣 (*mín rì yòng guǎng yǐ* — "the people's daily means are broad"). The full passages are in `jian-儉.md`.

**"Broad" leaves open what is broad:** a household, a state, the people's means. *"Reach far"* answered that question wrongly twice. It put the breadth on the frugal one's own range, and it turned width into distance. Distance is 遠's word: 遠曰反 (*yuǎn yuē fǎn* — "going far means returning," 25), 深矣，遠矣 (*shēn yǐ, yuǎn yǐ* — "deep, and far," 65), 不遠徙 (*bù yuǎn xǐ* — "not move far away," 80).

The new line also keeps chapter 67's parallel audible: *"Tenderness, and so you can be brave. / Frugality, and so you can be broad."*

### What is set aside

- **"Boundless."** Chosen for chapter 41 on 2026-09-07 because it explains the paradox: there is no edge to arrive at, so the broad seems not enough. The explanation costs the picture. 廣 is a roofed hall, and Wang Bi's 不可滿 (*bù kě mǎn* — "cannot be filled") is about how much the hall holds, not about a missing edge. *Boundless* is also the infinite that `ji-極.md` keeps out of the book as *limitless* and *infinite*, and it fails the hymn test (*boundless love*). At chapter 67 it fails outright, since frugality makes no one boundless.
- **"Reach far," "reach."** Distance, not width. *Far* is 遠's in three chapters, and *the far end* is 極's. *Reach* stands for other characters in ten lines.
- **"Vast."** 天網恢恢 (*tiān wǎng huī huī* — "nature's net is vast") at chapter 73. Two characters would share it.
- **"Wide."** 疏 (*shū* — loose, wide-meshed) holds it in the same line of chapter 73, *"Its mesh is wide."*
- **"Expansive."** A mood or a manner, and it moves: an expansive thing is spreading. 廣 is a span that stands.
- **"Ample."** The runner-up in September. It means more than enough, which makes chapter 41 a flat contradiction (*"ample… not enough"*), and *"you can be ample"* does not stand at 67.

### Working register

| Where 廣 stands | English | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 廣德若不足 (41) | *Broad integrity seems not enough.* | an adjective on 德 (*dé* — integrity) |
| 儉故能廣 (67) | *Frugality, and so you can be broad.* | a predicate after 能 (*néng* — can), as 勇 (*yǒng* — brave) is in the line before |
| 捨儉且廣 (67) | *to abandon frugality and grab at breadth* | the object of 且 (*qiě* — to grab, in Wang Bi's reading), a noun, as 勇 is *courage* in the same line |

The split between *broad* and *breadth* is grammatical, so it stays in `render:` and nothing goes in `flexions:`.

**曠 (*kuàng* — wide open) is not covered here.** Chapter 15's 曠兮其若谷 (*kuàng xī qí ruò gǔ* — "open, like a valley") is built on 廣, but 廣 is only its sound part there, beside 日 (*rì* — the sun), and `gu-谷.md` reads it as *open*. The part's role in a graph is not a reading of the line.

### Left open

- **Whether chapter 41's paradox survives the plainer word.** The September call held that *broad → not enough* reads as no paradox, and that is a fair worry. The paradox now rests on a picture the reader has to supply: a hall too broad to fill looks empty. Wang Bi supplies it, and the verse does not. One English for one character is paid for at chapter 41.
- **Whose breadth, at chapter 67.** *"You can be broad"* still has the frugal one as its subject, as the Chinese does. The English no longer contradicts the commentators, but it does not say what they all saw, that the breadth spreads outward to the household, the state and the people.
- **曠 has no entry.** Its English, *open*, holds in chapter 15 by the verse and by `gu-谷.md` alone.
