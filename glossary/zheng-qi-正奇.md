## 正 & 奇 (Zhèng, Qí) — Straight & Crooked

*Say "jung," falling, and "chee," rising. 正 stands seven times in six chapters, 奇 four times in three. Chapter 57 sets them side by side as a method, and chapter 58 turns one into the other.*

**Locked: 正 → "straight," "straighten" as a verb · 奇 → "crooked."** *(Shalom's call on the system, 2026-11-19; locked the same day.)* Three flexions where a compound has hardened: *on the mark* (正言, 78), *strange* (奇物, 57), *steps out of line* (為奇者, 74). *(Not "upright," a moral category in English. Not "norm," an abstraction no one could point at. Not "surprise," which names 奇 only in battle. Not "correct," which makes 正 a verdict.)* **Covered:** 直 (*zhí*) → *true*, 屈 (*qū*) → *bent*, so that no two of the four share a word.

### The graph: a foot that reaches its line

> 正，是也。从止，一以止。
> *"正: right. From 止 (zhǐ — a foot), with 一 (yī — a line) where it stops."* — Shuowen (說文, *Shuōwén* — Xu Shen's dictionary, c. 100 CE)

> 奇，異也。一曰不耦。从大从可。
> *"奇: different. One says: unpaired. From 大 (dà — a person) and 可 (kě — can)."* — Shuowen

**正 is a foot arriving where it was going**, the mark under the step; 征 (*zhēng* — to march on a campaign) is the same word with a road beside it. *Straight* is the line the foot keeps to reach it. **奇 is what does not fit the pair**: different, and *odd* as an odd number is odd, the one left over. In a pair of lines, 奇 is the one that bends away.

**Where the picture stops.** The dictionary's 是也 (*"right"*) is the derived sense, and the book uses it, at chapter 78. Everywhere else it keeps the line.

### Chapter 57 sets the method, and chapter 58 undoes it

> *Govern the state with the straight,*
> *wage war with the crooked,*
> *and win over the world by not meddling.*
>
> 以正治國，
> 以奇用兵，
> 以無事取天下。
>
> — chapter 57

> *Who knows where it ends? It has no straight —*
> *the straight turns crooked, the good turns monstrous.*
>
> 孰知其極？其無正。
> 正復為奇，善復為妖。
>
> — chapter 58

**Wang Bi reads the second as the end of the first.** On chapter 57: 以正治國則奇正起也 (*"govern the state with the straight, and the crooked and the straight rise up"*); 立正欲以息邪而奇兵用 (*"you set up the straight to stop the warped, and crooked troops are put to use"*). On chapter 58: 以正治國則便復以奇用兵矣，故曰正復為奇 (*"govern the state with the straight, and you are back to waging war with the crooked; so it says, the straight turns crooked"*). **The English has to let a reader hear one chapter unmake the other**, and it can only do that if 正 and 奇 read the same in both. The pair is also the strategists' own: 正 and 奇 are regular and irregular troops, the frontal force and the flanking one. In chapter 57 奇 is a way of fighting, not a vice.

**And 58's chain is back.** 其無正。正復為奇 hands 正 from one clause into the next, *"It has no straight — / the straight turns crooked,"* the dash holding the topic named on both sides, as standing rule 3 allows.

### Straightening, and what does it

> *Not desiring, and so still, the world will straighten itself.*
>
> 不欲以靜，天下將自正。
>
> — chapter 37

> *I love stillness, and the people straighten themselves.*
>
> 我好靜，而民自正；
>
> — chapter 57

> *Clear stillness sets the world straight.*
>
> 清靜為天下正。
>
> — chapter 45

**In every line where 正 is a verb, stillness does it.** Chapter 57 opens with the straight imposed and closes with the people straightening themselves, 自正 (*zì zhèng* — "straighten of themselves"): the same word, applied from above and then arising. Heshang Gong reads 57's 正 as a person straight in body, 正身之人 (*"a person whose body is straight"*), and its 奇 as 詐 (*zhà* — deceit); the verse keeps 奇 the bend in the line, not the vice, because the chapter needs it as a method.

### 直 (zhí — true)

> 直，正見也。从乚从十从目。
> *"直: seeing straight. From 乚 (yǐn — a hidden line), 十 (shí — ten) and 目 (mù — an eye)."* — Shuowen

**直 is the eye sighting along a line**, the carpenter checking whether a board is *true*. The dictionary defines it by 正, and the book uses both, in the same chapter at 58, so they cannot share *straight*. *True* is 直's own picture: a board sighted true, wood that grows true.

> *Bend, and stay whole. Be warped, and grow true.*
>
> 曲則全，枉則直，
>
> — chapter 22

> *Great trueness seems bent.*
>
> 大直若屈，
>
> — chapter 45

Wang Bi on 45: 隨物而直，直不在一，故若屈也 (*"it is true by following things; its trueness is not in one line, so it seems bent"*). On 58's 直而不肆, *"true but does not overreach"*: 以直導物令去其僻，而不以直激沸於物也 (*"it guides things with trueness so they leave their warp, and does not dash against things with it"*).

### 屈 (qū — bent)

**屈 is the bend that 直 seems to be at chapter 45**, and it takes *bent*, which leaves *crooked* to 奇 alone. At chapter 5, 虛而不屈 (*xū ér bù qū* — "empty, and does not give out"), the same character is a bellows that does not collapse, and the verse's *"never depleted"* keeps that sense; a `covers:` character takes no flexion list, so the exception is recorded here.

### What is set aside

**"Upright," "uprightness."** A moral category in English, the Confucian coloring the razor strips (`notes/translation.md` → *Ch 78 · 正言若反*). The book's 正 is a line before it is a virtue.

**"Norm," "fixed norm."** An abstraction, and it broke chapter 58's chain: *no fixed norm — / The straight* handed the reader two words for one character.

**"Surprise."** Right for 奇 in battle only, and it hid the pair: no reader of *surprise* hears chapter 58's *crooked*.

**"Correct."** It makes 正 a verdict on someone, where the line is a shape.

**"Order" for 正.** 治 (*zhì* — to govern, order)'s word, and 亂 (*luàn* — disorder)'s opposite (`glossary/luan-亂.md`).

### What no rule can enforce

***Straight* is ruled 正's, and *crooked* 奇's, and neither can be forbidden to the other characters.** 直 and 屈 are covered here, and a `covers:` character carries no `forbidden:` list. The reader holds it: in chapter 58, *straight* is 正 and *true* is 直; in chapter 45, *bent* is 屈.

### Left open

**Whether *crooked* is fair to chapter 57's general.** 奇 is a military virtue there, the flanking force, and *wage war with the crooked* sounds like a charge. The edition takes the cost so that chapter 58 can be heard undoing 57. Heshang Gong's 詐 (*deceit*) shows the word could carry the verdict in Han ears too.

**Chapter 78 stays on its mark.** 正言 keeps *on the mark*, the aim reading recorded at the chapter, so 正 has a second English in one line. *Straight words* was the first draft, set aside because *straight talk* means bluntness.
