## 先 & 後 (Xiān, Hòu) — Ahead & Behind

*Say "shyen," level, and "ho," falling. 先 stands eight times across six chapters, 後 eleven times across eight. Each also tells time, before and after; but where they stand together they are a place in a moving line, and the book's argument is that the one behind ends up ahead.*

**Locked: 先 → "ahead" · 後 → "behind" where the pair is a place in a line (62, 66) · "first" and "last" where it is a rank (7, 67) · "before" and "after" where they tell time.** *(Locked 2026-11-19, Shalom's call, after the chapter pass.)* *(Not "front" for 先, which belongs to 前 (qián — front), covered here. Not "the rear," an army's word. Not "humility" for 後, which turns a place into a virtue.)* **Covered:** 前 → *front*. **The rank lines keep *first* and *last*** by Shalom's call, chapter 7 for its snap, with the echo of Matthew 20:16 recorded as a known cost.

### The graph: a foot out in front, and a foot that drags

> 先，前進也。从儿，从之。
> *"先: to advance forward. From 儿 (rén — a person) and 之 (zhī — a foot setting out)."* — Shuowen (說文, *Shuōwén* — Xu Shen's dictionary, c. 100 CE)

> 後，遲也。从彳、幺、夊者，後也。
> *"後: late, slow. From 彳 (chì — a step), 幺 (yāo — small) and 夊 (suī — a dragging foot): one who comes after."* — Shuowen

**先 is a foot set out ahead of a person. 後 is a short step and a dragging foot.** Both are motion before they are rank: someone going on in front, someone coming along behind. The Shuowen defines 先 through 前, *forward*, and 後 through 遲, *late*, so the pair is already about a line of people moving, and where in it you are.

### Four chapters, one move, and the commentators make it one argument

> *So the sage puts their body last, and their body comes first;*
>
> 是以聖人後其身而身先，
>
> — chapter 7

> *If you want to go ahead of the people, you must put your body behind them.*
>
> 欲先民，必以身後之。
>
> — chapter 66

> *The third is never pushing to be first in the world.*
>
> 三曰不敢為天下先。
>
> — chapter 67

> *to abandon being last and grab at being first,*
>
> 捨後且先，
>
> — chapter 67

**Chapters 7 and 66 are the same sentence**, the body put behind and the person going ahead. Until 2026-11-19 the English gave these four lines three pairs of words, *last / first*, *ahead / behind* and *the rear / the front*, inside chapters that also use *first* for 一 (*yī* — one), *"The first is tenderness."*

**Both commentators read the four as one argument.** Heshang Gong glosses chapters 7 and 66 with the same four characters, 先人而後己, *"put others ahead and yourself behind."* Wang Bi joins them through chapter 28's rooster and hen: 雄先之屬，雌後之屬也。知為天下之先也，必後也。是以聖人後其身而身先也, *"the rooster belongs with going ahead, the hen with staying behind. Whoever knows how to be the world's front must stay behind; so the sage puts their body behind and their body is ahead."* And on chapter 67's third treasure: 唯後外其身，為物所歸，然後乃能立成器, *"only by putting oneself behind and outside, so that things come home to one, can one then stand and become chief of the vessels."* That is chapter 7's second line, 外其身 (*wài qí shēn* — "puts their body outside"), carried into chapter 67.

**So the English now splits by use, and only by use** (Shalom's calls, 2026-11-19). Where the pair is a **rank**, a place in the world's regard, it reads *last / first*: chapter 7's *"puts their body last, and their body comes first,"* chapter 67's *"never pushing to be first in the world"* and *"to abandon being last and grab at being first."* Where it is a **place in a line**, with someone to be ahead of, it reads *ahead / behind*: chapter 66's *"if you want to go ahead of the people, you must put your body behind them,"* and chapter 62's procession, *"go ahead of."* Heshang Gong on chapter 7 shows the move is a result, not a goal: 天下敬之，先以為長, *"the world respects them, and puts them ahead as its elder."* The sage does not go ahead; they are carried there.

### Chapter 66: 先 and 前 in one chapter, and the people carry the sage

Chapter 66 holds both words for the front. 欲先民 (*yù xiān mín* — "to go ahead of the people") is motion; 處前而民不害 (*chǔ qián ér mín bù hài* — "stands in front, and the people are not harmed") is a place. Heshang Gong glosses the first with the second, 欲在民之前也, *"wanting to be in front of the people,"* and that is the gloss, not the text: the book chose two characters, and the English keeps two words.

> 歬，不行而進謂之歬。从止在舟上。
> *"前 (written 歬): to advance without walking is 前. A foot on a boat."* — Shuowen

**前 is going forward without walking.** The foot stands still; the boat moves. Chapter 66's sage stands in front because the people put them there, and the next line says so: 天下樂推而不厭, *"the world gladly pushes them forward and never tires of them."* We do not claim the scribes heard the boat in 處前. The chapter's own picture is a person carried forward by others, and the graph is that picture.

### Before and after: the same two characters telling time

Away from the argument, the pair tells time, and English uses its time words:

> *Something mixed and complete, born before sky and earth.*
>
> 有物混成，先天地生。
>
> — chapter 25

Also chapter 4's 象帝之先 (*xiàng dì zhī xiān* — "as if before 帝"), *older than any god*, which is 帝 (*dì* — the ancestor-lord)'s locked phrase (`glossary/di-帝.md`); chapter 30's 大軍之後 (*dà jūn zhī hòu* — "after a great army"), *after*; and the conjunctions 而後 (*ér hòu*) and 然後 (*rán hòu*), *then*, at chapters 38 and 65. Chapter 62's 先 is a verb, *to go ahead of*, read *xiàn* in Heshang Gong's sound note (先悉薦反): jade discs carried ahead of a team of four horses, the order of a procession.

### 前 (qián — front)

前 stands three times. **Chapter 2's 前後相隨** (*qián hòu xiāng suí* — "front and back follow each other") is the book's plainest pair of places, and it is why 後 reads *back* where it is the back of a thing, there and at chapter 14 (隨之不見其後, *"follow it, and you cannot see its back"*). **Chapter 66's 處前**, *stands in front*, is above. **Chapter 38's 前識** (*qián shí* — "knowing ahead") is time, not place, and reads *knowing in advance*, the commentators diverging on whether it is real but premature knowing or mere guessing (`chapters/038.md`). It lives in this entry because chapter 66 sets it beside 先 and Heshang Gong glosses one with the other: the English must keep them apart, and only an entry holding both can say how.

### What is set aside

**"First" and "last," where the pair is a place in a line.** At chapters 62 and 66 there is someone to be ahead of, and *first* would make a rank of a position. Where the pair *is* a rank (7, 67), *first* and *last* are kept, and the cost is recorded: they are the book's move in Matthew's words, *"So the last shall be first, and the first last"* (Matthew 20:16). Matthew's reversal is a judgment handed down at the end of time; Laozi's is a mechanism, the one who stays behind being carried forward by those they stayed behind for. *First* is also 一's ordinal in chapter 67 (*"The first is tenderness"*), and Heshang Gong's 主，先也 (*"主 means going first"*) has put it on 主 (*zhǔ* — host) at chapter 69, which is `WORKLIST.md` T1-23.

**"Front," "the front," for 先.** 前's word, at chapters 2 and 66. Chapter 66 needs both characters, and the gloss that merges them is Heshang Gong's, not the text's.

**"The rear."** An army's word, and a third English for 後 where *behind* serves. Chapter 67's *the rear / the front* also turned two motions into two nouns of a battle line.

**"Lead," "leader."** 長 (*zhǎng* — chief) is the book's word for the one at the head, at chapter 67's *chief of the vessels*, and 倡 (*chàng* — to lead off) is Heshang Gong's gloss for the third treasure. 先 is a position in the line, not an office.

**"Precede," "precedence."** Latin, and courtly: the order of rank at a procession. Chapter 62 is a procession, and still the plain *go ahead of* says it.

**"Superior," "inferior."** A verdict on worth where the Chinese gives a place in a line (`process/principles/no-verdict-the-chinese-lacks.md`).

**"Humility," "humble," for 後.** The virtue word for a position. Heshang Gong's 執謙退 (*zhí qiān tuì* — "holding to modest withdrawal") is a gloss for chapter 67, and right as a gloss; in the verse it would make the sage's staying behind a moral quality to be admired, where the book makes it a place to stand, and one that works.

**Behind, and the female.** Wang Bi sets the hen with 後 at chapter 28. The rule of this edition holds that no English may be quoted to keep her under (`CLAUDE.md`, standing rule 1). *Behind* passes because the book never puts anyone there: in every line, the one behind has chosen it, and in chapters 7, 66 and 67 the one who chooses it ends up ahead.

### What no rule can enforce

***First* and *last* are flexions at chapters 7 and 67, and so can never be forbidden**; *first* is also 一's ordinal in chapter 67. ***Front*** is 前's, which shares chapters 2 and 66, so it cannot be forbidden for 先 either. ***The rear*** left the verse with chapter 67 and is held by this entry.

***Last* also belongs to other characters' idioms** (*last out the morning*, 終 (*zhōng* — to the end) at chapter 23), in chapters 先 and 後 do not reach.

### Left open

**Chapter 7 is decided: it keeps *last / first*** (Shalom's call, 2026-11-19). *"The sage puts their body last, and their body comes first"* stays for its snap, and the echo of Matthew 20:16 is a known cost, recorded here so no later pass removes it as an oversight. **Chapters 66 and 67 were read again in its light** (2026-11-19). Chapter 67 follows chapter 7: its third treasure keeps *first in the world*, and 捨後且先 reads *being last / being first*. Chapter 66 keeps *ahead / behind*, because there 先 takes *the people* as its object and is answered by *put your body behind them*. So the entry's English is now split by use: *last / first* where the pair is a rank (7, 67), *ahead / behind* where it is a place in a line or a procession (62, 66).

***Comes first* was weighed against *ends up ahead*, and kept.** *Ends up ahead* would have given the move one pair of words in all four lines and dropped the Gospel cadence; *comes first* keeps the line readers remember. Shalom's call.

**Chapter 69's 主 / 客 still reads *move first / move second***, Heshang Gong's gloss rendered in place of the text. When T1-23 is settled, it must not take *ahead* or *behind*, which would make 主 and 客 (*kè* — guest) look like 先 and 後.
