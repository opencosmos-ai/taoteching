## 事 (Shì) — Affairs, and the Hand That Holds Them

*Say "shr," fourth tone, falling hard. The second hand in the book — and the one nobody notices, because English has scattered it across eight different words.*

**Locked: 事 → "affairs" or "undertakings" as a noun; "serve" or "attend to" as a verb.** *(Never "techniques," never "processes." Reasoning below.)*

Nineteen lines in fourteen chapters, and this edition had been rendering them as *work*, *affairs*, *actions*, *techniques*, *processes*, *non-interference*, *serve*, and *tend*. Eight Englishes for one character — and two of them, *techniques* and *processes*, are the mechanistic overlay this edition exists to strip. Nothing was holding 事 together, so its best line has been invisible: **事 is a hand, and it is standing beside another hand.**

### The graph: a hand holding the badge of office

In the oracle bones, four characters that English keeps carefully apart were **one graph**: a hand (又, *yòu*) gripping an implement — a standard with a banner at the top, or a recording tool.

| Descendant | Became |
|---|---|
| **史** (*shǐ*) | the scribe, and then *history* — the hand that keeps the record |
| **吏** (*lì*) | the official — the hand that holds office |
| **使** (*shǐ*) | to send, to cause — the hand that dispatches |
| **事** (*shì*) | **the matter to be dealt with** — what the hand is holding |

So 事 is not "stuff that happens." It is **the thing you have been appointed to do**, the business in your grip. Its oldest sense is closer to *to serve in office* than to *an event*, which is why the verb sense in this book — 事天 (*shì tiān*, 59, serving nature) and 入事人 (*rù shì rén*, 61, entering to serve people) — is not a separate word but the graph read straight.

### The second hand

Here is what the scattering hid. The book's other great verb is **為** (*wéi*), whose oracle-bone graph is a human hand laid on the head of an **elephant** — handling something that has its own momentum. Put the two together and Laozi has **two hands**, and they are not doing the same thing:

- **為** — a hand on a living thing that was already going somewhere. To take that hand away is **無為**, *non-doing*.
- **事** — a hand closed around the implement of office. To open it is **無事**.

Chapter 63 sets them side by side on purpose, in a triple that has already decided one lock and now explains a second:

> 為無為，事無事，味無味。
> *do the not-doing · serve the not-serving · taste the flavorless*

Three ordinary verbs, each negated and then taken as its own object. That structure is why 無為 had to be **non-doing** rather than "non-action" — only a word that is both verb and noun survives all three members (`glossary/wuwei-無為.md`). And it is why 事 belongs in the same register: **not an abstraction, a gesture.** Something a person does with their hands and can stop doing.

### What the noun covers

事 is the matter itself, at every scale, and the range is worth seeing whole:

- **the business of governing** — 處無為之事 (2), *dwelling in the work of non-doing*
- **daily competence** — 事善能 (8), water is *skillful in its affairs*
- **the ruler's completed project** — 功成事遂 (17), *the work done, the undertaking finished*
- **what rebounds on you** — 其事好還 (30), of military force: *such undertakings tend to come back*
- **a ceremonial occasion** — 吉事 / 凶事 (31), auspicious and inauspicious **rites**, which is what sets the seating at a general's table
- **proliferating busyness** — 濟其事 (52), *multiplying your affairs*, and your life is beyond saving
- **the scale argument** — 天下難事 / 天下大事 (63), the world's *difficult* and *great* undertakings, both beginning small

That range is why no single English word can carry all nineteen lines, and the entry does not pretend otherwise. What it insists on is the **register**: every one of these is something undertaken, held, and capable of being put down.

### What is set aside

**"Techniques"** (30). 其事好還 became *"Such techniques inevitably cycle back."* A technique is a method you could refine; 事 is the undertaking itself. The line is not about doing warfare better — it is about the fact that taking up this particular implement puts something in motion that returns to you.

**"Processes"** (31). 吉事尚左，凶事尚右 became *"Life-generating processes amplify creative energy. Life-negating processes invoke destruction."* Two separate faults. **Process** is the mechanistic overlay — it turns a room full of people observing a protocol into physics. And 尚 (*shàng*) simply means *to honor, to place above*; nothing in the line says *amplify* or *invoke*. These are **rites**, and the chapter says so four lines later when it puts the generals in the places that funeral observance assigns them.

**"Actions"** (23) and **"work"** used loosely. Both are near enough to pass unnoticed and thin enough to erase the hand. 從事於道者 (23) is *one who takes up their undertakings within the Tao* — someone with business in their grip, conducting it in a particular way.

**"Non-interference"** for 無事 (48, 57). An interpretation, and a Latinate one, standing in a book that reaches for the plain and physical — and the only member of Chapter 57's quartet that was not a plain saying. Retired 2026-08-12 in favour of *meddle*; see below.

**"Tend"** for 無事, briefly tried and retired the same day. *Tend* is what you do to a garden to make it grow, so *not tending* implies neglect that kills — the reverse of the claim, which is that things flourish once the hand comes off (民自富, *the people grow rich of themselves*). A word that misdescribes the teaching is a worse fault than a word that is merely stiff.

### Working register

| The grammar | Where | Render |
|---|---|---|
| the matter undertaken | 8, 17, 30, 52, 63 | **affairs**, **undertakings** |
| a ceremonial occasion | 31 | **rites**, **occasions** |
| to serve, hold office under | 59, 61 | **serve** |
| to take up, conduct | 23, 64 | **attend to**, **take up** |
| 無事, naming the policy | 48, 57 | **meddle** (negated) |
| 事無事, the neutral verb | 63 | **serve** |

### 無事 — one character, two jobs

無事 renders **"meddle"** at Chapters 48 and 57, and **"serve"** at Chapter 63. That is not drift. The two places are doing different grammatical work, and the difference is visible in the Chinese.

**At 48 and 57, 無事 stands alone** as the name of a policy. Nothing in the line rhymes with it, so the English is free to name the failure it prevents — and *meddling* is exactly what the commentators describe. Wang Bi on 及其有事: 自已造也, *"it is manufactured by oneself."* Heshang Gong: 好有事，則政教煩，民不安 — *"fond of having undertakings, and governance and instruction turn troublesome, and the people are not at peace."* Officiousness, not violence, which is what makes it such a good diagnosis of bad rule.

> 48 · 取天下常以**無事** — *the world always comes to you by not meddling with it*
> 57 · 我**無事**，而民自富 — *I do not meddle, and the people enrich themselves*

That last line sits third in a quartet — 無為 / 好靜 / 無事 / 無欲 — and the plain verb keeps all four as plain sayings, which is what the Chinese has.

**At 63, 事 appears twice inside one line**, and the English has to hold in both positions at once:

> 為無為，**事無事**，味無味 — *do the not-doing, **serve the not-serving**, taste the flavorless*

Each beat is one character twice with 無 (*wú* — there is no) between. So the verb must be **neutral**: *do* is neutral, *taste* is neutral, and the paradox lives in the negation. *Meddle* fails twice over — you meddle *in* something, so the bare transitive is not English, and a verb that is already a rebuke flattens the line into a plain prohibition.

**"Serve" is 事's oldest verb sense**, the one the graph draws: a hand holding the badge of office is a hand *in service*. It is also already this edition's word for 事 at **Ch 59** (治人**事**天 → *governing people and serving nature*) and **Ch 61** (入**事**人 → *enter and serve people*). The middle beat therefore ties to the two chapters where 事 is unambiguously *serve*, and the triple keeps its even rhythm — one syllable against *do* and *taste*.

**The principle, stated so it holds elsewhere: the lock on 事 is on its register, not on a single English token.** A matter undertaken, held, and capable of being put down — never a *technique*, never a *process*. That is the same license 為 already carries as *do / handle / serve as*.

*(Note the loss the triple already accepts: 味無味 reads "taste the flavorless," using two English words for one character, because "flavorless" buys the chime with Ch 35's 淡乎其無味, where the Tao itself is bland and flavorless. The internal echo is worth having and is not sacred.)*
