## 儉 (Jiǎn) — Frugality

*Say "jyen," dipping. Three times in eighty-one chapters, all of them in chapter 67, where it is the second of the book's three treasures. Too small for any count to find, and half of the chapter's argument.*

**Locked: 儉 → "frugality," in all three lines.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-11-15.)* *(Not "economy," which now names a nation's markets. Not "thrift," which saves and puts by, the granary's work and 嗇's (sè — conserving). Not "austerity," which spends nothing and so can broaden nothing. Not "simplicity," which is forbidden for 樸 (pǔ — the uncarved) and names a style of life, not a way of spending. Not "moderation" or "restraint," which hold back an impulse where 儉 holds back an outlay. Not "conservation," whose verb is 嗇's at chapter 59.)*

### The graph: a person, and a cord wound round

> 儉，約也。从人僉聲。
> *"儉 is 約 (yuē — to bind). From 人 (rén — person), with 僉 (qiān) for sound."* — the *Shuowen Jiezi* (the Han dictionary)

> 約，纏束也。从糸勺聲。
> *"約 is to wind round and tie. From 糸 (mì — silk thread), with 勺 (sháo — a ladle) for sound."* — the *Shuowen Jiezi*

**The Shuowen defines frugality with a picture of cord wound round a bundle.** 儉 is a person, and 約, the word Xu Shen explains it by, is thread tied tight. What is frugal is held in, the way a bundle is held together: not thrown away, and not let spill.

**Where the picture stops.** The graph itself pictures only the person. 僉 (*qiān* — "all, together") is its sound, on the Shuowen's account, and reading *gathering everything in* out of it would be making a meaning from a sound part. The cord is in the definition, not in the character.

### Three lines, and in each one frugality buys breadth

> *I have three treasures. I hold them and keep them safe.*
> *The first is tenderness.*
> *The second is frugality.*
> *The third is never pushing to be first in the world.*
>
> 我有三寶，持而保之：
> 一曰慈，
> 二曰儉，
> 三曰不敢為天下先。
>
> — chapter 67

> *Tenderness, and so you can be brave.*
> *Frugality, and so you can be broad.*
> *Never pushing to be first in the world, and so you can become chief of the completed vessels.*
>
> 慈故能勇；
> 儉故能廣；
> 不敢為天下先，故能成器長。
>
> — chapter 67

> *Now, to abandon tenderness and grab at courage,*
> *to abandon frugality and grab at breadth,*
> *to abandon being last and grab at being first,*
> *that is death.*
>
> 今捨慈且勇，
> 捨儉且廣，
> 捨後且先，
> 死矣！
>
> — chapter 67

**In each line 儉 is never a lack for its own sake: it is what something wide is bought with.** The second line names the purchase, 儉故能廣 (*jiǎn gù néng guǎng* — "frugal, and so able to be broad"). The third names the failure: keeping the breadth and dropping its price. Wang Bi reads 且 (*qiě* — and, about to) as 取 (*qǔ* — to grab), 且猶取也, *"且 is like 取,"* so 捨儉且廣 is reaching straight for the wide thing without the frugality that would have paid for it.

### The middle treasure: a small thing that buys a wide one

**The three treasures share one shape, and 儉 is what shows it is a shape.** Each is a small thing that yields a large one: tenderness yields courage, frugality yields breadth, staying behind yields leadership. Each failure is the same failure: grabbing the large thing directly. 慈 (*cí* — tenderness) and 勇 (*yǒng* — courage) could be read as a paradox about the heart, and 先 (*xiān* — first) and 後 (*hòu* — behind) as a paradox about rank. **With 儉 and 廣 (*guǎng* — broad) between them, it is plainly arithmetic.** Spend little, and what you have goes far. The middle term makes the other two legible as one claim.

That is why an entry for 慈 alone left the chapter half-argued. Three occurrences put 儉 below every frequency threshold, the same way three occurrences hid 全 (*quán* — whole) until it turned out to be chapter 22's whole argument (`quan-全.md`).

### Han Fei: frugality is winter, and the breadth is the spring

Han Fei, who died in 233 BCE, is the oldest reader of this line, and his reading is seasonal before it is economic:

> 周公曰：「冬日之閉凍也不固，則春夏之長草木也不茂。」天地不能常侈常費，而況於人乎？……是以智士儉用其財則家富，聖人愛寶其神則精盛，人君重戰其卒則民衆，民衆則國廣。是以舉之曰：「儉，故能廣。」
> *"The Duke of Zhou said: 'If winter's closing-in and freezing is not firm, the growing of grass and trees in spring and summer will not be lush.' Sky and earth cannot always be lavish and always spending; how much less can people? … So when a person of good sense is frugal in using their wealth, the household grows rich; when the sage cherishes and treasures their spirit, their vital essence flourishes; when a ruler is grave about sending soldiers to war, the people are many, and when the people are many, the state is broad. Hence it is put: 'Frugal, and so able to be broad.'"* — the *Han Feizi*, 解老 (*jiě lǎo* — "Explaining Laozi")

**Frugality here is the closed ground in winter that makes the spring lush.** It passes the pointing test as few virtues do: you can point at a frozen field. It also says what 儉 spends sparingly, which is anything that can be spent: wealth, the spirit, 神 (*shén* — spirit), with its vital essence, 精 (*jīng* — vital essence), and soldiers. And Han Fei's 廣 is literal breadth: the state is broad because the people are many.

### Two commentators, two purses

> 節儉愛費，天下不匱，故能廣也。
> *"Sparing and frugal, careful of expenditure, so the world does not run short: that is how it can be broad."* — Wang Bi, on 儉故能廣

> 賦儉若取之於己也。
> *"Taxing frugally, as though taking it from oneself."* — Heshang Gong, on 二曰儉

> 天子身能節儉，故民日用廣矣。
> *"When the son of heaven can be frugal in their own person, the people's daily means are broad."* — Heshang Gong, on 儉故能廣

**Wang Bi keeps the household's purse, and Heshang Gong the ruler's,** and his frugality is a restraint on what is *taken*: tax levied as if it came out of the ruler's own pocket. Neither is ascetic. Heshang Gong also carries the word past money: two of his chapter titles are built on it, 儉武 (*jiǎn wǔ* — "frugal with arms") for chapter 30 and 儉欲 (*jiǎn yù* — "frugal with desire") for chapter 46. That is Han Fei's list again: wealth, soldiers, and what the spirit wants.

**He also names 儉's opposite, and the book has already rendered it.** On 捨儉且廣 he writes 舍其儉約，但為奢泰, *"they let go of frugal restraint and practise only extravagance and grandeur."* 奢泰 (*shē tài* — extravagance and grandeur) are the two things the sage lets go of in chapter 29:

> *Therefore the sage lets go of excess, lets go of extravagance, lets go of grandeur.*
>
> 是以聖人去甚，去奢，去泰。
>
> — chapter 29

So the English already holds the word frugality is set against, and the two chapters answer each other: the sage of chapter 29 lets go of what the grabbers of chapter 67 grab.

### 嗇 (sè — conserving) stores the harvest, and keeps its own English

> *When governing people and serving nature, nothing compares to conserving.*
> *Only conserving is called accepting early,*
>
> 治人事天，莫若嗇。
> 夫唯嗇，是謂早服；
>
> — chapter 59

**嗇 is 儉's nearest kin in the book, and a different picture.**

> 嗇，愛濇也。从來从㐭。來者，㐭而藏之。故田夫謂之嗇夫。
> *"嗇 is to cherish and hold back. From 來 (lái — wheat) and 㐭 (lǐn — granary): the wheat comes in, and the granary stores it. That is why a field-hand is called a 嗇夫 (sè fū — 'the one who stores')."* — the *Shuowen Jiezi*

Wang Bi opens chapter 59 the same way: 嗇，農夫, *"嗇 is the farmer."* Han Fei defines it by the arithmetic he uses for 儉: 多費之謂侈……少費之謂嗇, *"spending much is called 侈 (chǐ — lavishness) … spending little is called 嗇."* Heshang Gong gives it the same opposite, 治國者當愛民則不為奢泰, *"one who governs a state should cherish the people, and so not practise extravagance and grandeur."*

**So the two words share an opposite and keep separate pictures:** 嗇 is the harvest put away, 儉 the bundle tied tight. They also do different work. 嗇 is the way to govern, in a chain that runs to deep roots. 儉 is a treasure, and it is spent to buy breadth. *Conserving* is the granary's English, and it stands in chapter 59's verse. *Frugality* is 67's. One English for both would join two lines the book never puts side by side.

The family has a third member, and it is a different kind of word. 知足 (*zhī zú* — knowing you have enough) is a *knowing*: it knows where the line is. 嗇 stores, and 儉 spends little. `zhizu-知足.md` sets aside *content with little* for exactly this reason: it turns knowing enough into frugality.

### What is set aside

- **"Economy."** Once exact: the Greek word is the management of a household, which is Wang Bi's reading. In English now it names a nation's markets, and *"the second is economy"* reads as fiscal policy.
- **"Thrift."** The root is good (*thrift* is what makes you *thrive*), but the word saves and puts by: the savings bank, the thrift society, the self-improvement sermon. Putting by is the granary's work, which is 嗇's. 儉 in chapter 67 is not a reserve. It is a way of spending that makes what is spent go far.
- **"Austerity."** Self-denial for its own sake, the hair shirt and the budget cut. It fails the hardest line outright: austerity spends nothing, so it can broaden nothing, and 儉故能廣 says frugality is how you become broad. All three commentators read 儉 as economy, never as a discipline of the body.
- **"Simplicity."** Forbidden for 樸 (*pǔ* — the uncarved), whose plainness the book argues in its own chapters. And simplicity is a style of life, where 儉 is a way of spending.
- **"Moderation."** The mean between too much and too little, a Greek doctrine. 儉 is not a midpoint: it leans toward less, and the chapter sets it against grabbing, not against having too little.
- **"Restraint."** The nearest English to the Shuowen's cord: to restrain is to hold back with a tie. In English, though, restraint holds back an *impulse*, which makes it a moral self-control and a sermon word, and it drops the purse all three commentators find. 儉 holds back an *outlay*.
- **"Conservation," "conserving."** The source table's alternative gloss. *Conserving* is 嗇's in chapter 59, and *conservation* now names nature reserves.
- **"Sparing."** The commentators' own sense (節, *jié* — to be sparing, to measure out), but it is an adjective or a verb. The chapter counts its treasures as nouns, and *"the second is sparing"* does not stand.

**Why "frugality" holds.** It passes the hardest line, and in its own idiom: frugality is what makes a little go a long way, which is 儉故能廣 said in English. Its root is a harvest word as well. Latin *frugalis* comes from *frux*, "fruit, produce," and the frugal are those who make the crop last. That root keeps it a word about goods, not about the soul.

### Working register

| Where 儉 stands | English | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 二曰儉 | *The second is frugality.* | the name of a treasure, counted as a noun |
| 儉故能廣 | *Frugality, and so you can be broad.* | the condition, standing as topic, as 慈 does in the line before it |
| 捨儉且廣 | *to abandon frugality and grab at breadth* | the object of 捨 (*shě* — to let go of) |

One English, a noun every time. The three treasures stand in parallel three times over, and the English repeats as the Chinese does, so nothing goes in `flexions:`. **It is never *be frugal*:** an imperative would turn a treasure the speaker holds into advice to the reader, and the chapter never advises.

### Left open

- **Whose breadth.** All three of the oldest readings put the breadth on *other people*: Han Fei's household grows rich and his state broad with people, Wang Bi's world never runs short, and Heshang Gong's people have broad daily means. *"And so you can be broad"* keeps the frugal one as subject, as 能 (*néng* — can) does in the Chinese. It no longer turns the breadth into the frugal one's own range, but it does not say what every commentator saw: frugality's breadth goes outward, and in Heshang Gong it is the powerful sparing what they take (`guang-廣.md`).
- **The oldest graphs, and the silks.** The oracle-bone and bronze record for 儉 has not been checked for this entry. Chinese Wikisource's collated text of the Mawangdui silks reads 二曰檢 (*èr yuē jiǎn*), writing 儉's sound part with 木 (*mù* — wood) in place of 人, and 茲 (*zī*) for 慈. Neither reading is confirmed against a printed edition or recorded in `sources/variants.yaml`, so nothing here rests on them (`WORKLIST.md` T2-80).
- **嗇 has no entry.** Its English, *conserving*, holds in chapter 59 by the verse alone. A `covers:` line here would lock it beside 儉, and that is a ruling for Shalom, not for this entry (`glossary/TRIAGE.md`).
