## 沖 (Chōng) — Empty

*Say "chong," level. Three appearances, one in each of three chapters. One written character standing for two words: an empty vessel in chapters 4 and 45, and in chapter 42 a word the older copies write 中 (zhōng — middle).*

**Locked: 沖 → "empty" at chapters 4 and 45, and "within" at chapter 42.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-11-15.)* *(Not "blending," which no witness and no commentator supports, and which 和 (hé — harmony) may print in these chapters, see the last section but one. Not "surging," the graph's own sense, which neither of its empty lines can bear. Not "emptiness" or "the void," nouns that turn a quality of the Tao into a doctrine of it.)*

### The graph: water, and a sound

沖 is 氵 (water) beside 中 (zhōng — middle). The *Shuowen* defines it as moving water, with no emptiness in it:

> 沖，涌搖也。从水、中。讀若動。
> *"沖: surging and shaking. From 水 (shuǐ — water) and 中 (zhōng — middle). Read like 動 (dòng — to move)."* — the *Shuowen Jiezi* (the Han dictionary)

**中 is there for its sound.** The meaning, surging and shaking, has nothing of the middle in it. The text we vendor writes 从水、中 without 聲 (shēng — "for sound"), but the same dictionary says it outright of 中's other compounds: 忠 (zhōng — loyal) is 从心中聲 (*cóng xīn, zhōng shēng* — "from heart, with 中 for sound"), and the empty vessel is the same:

> 盅，器虛也。从皿中聲。《老子》曰：「道盅而用之。」
> *"盅 (zhōng): a vessel's emptiness. From 皿 (mǐn — a vessel), with 中 for sound. The Laozi says: 'the Tao is 盅, and using it…'"* — the *Shuowen Jiezi*

**So Xu Shen's own copy of the Laozi wrote chapter 4's word with the empty-vessel character.** 盅 and 沖 share their sound part, and a scribe writing by sound could set down either. That is how the surging-water graph came to carry an empty vessel.

**And the vessel answers a vessel.** 盈 (yíng — full), the word set against 沖 in both of its empty lines, is 滿器也 (*mǎn qì yě* — "a full vessel"), from the same 皿 (mǐn — a vessel). Read as 盅, each line sets a full vessel against an empty one.

### Three lines, and the copies split them two and one

> *The Tao is empty, and using it, it never fills.*
>
> 道沖，而用之或不盈。
>
> — chapter 4

> *Great fullness seems empty, yet when used, it never runs dry.*
>
> 大盈若沖，其用不窮。
>
> — chapter 45

> *The countless things carry yin on their backs and embrace yang with their arms, and the breath within makes harmony.*
>
> 萬物負陰而抱陽，沖氣以為和。
>
> — chapter 42

**In chapters 4 and 45, 沖 stands against 盈 (yíng — full), and in chapter 42 it stands beside 氣 (qì — breath).** The received text spells all three alike. The older copies do not:

| | Ch 4 | Ch 45 | Ch 42 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guodian slips, c. 300 BCE | not among the slips | transcriptions differ, 盅 or 中 | not among the slips |
| Mawangdui silk A, c. 200 BCE | lost to damage | 沖 | 中 (zhōng — middle) |
| Mawangdui silk B, c. 175 BCE | 沖 | 沖 | lost to damage |
| Beida slips, Western Han | not yet checked | not yet checked | 中, on a secondhand transcription |
| Fu Yi, Tang | 盅 (zhōng — empty vessel) | 盅 | 衝 (chōng — to rush) |
| the *Shuowen*'s quotation | 盅 | | |
| Wang Bi, received | 沖 | 沖 | 沖 |

**Every witness that tells the places apart sets chapter 42 against chapters 4 and 45.** Silk A writes 沖 in full at chapter 45 and a bare 中 at chapter 42: one scribe, two spellings. Fu Yi writes 盅 at chapters 4 and 45 and 衝 at chapter 42. Only the received text levels them, and so one character came to mean both *empty* and something like *surging* or *blending*. They were never one word.

### Chapter 42: the breath within

**Silk A's 中 is a word here, not a short spelling of 沖.** A sound part written bare can stand for the whole graph, and the silks do this often. Three things say this one does not:

- Silk A writes the full 沖 at chapter 45, so its scribe had the longer graph and did not use it here.
- Beida, a separate copy, writes 中 at the same place.
- Heshang Gong, a Han reader, takes the received 沖 as 中 at chapters 4 and 42:

> 冲中也。道匿名藏譽，其用在中。
> *"沖 means 中 (zhōng — middle). The Tao hides its name and conceals its fame; its use lies in the middle."* — Heshang Gong, on chapter 4

> 萬物中皆有元氣，得以和柔，若胸中有藏，骨中有髓，草木中有空虛，與氣通，故得久生也。
> *"Within each of the countless things there is the original breath, and by it they are harmonious and soft: as the chest has its organs, and the bones their marrow, and grasses and trees their hollows, open to the breath, and so they live long."* — Heshang Gong, on chapter 42

**中 is *within*, as it is in chapter 21**, where 其中有精 (*qí zhōng yǒu jīng*) reads *"within it there is vital essence."* The line's picture is a body. Yin is carried on the back, yang is held in the arms, and the breath is within, in the chest. You can point at all three.

**Within stands bare, with no "them,"** because the Chinese does not say the middle of what. It may be the breath between yin and yang, which makes the third of the chapter's 二生三 (*èr shēng sān* — "the two births the three"). Or it may be Heshang Gong's breath inside each thing. *The breath within* holds both.

### What the commentators did with it

**Wang Bi reads chapters 4 and 45 against fullness**, which is the empty-vessel reading:

> 沖而用之，用乃不能窮。滿以造實，實來則溢。
> *"Use it as 沖, and the use cannot be exhausted. Filling it makes it solid, and when solidity comes, it overflows."* — Wang Bi, on chapter 4

> 大盈充足，隨物而與，無所愛矜，故若沖也。
> *"Great fullness is ample: it gives as things need, and holds nothing back for itself, and so it seems 沖."* — Wang Bi, on chapter 45

At chapter 42 he says only 雖有萬形，沖氣一焉 (*suī yǒu wàn xíng, chōng qì yī yān* — "though there are countless forms, the 沖 breath is one"), which does not settle the word.

**Heshang Gong reads 中 at chapters 4 and 42**, and at chapter 45 reads 沖 as humility: 如冲者，貴不敢驕也，富不敢奢也 (*rú chōng zhě, guì bù gǎn jiāo yě, fù bù gǎn shē yě* — "seeming 沖: noble, yet not daring to be proud; rich, yet not daring to be lavish"). At chapter 4 the English chooses Wang Bi's empty vessel over his middle. The *Shuowen*'s quotation, Fu Yi's 盅, and the line's own 不盈 (bù yíng — never fills) decide it.

### What is set aside

**"Blending."** No witness writes a word for it, and no commentator reads it. It makes the countless things into a cook mixing the breath, which is something done on purpose. In the Chinese, the breath is what does the making. *Blend* is also what 混 (hùn — to merge) is kept from in `glossary/hun-混.md`, for the same reason.

**"Surging"** is the graph's own meaning, and Fu Yi's 衝 at chapter 42 is real evidence for a rushing breath. It is set aside there because the two oldest copies write 中, and set aside at chapters 4 and 45 because a surge cannot stand against fullness the way those lines need.

**"Emptiness" and "the void."** Both are nouns, and 沖 is a quality: the Tao *is* 沖, great fullness *seems* 沖. The noun turns a description into a doctrine and brings in the Buddhist void, which `glossary/xu-虛.md` declines for the same reason.

**"Hollow"** is a fair picture of 盅, and Heshang Gong's plant stems are hollow. But *hollow* says a shape and *empty* says a lack of contents, and 盅 is defined by the contents: 器虛 (*qì xū* — a vessel's emptiness).

**"The middle" or "the center" at chapter 42.** Either is a fair rendering of 中, but set beside 和 (hé — harmony) it makes the 中和 (*zhōng hé* — centered harmony) of the *Zhongyong* (the Confucian "Doctrine of the Mean"). That is a doctrine this line does not state. *Within* says where the breath is without borrowing that frame.

### Working register

| Line | English | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 道沖 (4) | *The Tao is empty* | read as 盅 (zhōng — an empty vessel), against 不盈 (bù yíng — never fills) |
| 大盈若沖 (45) | *Great fullness seems empty* | read as 盅, the opposite in the 大X若Y (*dà X ruò Y* — "great X seems Y") frame |
| 沖氣以為和 (42) | *and the breath within makes harmony* | follows silk A's 中 (zhōng — middle); recorded as a flexion |

**Empty is also 虛's (xū) English**, in five chapters. Neither chapter 4 nor chapter 45 has 虛 in it, so the two never meet in a line, and the dictionary links them: it defines 盅 by 虛, a vessel's emptiness. A reader who sees *empty* in English cannot tell which character stands behind it, and the atlas can.

### What no rule can enforce

**"Blend" and "blending" are ruled out for 沖 and cannot go on the `forbidden:` list.** 和 (hé — to harmonize) stands in chapters 4 and 42 beside 沖, and *blend* is a fair English for it in another line. Chapter 4's 和其光 (*hé qí guāng*) now reads *"dims its own light."* A forbidden *blend* would fire on a line that may one day print it rightly for 和. The checker keys on which characters are in a chapter, and cannot tell the two apart (`process/principles/ruling-and-enforcement-are-two-facts.md`).

### Left open

- **Beida has not been checked at chapters 4 and 45**, and its 中 at chapter 42 rests on a secondhand transcription. The printed edition is where to confirm it (`WORKLIST.md` T2-79).
- **The Guodian graph at chapter 45** is transcribed both 盅 and 中. If it is 中, the oldest copy writes the empty line with the bare sound part, and the case for silk A's 中 as a separate word at chapter 42 is weaker (`WORKLIST.md` T2-79).
- **Fu Yi's 衝 at chapter 42** is a third reading, a rushing breath. It is let go for the two excavated copies, and the line loses its motion for it.
- **Chapter 42's Source table prints 沖 beside *within*.** A reader who reads the Chinese alone meets the graph without the word the older copies chose, and finds it only in the notes. The same holds for 學父 (*xué fù* — "father of learning") two lines down.
- **Duan Yucai's edition of the *Shuowen* has not been checked** for 沖's entry. It may write 中聲 where the vendored text does not. The reading of 中 as the sound part rests on the dictionary's other 中 compounds and on the meaning, which has no middle in it. Where Xu Shen holds that a part gives both sound and meaning, he writes 亦聲 (*yì shēng* — "also for sound"), and he does not write it here.
