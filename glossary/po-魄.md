## 魄 (Pò) — Aliveness

*Say "paw," falling. One appearance in the whole book, in the first line of chapter 10.*

**Locked: 魄 → "aliveness."** *(Never "soul," never "spirit," never "life force." Not "breath," "energy" or "life" either, but those three are rulings no check can hold. See the last section but one.)*

*The line is* 載營魄抱一，能無離乎 *: "Housing your aliveness and embracing the one, can you keep these from parting?" 營 (yíng — a walled dwelling), the character beside it, is covered here as "housing."*

### The graph: a ghost, and the moon's pale light

> 魄，**陰神**也。从鬼白聲。
> *"魄 is the yin spirit. From 鬼 (guǐ — a ghost), with 白 (bái — white) for the sound."* — 說文解字

**鬼** is the same character chapter 60 renders *ghosts*. In the oracle bones it is a figure with an outsized head, usually read as a mask: the dead, or someone standing in for them. **白**'s original picture is unknown. A thumb (郭沫若, *Guō Mòruò*), a container and a human head have all been proposed, and none has won. 說文 counts it as sound alone.

**The graph is young, and the word is old.** 魄 has no oracle-bone or bronze form. The word does: Western Zhou bronzes date events by the moon's phases, and they write *pò* as **霸**, a moon (月) with a sound part. 既生霸 is *"after the light is born,"* 既死霸 *"after the light has died."* 說文 under 霸: 月始生，霸然也, *"the moon first coming to life, pale and bright."* 段玉裁 (*Duàn Yùcái*) notes the hand-over, 後代魄行而霸廢矣, *"in later ages 魄 took over and 霸 fell out of use"*. The moon sense survived inside it: 李軌 (*Lǐ Guǐ*), on the 法言, has 魄，光也, *"魄 is light,"* and 孟康 (*Mèng Kāng*) has 魄，月質也, *"魄 is the moon's body."*

**So 白 may be more than a sound.** A Han apocryphal text quoted by 孔穎達 (*Kǒng Yǐngdá*) puns the two outright: 魄，白也…白，明白也, *"魄 is white… white is brightness."* 說文 does not say 白亦聲 (*"白 also gives the sound"*), its usual mark for a part that carries meaning and sound at once, and the atlas reports 說文. This entry records that Han readers heard more.

*A calibration: we do not claim Laozi heard the moon in this word. By his time 魄 was a word for a part of a living person, and the evidence for that is what decides the English.*

### What 魄 is: the life that clings to the form

The locus classicus is 子產 (*Zǐ Chǎn*) in the 左傳 (*Zuǒ Zhuàn*), explaining how a man eight years dead could walk as a ghost:

> 人生始化曰魄，既生魄，陽曰魂。用物精多，則魂魄強。
> *"When a person first comes into being, that is called 魄. Once the 魄 is born, its yang side is called 魂 (hún). Draw on much 精 (jīng — vital essence) and the 魂 and 魄 grow strong."*

The commentators read it as plainly bodily, and the readings agree:

| source | says | in English |
|---|---|---|
| 杜預 (*Dù Yù*), on the 左傳 | 魄，**形**也 | *"魄 is the form"* |
| 孔穎達, on the same line | **附形之靈**為魄，附氣之神為魂 | *"the life clinging to the form is the 魄; the spirit clinging to the breath is the 魂"* |
| 孔穎達, continuing | 初生之時，**耳目心識，手足運動，啼呼為聲**，此則魄之靈也 | *"at birth, the ears, eyes and heart taking things in, the hands and feet moving, the crying that is its voice: this is the life of the 魄"* |
| 鄭玄 (*Zhèng Xuán*), on the 禮記 | **耳目之聰明**為魄 | *"the sharpness of ear and eye is the 魄"* |
| 白虎通 (*Báihǔ Tōng*) | 魄者迫也，猶迫迫然**箸於人**也 | *"魄 means pressed close: it clings tightly to a person"* |
| 禮記, 郊特牲 | 魂氣歸于天，**形魄歸于地** | *"the 魂 and the breath return to the sky; the form and the 魄 return to the earth"* |

Five things follow. 魄 is **first**, the life a person has before anything else. It is **bodily**, attached to the form and not to the breath. It is **sensing and moving**, and 孔穎達's example is a newborn. It **clings**. And it comes **in degrees**, strong or weak, as the 左傳 says.

王弼 (*Wáng Bì*), whose commentary this edition reads beside the text, puts 魄 on the same side in his own vocabulary. At chapter 4 the earth is 形魄, *"form and 魄,"* against the sky's 精象, *"essence and image"*: 地雖形魄不法於天則不能全其寧, *"the earth, though it is form and 魄, cannot keep its rest unless it models itself on the sky."* At chapter 25: 形魄不及精象, *"form and 魄 do not reach essence and image."*

### Chapter 10: the breath beside it

> 載營魄抱一，能無離乎
> 專氣致柔，能如嬰兒乎
> *"Housing your aliveness and embracing the one, can you keep these from parting?*
> *Gathering the breath to softness, can you be like an infant?"*

**The couplet names the two halves of a living person that the 禮記 pairs.** 祭義: 氣也者，神之盛也；魄也者，鬼之盛也, *"breath is the fullness of spirit; the 魄 is the fullness of ghost."* Aliveness stands in the first line and breath in the second. The infant the second line asks for is 孔穎達's picture of the 魄 itself.

**魂 (*hún*), the 魄's usual partner, never appears in the book.** 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) supplies it anyway: 營魄，魂魄也…魂在肝，魄在肺, *"營魄 is the 魂 and the 魄… the 魂 in the liver, the 魄 in the lungs."* The pairing is older than he is. It is in the 左傳 above, and in 韓非子 (*Hán Fēizǐ*)'s own commentary on chapter 60, 魂魄去而精神亂, *"when the 魂 and 魄 leave, essence and spirit fall into disorder."* What is Han is the **organ map**. In five-phase medicine the lungs are metal, autumn and white, and that is the second road by which whiteness reaches 魄. It runs through a system this text does not use. Michael Brashier has argued that even the two-soul scheme was a scholars' model, and that Han grave inscriptions set the 魂 and 魄 together at the tomb against the 神 (*shén* — spirit) at the shrine.

**Who houses whom.** 王弼: 載猶處也。營魄，人之常居處也…言人能處常居之宅, *"載 is like 'to dwell.' 營魄 is a person's constant dwelling… a person who can dwell in the house where they always live."* For him the 營魄 is the house and the person lives in it. ***Housing* takes the other direction**: the person holds the 魄. That is 王逸 (*Wáng Yì*)'s reading of the same phrase in the 楚辭 (*Chǔ Cí*), 載營魄而登霞兮, *"bearing my 營魄 I climb the rosy clouds,"* glossed 抱我魂靈而上升也, *"embracing my soul I rise."* It is also 河上公's, 當愛養之, *"you must cherish and nourish it."* The verb stands on 營's graph, 說文 營，市居也, *"a walled residence."*

**The witnesses.** The Mawangdui silk B text reads 戴營魄抱一，能毌離乎, with 戴 (*dài* — to carry on the head) for 載. The Peking University Han slips read 載**熒**魄, with 熒 (*yíng* — a glimmer) for 營. That is one witness, and it is a reason to look rather than a reading, but it puts the moon's pale light back into the line. Guodian has no chapter 10.

### Why "aliveness"

- **It is the body's own.** Nothing in it leaves the body or lives elsewhere, and *housing* already says where it is kept.
- **It carries the senses.** English *alive to* means sensitive to, which is 鄭玄's sharp ear and eye. *Vigor* had motion only.
- **It comes in degrees.** You can be more or less alive, as the 左傳's 魄 grows strong.
- **It is the infant.** 孔穎達's newborn, eyes open, limbs moving, crying, is aliveness you could point at, and chapter 10 asks for the infant one line later.
- **It brings nothing in.** No theology, no breath, no energy and no sex.

### What is set aside, and why

**"soul"** is what every English translation prints: Waley *the physical-soul*, Lau *the bodily soul*, Lynn *the earthbound soul*, Legge *the animal soul*, and plain *soul* in Henricks, Mair and Ivanhoe. Each qualifier is an attempt to take back what the noun imports, a separable, immaterial self, and the qualifiers are the evidence that it does. Already refused for 精 (*jīng*) and 身 (*shēn*) on the same grounds.

**"the corporeal soul"** is the accurate label, and it is right in a note. In the verse it is a technical term for the overlay; see `process/principles/perform-dont-label.md`.

**"spirit"** is 神's (6, 39, 60), and 神 is the half the 禮記 sets against the 魄.

**"life force"** and **"life-energy"** are the popular English for 氣 (*qì* — breath), which is the other half. *Energy* is 氣's own word at chapter 55.

**"vitality"** shares its root with two locked phrases: 精 is *vital essence* (21, 55) and 氣 is *vital breath* (42) and *vital energy* (55). The 左傳's definition would then read *"draw on much vital essence and the vitality grows strong,"* and the distinction it draws is lost.

**"vigor"** leans toward virility, and the sexual vitality in this book is 精's, at chapter 55's 朘作.

**"the animating part"** sends 魄 the wrong way. *Anima* is breath, and breath is 氣's, in the next line.

**"the quick"** is the creed's *the quick and the dead*, and chapter 55 already *quickens*.

**"body"** and **"flesh"**: *body* is 身's (13, 54, 76), and *flesh* is Pauline, Romans 8, the hymn test.

### The working register

One occurrence, a noun, the object of 載 (*zài* — to carry, to ride, to dwell in). The two characters before it are rendered together by one verb:

| Chinese | English | renders |
|---|---|---|
| 載營 | *housing* | 營's graph, a walled dwelling, with 載's carrying |
| 魄 | *your aliveness* | 魄. *Your* is supplied, as English needs a possessor |

### What no rule can enforce

**"breath," "energy" and "life" are ruled out for 魄 and are not on the `forbidden:` list, because they cannot be.** 氣 stands in chapter 10 and prints *breath* there. *Energy* is its English at chapter 55, and a future reading of chapter 10 could use it. 生 (*shēng*) stands in chapter 10 too (生之畜之), and *life* is its word elsewhere. `check_locks.py` gates on whether a character is present in the chapter, and all three characters are. So *soul*, *spirit*, *life force* and *vigor* are held by the build; *breath*, *energy* and *life* are held by this entry and by the reader. See `process/principles/ruling-and-enforcement-are-two-facts.md`.

### Left open

**The ghost.** Aliveness ends at death; the 魄 does not. 子產's whole point is that a strong 魄 can outlast the person: the 禮記 sends it into the ground with the body, and it is what lingers at the tomb. The English keeps the living half of the word and drops the half that goes into the earth, and it has to, because the only English that keeps both is *soul*. Laozi's line is about a living person keeping two things from parting, so the ghost is offstage. It is not gone.

**Whether 白 carries meaning.** 說文 says sound. The 霸 word family and a Han pun say more. 明白 (*míng bái* — clarity) stands four lines below in this chapter, and `WORKLIST` T2-23 already asks whether that compound is bound. Whether the chapter hears 魄 and 明白 together is a reason to look and not a reading (`process/principles/distribution-locates.md`).

**The direction of 載.** 王弼 has the person inside the 營魄; 王逸 and 河上公 have the person holding it. The verse follows the second. 王弼 is the commentator this edition most often follows, and here it does not.
