## 畏 (Wèi) — Fear

*Say "way," falling. Seven lines across six chapters. Fear of something that can strike: a ruler, authority, death.*

**Locked: 畏 → "fear," with *wary* at chapter 15.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-11-20; no verse changed.)* *(Not "awe" or "reverence," which turn a fear into worship. Not "dread," the feeling, where 畏 always has an object. Not "frighten," which is 懼 (jù)'s act.)*

### The graph: a ghost with a stick

**In the oracle bones and the early bronzes, 畏 is a ghost wielding a stick**: 鬼 (*guǐ* — a ghost), with 卜 (*bǔ*), a stick, in its hand, and the ghost gives the sound too (`dong-chinese`). The Shuowen, a millennium later, reads the same shape as a ghost and a tiger:

> 畏，惡也。从甶，虎省。鬼頭而虎爪，可畏也。
> *"畏 is to dread. From 甶 (fú — a ghost's head) and part of 虎 (hǔ — a tiger). A ghost's head and a tiger's claws: a thing to dread."*

**Either way the picture is of the thing feared, not of the person afraid.** The ghost has a weapon, or claws. 畏 is fear that has a source standing in front of it. **Where the picture stops:** the book has no ghosts in its 畏 lines. What it fears is rulers, authority, death, and straying from the Tao. 鬼 (*guǐ* — ghosts) appears once, at chapter 60, and there *"their spirit does no harm to people"* (`glossary/shen-ling-神靈.md`).

### Seven lines, and every fear has an object

> *Next, those who are feared.*
>
> 其次，畏之；
>
> — chapter 17

> *What people fear must be feared.*
>
> 人之所畏，不可不畏。
>
> — chapter 20

> *If I had just a scrap of knowledge, walking the path of the great Tao, my only fear would be to stray from it.*
>
> 使我介然有知，行於大道，唯施是畏。
>
> — chapter 53

> *When the people do not fear authority,*
> *the great collapse comes.*
>
> 民不畏威，
> 則大威至。
>
> — chapter 72

> *When the people do not fear death,*
> *why try to frighten them with death?*
>
> 民不畏死，
> 奈何以死懼之？
>
> — chapter 74

> *Suppose the people always feared death,*
> *and I could seize whoever steps out of line and kill them.*
> *Who would still push?*
>
> 若使民常畏死，
> 而為奇者，吾得執而殺之，
> 孰敢？
>
> — chapter 74

**In every line 畏 takes an object, said or understood**: a ruler (17), what people fear (20), straying (53), authority (72), death (74). It is never a mood. **And in three of the seven, the fear is the ruler's tool**: the ruler who is feared (17), the authority the people stop fearing (72), the death a ruler would use to frighten them (74). Wang Bi on 17: the ruler feared is one 不復能以恩仁令物而賴威權也 (*"no longer able to move things by kindness, and relying on authority"*). Heshang Gong: 設刑法以治之 (*"setting up punishments and laws to govern them"*). The book's verdict is that this fear stops working.

**The ruler's authority is fear's own word.** 威 (*wēi* — authority) at 72 is a different graph, 女 (*nǚ* — woman) beneath 戌 (*xū* — an axe), but in Old Chinese it is the same sound as 畏, *ʔuj against 畏's *ʔuj-s (Baxter and Sagart, via `dong-chinese`): the power and the fear of it, one word in the mouth. So 民不畏威 is close to *the people do not fear the fearsome*, and English cannot say it. *"Fear dread"* was tried and refused (`notes/translation.md` → *Ch 72*).

### Chapter 20: the sage fears too

**What people fear must be feared.** Wang Bi does not soften it: 故人之所畏，吾亦畏焉 (*"so what people fear, I fear too"*). He gives the reason a line earlier: 畏譽而進，何異畏刑 (*"to fear praise and push forward, how is that different from fearing punishment?"*). The chapter that opens *"Cut off learning and there is no worry"* does not put its speaker above fear. They fear what everyone fears, and do not make a use of it.

### Chapter 15: wary, and the commentators split

> *Hesitant, like someone wary of the neighbors on all four sides.*
>
> 猶兮若畏四鄰；
>
> — chapter 15

**Here 畏 is inside a likeness, and the commentators hear two different fears.**

> 四鄰合攻中央之主，猶然不知所趣向者也。
> *"The four neighbors join to attack the ruler at the center, who hesitates and does not know which way to turn."* — Wang Bi

> 其進退猶猶如拘制，若人犯法畏四隣知之也。
> *"Their advancing and retreating is hesitant, as if held back, like someone who has broken the law fearing the four neighbors will find out."* — Heshang Gong

**Wang Bi's is attack; Heshang Gong's is being seen.** *Fearing the neighbors* would name them the danger and take Wang Bi's side. ***Wary of*** keeps the watching, with or without the threat, and holds both. It is the one flexion, scoped to this line in `flexions:`. Heshang Gong reaches for 畏 again in the next line, 如客畏主人 (*"like a guest who fears the host"*), where the verse reads *"Dignified, like a guest."*

### What is set aside

**"Awe," "reverence," "revere."** The overlay. The *Analects* gives 君子 (*jūn zǐ* — the noble) three 畏, the first 畏天命 (*wèi tiān mìng* — "fear the allotted course of nature"), and Legge's English (1861) made it *"stands in awe of the ordinances of Heaven."* That reading turns a fear with an object into worship, and it fails the hymn test outright. The book's 畏 is a people's fear of a ruler and of death; nobody in it worships. *Awe* is listed with a leading space, so that *aware* does not trip it.

**"Dread."** The feeling, where 畏 is always fear *of* something. Shalom refused it at chapter 72, *"You fear a thing; dread is the feeling"* (`notes/translation.md` → *Ch 72*).

**"Be afraid of," "afraid."** Not wrong, and no line needs it: *fear* is shorter and takes an object directly.

**"Frighten," "terrify."** 懼 (*jù* — to startle)'s act, at chapter 74, where the line is built on the difference: 畏 the fear held, 懼 the fear caused, *fear* against *frighten*.

### Working register

| line | form | English |
|---|---|---|
| 畏之 (17) | passive in English: *they fear them* | *those who are feared* |
| 人之所畏 · 不可不畏 (20) | 所 + verb · 不可不 + verb | *what people fear* · *must be feared* (`glossary/ke-可.md`) |
| 唯施是畏 (53) | the object fronted, 唯 X 是 V | *my only fear would be* (`glossary/wei-唯.md`) |
| 民不畏威 (72) · 民不畏死 · 常畏死 (74) | verb + object | *fear authority* · *fear death* · *always feared death* |
| 若畏四鄰 (15) | inside a likeness | *wary of* (flexion) |

### What no rule can enforce

**"Fear" for another character.** 懼 (*jù*) at 74 could slip into *fear*, and nothing gates it; `concordance.py --english "fear"` is the reverse check. The forbidden list catches only the overlay words.

### Left open

**Chapter 53's object.** The verse reads 施 (*shī*) as 迤 (*yǐ* — to stray), *"my only fear would be to stray from it,"* for the byways the next line names. Both commentators read 施 as acting, Wang Bi's 唯施為之是畏 (*"only acting would I fear"*). The fear is the same word either way; what it fears is Shalom's call of 2026-11-19 (`notes/translation.md` → *Ch 53*).

**Chapter 17's ruler.** *Those who are feared* follows Wang Bi and Heshang Gong in making the ruler the feared one. Where a ruler fears the people, the book never says so with 畏.
