## 極 (Jí) — The Far End

*Say "jee," rising. Six lines across five chapters, and the graph beneath it is a standing body.*

**Recommended: 極 → "the far end" — the point at which a thing runs out.** *(Not "limitless," not "the Absolute," not "the Ultimate," not "infinite," not "extreme," not "utmost." The compound 無極 → "the endless.")* *Two lines remain unsettled; see the close.*

極 is the late graph. The original is **亟 (*jí*)**, and in the oracle-bone script 亟 is a standing person with one horizontal stroke above the head and one below the feet — traditionally read 極于頂 (*jí yú dǐng* — "the pole at the crown") and 極于踵 (*jí yú zhǒng* — "the pole at the heel"). A figure drawn between the two things a body can touch: what is over it, and what it stands on. Bronze forms add 口 (*kǒu* — a mouth) and 又 (*yòu* — a hand).

There is no fence in the graph. There is a person at full extension, and the two places their reach runs out.

Then 木 (*mù* — wood) was added, and the same idea became a building. 說文解字 (*Shuōwén Jiězì*, c. 100 CE) on 極: **棟也。从木亟聲** — *"the ridgepole. From 木, with 亟 for sound."* The 棟 (*dòng*) is the topmost beam of a roof, the timber the rafters lean into from both slopes. Not an ornament set on top of the house: **the last structural member, the one past which there is no more house.**

A body at full stretch; a roof at its topmost beam. Both are terminal points of something extended. Both are finite, because the thing that reached them is. And neither is a boundary imposed from outside — which is what separates 極 from every English word for a limit.

### The commentators gloss it with one word, seven times

Wherever 極 appears, 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*, d. 249 CE) and 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*, Han) reach for **窮 (*qióng* — to exhaust, to use up)**:

| Chapter | Line | The gloss |
|---|---|---|
| 28 | 復歸於無極 | 王弼: 不可窮也 — *"it cannot be exhausted"* |
| 28 | 復歸於無極 | 河上公: 歸身於無窮極也 — *"return the body to what has no exhausting 極"* |
| 58 | 孰知其極 | 河上公: 禍福更相生，誰能知其窮極時 — *"disaster and fortune give birth to each other in turn; who can know the time of their exhausting 極?"* |
| 59 | 莫知其極 | 王弼: 道無窮也 — *"the Tao is inexhaustible"* |
| 59 | 莫知其極 | 河上公: 莫有知己德之窮極也 — *"no one knows the exhausting 極 of their own integrity"* |
| 25 | on 自然 | 王弼: 窮極之辭也 — *"an expression of the exhausted 極"* |
| 36 | on 柔弱 | 王弼: 柔弱同通，不可窮極 — *"the soft and the yielding alike run through; they cannot be run out to the 極"* |

Seven instances, one word. **極 is not how far a thing extends. It is where a thing runs out** — a claim about supply, not about size.

That is also what 無極 (*wú jí*) says at chapter 28. Not a thing of unbounded extent: a thing you cannot use up. A roof with no topmost beam.

### The hardest line: 致虛極

致 (*zhì* — to send all the way there) 虛 (*xū* — empty) 極 (*jí*) invites an objection that turns out to be instructive. If emptiness has no measure, then reaching its 極 is reaching the limit of the limitless.

**The objection dissolves, because 虛 in this book is never boundless.** Every occurrence is a container with its contents gone — a heart at chapter 3, a bellows at 5, hollow words at 22, a granary at 53. 說文 makes 虛 a settlement-mound, a unit of surveyed land. *(See `xu-虛.md`.)*

**And the graph closes the question. The 極 in 致虛極 does not belong to emptiness. It belongs to the person doing the emptying** — the standing body, crown to heel, at the end of its reach. The line does not measure the hollow. It measures how far you go into it.

What is left is a better difficulty, and a real one. **The verb is a travelling and the method is a subtraction.** 致 is 夊 (*suī* — a dragging foot) beneath 至 (*zhì* — to arrive); 說文: 送詣也, *"to send all the way there."* You go on foot. But 河上公 says how you go: 得道之人捐情去欲，五內清淨，至於虛極 — *"one who has gained the Tao sheds feeling and casts off desire; the five viscera are clear and still; and so arrives at 虛極."* Chapter 48 gives the same movement in the same grammar: 損之又損，以至於無為 — *"reduce, and reduce again, until arriving at non-doing."*

**You travel by taking away.** The only way to reach the far end of a hollow is to keep removing until there is nothing left to remove.

### What is set aside

**limit** — the word English reaches for first, and the reason the rest of the list is needed. A limit is a fence: a stop set from outside, marking where you are not allowed to go. 極 is the far point of a reach, measured by the reacher. The two are opposite kinds of word, and only one of them has a person in it.

**limitless** — the wrong axis. It answers *how big*, where every commentator answers *how much is left*.

**the Absolute, the Ultimate** — 極 is the load-bearing beam of a specific roof. Both import a metaphysical absolute the graph does not hold, and *the Ultimate* additionally drags in 太極 (*tài jí* — "the supreme ridgepole"), Song-dynasty cosmology reading its own system back into a Warring States word. The failure has the same shape as 一 (*yī* — one) rendered *the One*.

**infinite** — a ridgepole is finite, a body's reach is finite, and 說文 offers no other picture.

**extreme** — belongs to 甚 (*shèn* — excessive), which carries it at chapters 29 and 44. English *extreme* also means immoderation, a verdict 極 does not pass.

**utmost** — belongs to 至 (*zhì* — to arrive at), which carries it twice in chapter 55: 精之至也 and 和之至也, *"vital essence at its utmost," "harmony at its utmost."*

### The working register

**極 flexes by grammar, and the flexions are not interchangeable.**

| Form | Chapters | English |
|---|---|---|
| 無極, the compound | 28 | the endless |
| 知其極, the far point of a process | 58, 59 | where it ends |
| 極 after a verb and its object | 16 | the terminal degree of the verb, not a noun |
| 古之極, the far point of a practice | 68 | *(unsettled — see below)* |

**The line-final position at chapter 16 is grammatical, not decorative.** 致虛極 and 守靜篤 (*shǒu jìng dǔ* — "hold to stillness, 篤") are the same three-beat shape twice: verb, object, terminal degree word. 篤 (*dǔ* — steady; 說文 馬行頓遟, *"a horse's gait, halting and slow"*) cannot be a noun head — nobody arrives at the thickness of stillness — so 極 in the matching slot is doing the same adverbial work. 王弼 confirms it from the other side by binding the two into a single compound across both lines: 言致虛，物之極篤 — *"reaching emptiness is things' 極篤"* — and again 是物之極篤也. He can only pair them if they are the same kind of word.

### The tension, left open

**Chapter 68's 古之極 is the one appearance this entry cannot place.** 是謂配天，古之極 — *"this is called being 配 (pèi — matched, as one of a pair) with 天 (tiān — sky), the 極 of the ancients."* It is not a process whose end is unknowable, and it is not adverbial. It reads as *the far point the ancients reached*, which is exactly the graph — but that line is already contested at 配天, and settling one half would settle both. *(See `tian-天.md`.)*

**And the book reaches a 極 exactly once.** Chapter 16 is the only imperative: go there. Everywhere else the word is denied — unknowable at 58 and at 59 twice, negated outright at 28. Four refusals and one instruction, and the instruction is the one addressed to a person rather than to a process. Whether that is the text distinguishing what a body can do from what a world does, or a word used loosely across a compiled book, the six lines do not say.
