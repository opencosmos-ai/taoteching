## 持 (Chí) — To Hold

*Say "chr," rising. Three lines across three chapters — and the root of the whole field.*

**Locked: 持 → "hold."** *(Never "grip," never "clutch." Never *grasp* either — but that one is a ruling no check can hold, and it has a section of its own below.)*

### The graph: a hand, and the word every other holding-word is defined by

> 持，**握**也。从手寺聲。
> *"持 is to grip. From 手 (shǒu — hand), with 寺 as the sound."* — 說文解字

Xu Shen then defines 握 (*wò*) straight back: 握，**搤持**也. The two send you to each other, which looks like a failure of a dictionary and is in fact the useful signal. **持 is where the definitions stop.** It is the plain closing of a hand on a thing, with nothing added — no restraint, no post, no child, no verdict.

You can watch the rest of the family get built out of it. 說文 defines the other hand-verbs in the Laozi *by* 持:

| | 說文 | |
|---|---|---|
| **搏** (*bó*) — chapters 14, 55 | **索持**也 | *"to search out and grip"* |
| **據** (*jù*) — chapter 55 | **杖持**也 | *"to grip as with a staff"* |

And 寺, treated as merely phonetic here, seeds a family sorted by *which part of you does the holding* — a table that also stands in `shi-恃.md`:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **持** (*chí*) | 扌 hand + 寺 | to hold, in the hand |
| **侍** (*shì*) | 亻 person + 寺 | to attend upon, with the body |
| **待** (*dài*) | 彳 step + 寺 | to wait for |
| **恃** (*shì*) | 忄 heart + 寺 | to lean your weight on |

### Three lines, and they are the neutral ones

持而盈之 (9) · 其安易**持** (64) · **持**而保之 (67). None of them carries a verdict. Chapter 64 makes that explicit by putting 持 and 執 (*zhí* — to grasp, to take into custody) in the same chapter, seventeen lines apart, doing opposite work:

> 其安易**持** — *"what is at rest is easy to hold."*
> **執**者失之 — *"those who grasp it lose it."*

That is the chapter's argument in two verbs. Hold a thing while it is still at rest and there is nothing to it; close your hand on it to keep it and you lose it. An English that gives both characters the same word deletes the sentence.

Chapter 67 sets 持 beside 保 (*bǎo* — to keep safe) in a single line, 持而**保**之, and 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) expands it with a third: 抱持而保倚, *"embracing, holding, leaning on for safety."* Three verbs for one gesture, because in Chinese they are three different gestures.

### What is set aside

**"grip"** and **"clutch"** both add force the character does not have. 持 is what you do with a cup; 握 is the tightening, and 執 is the closing that will not let go. Chapter 64's 其安易持 is about how little effort a thing takes while it is still quiet, and a word with tension in it argues the opposite.

**"sustain"** abstracts a hand into a process, and lands on the mechanistic register this edition watches for.

### What no rule can enforce

***"grasp"* is ruled out in the verse, and it is not on the `forbidden:` list, because it cannot be.** Those are two different facts: the ruling holds, and the enforcement does not exist. 執 stands with 持 in chapter 64, and *grasp* is 執's word there. A rule keyed to 持's presence would fire on 執's own line. Nor can *hold to* be reserved here: 守 (*shǒu*) stands with 持 in chapters 9 and 67. Three of the four holding characters meet on chapter 67's page — 持 · 保 · 守, with 寶 chiming against 保 — and the whole burden of keeping them apart falls on the entries and on the reader. See `zhi-執.md`, `shou-守.md`, `bao-保.md`.

### Left open

**Chapter 9's opening line.** 持而盈之，不如其已 is a two-verb condition — *hold it and fill it* — and the second verb, 盈 (*yíng* — to fill), is the character 說文 defines as 滿器也, *"a full vessel."* Whether the English should name the vessel the Chinese only implies is unsettled, and 盈 has no entry yet.
