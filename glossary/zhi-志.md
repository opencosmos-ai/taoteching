## 志 (Zhì) — Will

*Say "jr," falling. Three lines across three chapters. The heart going toward something: the drive, and once the place it is aimed.*

**Locked: 志 → "will" where it is the heart's drive (3, 33), and "aims" at chapter 31, where 得志 is the target reached.** *(Shalom's calls, 2026-11-20.)* *(Not "ambition," which passes a slight verdict the character does not. Not "purpose," which is a plan more than a drive. Not "willpower," which is a modern self-help word.)*

### The graph: the heart, going

> 志，意也。从心之聲。
> *"志 is intent. From 心 (xīn — the heart), with 之 (zhī) for sound."*

**之 is a foot setting out, *to go*, and here it gives the sound; the heart is the sense.** The reading most dictionaries offer, the heart going toward something, uses the sound part's own meaning, and the Shuowen does not claim it. **Where the picture stops:** the heart is where the will starts. Chapter 3 says what happens to it when the heart is emptied, and chapter 33 what it does in a strong stride.

### Three lines: yielded, possessed, and unreached

> *teaching their will to yield and strengthening their bones,*
>
> 弱其志，強其骨。
>
> — chapter 3

> *One who knows they have enough is wealthy.*
> *One who walks with strength has will.*
>
> 知足者富，
> 強行者有志。
>
> — chapter 33

> *And those who delight in killing people cannot achieve their aims in the world.*
>
> 則不可得志於天下矣。
>
> — chapter 31

**The book yields the will at chapter 3 and praises having it at chapter 33, and only one word lets a reader see that a yielded will is not no will** (Shalom's call, 2026-11-20; it had read *ambition* at 3 and *purpose* at 33). Wang Bi links them. At 3: 志生事以亂…心虛則志弱也 (*"the will gives rise to affairs, and so to disorder… when the heart is emptied, the will yields"*). At 33: 勤能行之，其志必獲 (*"keep at it diligently, and the will surely attains"*). Heshang Gong reads 33's will as turned toward the Tao: 有意於道，道亦有意於人 (*"they have intent toward the Tao, and the Tao has intent toward them"*). So the will that yields at 3 is the heart's push to make affairs; the will that walks at 33 is the push that keeps going. One word, two directions.

**At chapter 31 the will is its target.** 得志 (*dé zhì*) is the idiom for an aim reached, and Shalom heard *"get their will"* as wrong: *will is the drive, not the result*. Heshang Gong: 為人君而樂殺人，此不可使得志於天下 (*"a ruler who delights in killing must not be allowed to achieve their aim in the world"*). So 31 reads *achieve their aims*, with 得 (*dé* — get) taking *achieve* as its own flexion (`glossary/de-shi-得失.md`).

### What is set aside

**"Ambition."** Chapter 3's word until 2026-11-20. It carries a mild charge in English, ambition being what the modest lack, and it would make chapter 33 praise a vice or chapter 3 tame a virtue. 志 is neutral; the book's verdict comes from 弱 (*ruò* — yield) and 強 (*qiáng* — strong) around it.

**"Purpose."** Chapter 33's word until 2026-11-20. A purpose is a plan, the end in view; 志 at 33 is the drive in the stride.

**"Willpower."** Nineteenth-century and self-help; it fails the pointing test.

**"Aspiration," "resolve."** *Aspiration* reaches upward, a hymn's direction. *Resolve* is a decision made, close but colder; Wang Bi uses 有志 (*yǒu zhì*) of the highest in service at chapter 41 as resolve to practise, and the English there renders 勤 (*qín* — strain), not 志 (`glossary/qin-勤.md`).

**"Have their way," "what they want."** The idioms for 31, and each borrows another lock: *way* is 道 (*dào*)'s, *want* is 欲 (*yù*)'s.

### Working register

| line | form | English |
|---|---|---|
| 弱其志 (3) | object of the causative | *teaching their will to yield* |
| 強行者有志 (33) | object of 有 | *has will* |
| 得志 (31) | the idiom, the target reached | *achieve their aims* (flexion) |

### What no rule can enforce

**"Will" the auxiliary.** English *will* is also the future tense, in every chapter. Nothing can tell the noun from the verb but the reader, and `concordance.py --english "will"` returns hundreds of lines.

### Left open

**Chapter 33's *has will*** is stiffer than *has purpose*, in a couplet Shalom set on 2026-10-30. The couplet's grammar holds: *has* stands only for 有 (*yǒu* — has).

**Wang Bi's two wills.** At 3 the will makes affairs and disorder; at 33 it attains. Whether the book means two wills or one will in two states, the English lets the reader decide.
