## 國 (Guó) — State

*Say "gwaw," rising. Twenty-five times across ten chapters, and three more as 邦 (bāng) in chapter 54. One domain among others, with a ruler and people in it: the thing you govern, as the world is not.*

**Locked: 國 → "state."** *(Shalom's call, 2026-10-14, set at chapters 18 and 54; the entry 2026-11-20.)* **邦 (*bāng*) is the same word, covered here, and reads *state* too.** *(Not "nation," which makes a people out of a domain. Not "country," which makes it a landscape or a homeland. Not "kingdom," which crowns someone the text does not name. Not "realm," a court's word.)*

### The graph: a weapon guarding a piece of ground, and a wall around it

**The Shuowen defines 國 and 邦 by each other**, and gives the older graph inside 國 the same gloss:

> 國，邦也。从囗从或。
> *"國 is 邦. From 囗 (wéi — an enclosure) and 或 (huò)."*
>
> 或，邦也。从囗从戈，以守一。一，地也。
> *"或 is 邦. From 囗 (wéi — an enclosure) and 戈 (gē — a dagger-axe), to guard the one. The one is the ground."*
>
> 邦，國也。从邑丰聲。
> *"邦 is 國. From 邑 (yì — a settlement), with 丰 (fēng) for sound."*

**或 is a dagger-axe standing guard over a stretch of ground marked by a line**, and 國 draws a wall around the whole. A state is held ground: bounded, guarded, and lived in. The same 或 sits under 域 (*yù* — a bounded stretch of ground) at chapter 25 (`notes/translation.md`). **Where the picture stops:** nothing in the graph says who holds it, or how many people are inside. The book fills that in, line by line, with a ruler and *the people* (`glossary/min-民.md`).

### 邦 (bāng — state)

**邦 is 國's older name, and the two differ only by a taboo.** The founder of the Han dynasty was Liu Bang, and his personal name could not be written once he ruled. Mawangdui silk A, copied before the taboo, writes 邦 wherever the graph survives; silk B, copied under it, already writes 國, as our base does (`sources/variants.yaml`). Han Fei, older still, reads 邦 at chapter 36's 邦之利器 (*"the state's beneficial tools"*) and at chapter 54.

> *Cultivate this in your state, and its integrity becomes bountiful.*
>
> 修之於邦，其德乃豐；
>
> — chapter 54

**Our base keeps 邦 only in chapter 54**, three times, where Wang Bi's text has 國. It is one word, and the English gives it one word.

### Twenty-five lines, and a state is always one among others

> *Governing a large state is like cooking a small fish.*
>
> 治大國，若烹小鮮。
>
> — chapter 60

> *So the large state, by going below the small state, takes the small state.*
> *The small state, by going below the large state, takes the large state.*
>
> 故大國以下小國，則取小國；
> 小國以下大國，則取大國。
>
> — chapter 61

> *A small state, few people.*
>
> 小國寡民。
>
> — chapter 80

> *Neighboring states lie within sight of each other.*
> *They hear each other's chickens and dogs.*
>
> 隣國相望，
> 雞犬之聲相聞，
>
> — chapter 80

**A state in this book is large or small, and has neighbors.** Chapter 61 is a treatise on two states, the large going low to the small. Chapter 80's small state hears the next one's chickens. That plurality is what *state* keeps and *nation* or *the land* would blur: a 國 is a unit among units, with a border you could walk to.

**And a state is governed.** 治國 (*zhì guó* — govern the state) stands at 10, 57 and 65 ×2, and 治大國 (*zhì dà guó* — govern a large state) at 60. Heshang Gong on chapter 10: 治國者愛民則國安 — *"one who governs a state loves the people, and the state is at peace."* Wang Bi on 59: 以有窮而莅國，非能有國也 — *"to oversee a state with something that has limits is not to be able to have the state."* Chapter 78 names its two burdens: *"whoever takes on the shame of the state / is called the keeper of the soil and the grain."*

### The state and the world are two things, and the book keeps them apart

> *Govern the state with the straight,*
> *wage war with the crooked,*
> *and win over the world by not meddling.*
>
> 以正治國，
> 以奇用兵，
> 以無事取天下。
>
> — chapter 57

**Three objects, three ways.** A state is governed; war is waged; the world is won over, and only by not meddling. Chapter 29 says why the world cannot be governed as a state is: it is a sacred vessel, 不可為也 (*bù kě wéi yě* — "it cannot be handled"). Chapter 54's ladder gives the order: body, family, community, **state**, world, with 天下 (*tiān xià* — the world) above the state and never the same thing. Wang Bi on 57: 以道治國則國平 — *"govern the state by the Tao, and the state is at peace."* The English holds the ladder by keeping *state* for 國 and *the world* for 天下 (`glossary/tian-天.md`), so that no reader takes the sage's world for a large state.

### What is set aside

**"Nation," "nations."** A people bound by descent, language or feeling, and the word of the modern nation-state. 國 is a held and governed domain; its people are 民 (*mín* — the people), and they are ruled, not constituted by it.

**"Country," "countries."** A landscape or a homeland, *my country*. It also leans toward the countryside, which is 鄉 (*xiāng* — community)'s rung at chapter 54.

**"Kingdom."** Puts a king on a throne the line does not name. The book's ruler is 王 (*wáng* — ruler), whose graph is an axe-head, not a person (`glossary/wang-王.md`), and *king* would gender him.

**"Realm."** Courtly and medieval, and now a fantasy word. It fails the pointing test: you can point at a state's border, not at a realm's.

**"The land."** 地 (*dì* — earth)'s word, and a state is more than its ground.

### What no rule can enforce

**邦 has no frontmatter of its own.** A cover is listed in the index but no rule reads it, so *country* could return at chapter 54 unseen. `concordance.py --english "country"`, run by hand, is the check.

**天下's *world* is held by its own entry.** Nothing here stops a later line from rendering 國 as *the world*, or 天下 as *the state*; the reverse check on each English word is the guard.

### Left open

**國家 (*guó jiā*) renders 國 alone.** At chapters 18 and 57, 國家 is the compound *the state*, as Wang Bi uses it (民强則國家弱, *"when the people are strong, the state is weak"*), and the English drops 家 (*jiā* — household). In older usage 國家 can be *the state and the great houses*, two rungs; Mencius lists 天下國家 (*"the world, the state, the household"*). Chapter 54 renders 家 *family* on its own rung. The compound reading stands; the two-rung reading is the one this entry declines.

**Chapter 18 is plural, chapter 57 singular.** 國家昏亂 reads *"When states grow clouded"* and 國家滋昏 *"the more clouded the state becomes."* Both lines put 昏 (*hūn* — clouded) on 國家. The plural at 18 is Shalom's call of 2026-10-14, a general statement against chapter 57's one state.

**大國's *large*.** Six lines read *the large state*, where 大 (*dà*) is locked to *great*. `glossary/da-大.md` leaves open whether that is a flexion or a fault.
