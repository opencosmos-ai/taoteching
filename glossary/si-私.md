## 私 (Sī) — The Private Self

*Say "sih," level. Three lines across two chapters. The self that curls around itself: emptied, completed, and diminished.*

**Locked: 私 → "the private self," in all three lines.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-11-20, at chapter 19; chapter 7's two lines read so already.)* *(Not "the self" alone, which is 我吾 (wǒ wú)'s lock. Not "selfish" or "selfless," which turn a self into a verdict. Not "ego," a twentieth-century psychology.)* 公 (*gōng* — impartiality) is its counter-term, and argues the pair: `glossary/gong-公.md`.

### The graph: a curl that encircles itself

**The private sense is in the sound part, 厶 (*sī*): a small closed curl.** Han Fei tells its origin in *The Five Vermin*: 古者蒼頡之作書也，自環者謂之私，背私謂之公 (*"when Cangjie made writing in ancient times, what encircles itself he called 私; what turns its back on 私 he called 公"*). **The Shuowen's 私 itself is a crop word:**

> 私，禾也。从禾厶聲。北道名禾主人曰私主人。
> *"私 is a grain. From 禾 (hé — a grain stalk), with 厶 (sī) for sound. In the northern regions the owner of the grain is called the 私 owner."*

**So the word holds the curl and the harvest you keep for yourself.** *Private* in English has the same root sense, what is held apart. **Where the picture stops:** the book never shows the grain. Its 私 is a self, and in chapter 7 a self that is completed by being let go.

### Three lines, and the self is completed by letting it go

> *Is it not because they have no private self?*
> *And so their private self can be completed.*
>
> 非以其無私邪？
> 故能成其私。
>
> — chapter 7

> *show the undyed silk, embrace the uncarved wood,*
> *diminish the private self, diminish desire.*
>
> 見素抱樸，
> 少私寡欲。
>
> — chapter 19

**Chapter 7 is a paradox that needs one word for both halves**: the sage has no private self, and so it can be completed. Wang Bi: 無私者，無為於身也。身先身存，故曰能成其私也 (*"having no private self is not acting for oneself; the body comes first and survives, so it is said they complete their private self"*). Heshang Gong makes it 公's work: 非以其公正無私所致乎 (*"is it not brought about by impartiality and having no private self?"*).

**Chapter 19 says how, and Heshang Gong ties it back to chapter 7:** 少私者，正無私也；寡欲者，當知足也 (*"to diminish the private self is precisely to have no private self; to want little is to know enough"*). So 19's diminishing is 7's emptying, and the English gives them one word (Shalom's call, 2026-11-20; 19 had read *the self*). 私 sits beside 欲 (*yù* — desire) as one of the two things to thin out, and 寡欲 (*guǎ yù*) is 知足 (*zhī zú* — knowing you have enough) by Heshang Gong's gloss.

### What is set aside

**"The self" alone.** The lock of 我吾 (*wǒ wú* — the self seen, the self seeing), and it loses the curl: 私 is not the self, but the part of it that holds itself apart. Chapter 19 read so until 2026-11-20.

**"Selfish," "selfless," "selfishness."** Verdicts on a character that describes. *Selfless* also flattens chapter 7's paradox: a selfless sage cannot complete their self.

**"Ego."** A twentieth-century psychology's word, and it fails the pointing test. 身 (*shēn* — body) forbids *the ego* too.

**"Private interest," "the private."** Close, and Heshang Gong's 公正無私 reads it so. *The private* alone names a sphere, not the curl of a self around itself; chapter 7's *complete their private* would not stand.

### Working register

| line | form | English |
|---|---|---|
| 無私 (7) | object of 無 | *have no private self* |
| 成其私 (7) | object of 成, passive in English | *their private self can be completed* |
| 少私 (19) | object of 少 | *diminish the private self* |

### What no rule can enforce

**"Self" for 私 in a chapter that holds 我吾.** Chapters 7 and 19 hold neither 我 nor 吾, so the gate keys only on the words above. `concordance.py --english "private self"` should find these three lines and no others.

### Left open

**Chapter 7's voice.** The frame 故能成其X (*gù néng chéng qí X* — "and so can complete their X") reads passive at 7, 34 and 63 since 2026-11-20 (Shalom's call, `glossary/cheng-成.md`): no one completes the private self. The sharper active paradox, *they can complete it*, is given up.

**Chapter 19's rhythm.** The four-character close, 見素抱樸，少私寡欲, now reads unevenly in English: *diminish the private self* against *diminish desire*. The word was chosen over the rhythm.
