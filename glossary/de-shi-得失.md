## 得 & 失 (Dé, Shī) — Getting & Losing

*Say "duh," rising, and "shr," level. 得 stands thirty-three times in thirty lines across nineteen chapters; 失 seventeen times across eight. The commonest verbs of having in the language, and the book weighs them against each other in chapter after chapter.*

**Recommended: 得 → "get" · 失 → "lose."** *(Not "attain" or "obtain," which make getting an achievement, and *attain* the Buddhist one. Not "find," which at chapter 62 turns a saying into the Sermon on the Mount. Not "gain," a ledger word that makes 得 profit.)* **Four flexions:** *received* at chapters 39 and 22 (where the getting is the one), *unavoidable* for the idiom 不得已 (bù dé yǐ — it cannot be helped) at chapters 30 and 31, *touch* at chapter 14, and *find* at chapter 62, the last two kept by Shalom's call (2026-11-19).

### The graph: a step, and a hand letting go

> 得，行有所得也。从彳，㝵聲。
> *"得: to go and get something. From 彳 (chì — a step), with 㝵 (dé) for the sound."* — Shuowen (說文, *Shuōwén* — Xu Shen's dictionary, c. 100 CE)

> 失，縱也。从手，乙聲。
> *"失: to let go. From 手 (shǒu — a hand), with 乙 (yǐ) for the sound."* — Shuowen

**得 is getting by going: a step toward a thing, and the thing in hand.** Its sound part 㝵 is the older graph of the word itself, 貝 (*bèi* — a cowry) and 又 (*yòu* — a hand): picking up a shell, the money of the day. The step was added later, *picked up on the road*. **失 is a hand opening.** The Shuowen's 縱 is *to release*, the hand that lets something slip out. One character closes the hand, the other opens it, and the book never lets you have the first without the second.

**And 得 is 德 (dé — integrity)'s twin in sound**, which the old dictionaries pun on: 德者，得也, *"integrity is what you got"* (`glossary/de-德.md`). The book's integrity is the getting that is not a grabbing.

### Thirty lines, and what the book does with having

> *Getting it is a shock.*
> *Losing it is a shock.*
>
> 得之若驚，
> 失之若驚，
>
> — chapter 13

**Chapter 13 sets the pair side by side, and gives them one reaction.** Getting favor and losing it are the same shock (Heshang Gong: 得寵榮驚者，處高位如臨深危也, *"one shocked by getting favor stands in a high place as if over a deep drop"*). Chapter 44 asks which is the sickness, 得與亡孰病 (*dé yǔ wáng shú bìng* — "getting or losing, which is the sickness?"), and Wang Bi answers for the reader: 得多利而亡其身，何者為病也, *"getting much profit and losing your body: which is the sickness?"* Chapter 23 makes the pair a law of company, the Tao gladly *gets* those who are the same as the Tao, and loss gladly gets those who are the same as loss.

> *Sky received the one, and so became clear.*
>
> 天得一以清；
>
> — chapter 39

**Chapter 39 is the hardest line, and Wang Bi reads both halves of the pair into it.** 物皆各得此一以成，既成而舍以居成，居成則失其母，故皆裂發歇竭滅蹶也, *"each thing gets this one and is completed; once completed, it lets the one go to settle in its completion, and settled there it loses its mother, and so all split, burst, cease, run dry, die out and topple."* The one is gotten, then let go, then lost. That is why the English takes *received* there: the line is the first step of Wang Bi's three, and *got* in a list of sky, earth and spirit sounds like grabbing, which is what the one is not. The flexion is scoped to chapter 39, and it was the chapter's English from its rebuilding (`WORKLIST-CLOSED.md` D39).

### Chapter 62: seek, and you find, kept with its echo

> *Did they not say: seek, and you find;*
> *commit a crime, and you are spared?*
>
> 不曰：求以得，
> 有罪以免邪？
>
> — chapter 62

***Seek, and you find* is Matthew 7:7**, *"seek, and ye shall find,"* word for word. It fails the hymn test on the strongest possible ground, and it changes the saying: Matthew's seeker finds God's answer; the ancients' seeker simply gets what they seek. Wang Bi: 以求則得求，以免則得免, *"seek, and you get what you seek; seek to be spared, and you are spared."* *Seek, and you get* was proposed for this reason. **The line keeps *find*** (Shalom's call, 2026-11-19), and the echo of Matthew is recorded here as a known cost, so that no later pass takes it for an oversight. Wang Bi's plainer getting stays in this entry for a reader who wants it.

### Getting as a modal, and the idiom

**Before a verb, 得 is *can*, *get to*.** 搏之不得 (*bó zhī bù dé* — "reach for it, and you cannot get it," 14) reads *touch*, and keeps it: Heshang Gong frames chapter 14's triad as colour, sound and form, 無色 · 無聲 · 無形, so the three lines are sight, hearing and touch. Chinese has 見 (*jiàn* — to see) and 聞 (*wén* — to hear) for the first two and uses the generic 得 for the third; English has *touch*. *Grasp* was the line until 2026-09-08 and is ruled out: it is 執 (*zhí*)'s locked English, and 執古之道 (*"Grasp the ancient Tao"*) stands thirteen lines below, so the English would tell the reader they cannot grasp it and then to grasp it. The Chinese uses two characters for two acts (`chapters/014.md`). Then 不可得而親 and its five siblings at chapter 56, where 可 (*kě* — can)'s *cannot* carries the line; 吾得執而殺之 (*wú dé zhí ér shā zhī* — "I could seize them and kill them," 74). The English modal absorbs it, as English does.

**不得其死 (*bù dé qí sǐ* — "not get their death") at chapter 42 is an idiom**, dying before one's span. Heshang Gong: 不得以壽命死也, *"they do not get to die of their allotted years."* *Do not die a natural death* renders it.

**不得已 has two parses, and the English must keep them apart.** At chapters 30 and 31 it is the idiom *it cannot be helped*: force used only when there is no other way. At chapter 29, 吾見其不得已 (*wú jiàn qí bù dé yǐ*), Heshang Gong reads 不得 plus a closing 已: 我見其不得天道人心已明矣, *"I see they do not get the Tao of nature or the people's hearts; that is clear."* So chapter 29 is a getting, *"I see they will not get it"* (Shalom's call, 2026-11-19), and the flexion *unavoidable* is scoped to 30 and 31 only.

**難得之貨 is 貨 (huò — goods)'s fixed phrase**, *rare goods* (3, 12, 64), and 得 is absorbed in it (`glossary/huo-貨.md`).

### 失: one hand, and it opens

失 is *lose* in every line but one: 23's *loss* (a noun of it), 26's *lightness loses the root*, 29 and 64's *those who grasp it lose it*, 33's *does not lose their place*, 38's four losses down the ladder. Wang Bi on chapter 23 says what losing is: 失，累多也, *"to lose is to carry too much."* The hand that grasps too much is the hand that lets go.

**The exception is chapter 73's 疏而不失** (*shū ér bù shī* — "wide-meshed, yet it loses nothing"), which reads *nothing slips through*. The idiom is good English and it performs the opening hand. It also drops the verb the book uses for the grasper's loss at 29 and 64, and so hides that nature's net, unlike the grasper, loses nothing. Heshang Gong: 無有所失, *"nothing is lost."*

### What is set aside

**"Attain," "attainment."** The achievement word, and in Buddhist English the word for realization; 無所得 (*wú suǒ dé* — "nothing to attain") is the Heart Sutra's. It makes 得 a spiritual summit, and the book's getting is plain.

**"Obtain."** Latin, and the register of a requisition form.

**"Gain."** A ledger word: profit, the increase on an investment. At chapter 44 it makes the question one of accounts, which is Wang Bi's *getting much profit* but not the line's bare *getting*. It cannot be forbidden yet, since chapters 22 and 44 print it.

**"Acquire."** Acquisition is buying and collecting; chapter 46's 欲得 (*yù dé* — wanting to get) is plainer. With `glossary/yu-欲.md`, the line becomes *wanting to get* (`WORKLIST.md` T2-86).

**"Find," elsewhere.** Kept at chapter 62 by Shalom's call, with its Matthew echo. At chapter 52, 既得其母 (*jì dé qí mǔ* — "once you get the mother"), it is natural English and harmless, and it is held open below.

**"Forfeit."** For 失: a penalty, which the book's losing never is. Losing is what a grasping hand does to itself.

### What no rule can enforce

***Gain* and *acquire* are ruled out for 得, and cannot be forbidden yet**, because chapters 44 and 46 print them now. *Find* is a flexion at 62 and held open at 52, so it can never go on the list.

***Loss* at chapter 44 renders 亡 (*wáng* — to be gone), not 失.** Chapter 44 holds no 失, so no rule sees two characters sharing one English. 亡 is gone, perished, as at chapter 33's 死而不亡 (*"dies but does not vanish"*), and it wants its own word when chapter 44 is next open.

### Left open

**Three lines would change, and each is Shalom's call.** 44 (*Gain or loss* → *Getting or losing*, with 亡 as above), 46 (with T2-86, *wanting to get*), 73 (*nothing slips through* → *it loses nothing*). Chapter 62 keeps *find*.

**Chapter 52's *find*.** *Find our mother, and you know her children* is good English, and Wang Bi's 得本以知末 (*"get the root to know the branches"*) is a getting. *Find* suggests she was lost, which chapter 52 does not say; *get* sounds like taking possession of a mother. Left as it stands until the line is read again.

**Chapter 39 waits on 靈.** Its seven 得 keep *received*, and the chapter is to be rewritten once, with `glossary/shen-ling-神靈.md`'s *potent* and `glossary/gui-jian-貴賤.md`'s pricing together.
