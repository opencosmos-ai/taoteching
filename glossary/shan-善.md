## 善 (Shàn) — Masterful

*Say "shahn," fourth tone, falling. Forty-five lines in eighteen chapters — one of the largest words in the book, and the one that decides whether Laozi reads as a moralist.*

**Locked: 善 → "masterful," or "masterful at," wherever it names skill. "Good" only where the text is naming *good* as a category — which it does in order to criticize it.**

Render 善 as *good* throughout, as the English tradition does, and you produce a Laozi who divides people into the good and the bad, prizes goodness, and counsels being nice to the wicked. Almost none of that is in the Chinese. **Roughly thirty of the forty-five occurrences mean skilled at something**, and where the word *is* used as a moral category, the book's move is to warn you against the category.

### The character is a sheep, and it has two siblings

The *Shuowen Jiezi* gives the old form 譱 and glosses it: 吉也。从誩，从羊。**此與義、美同意** — *"auspicious; from 誩 and 羊; this has the same sense as 義 and 美."*

- **羊** (*yáng*) — a sheep, seen head-on: the horns, the face. The sacrificial animal.
- **誩** (*jìng*) — two 言 (*yán* — speech): two people speaking in turn, weighing a matter.

And the Shuowen's own cross-reference is the finding: **善 belongs to a family of three, and all three wear the sheep.**

| | Built from | Became |
|---|---|---|
| **美** (*měi*) | 羊 over 大 (a big person) — a large sheep | **beauty** |
| **義** (*yì*) | 羊 over 我 (a weapon, then *I*) | **duty, right conduct** |
| **善** (*shàn*) | 羊 over speech | **good** |

Its oldest sense survives in the character 膳 (*shàn* — the prepared dish, the meat offering), which is 善 with the flesh radical added back. **So 善 begins as *fit for the altar*: well-made, sound, up to standard, good of its kind.** Not morally good — **good at being what it is.**

That is precisely the sense Laozi uses, over and over.

### What the text actually does with it

**Thirty occurrences are 善 + a verb — skilled at doing something**, and they are the book's most concrete lines:

> Ch 27 · 善行無轍跡，善言無瑕謫，善數不用籌策，善閉無關楗而不可開，善結無繩約而不可解
> *masterful travelling leaves no tracks · masterful speech is flawless · masterful reckoning uses no counting rods · masterful shutting uses no bolts, yet cannot be opened · masterful tying uses no rope, yet cannot be untied*

A traveller, a speaker, an accountant, a locksmith, a knot-tier. **Five trades in five lines.** **Chapter 8 is the showcase: nine occurrences in thirteen lines**, more concentrated than anywhere else in the book — 上善, 善利, then the seven couplets 善地, 善淵, 善仁, 善信, 善治, 善能, 善時: masterful at ground, at depth, at giving, at speech, at governing, at what can be done, at timing. Chapter 68 gives four: the masterful officer is not martial, the masterful fighter does not rage, the masterful conqueror does not engage, the masterful user of people puts themselves below. Chapter 73 gives three more of the Tao itself: 不爭而**善**勝，不言而**善**應 — *does not contend yet masterfully prevails; does not speak yet masterfully answers.*

**None of that is ethics. All of it is craft.**

### Where 善 *is* moral, the book is attacking the category

Six occurrences use 善 as an abstract quality — and in the most important of them, Laozi is not recommending it. He is pointing at what happens when it becomes a name.

> Ch 2 · 天下皆知**美**之為美，斯惡已；皆知**善**之為善，斯不善已。
> *"When all the world knows beauty as beauty, ugliness arises; when all know good as good, the not-good arises."*

**He puts the two sheep-words side by side** — 美 and 善, beauty and good, the pair the *Shuowen* says share a sense — and shows both curdling at the moment they are made into categories. The not-good is not a pre-existing evil the good opposes. It is **manufactured by the act of naming the good.**

Chapter 58 finishes the thought: 正復為奇，**善**復為妖 — *the straight turns crooked, the good turns monstrous.* And Chapter 20 sets 善 against 惡 (*è* — bad) only to ask 相去若何, *what is the distance between them?*

So the book's two uses of 善 are not in tension. **They are one argument: be good at things, and be wary of Goodness.**

### Why this decides three chapters

Chapters 27, 49 and 62 make one claim in three vocabularies — nobody gets thrown away (see the Thread in `notes/reading.md`) — and **the rendering of 善 decides what kind of claim it is.**

Held as *good* and *bad*, Chapter 62 becomes a doctrine of grace: the Tao shelters sinners, and 罪 (*zuì* — **crime**) three lines later completes the gospel. Held as **masterful** and **unmasterful**, it becomes an argument about **use**:

> Ch 27 · 故善人者，不善人之師；不善人者，善人之**資**。
> *"So the masterful are the teacher of the unmasterful, and the unmasterful are the masterful's **資** (zī — raw material, working stock)."*

That is not mercy. It is a claim that **nobody is discarded because nobody is waste** — which is why the chapter puts it beside a locksmith and a knot-tier, and why 不貴其師，不愛其資 (*failing to value the teacher or love the raw material*) is called 大迷, great confusion.

### What is set aside

**"Virtuous," "righteous," "saintly."** Each turns a sheep fit for the altar into a person fit for heaven. 義 (*yì* — duty) is a different character and gets its own treatment; **benevolent** belongs to 仁 (*rén* — humaneness), which Chapter 8's 與善仁 uses in the same line as 善 and must stay distinguishable from it.

**"Good" used everywhere.** Not forbidden — it is right in Chapters 2, 20 and 58, where the category is the subject. But applied to 善行 or 善結 it flattens a locksmith into a saint.

**"Skilful" and "expert."** Accurate and bloodless. *Masterful* holds the register the trades give it, and it is what makes 善人 / 不善人 read as **the adept and the inept** rather than the righteous and the wicked.

### Working register

| The grammar | Where | Render |
|---|---|---|
| 善 + verb — skilled at | 8, 15, 27, 30, 41, 50, 54, 65, 66, 68, 73 | **masterful at** |
| 善人 / 不善人 — the kind of person | 27, 49, 62 | **the masterful / the unmasterful** |
| 善 as the named category | 2, 20, 58 | **good** — because the category is the target |
| 上善 — the superlative | 8 | **the most masterful** (settled 2026-09-02, below) |
| 善 as a verb, "to reckon as 善" | 49 | **count as masterful** |

### Chapter 49 — decided, and it follows Wang Bi

*(settled 2026-08-14.)* 善者，吾善之；不善者，吾亦善之 uses 善 four times in two lines — twice for the kind of person, twice as a **verb**, *to reckon as 善*. It now reads **"The masterful, I count as masterful; the unmasterful, I count as masterful too."**

**Wang Bi reads the chapter in Chapter 27's own vocabulary**, which is what decided it. His gloss on the couplet is 各**因**其**用**，則善不失也 — *"go along with each according to their **use**, and then 善 is not lost."* His gloss on the tag 德善 is three characters — **無棄人也**, *"there are no discarded people"* — which is Chapter 27's own line, 故無棄人, quoted here. And he closes the chapter with 能者與之，**資**者取之, *"those with ability, go along with; those who are **raw material**, take them up"* — 資 (*zī*) being the exact character Chapter 27 gives the unmasterful.

**Heshang Gong reads it morally** and it stays on the record: 百姓雖有不善者，聖人化之使善也 — *"though some of the people are not good, the sage transforms them and makes them good."*

**The English cost, named.** *Count as masterful* is heavier in the mouth than *am good to*. What it buys is the actual claim: 善之 is **appraisal**, not kindness. The sage is not being nice to the incompetent — they are **refusing to run the grading**, which is the same move as 何棄之有 (62) and 無棄人 (27), and a sharper thing to say.

**What it commits.** Ch 79 (常與善人) and Ch 81 (善者不辯，辯者不善) are undrafted and now inherit this: *"the masterful do not argue; arguers are not masterful."* Stranger than *"the good do not argue,"* and consistent with the rest.

### Chapter 8 — decided 2026-09-02, and it settles the grammar for the whole entry

**上善若水 now reads "The most masterful are like water."** *(Shalom's call.)* 上 (*shàng* — highest) is settled at ch 38, where 上德 / 上仁 / 上義 / 上禮 all read *the highest*, so *"supreme"* was the outlier. **The category reading is unavailable here**: *good* is reserved for chapters 2, 20 and 58, where the book attacks the category, and using it at ch 8 would make this chapter endorse what ch 2 dismantles. 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) reads 上善 as a **person** — 上善之人如水之性, *"the person of the highest 善 is like the nature of water"* — which is this entry's own 善人 (*shàn rén* — the masterful). **The cost:** the plural person decides something the Chinese leaves open, since 上善 could be the quality itself. ***The highest excellence*** was rejected as bloodless and as Legge's phrase; ***true mastery*** was rejected because 真 (*zhēn* — genuine) is a live character in this book (21, 41, 54) and 上 is a graded rank word whose middle rung is printed at ch 41 (上士/中士/下士).

**善 governs a verb — eighteen times out of eighteen, and this is the proof the entry has been asserting without evidence.** The *Working register* table above claims a "善 + verb" grammar. Ch 8 is the only place in the book where the claim could fail, because its seven couplets are the only 善 + X where X could pass for a noun, and X善Y admits both parses in classical Chinese: 善 as a verb taking Y (*masterful **at** Y*), or 善 as an adjective modifying Y (*masterful Y*).

Everywhere else, X is a verb:

> 善行 *travel* · 善言 *speak* · 善計 *reckon* · 善閉 *shut* · 善結 *tie* (27) · 善救 *rescue* ×2 (27) · 善為 *handle* (15, 65) · 善貸 *give* (41) · 善攝 *nurture* (50) · 善建 *establish* · 善抱 *embrace* (54) · 善下 *go below* (66) · 善戰 *fight* · 善勝 *overcome* ×2 (68, 73) · 善用 *use* (68) · 善應 *respond* · 善謀 *plan* (73)

**Eighteen verbs, zero nouns.** And ch 8 sets the pattern inside its own chapter, four lines above the stanza: 水善**利**萬物, where 利 (*lì* — to benefit) is unambiguously verbal. The adjectival parse fails on its own terms as well — it yields *masterful order*, *masterful ability*, *masterful timing*, which read, and *masterful ground*, *masterful depth*, *masterful humaneness*, *masterful trust*, which do not. **A reading that survives three of seven loses to one that survives all seven.**

The seven now run in one shape: *In settling, masterful at ground. In the heart, masterful at depth. In giving, masterful at humaneness. In speech, masterful at trust. In governing, masterful at order. In affairs, masterful at what can be done. In moving, masterful at timing.*

**能 (*néng* — able) took *what can be done* rather than *ability***, because 能 has 32 lines in the book and 31 are the plain modal *can*; ch 8's is the only noun. Building the English out of *can* keeps it in the book's own vocabulary and removes the abstract-noun stack beside *masterful*. The cost is a slight drift of the capacity from the agent to the situation.

### What stays open


**淵 (*yuān* — a deep pool), inside ch 8's stanza, is not yet settled** and reads three ways across the book: *fathomless* (4), *depth* (8), *the deep abyss* (36). 說文解字 makes it banked water — 回水也…左右，岸也, *"swirling water… left and right are the banks"* — so *limitless*, which stood at ch 8 until 2026-09-02, was precisely backwards.
