## 殆 (Dài) — Danger

*Say "dye," falling. Five lines across five chapters, and in every one of them it is denied: no danger.*

**Locked: 殆 → "danger," in all five lines.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-11-20; the verse has read* danger *since 2026-08-31.)* *(Not "depletion" or "inexhaustible," which put an energy store in a character built on a bone. Not "harm," which is 害 (hài)'s. Not "peril," the dictionary's own word, kept out so that one English holds one character.)*

### The graph: a splintered bone

> 殆，危也。从歺，台聲。
> *"殆 is danger. From 歺 (è — splintered bone), with 台 (tái) for sound."*

**歺 is the bone left when a body has broken apart**, the part that stands for death and decay in 死 (*sǐ* — death) and 殃 (*yāng* — calamity). 殆 is danger with the bone in it: the kind of trouble a body does not come back from. **Where the picture stops:** the bone names the stakes, not the source. Nothing in the graph says what the danger is, and the book never says either. It only says when it does not come.

### Five lines, and the danger never arrives

> *And to the end of your days, no danger will find you.*
>
> 没身不殆。
>
> — chapter 16

> *It moves in a circle and is never in danger,*
>
> 周行而不殆，
>
> — chapter 25

> *Knowing when to stop, you come to no danger.*
>
> 知止可以不殆。
>
> — chapter 32

> *Know when to stop, and you come to no danger.*
>
> 知止不殆，
>
> — chapter 44

> *Know her children, return and hold fast to our mother,*
> *and to the end of your days, no danger will find you.*
>
> 既知其子，復守其母，
> 没身不殆。
>
> — chapter 52

**Every 殆 in the book stands after 不 (*bù* — not).** Danger is what the right way of living escapes: to the end of a life (16, 52), by knowing when to stop (32, 44), and, once, for the Tao itself, which goes round and is never in danger (25). The book has a word for the threat and uses it only to say the threat is answered.

**The repeats read alike.** 没身不殆 (*mò shēn bù dài*) closes 16 and 52 in the same words, and 知止不殆 (*zhī zhǐ bù dài*) stands at 32 and 44 (32 adds 可以, which our base alone prints: `notes/manuscript.md` → *Ch 32*). Each pair carries one English.

### The commentators agree, and they say peril

**Both commentators gloss 殆 with 危 (*wēi* — danger), the Shuowen's own word for it.** Heshang Gong at 16: 乃與天地俱没，不危殆也 (*"then one sinks away together with sky and earth, not in peril"*); at 44: 則身不危殆也 (*"then the body is not in peril"*); at 52 the whole gloss is 不危殆也. Wang Bi at 25: 周行無所不至而免殆 (*"moving in a circle, it reaches everywhere and escapes danger"*), where the Yongle Dadian prints 危 for 免. **No one reads exhaustion into it.** Five lines, two commentators, one sense, which is rare in this book.

### What is set aside

**"Depletion," "exhausted," "inexhaustible."** The energy reading: a store that does or does not run out. It fails the graph, a bone, not a reservoir, and it is this edition's besetting temptation, the mechanistic. *Inexhaustible* is also 窮 (*qióng* — to run out)'s word, at chapters 6 and 35.

**"Harm."** 害 (*hài* — harm)'s word, at chapters 35, 56, 66, 73 and 81. Two characters wearing one English would merge in the reader.

**"Peril," "perish."** *Peril* is the Shuowen's 危 and the commentators', and it is not wrong. It is left out so that *danger* stays one character's word, and because *peril* drifts toward the ornate. *Perish* puts the death of the bone into a line that only denies it.

**"Threaten," "threatened."** Puts an agent behind the danger, a someone who threatens, and the book names none.

### Working register

| line | form | English |
|---|---|---|
| 没身不殆 (16, 52) | 不殆 after a span | *no danger will find you* |
| 周行而不殆 (25) | 不殆 of the Tao | *never in danger* |
| 知止可以不殆 (32) · 知止不殆 (44) | 不殆 as a result | *you come to no danger* |

**The noun carries all five.** The English varies its verb (*find*, *come to*, *be in*) because 不殆 takes no verb of its own; *danger* stays fixed.

### What no rule can enforce

**"Harm" is ruled out and left off the list.** 害 (*hài*) never shares a chapter with 殆, so the gate could hold it, but *harm* on this list would raise a notice on every one of 害's own lines and bury the notices worth reading (`WORKLIST.md` T5-5). `concordance.py --english "harm"` is the check.

**"Danger" in a chapter without 殆.** The gate cannot see it. Ch 15 once read *"aware of danger from all sides"* with no 殆 in it; `concordance.py --english "danger"` is the check, and it now finds five lines, all backed.

### Left open

**Chapter 16's subject.** 没身不殆 closes a ladder that climbs from impartiality to the Tao, and Heshang Gong makes the one who climbed it the one who is not in danger. The English *"no danger will find you"* follows him, in the second person, which the Chinese does not print. Chapter 52 is addressed to a reader, so there the *you* is the text's own; at 16 it is borrowed from 52.

**Whether the bone is heard.** 殆 shares its bone with 死 (*sǐ* — death) (`glossary/si-死.md`), and twice it closes a line that begins with 没身 (*mò shēn* — to the end of one's days). *Danger* is plain and right, and no English for danger keeps the bone.
