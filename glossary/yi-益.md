## 益 (Yì) — Increase, Abundance

*Say "ee," falling. Five lines across four chapters. Water standing above the rim of a vessel: more than it holds.*

**Locked: 益 → "increase" where it is a process beside 損 (sǔn — decrease) (42, 48, 55), and "abundance" at chapter 43, the overflow itself.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-11-20.)* *(Not "benefit," which is 利 (lì)'s. Not "profit" or "advantage," which make the overflow a gain over someone. Not "gain," which is 得 (dé)'s.)*

### The graph: water above a vessel's rim

> 益，饒也。从水、皿。皿，益之意也。
> *"益 is plenty. From 水 (shuǐ — water) and 皿 (mǐn — a vessel). The vessel is what gives the sense."*

**The graph sets water above a bowl, more than it holds, running over the rim.** The overflow was later written 溢 (*yì* — to overflow), with water added again at the side, and 益 kept the sense of more. **The Shuowen's gloss, 饒 (*ráo* — plenty), is the same it gives 餘 (*yú* — more than enough)** (`glossary/yu-餘.md`): the two characters are plenty seen from two sides, food left on the table and water over the rim. **Where the picture stops:** the bowl never says whether the overflow is good. The book says it twice each way.

### Five lines, and increase is a double-edged law

> *So it is with things: some are decreased and so increased, some increased and so decreased.*
>
> 故物或損之而益，或益之而損。
>
> — chapter 42

> *In pursuit of learning, you increase daily;*
> *in pursuit of the Tao, you decrease daily.*
>
> 為學日益，
> 為道日損。
>
> — chapter 48

> *Increasing life is called an ill omen.*
>
> 益生曰祥。
>
> — chapter 55

**Beside 損, 益 is the half of a law that cuts both ways.** Chapter 42 states it: decreased and so increased, increased and so decreased. Heshang Gong gives the second half its picture: 夫增高者崩，貪富者致患 (*"build high and it falls; grasp at wealth and trouble comes"*). Chapter 48 puts learning on the increasing side and the Tao on the decreasing one, *"decrease, and decrease again, until you reach non-doing."* Chapter 55 forbids it outright for life: Wang Bi, 生不可益，益之則夭也 (*"life cannot be increased; increase it and it dies young"*). **In these lines *increase* keeps the pair with *decrease*, and the law keeps its two edges.**

### Chapter 43: the abundance of non-doing

> *The softest thing in the world gallops over the hardest thing in the world.*
> *What is not there enters the seamless.*
> *Therefore I know the abundance of non-doing.*
>
> 天下之至柔，馳騁天下之至堅。
> 無有入無間，
> 吾是以知無為之有益。
>
> — chapter 43

> *The unspoken teaching, the abundance of non-doing —*
> *few ever reach this.*
>
> 不言之教，無為之益，
> 天下希及之。
>
> — chapter 43

**Here 益 is a noun with no 損 beside it, and the vessel is running over** (Shalom's call, 2026-11-20; it had read *benefit*). Chapter 43 follows chapter 42 directly, and takes up its last word: what is decreased to nothing, non-doing, overflows. Wang Bi says how: 虛無柔弱，無所不通 (*"empty, soft and yielding, there is nothing it does not pass through"*). Heshang Gong says for whom: 吾見道無為而萬物自化成，是以知無為之有益於人也 (*"I see the Tao does not act and the countless things transform of themselves; so I know non-doing is beneficial to people"*). **An overflow goes out to others, and *abundance* carries both**: the vessel's plenty, and what spills from it to the people around. *Benefit* would have said Heshang Gong's half alone, in 利's word.

### What is set aside

**"Benefit."** 利 (*lì*)'s word in ten lines (`glossary/li-利.md`). It is Heshang Gong's reading at 43, and it drops the vessel.

**"Profit," "advantage."** The overflow made into a gain over someone. Both are ruled out for 利 too.

**"Gain."** 得 (*dé* — get)'s, at chapters 22, 39 and 44.

**"Add," "addition."** Arithmetic, where the graph is a bowl. *Increase* keeps the growing, and *abundance* the overflow.

**"Excess," "overflow" as the English.** *Excess* is a verdict (`glossary/yu-餘.md`). *Overflow* is the graph's picture and not the word: the book's 益 is the plenty, not the spilling.

### Working register

| line | form | English |
|---|---|---|
| 損之而益 · 益之而損 (42) | beside 損 | *increased* · *increased* |
| 為學日益 (48) | beside 日損 | *you increase daily* |
| 益生 (55) | verb + object | *increasing life* |
| 無為之有益 · 無為之益 (43) | noun, alone | *the abundance of non-doing* (flexion) |

### What no rule can enforce

**"Abundance" is shared with 餘's *abundant* at chapter 54**, which Shalom kept as 餘's flexion. The Shuowen glosses both with 饒, so the share is honest, and no tool can see it.

**"Benefit" is ruled out and left off the list.** Forbidding it here would raise a notice on 利's ten lines. `concordance.py --english "benefit"` should find 利's lines only.

### Left open

**損 (sǔn — decrease) has no entry.** It reads *decrease* beside 益 at 42 and 48, and *takes from* at chapter 77 (損有餘, *"takes from where there is more than enough"*), where it is the way of nature paring a surplus. Whether the pair 損 / 益 wants one entry, a lexical pair in the glossary's sense, is open.

**The 42–43 seam.** The received text puts 益 at the end of one chapter and the start of the next. *Increased* then *abundance* lets the reader hear a link, but not that it is one word.
