## 令 (Lìng) — Command

*Say "ling," falling. Eight lines across four chapters. The order given, and, more often, the plain causative: this makes people do that.*

**Locked: 令 → "command" where it is an order (32, 57), and "make" where it is the causative, 令 + a person + a verb (12, 19).** *(Shalom's calls, 2026-11-20.)* **命 (*mìng*), its twin, shares *command* at chapter 51** (`glossary/ming-命.md`). *(Not "decree" or "edict," which bring a throne room. Not "ordain," which brings a priest.)*

### The graph: a summons, and someone kneeling to receive it

> 令，發號也。从亼、卪。
> *"令 is to issue a call. From 亼 (jí — the gathering sign) and 卪 (jié — a kneeling figure)."*

**Above, a sign of gathering, read as a mouth turned down or a bell rung to call people in; below, a person on their knees.** The scene is an order given and received. **In the oracle bones 令 and 命 are one graph**; the Western Zhou bronzes add 口 (*kǒu* — a mouth) to make 命, and by the seal script they are two words (`glossary/ming-命.md` → *the graph*). The Shuowen still defines 命 through 令: 命，使也。从口从令 (*"命 is to send; from a mouth and 令"*). **Where the picture stops:** the book's commands are mostly absent. *No one commands* the people (32), and the commands that multiply make thieves (57).

### Three lines, and the command is what the book does without

> *No one commands the people, and they even out of themselves.*
>
> 民莫之令而自均。
>
> — chapter 32

> *This honoring of the Tao, this prizing of integrity — no one commands it.*
> *It is always so, of itself.*
>
> 夫莫之命而常自然。
>
> — chapter 51

> *The more laws and commands are proclaimed, the more thieves there are.*
>
> 法令滋彰，盜賊多有。
>
> — chapter 57

**The frame 莫之X而 (*mò zhī X ér* — "no one Xs it, and…") stands at 32 with 令 and at 51 with 命, and both read *commands*** (Shalom's call, 2026-11-20). The two were one graph, and the commentators read one act. Wang Bi at 32: 我守其真性無為，則民不令而自均也 (*"I keep my true nature and do not act, and the people even out with no one commanding"*), and of the streams in the next lines, 非江海召之 (*"it is not that the river and sea summon them"*). Heshang Gong at 51: 道一不命召萬物，而常自然應之如影響 (*"the Tao does not command or summon the countless things, yet they always respond of themselves, like shadow and echo"*). **The command the book names is the one that is not given.**

**At chapter 57 it is given, and fails.** 法令 (*fǎ lìng*) is law and command together, and *laws and commands* keeps both (Shalom's call, 2026-11-20; it had read *laws*). Heshang Gong, one line before: 令煩則姦生 (*"when commands are many, treachery arises"*). His own text reads 法物 (*fǎ wù* — fine things) here, which makes the line about luxury; we keep the base (fork 129, `notes/manuscript.md` → *Ch 57*).

### The causative: this makes people do that

> *The five colors make people's eyes blind.*
> *The five tones make people's ears deaf.*
> *The five flavors make people's palates dull.*
>
> 五色令人目盲；
> 五音令人耳聾；
> 五味令人口爽；
>
> — chapter 12

**Most of the book's 令 is grammar: 令 + a person + what happens to them**, *make people blind*. Chapter 12 runs it five times, and Wang Bi gives the mechanism: 耳目口心皆順其性也，不以順性命，反以傷自然 (*"ears, eyes, mouth and heart all follow their nature; not used to follow it, they turn to harm what is so of itself"*). Chapter 19 uses it to hand something over: 故令有所屬, *"So give them something to belong to,"* where 令 is *let them have*. **The command is still in the causative**, an order made into a cause, and the English keeps it plain: *make*, and at 19 *give them*.

### What is set aside

**"Decree," "edict."** A throne room's words, and the overlay the book's 32 and 51 most refuse: a ruler or a heaven issuing what must be. *Decree* is also forbidden for 命.

**"Ordain."** A priest's word, and it fails the hymn test outright.

**"Order."** Not wrong for the noun, but *order* is 治 (*zhì* — govern)'s flexion at chapters 8 and 64 (`glossary/zhi-治.md`), and *in order* would blur the two.

**"Cause," "cause to."** The causative's meaning, and stiff: *the five colors cause people's eyes to go blind* says the grammar, where *make* says the line.

### Working register

| line | form | English |
|---|---|---|
| 民莫之令 (32) | verb, in 莫之X而 | *commands* |
| 法令 (57) | noun, with 法 | *laws and commands* |
| 令人目盲 · 耳聾 · 口爽 · 心發狂 (12) | 令 + person + verb | *make people's…* |
| 令人行妨 (12) | 令 + person + verb | *hinder people's conduct*, the causative inside the verb |
| 故令有所屬 (19) | 令 + (them) + verb | *give them something to belong to* |

### What no rule can enforce

**The causative leaves no English word to check.** *Make* is too common to lock, and at 12's last line and at 19 the causative is carried by the verb (*hinder*, *give*). The reader holds it.

**命 shares *command*** at chapter 51. `concordance.py --english "command"` credits one entry; read its *not backed* line as the other's.

### Left open

**The excavated witnesses at chapter 57.** Heshang Gong reads 法物, fine things. Whether the silks or the Guodian slips read 令 or 物 at this line is not yet read here, and a reading of 物 would reopen fork 129.

**Chapter 12's last line.** *"Rare goods hinder people's conduct"* folds 令 into *hinder*, where the four lines before keep *make*. *"Rare goods make people's conduct hindered"* would keep the series and lose the English.
