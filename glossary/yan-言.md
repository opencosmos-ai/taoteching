## 言 (Yán) — Words, and Speaking

*Say "yen," rising. Twenty-one appearances in nineteen lines across eighteen chapters. A book that distrusts speech, written in it, and it knows.*

**Recommended: 言 → "words" for what is said · "speech" for the saying of it · "speak" as a verb.** *(Not "discourse," "rhetoric" or "eloquence," which are 語 (yǔ — debate)'s ground and the Shuowen's opposite of 言. Not "the Word," whose capital is John's Logos.)* **Two flexions:** *saying* where a chapter quotes a proverb (41, 69), and *say* where 言 opens an explanation (31).

### The graph: a mouth, speaking straight out

> 言，直言曰言，論難曰語。从口，䇂聲。
> *"言: speaking straight out is 言; debating is 語 (yǔ — argument). From 口 (kǒu — a mouth), with 䇂 for the sound."* — Shuowen (說文, *Shuōwén* — Xu Shen's dictionary, c. 100 CE)

**The Shuowen defines 言 by what it is not.** 言 is plain speech, said straight; 語 is speech turned against someone, the give and take of argument. Laozi never uses 語. Every speech word in the book is the plain one, and the book's quarrel is not with argument but with the plain act of talking: 知者不言 (*zhī zhě bù yán* — "those who know do not speak").

### Nineteen lines: what is said, and the saying of it

**As a noun, 言 is words, the things said.**

> *Trustworthy words are not pretty.*
> *Pretty words are not trustworthy.*
>
> 信言不美，
> 美言不信。
>
> — chapter 81

Also 22's *empty words*, 62's *fine words can buy honor*, 70's *my words* and *words have an ancestor*, 78's *words on the mark seem like their opposite*.

**As a verb, 言 is to speak.**

> *Those who know do not speak.*
> *Those who speak do not know.*
>
> 知者不言，
> 言者不知。
>
> — chapter 56

Also 73's *it does not speak, yet is masterful at answering*, and 66's *you must speak from below them*.

**As the act, a verbal noun, 言 is speech**: 8's *in speech, masterful at trust*, 23's *brevity in speech*, 27's *masterful speaking*, 5's 多言 (*duō yán* — much speech).

**The book's speech is never praised for being beautiful or clever.** It is praised for being trustworthy (8, 81), on the mark (78), few (23), or absent (2, 43, 56, 73). The sky is the model: Heshang Gong on chapter 73, 天不言，萬物自動應以時, *"the sky does not speak, and the countless things move of themselves, answering in their season."*

### The teaching without words, twice, and it must read the same

> *and carries out the teaching without words.*
>
> 行不言之教。
>
> — chapter 2

> *The wordless teaching, the benefit of non-doing —*
>
> 不言之教，無為之益，
>
> — chapter 43

**不言之教 (*bù yán zhī jiào*) is a formula, verbatim in both chapters, and it reads two ways.** A formula repeated in the Chinese is repeated in the English. *The teaching without words* keeps 不言 what it is everywhere else in the book, *not speaking* (56, 73). The recommendation is chapter 43 → *"The teaching without words, the benefit of non-doing —"*. Wang Bi on chapter 17 names the pair together, 居無為之事，行不言之教, *"dwelling in the affairs of non-doing, carrying out the teaching without words,"* which is chapter 2's line, and chapter 43 sets them side by side again.

### Chapter 17: they prize their words, and their words are answered

> *The greatest leaders speak sparingly.*
>
> 悠兮其貴言。
>
> — chapter 17

**The line has 貴 (*guì* — to prize) and 言, and our English renders neither.** 悠兮其貴言 is *"unhurried, they prize their words."* Wang Bi says what the prizing is: 無物可以易其言，言必有應，故曰悠兮其貴言也, *"nothing can take the place of their words, and their words always meet an answer; so it says, unhurried, they prize their words."* The greatest leaders speak little because each word counts, and each is answered. *Speak sparingly* keeps the result and loses the reason, and 貴's price word with it (`glossary/gui-jian-貴賤.md`). Recommended: *"Unhurried, they prize their words."*

### What the commentators did with it

**Heshang Gong reads speech as a cost.** On chapter 56: 知者貴行不貴言也, *"the knower prizes action and does not prize words,"* and 駟不及舌，多言多患, *"a team of four horses cannot catch the tongue; many words, many troubles."* On chapter 81: 滋美之言者，孳孳華詞，不信者，飾偽多空虛也, *"pretty words are phrase upon ornate phrase; untrustworthy, they are dressed falsehood, mostly empty."*

**And he lets the book's own words off.** On chapter 70: 吾所言省而易知，約而易行, *"what I say is spare and easy to know, brief and easy to carry out."* A book against talking is allowed to talk if it talks like this.

### What is set aside

**"Utterance."** A linguist's word, and Latinate. It makes speech a specimen.

**"Discourse."** The academy's word for speech in general, and the Shuowen's 語, extended argument, which Laozi never uses.

**"Rhetoric," "eloquence," "eloquent."** The art of speaking well, which is exactly what chapter 81 distrusts in 美言 (*měi yán* — pretty words). Ch 81's 辯 (*biàn* — to argue) already reads *argue*; giving 言 an eloquence word would hand it the very quality the book refuses.

**"The Word."** John 1:1, *"In the beginning was the Word,"* the Logos. The Chinese Union Bible rendered Logos as 道, not 言, but the English capital is still the creed's, and *the Word* in a chapter about not speaking would read as theology. Forbidden with its capital, so the plain *word* stays free.

**"Silent," for 不言.** Silence is a state; 不言 is the act of not speaking, done by someone who could. Chapter 56's knower *does not speak*; chapter 73's sky *does not speak, yet answers*. It cannot be forbidden, because 希 (*xī* — sparse) and other characters share these chapters, and it is held by the entry.

**"Talk."** Casual where the book is not, and *talk* has no plain noun for the things said.

### What no rule can enforce

***Words* in chapter 22's last line renders no 言.** 誠全而歸之 (*chéng quán ér guī zhī* — "truly whole, return to it") reads *"These words hold. Return to them. Stay whole."* 誠 (*chéng* — truly) has 言 in its graph, words that are made good, and the English leans on that; but the line prints no 言, and `concordance.py --english "word"` is the only check that sees it.

***Silent* and *wordless* are held by argument.** *Wordless* can only be ruled once chapter 43 changes, since it prints it now.

### Left open

**Two lines would change, and both are Shalom's call.** Chapter 43's *the wordless teaching* → *the teaching without words*, to match chapter 2; chapter 17's *the greatest leaders speak sparingly* → *unhurried, they prize their words*, which also restores 貴 (`WORKLIST.md` T2-36) and drops *the greatest leaders*, supplied from the chapter's first line.

**Chapter 5's 多言數窮 loses 多 (*duō* — much).** *"Speaking of this is a dead end"* renders 言 and drops the *much*, which is the line's point: not speech but too much of it, against 守中 (*shǒu zhōng* — "hold to the center"). And 數 is contested: Wang Bi's sound note reads it as reckoning, a later note as *often*. The line wants its own reading.

**The book distrusts speech and is made of it.** Chapter 70 claims its own words are *very easy to know, very easy to follow*, and that no one knows or follows them. The English should not soften either half.
