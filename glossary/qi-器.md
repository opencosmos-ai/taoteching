## 器 (Qì) — Vessel

*Say "chee," falling. Twelve appearances across nine chapters. A concrete object that abstraction keeps reaching for — and the one word in this glossary that the mechanistic overlay attacks directly.*

**Locked: 器 → "vessel" / "tool" / "implement."** *(Not "system," not "mechanism," not "machine.")*

Chapter 29 read 天下神器 as *"the world is a sacred system."* That single substitution costs the chapter its argument, because the next clause is 不可執也 — *it cannot be grasped* — and then 執者失之, *those who grasp it lose it.* **You can grasp at a vessel and break it. You cannot grasp a system.** The image and the warning are one gesture, and abstraction severs them.

The danger runs the other way too. Chapter 11's whole case depends on 器 staying a **pot**: 埏埴以為器 — *knead clay to make a vessel* — and the vessel is useful only because of the hollow. Let 器 drift toward "structure" or "function" and the argument evaporates, because a structure has no inside.

### The graph: a dog guarding the crockery

器 is four 口 (*kǒu* — mouth, opening) around 犬 (*quǎn* — dog). The *Shuowen Jiezi* (~100 CE) is unusually plain about it:

> 皿也。象器之口，犬所以守之。
> *A vessel. It depicts the mouths of containers, with a dog to guard them.*

The picture is a **yard of open pots with a dog watching over them.** It is worth sitting with, because it says two things at once. 器 is a *made* thing — fired, shaped, owned. And it is a thing **worth guarding**: valuable enough that you set an animal on it.

That second half is what makes 神器 (*shén qì*) in Chapter 29 land. In the late Zhou a 神器 was concrete: the **ritual bronzes**, above all the 九鼎 (*jiǔ dǐng* — the nine cauldrons), whose possession was the visible warrant of the mandate to rule. The chapter opens on 將欲取天下 — *wishing to seize the world* — and answers that the world is a sacred vessel and cannot be grasped. To a contemporary ear that is not a metaphor. It is a sentence about reaching for the cauldrons. *(See the Reading Note at Chapter 29.)*

### The evidence — 器 is what wholeness becomes

**The fall from the uncarved.** Chapter 28: 樸散則為器 — *when the uncarved wood is broken up, it becomes 器.* This is the hinge of the whole 樸 argument. 器 is the **plural, specialized, useful** state on the far side of a loss: one log becomes many tools, each good for something and none good for everything. The word is not an insult, but it names a diminishment.

**And yet 器 is exemplary.** Chapter 11 makes a clay pot one of three proofs that emptiness is what works — 有器之用, *there is the use of the vessel* — alongside the wheel's hub and the room's windows. The vessel here is not fallen; it is the teacher.

**And weapons are 器.** Chapter 31 says it three times: 夫佳兵者，不祥之器 · 兵者不祥之器 · 非君子之器 — *fine weapons are implements of ill omen; weapons are implements of ill omen; they are not the implements of a person of quality.* Chapter 57's 人多利器，國家滋昏 (*the more sharp implements among the people, the darker the state grows*) and Chapter 36's 國之利器不可以示人 (*the sharp instruments of the state must not be displayed*) run the same way. Here "implement" or "instrument" serves better than "vessel," and the flexion is licensed.

**And a person can be one.** Chapter 41's 大器晚成 — in our base text *"the great vessel is completed late,"* and in the Mawangdui and Guodian witnesses 免成 / 曼成, *never completed*, which we follow. Classical Chinese applies 器 to people as **capacity** — the size of what someone can hold — and the idiom survives in modern Chinese as *a person of great capacity matures slowly*. Chapter 67's 故能成器長 (*and so can become the elder of the vessels*) uses the same personal sense.

### What is set aside, and why

**"System"** is the mechanistic overlay, and this project's own besetting temptation. It abstracts a fired clay pot into a set of relations, breaks Chapter 29's grasping image, and empties Chapter 11's hollow. It also fails the pointing test twice over: Laozi could point at the pot and at the dog.

**"Mechanism," "machine," "apparatus"** import moving parts and a designer. 器 has neither. A pot does nothing; that is the point of Chapter 11.

**"Utensil"** is not wrong so much as small and domestic — it makes 神器 (the world) and 不祥之器 (weapons of ill omen) faintly absurd. Keep it out of the register.

**"Organ"** is a real classical sense of 器 and is right to leave unused here: nothing in these nine chapters is anatomical.

**"Vessel" everywhere** would also be an error. Chapter 31's weapons are not vessels, and Chapter 28's carved tools are not vessels. The lock is a small family — *vessel, tool, implement* — held together by being **made, concrete, and usable**, and it flexes by what the chapter has in hand.

### Working register

- containers, and the world as one → **vessel** (有器之用 → "the use of the vessel"; 神器 → "a sacred vessel"; 大器 → "the great vessel")
- what the uncarved becomes → **tools** (樸散則為器 → "when the uncarved wood is carved, it is made into tools")
- weapons and sharp things → **implement / instrument** (不祥之器 → "implements of ill omen"; 利器 → "sharp instruments")
- of a person → **capacity** (器長 → the elder among them; 大器晚成 and its variants)

**Never** "system," "mechanism," "machine," "utensil."

### Left open

**Is 器 fallen or exemplary?** The text will not say.

Chapter 28 makes 器 the **result of breaking wholeness**: the uncarved log scattered into specialized tools, and the chapter's whole movement is a call to 復歸於樸 — *return to the uncarved.* On that reading 器 is a diminishment, and the sage's task is to be the log and not the chisel.

Chapter 11 makes 器 the **model of how anything works at all**, and Chapter 29 makes it **sacred**. On that reading being a vessel is not a fall but the condition of usefulness, and the hollow at the centre is the same emptiness the Tao is praised for.

Both are in the book, unreconciled, and we do not reconcile them. The nearest thing to a resolution is that Chapter 28 objects to being **one** tool — 樸散, *scattered*, made plural and partial — while Chapter 11 admires the vessel for **what it lacks**. A pot is not a specialized blade. But that reading is ours, and the text does not state it.
