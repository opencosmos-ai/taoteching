## 亂 (Luàn) — Disorder

*Say "lwahn," falling. Four lines across four chapters, and the dictionary defines it as its own opposite.*

**Recommended: 亂 → "disorder."** *(Not "chaos," not "chaotic." Not "turmoil.")* *Left open pending 治 (zhì), which this character cannot be settled apart from — see the close.*

### The graph: tangled silk, and the hand that sorts it

> 亂，**治**也。从乙，乙，治之也；从𤔔。
> *"亂 is **to bring to order**. From 乙 (yǐ), and 乙 is what orders it; and from 𤔔 (luàn)."* — 說文解字

Read that again, because it says the opposite of what the character is used to mean. **說文's headword gloss for 亂 is 治 — to govern, to put in order.**

The graph explains how. 𤔔 is a spool of **tangled silk** with a hand above and a hand below, working at it. 乙 is the small hooked stroke the dictionary calls *what orders it* — a tool, or a finger, entering the tangle. So the picture is not a mess. **It is a mess with hands in it**, and Chinese let the one graph carry both the snarl and the act of picking it apart. Lexicographers call this a 反訓 (*fǎnxùn*) — a word that faces both ways.

This matters for the English, and not only as a curiosity. A reader who meets *chaos* meets something nobody is working on.

### Where it stands, and what it is set against

| ch | line | what 亂 is set against |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 不見可欲，使民心不**亂** | the sage's 治 (*zhì*), three lines later, and 無不治 at the close |
| 18 | 國家昏**亂**，有忠臣 | 昏 (*hūn* — clouded) beside it; 忠臣, loyal ministers, as the symptom |
| 38 | 夫禮者，忠信之薄，而**亂**之首 | 忠信 (*zhōng xìn* — loyalty and trust), worn thin |
| 64 | 為之於未有，**治**之於未**亂** | **治 itself, in the same clause** |

**Chapter 64 prints the pair the dictionary describes**: 治之於未亂 — *order it before it falls into disorder*. Two of the four chapters set 亂 directly against 治, and 說文 defines one by the other. The English cannot afford to hide that relation, which is the whole argument against *chaos*: *order* and *disorder* share a root, and the text does too.

**王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) reads 64 as a matter of scale, not of kind** — 治之於未亂 gets the gloss 謂㣲脆也, *"this refers to what is faint and brittle."* The disorder is not a different condition arriving from outside; it is the same thing, further along. You pick the tangle apart while it is still a thread or two.

### What is set aside

**"chaos"** is the one to refuse, and it is the current rendering at chapter 18.

It is a **cosmological** word in English — Hesiod's Χάος, the void before creation, *chaos and old night* — and it points at the wrong end of this book's cosmology. Where Laozi does describe an undifferentiated beginning he has a different word for it, 混成 (*hùn chéng*, ch 25), rendered *"something formed in the undifferentiated."* 亂 is not that. **亂 is a political and domestic word**: a state coming apart, a household out of harmony, a heart stirred up by what it has been shown. Concrete, local, and fixable by somebody.

And *chaos* cannot sit on the same axis as 治. It has no antonym inside it. Put it at 64 and the clause 治之於未亂 becomes *"order it before it is chaotic"* — which is what the verse currently says, half the pair in English and half of it lost.

**"turmoil"** is better than *chaos* and still wrong: it is a word about agitation and feeling, so it carries the heart at ch 3 and drops the constitutional sense at 18 and 38. It also has no relation to *order*.

**"unravel"** is the near miss, and worth recording because it almost wins. It **performs the graph** — silk coming undone — and in English it faces both ways exactly as 亂 does, since one unravels a mystery and a plan unravels. *"Order it before it unravels"* (64) and *"the people's hearts do not unravel"* (3) are both better lines than the recommendation. It fails at 38: 亂之**首** is *the head of* 亂, and *"the head of the unravelling"* is not English. One chapter should not decide four, but a rendering that breaks in one of four is not yet a lock.

### The working register

- as the state a thing falls into → **disorder** (亂之首 → "the head of disorder")
- as what a thing is not → **thrown into disorder** (使民心不亂 → "the people's hearts are not thrown into disorder")
- beside 治, as its opposite → keep both English words visibly related (治之於未亂 → "order it before it falls into disorder")

**Never** "chaos," "chaotic."

### Left open — and it is not a detail

**This character cannot be settled apart from 治 (*zhì*), and 治 has no entry.** 說文 defines 亂 *as* 治; chapters 3 and 64 print them together; and 治 is the larger word by far — **twelve lines across nine chapters** against 亂's four. It currently reads at least two ways itself: *govern* at 3, 60, 65 and 75, *order* at 8 and 64. Until that is decided, the recommendation here is provisional: if 治 lands somewhere other than *order*, the argument for *disorder* loses its best support.

**And a third character is already in the room.** *Order* is also doing duty for 正 (*zhèng*) at chapter 45 — 清靜為天下正, *"clear stillness brings order to the world."* **正 is one of the three calls Shalom has deferred** (`WORKLIST.md` T4-2, 正/奇), so it must not be settled inside this entry. It is named here so that whoever takes 治 knows the room has three characters in it and not two.
