## 希 (Xī) — Sparse

*Say "shee," level. Six lines in six chapters, one each. One property everywhere: thinly spread, whether across a crowd or across a sound.*

**Recommended: 希 → "few" where it counts people or cases · "barely heard" where it measures a sound.** *(Not "rare," which belongs to 難得之貨 (nán dé zhī huò — rare goods) and to 貨 (huò — goods)'s lock. Not "silent," which says the sound is gone; 希 says it is thin.)* **Two flexions, both Shalom's calls:** *the inaudible* at chapter 14, where the book defines the word, and *brevity* at chapter 23. No verse changes.

### The graph: crossed threads over cloth

**The Shuowen has no entry for 希.** It is one of the thirty-four characters of this book that Xu Shen's dictionary does not file (`sources/shuowen/entries.md`, *Not matched*). Its oldest form is 𢁫, read as 爻 (*yáo* — crossed lines) over 巾 (*jīn* — a cloth), and it is the parent of 稀 (*xī*), the later graph for *sparse*, which adds 禾 (*hé* — grain): seedlings set far apart.

**Crossed threads in a cloth, set wide: a loose weave you can see through.** That is a reading of the parts, not a definition anyone wrote down, and this entry holds it lightly. What it explains is real. Chinese uses one word for few people and for faint sound because both are the same thing, a fabric with more gap than thread.

### Six lines, and the medium changes, never the word

> *Listen, but you cannot hear it — its name is the inaudible.*
>
> 聽之不聞，名曰希；
>
> — chapter 14

> *Brevity in speech happens of itself.*
>
> 希言自然。
>
> — chapter 23

> *The great sound is barely heard.*
>
> 大音希聲，
>
> — chapter 41

> *few ever reach this.*
>
> 天下希及之。
>
> — chapter 43

> *Those who know me are few.*
> *Those who pattern themselves on me are precious.*
>
> 知我者希，
> 則我者貴。
>
> — chapter 70

> *Of those who carve in place of the master carpenter,*
> *few fail to cut their own hand.*
>
> 夫代大匠斫，
> 希有不傷其手矣。
>
> — chapter 74

**In every line 希 thins something out, and what it thins tells the English which word to use.** Three lines count: those who reach non-doing (43), those who know the speaker (70), those who carve and keep their hands whole (74). English counts with *few*. Three lines listen: the thing that cannot be heard (14), speech (23), the great sound (41). English listens with *barely* and *inaudible*. The split is English's, not the character's, and it is drawn where English has to draw it.

### Chapter 70: few, and therefore prized

The couplet puts 希 beside 貴 (*guì* — prized), and the second line follows from the first. Heshang Gong: 希，少也。惟達道者乃能知我，故為貴也, *"希 means few. Only one who has reached the Tao can know me, and so they are prized."* Wang Bi turns it on the speaker: 唯深，故知之者希也。知我益希，我亦無匹, *"only because it is deep are those who know it few. The fewer know me, the more I am without match."*

**The same inference, scarcity making value, is the one chapter 3 and chapter 64 refuse for goods.** The sage *"does not prize rare goods."* `glossary/huo-貨.md` holds the tension: the book lets rarity confer value on understanding while refusing it to jade. That is why *rare* cannot be 希's English: it would make chapter 70's few the same thing as the goods the sage will not prize.

### The commentators split on the sound, and the verse takes one from each

**Wang Bi hears inaudibility, every time.** At chapter 23 and again at 41 he defines 希 by quoting chapter 14 outright, 聽之不聞名曰希, *"listen and it is not heard: its name is 希."* At 41 he says why the great sound must be so: 有聲則有分，有分則不宮而商矣。分則不能統眾，故有聲者非大音也, *"if there is sound there is division; with division, if it is not the first note it is the second. Divided, it cannot unite the many; so whatever has sound is not the great sound."*

**Heshang Gong hears quantity, every time.** At 23: 希言者是愛言也, *"sparse speech is cherishing words."* At 41 he makes the great sound a figure for the same thing: 大音猶雷霆待時而動，喻常愛氣希言也, *"the great sound is like thunder, waiting for its season to move: a figure for always cherishing the breath and speaking sparingly."*

**Our English follows Heshang Gong at 23 and Wang Bi at 41.** Chapter 23's storm decided its line: a squall and a downpour are too much at once, and 河上公's *too much against too little* is the chapter's own argument (`chapters/023.md`, Shalom's call). Chapter 41's paradoxes decided the other: *the great sound is barely heard* stands with *the great image has no form*, and both are about perception. Each line chose its commentator by its own chapter, and neither commentator's reading is lost to the book (`process/principles/divergence-stays-open.md`).

### Chapter 14 names the word, and the verse gives its meaning

視之不見，名曰夷 · 聽之不聞，名曰希 · 搏之不得，名曰微 is a definition passage: *its name is called* 夷 (*yí* — level), 希, 微 (*wēi* — faint), three times. **The verse renders the three by what they mean in that stanza**, *the invisible · the inaudible · the intangible*, following Heshang Gong's 無色曰夷，無聲曰希，無形曰微 (*"colourless is called 夷, soundless is called 希, formless is called 微"*). Rendering the words themselves, *the level · the sparse · the faint*, would make all three threads through the book, and Wang Bi argues for exactly that. It was declined because chapter 14 comes early and three bare adjectives there would have nothing to hang on (Shalom's call, 2026-09-10; `WORKLIST-CLOSED.md` T2-52). The chapter notes at 23, 41, 43, 70 and 74 each point back to chapter 14.

### What is set aside

**"Rare," "rarely," "rarity."** Spent on 難得之貨, *rare goods*, a fixed phrase locked in `glossary/huo-貨.md` (chapters 3, 12, 64). Chapter 70 would then say the same of the sage's few that chapter 64 says of the goods the sage refuses.

**"Scarce."** Already forbidden for 貨: it names a need going unmet, a market word. 希 is a count, not a shortage.

**"Seldom."** Right in sense and wrong in reach: it counts occasions only, so it cannot serve chapter 70's people, and it splits the counting lines into two words where one will do.

**"Silent," "silence."** 希 is thin sound, not none, and Wang Bi's great sound is *the sound that cannot be heard*, not the absence of sound. *Silent* also cannot be forbidden, because 不言 (*bù yán* — not speaking) may print it in chapter 43 (see below).

**"Whisper," "quiet."** Volume, which neither commentator means. Chapter 23 tried *"speech barely heard"* and dropped it for that reason: Heshang Gong means a quantity of words, Wang Bi speech too bland to notice, and a whisper is neither (`chapters/023.md`).

**"The sparse," "sparse," in the verse.** The graph's own word, and the right gloss. In the verse it is a weaver's adjective with no noun to hold it at 14, and an awkward one at 43 and 74, where English counts with *few*.

| 知我者希 (70) | a count of people | *few* |
| 希有不傷其手 (74) | a count of cases | *few* |

### What no rule can enforce

***Silent* is ruled out for 希, and cannot be forbidden.** Chapter 43 holds 不言之教 (*bù yán zhī jiào* — "the teaching without words"), which may legitimately read *silent teaching*.

***Inaudible* is ruled out everywhere but chapter 14**, and cannot be forbidden either, because a word that is both a declared flexion and forbidden is an error in the build. Chapter 41 is where it would tempt, and the entry holds it there by argument.

***Few* is not 希's alone.** 少 (*shǎo* — little) reads *few* at chapter 63 (*"many or few"*) and 寡 (*guǎ* — little) at chapter 80 (*"few people"*). Neither shares a chapter with 希, so no rule sees them; `concordance.py --english "few"`, run by hand, is the only check.

### Left open

**Whether the sound lines and the counting lines should meet.** Wang Bi makes chapters 14, 23 and 41 one word by quoting the same definition at all three, and *rarity and inaudibility are one property* is the strongest thing this character teaches. The verse gives it three Englishes and the notes carry the thread. That was chosen for chapter 14's clarity, and it stands; but it is the place a later reading of the book could reasonably decide the other way.

**Chapter 74 counts cases, and its English counts people.** 希有不傷其手 is literally *"rarely is there one who does not cut their hand"*; *few fail to cut their own hand* makes it a count of carvers. The sense survives; the frame shifts. Noted, not worth a rewrite on its own.
