## 主 (Zhǔ) — Master

*Say "joo," falling and rising. Six lines across five chapters. The one a house, a state or a thing gathers round: whoever keeps the fire.*

**Locked: 主 → "master," with *ruler* at chapter 30, *host* at 69 and *keeper* at 78.** *(Shalom's calls, 2026-11-20, taken line by line; host is Shalom's call of 2026-11-19.)* *(Not "lord," which is male only. Not "owner," which is all property and no house. Not "sovereign," 王 (wáng)'s summit.)*

### The graph: the flame standing in a lamp

> 主，鐙中火主也……从丶，丶亦聲。
> *"主 is the flame at the center of a lamp… From 丶 (zhǔ — a dot), and the dot is also its sound."* The rest of the graph, the dictionary says, pictures the lamp.

**The dot on top is the flame, and the rest is the lamp that holds it up.** The flame is where the light comes from, and where a household sits at night. So 主 became the one at the center of a house, the master of it, against the 客 (*kè* — guest) who comes under someone else's roof. The house widens into the state, and 人主 (*rén zhǔ* — the master of people) is the standard Warring States word for the one who holds one. **Where the picture stops:** a flame presides by giving light, not by giving orders. The orders are 君 (*jūn* — ruler)'s mouth (`glossary/jun-君.md`).

### Six lines, and the book's one refusal

> *How can the master of ten thousand chariots*
> *make their own body light in the world?*
>
> 奈何萬乘之主，
> 而以身輕天下？
>
> — chapter 26

> *One who supports a ruler with the Tao does not force the world with weapons.*
>
> 以道佐人主者，不以兵強天下。
>
> — chapter 30

> *The Tao clothes and nourishes the countless things, yet it does not act as their master.*
>
> 衣養萬物而不為主，
>
> — chapter 34

> *The countless things return to it, yet it does not act as their master. It can be named great.*
>
> 萬物歸焉而不為主，可名為大。
>
> — chapter 34

> *I do not push to be the host. I am the guest.*
>
> 吾不敢為主，而為客；
>
> — chapter 69

> *whoever takes on the shame of the state*
> *is called the keeper of the soil and the grain.*
>
> 受國之垢，
> 是謂社稷主；
>
> — chapter 78

**The people are masters of something, and the Tao is master of nothing.** Chapter 26's master holds a state of ten thousand chariots, a great state measured by what it owns. Chapter 30's 人主 is the one an adviser serves. Chapter 34 says twice, in the same words, that the Tao feeds the countless things and draws them home, and does not become what those two are. **Heshang Gong makes the contrast explicit:**

> 道雖愛養萬物，不如人主有所放取。
> *"Though the Tao loves and nourishes the countless things, it is not like a master of people, who takes as they please."* — Heshang Gong, chapter 34

> 萬物皆歸道受氣，道非如人主有所禁止也。
> *"The countless things all return to the Tao and receive breath, and the Tao is not like a master of people, who forbids."* — Heshang Gong, chapter 34

Wang Bi reads chapter 30 the other way round, from the master's side: 以道佐人主，尚不可以兵強於天下，況人主躬於道者乎 — *"if even one who assists a master of people with the Tao must not force the world with weapons, how much less a master who embodies the Tao."*

### Master, ruler, host, keeper: one character, chosen line by line

**主 takes four Englishes, and each is the master in the sense its line needs** (Shalom's calls, 2026-11-20, one line at a time).

- ***Master*** at 26 and 34. At 26 it is the holder of chariots, and it keeps 主 apart from 君's *ruler* three lines away; both commentators need that, because they read 躁則失君 (*zào zé shī jūn* — "restlessness loses the ruler") as this master losing the ruler's seat. At 34 what the Tao refuses is ownership and control over things, not an office, and Wang Bi reads it so.
- ***Ruler*** at 30, shared with 君. 人主 is the holder of a state, the one an adviser serves, and the commentators gloss 君 and 主 by each other: Wang Bi's 君，萬物之主也 (*"the ruler is the master of the countless things,"* chapter 70), Heshang Gong's 君主 (*jūn zhǔ*) at 78. *Master* here would print four lines before 善 (*shàn*)'s *the masterful one*, and make the two sound like one person.
- ***Host*** at 69, against 客 (*kè* — guest): the master of the house, in its home sense (`glossary/xian-hou-先後.md`).
- ***Keeper*** at 78, 社稷主 (*shè jì zhǔ*): the one who takes on the state's shame keeps its soil and grain, and no one is master of the soil (`notes/translation.md` → *Ch 78*).

**The cost: a reader cannot follow 主 by one word.** The Chinese reader hears the same flame at 26, 34 and 69; the English reader hears three words. The entry is where the thread is kept.

### 奧 (ào) and the silks' 注 (zhù) at chapter 62

**Both Mawangdui silks read 注 (*zhù*) where our base reads 奧 (*ào* — the inner corner)**, in 道者萬物之奧 (*"the Tao is the inner corner of the countless things"*), and Gao Ming takes 注 as a loan for 主. That would make the Tao *the master of the countless things*, which chapter 34 denies twice in the same two words. **We keep 奧** (`notes/manuscript.md`, `notes/translation.md` → *奧*).

### What is set aside

**"Lord."** Male only: it lives in a pair with *lady*, as *king* does with *queen* (Shalom's call, 2026-11-20). It read at chapter 26 until that day.

**"Owner."** The master of a thing without the house around it, and it cannot stand at 30 (*"supports an owner"*).

**"Sovereign."** 王 (*wáng*)'s word, the summit the world goes to (`glossary/wang-王.md`). Heshang Gong glosses 26's 主 as 王, and the Siku Wang Bi prints 王 there, but the book's 主 is a center, not a summit.

**"Overlord."** Domination on top of mastery, and male.

**"Chief," "head," "commander."** *Chief* is 長 (*zhǎng*)'s (28, 67), *head* 首 (*shǒu*)'s (38), *command* 令 (*lìng*)'s and 命 (*mìng*)'s.

### Working register

| line | form | English |
|---|---|---|
| 萬乘之主 (26) | the holder of a state's chariots | *the master of ten thousand chariots* |
| 人主 (30) | the holder of a state | *a ruler* (flexion) |
| 不為主 (34 ×2) | 為 (*wéi* — to act as) + 主 | *does not act as their master* |
| 為主 · 為客 (69) | against 客 | *the host* (flexion) |
| 社稷主 (78) | the soil and the grain | *the keeper* (flexion) |

### What no rule can enforce

**"Lord" is ruled out and not forbidden.** The I Ching vendors these locks and prints *lord* for 公 (*gōng*) in hexagrams that also hold 主 (3 and 50), so *lord* on this list would fail that book's gate (`WORKLIST.md` T5-29). `concordance.py --english "lord"` is the check here.

**"Master" has three claimants.** 主 renders it; 大 (*dà*) licenses it at chapter 74's *master carpenter*; 善 (*shàn*)'s *masterful* contains it. `concordance.py --english "master"` credits one entry, so read its *not backed* list by hand.

**"Ruler" is shared with 君** at chapter 30 only, by flexion. They never stand in one chapter there.

### Left open

**Whether the three Englishes hide a thread worth hearing.** 26's master of chariots and 34's Tao that is master of nothing are one word now; 30's ruler, between them, is the line Heshang Gong reads 34 against, and it reads *ruler*. The contrast is audible between 26 and 34 and must be carried across 30 by the reader.

**69's 主 is a military term.** Heshang Gong glosses it 主，先也 (*"主 means first"*): the side that opens the fighting. *Host* keeps the image the text prints; the term of art lives in `notes/translation.md` → *Ch 69*.
