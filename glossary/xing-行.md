## 行 (Xíng) — Going, and How a Person Goes

*Say "shing," rising. Nineteen lines across seventeen chapters. One graph, read three ways: xíng, to go; xìng, a person's conduct; háng, a row.*

**Recommended: 行 → "go" as a verb of motion · "carry out" when it takes an object · "conduct" as a person's noun.** *(Not "action," not "execute," not "do." That 行 must not wear "act" is a ruling no check can hold; see* What no rule can enforce *below.)* **Settled within it:** *conduct* at ch 12 (Shalom's call, 2026-10-20) and ch 62; *follow* at ch 70 and 78 (Shalom's call); *ranks* for the second 行 at ch 69.

### The graph: a crossroads, and the steps taken in it

**The oldest graph is a crossroads**: two streets meeting, seen from above. **Our vendored 說文解字 (*Shuōwén Jiězì*, c. 100 CE) reads it as the walking instead of the place: 人之步趨也。从彳从亍** — *"a person's stepping and hurrying. From 彳 and 亍."* 彳 (*chì*) is a short step and 亍 (*chù*) its partner, so 許慎 (*Xǔ Shèn*) splits the crossroads down the middle into a left foot and a right. The picture and the dictionary meet in the same thing: a place made for going, and the going done there.

**行 is the ground the book's road words stand on.** 彳 is the left half of 行, and it carries the walking into the characters the book cares about most:

- **道** (*dào* — the Tao). In the Zhou bronzes it is usually written as 行 with a head standing inside it, and most of the Guodian copyists (c. 300 BCE) draw **a person standing inside 行**. `dao-道.md`.
- **德** (*dé* — integrity), built on 彳: a step taken straight. `de-德.md`.
- **得** (*dé* — to get). 說文: 行有所得也, *"walking and getting something."*
- **復** (*fù* — to go back), whose sound element 复 說文 glosses 行故道也, *"to travel the old road."* `fuming-復命.md`.

So 行 is not one verb among many. It is the motion the whole vocabulary is drawn from, and that is the case for keeping its English physical wherever the line allows.

### Three readings, sorted by grammar

**The sense follows the grammar, so it can be read off the line.** 行 with no object is going. 行 with an object is carrying something out. 行 as a noun owned by a person is that person's conduct.

**1. To go: the root, in most of the book.**

> 周行而不殆 (25) — *it moves in a circle, never in danger.*
> 終日行不離輜重 (26) — *travels all day without leaving the baggage wagons.*
> 善行無轍跡 (27) — *masterful traveling leaves no tracks.*
> 故物或行或隨 (29) — *so among things, some go ahead, some follow.*
> 陸行不遇兕虎 (50) — *can travel the land without meeting rhinos or tigers.*
> 千里之行，始於足下 (64) — *the thousand-mile journey begins beneath your feet.*

The same sense stands at 24 (跨者不行, *get nowhere*), 33 (強行者, *forces themselves onward*), 47 (不行而知, *knows without venturing*), 53 (行於大道, *walking the path of the great Tao*) and 69 (the first 行 of 行無行, *marching*). Ch 64's 千里之行 is the going made a noun, *the journey*, and it is still motion.

**2. To carry out: when 行 takes an object.** The object is a teaching, a set of words, or the Tao.

> 行不言之教 (2) — *carries out the teaching without words.*
> 上士聞道，勤而行之 (41) — *those highest in service hear the Tao and labor to practice it.*
> 甚易行…莫能行 (70) — *very easy to follow… no one can follow them.*

王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) ties the second sense back to the book's center at ch 70: 無為而成，故曰甚易行也 — *"it is accomplished by not-doing, therefore it says 'very easy to carry out.'"* What is carried out is the not-handling. That is why *do* cannot stand here: 為 (*wéi* — to do, to handle) is the word the line is a commentary on.

**3. Conduct: 行 as a person's own.**

> 美行可以加人 (62) — *fine conduct can raise a person above others.*
> 難得之貨，令人行妨 (12) — *rare goods hinder people's conduct.*

**The tradition marks this reading in the sound.** 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*)'s text carries the note 行下孟反, a spelling that gives *xìng*, at ch 12, 24, 27 and 62. That is the conduct reading, against *xíng*, the walking one. The Mawangdui silks give ch 12 as 使人之行, *"makes a person's 行,"* with 之 (*zhī* — 's) making it a noun the person owns. 人之行 is the stock phrase for a person's conduct.

**And 王弼's road at ch 12 is a road of conduct.** He glosses the line 難得之貨，塞人正路, *"rare goods block a person's straight road,"* and the phrase is Mencius's: 義，人之正路也, *"duty is a person's straight road"* (孟子 (*Mèngzǐ*) · 離婁上; not in our sources). The commentators read the same thing there, conduct. They differ only on what is done to it. `chapters/012.md`.

**4. A row: háng, once.** 行無行 (69) uses the graph twice, in two words. 王弼: 行謂行陳也, *"行 here means 行陳, the battle line."* 河上公 marks the second one 户剛反, which gives *háng*. *"Marching where there are no ranks."* `notes/translation.md` → *Ch 69 · 行無行*.

### What is set aside, and why

**"action," "actions."** *Act* and *action* carry seventeen lines of this book, almost all of them 為 and two of them 作 (*zuò* — to arise), and they sit on 無為 (*wú wéi* — non-doing), the axis the book turns on. Ch 12 shows what goes wrong: *"rare goods make actions corrupt"* sat one line above *"the sage acts for the belly,"* and a reader had no way to know they were different characters. *Action* is also abstract where 行 is a body moving.

**"execute."** Mechanistic on its face, and already forbidden for 為. It was the source table's gloss at ch 70, where it is refused in `notes/translation.md` → *Ch 70 · 行*.

**"do."** 為's locked word. Ch 70 holds no 為 at all, which is exactly why giving 行 *do* there would be wrong: a lock is a claim in both directions.

**"steps."** Proposed at ch 12 and refused. It keeps the root, *walking*, and loses the extension, *how a person goes*, which is the line's whole sense there.

**"behavior."** The modern word for conduct, and it carries the laboratory with it: behaviorism, behavior to be modified. *Conduct* is older and still has the going in it, the way a person carries themselves along.

**"deeds," "works."** King James English, *"by their works"*. Both fail the hymn test, and both count things done where 行 is a way of going.

### The working register

| Grammar | English | Lines |
|---|---|---|
| no object, motion | *go · travel · move · walk · march*, as the line's idiom needs | 24 25 26 27 29 33 47 50 53 64 69 |
| an object: a teaching, words, the Tao | *carry out · practice*; *follow* at 70 and 78 | 2 41 70 78 |
| a person's noun | **conduct** | 12 62 |
| háng, a row | **ranks** | 69 |

**The English for motion is left to the idiom, not locked.** *Travel* (26, 27, 50) and *walk* (53) are the same sense in different collocations. A single word forced across all of them would buy consistency with nothing to show for it, because the senses are not in tension. They are one going. What has to hold is the line *between* the readings: the going stays physical, the carrying-out takes an object, and only a person's own 行 is *conduct*.

### What no rule can enforce

**"act" cannot be forbidden for 行.** 為 legitimately prints *act* in chapters that also hold 行: ch 2 (*"the sage acts, yet relies on nothing"*), ch 12 (*"the sage acts for the belly"*), ch 47 (*"accomplishes without acting"*). The checker gates on whether a character is present in the chapter and cannot say *right for 為, wrong for 行, same chapter.* The match is also by substring, so *act* would flag every *practice*. **The ruling holds for the reader**: in this book 行 never takes *act*. *Action* and *execute* can go on `forbidden:`, because no chapter holding 行 uses either for anything else.

**"follow" is shared, on the record.** At ch 70 and 78 it renders 行. It also carries 從 (*cóng*, ch 21), 隨 (*suí*, ch 2 and 29), 徒 (*tú*, ch 50) and a bare 之 (ch 14), and ch 29 holds 行 and 隨 in one line: *"some go ahead, some follow."* The cost was weighed and accepted. `notes/translation.md` → *Ch 70 · 行*.

### Left open

**Ch 24's 贅行 is the one place both commentators read conduct and the verse reads motion.** 王弼: 若卻至之行，盛饌之餘也, *"like the conduct of 卻至 (Xì Zhì), the leftovers of a rich feast."* 河上公 reads 贅 as greed, 為貪行, *"it is greedy conduct,"* and marks the sound *xìng*. The verse has *"leftover food and wasted motion,"* which ties the summary back to the chapter's opening figures, the one on tiptoe and the one taking long strides, and declines 河上公's moral reading (`chapters/024.md`). But *conduct* passes no verdict by itself, as ch 12 now shows, so the reason for refusing it there is weaker than it was.

**Ch 27 is the other place 河上公 marks *xìng*.** 善行道者求之於身，不下堂不出門，故無轍迹 — *"one masterful at practicing the Tao seeks it in the self, never leaves the hall or goes out the door, and so leaves no tracks."* For him 善行 is masterful conduct. 王弼 keeps the road, 順自然而行, *"goes along with what is so of itself."* The verse follows 王弼, and the tracks make the walking literal. Both readings are live, and the English has chosen one.

**Ch 47's *venturing* is 出's word three lines up.** 其出彌遠，其知彌少 — *"the further out you venture, the less you know"* — puts *venture* on 出 (*chū* — to go out). Then 不行而知 gives *"knows without venturing"* to 行. Two characters, one English, one chapter. 王弼 reads plain going, 雖不行而慮可知也, *"though not going, by reflection one can know."*

**The carrying-out sense wears three Englishes.** *Carries out* at 2, *practice* at 41, *follow* at 70 and 78. *Practise* was refused at ch 70 as abstraction drift and survives at 41. Whether 2 and 41 should join 70's *follow*, or 70 is the exception, is not settled by this entry.
