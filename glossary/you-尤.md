## 尤 (Yóu) — Blame

*Say "yo," rising. One line in the whole book.*

**Locked: 尤 → "blame."** *(Not "fault," which is 咎's. Not "blameless," which turns an absence into a verdict.)*

### The graph: what stands out

> 尢，**異**也。从乙又聲。
> *"Out of the ordinary. From 乙 (yǐ — a shoot coming up bent), with 又 (yòu) for sound."* — 說文解字 (*Shuōwén Jiězì*, the Han dictionary), which files the character under its Han graph 尢

The everyday sense *especially*, as in 尤其 (*yóu qí*), is the same idea: the one that stands out from the rest. What stands out is also what gets singled out, and classical Chinese uses 尤 for both the slip and the singling-out. The Analects has each. 言寡尤 (*yán guǎ yóu* — "few slips in speech," 2.18) is the noun, the error that is yours. 不怨天，不尤人 (*bù yuàn tiān, bù yóu rén* — "does not resent heaven, does not blame others," 14.37) is the verb, with other people as its object.

The oldest graph is contested. The oracle-bone form long read as 尤 has been reread as 拇 (*mǔ* — thumb), which leaves no secure 尤 before the Western Zhou (季旭昇 *Jì Xùshēng*, 2009). Nothing here rests on it.

### One line, and it points outward

> 夫唯不爭，故無尤 (*fú wéi bù zhēng, gù wú yóu* — "only because it does not contend, therefore no 尤")
> *Only because it does not contend / is there no blame.* — ch 8

**The only commentary on the line makes 尤 something done to water.** 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*): 水性如是，故天下无有怨尤水者也 — *"water's nature is like this, so in all the world there is no one who resents or blames water."* 怨尤 (*yuàn yóu* — to resent, to blame) is the Analects' pair, and water is its object. 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) says nothing about the word.

**The frame agrees.** Wherever the book says what not contending earns, the answer is how other people respond. Ch 22 and 66: 天下莫能與之爭 (*tiān xià mò néng yǔ zhī zhēng* — "no one in the world can contend with them"). Ch 72, in the same 夫唯 (*fú wéi* — only because) frame: 夫唯不厭，是以不厭 — *"only because you do not oppress them do they not tire of you."* Water does not contend, so nobody has anything to hold against it.

### 尤 and 咎: blame and fault

The book has two characters for something gone wrong, and they face opposite ways.

| | 尤 (*yóu*) — **blame** | 咎 (*jiù*) — **fault** |
|---|---|---|
| 說文 | 異也 — out of the ordinary | 災也 — disaster; a person at odds |
| where it comes from | other people, who hold it against you | yourself: 自遺其咎 (*zì yí qí jiù* — you leave it to yourself), ch 9 |
| 河上公 | 怨尤水者 — *those who resent and blame water* | 必被禍患 — *will surely suffer disaster and trouble* |
| chapters | 8 | 9, 46 |

Ch 8 and ch 9 sit side by side. With one English word across both, a reader would meet water that has *no fault* and, on the next page, the arrogant who *leave themselves the fault*, and take it as a deliberate pairing. The Chinese uses two characters and pairs nothing.

### What is set aside

**"fault"** is a real sense of 尤, as 言寡尤 shows, and it is 咎's word in this book (`jiu-咎.md`). It also moves the claim inside water: *there is no fault* reads as *nothing is wrong with it*, where the line, as its one commentator reads it, says *nobody holds anything against it*.

**"blameless"** turns an absence into a virtue. *No one blames water* is a report; *water is blameless* is a verdict on its purity, and it would sit comfortably in a sermon.

### This lock stays in this book

**In the I Ching, 尤 cannot be *blame*.** That book gives *blame* to 咎, its verdict word, and 尤 stands beside it in six lines of the 小象傳 (*Xiǎo Xiàng Zhuàn* — the commentary on the lines): 終无尤也 (*zhōng wú yóu yě* — "in the end, no 尤"), five times, and 中无尤也 (*zhōng wú yóu yě* — "at the center, no 尤") once. Inside one book two graphs cannot share a word. *(Shalom's call, 2026-10-20: the books diverge on purpose.)* So this entry carries `binds: [taoteching]`, and the I Ching, which vendors this glossary's locks as its own, decides 尤 for itself.

### Left open

**The I Ching's echo.** *No blame* is the familiar English of 无咎 (*wú jiù* — "no 咎"), the I Ching's most repeated formula, so a reader who knows that book hears 咎 in this line, the very character this edition renders *fault*. The echo comes from outside the book and is accepted: inside it, nothing collides.

**Who blames is never said.** 無尤 (*wú yóu* — no blame) names no one; 河上公 supplies *all the world*. The English keeps the line agentless, as the Chinese does, and *blame* still implies someone who would have done the blaming. That is the word's cost, and the Chinese carries it too.
