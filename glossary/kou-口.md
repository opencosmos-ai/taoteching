## 口 (Kǒu) — Mouth

*Say "ko," falling and rising. Two lines across two chapters, and both are about taste. The organ that speaks and eats.*

**Locked: 口 → "mouth," in both lines.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-11-20, at chapter 12; chapter 35 has read* mouth *since its draft.)* *(Not "palate," which names only the tasting part. Not "lips" or "tongue," which pick a part again.)*

### The graph: an open mouth

> 口，人所以言食也。象形。
> *"口 is what a person speaks and eats with. A pictograph."*

**The dictionary's definition is two verbs, and the book's two lines sit between them.** The graph is the mouth drawn open, and it is the part in a long family the glossary already holds: 名 (*míng* — name, a mouth calling at dusk), 和 (*hé* — harmony, mouths answering), 君 (*jūn* — ruler, the mouth that commands), 可 (*kě* — can, the mouth saying yes). **Where the picture stops:** the book never shows the mouth speaking. Its two lines are taste, and one of them is the Tao.

### Two lines, and the mouth loses its taste, then meets the tasteless

> *The five colors make people's eyes blind.*
> *The five tones make people's ears deaf.*
> *The five flavors make people's mouths dull.*
>
> 五色令人目盲；
> 五音令人耳聾；
> 五味令人口爽；
>
> — chapter 12

> *The Tao is bland in the mouth. It has no flavor.*
>
> 道之出口，淡乎其無味。
>
> — chapter 35

**Chapter 12 names each organ whole: eyes, ears, mouths, and in the next line, hearts.** It read *palates* until 2026-11-20 (Shalom's call), which named only the tasting part and set that one line apart from its series. Wang Bi: 爽，差失也，失口之用 (*"爽 is to err and lose: losing the use of the mouth"*). **Heshang Gong reads ahead to chapter 35:** 人嗜五味於口則口亡，言失於道味也 (*"indulge the five flavors in the mouth and the mouth is lost: it loses the taste of the Tao"*). The mouth dulled by flavor at 12 is the one in which the Tao is bland at 35.

**At chapter 35 the commentators split on which way the Tao passes the mouth.** Wang Bi rewrites 出口 as 出言, *"coming out in words"*; both Mawangdui silks read 出言 too (`WORKLIST.md` T2-80). Heshang Gong keeps the organ and doubles it: 道出入於口，淡淡非如五味 (*"the Tao goes out and in at the mouth, bland, not like the five flavors"*). ***In the mouth*** is ordinary English for speech and for taste both, and leaves the fork open (`notes/translation.md` → *Ch 35*).

### What is set aside

**"Palate."** The roof of the mouth, the tasting part. Exact for flavor, and it picks eating over speech in a book whose other 口 may be speech. Chapter 12 read so until 2026-11-20.

**"Tongue," "lips," "taste."** Each a part or a sense, where the series names the organs.

**"Spoken aloud" at chapter 35.** Wang Bi's side of the fork, settled in the English; declined when the chapter was drafted.

### Working register

| line | form | English |
|---|---|---|
| 令人口爽 (12) | the organ, in a series | *mouths* |
| 道之出口 (35) | the organ the Tao passes | *in the mouth* |

### What no rule can enforce

**口 as a part in other characters.** The atlas shows it inside 名, 和, 君, 可, 命 and the rest, by its graphic sense; nothing here governs those.

### Left open

**Chapter 35's direction.** *In the mouth* leaves Wang Bi's speech and Heshang Gong's taste both open, and the silks' 出言 leans to speech. If the silk reading is confirmed against a printed edition (`WORKLIST.md` T2-80), the line may want to say so.
