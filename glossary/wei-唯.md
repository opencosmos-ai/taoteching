## 唯 (Wéi) — Only

*Say "way," rising. Sixteen lines across fifteen chapters. Twelve of them open 夫唯 (fú wéi — "now, only").*

**Locked: 唯 → "only." The frame 夫唯 X，是以 / 故 Y → "Only because X, Y," with the inversion English uses after a fronted *only*: *"Only because it is great does it look like nothing."*** *(Not "precisely because," not "only when," not bare "because." Not "alone," which is 獨's, but that ruling no check can hold. See the last section but one.)* *Shalom's call, 2026-10-19.*

*惟 (wéi), the same word written with the heart radical, stands twice in ch 21 and is covered here.*

The book uses 唯 to single one thing out. Only the Tao lends and completes (41). Only those who are with the Tao have enough to give the world (77). And nine times it builds a sentence on the word: **only** because it does not contend, **only** because it is great, **only** because they are never full. The cause it names is the whole cause. Drop the *only* and the line becomes an ordinary explanation, one reason among several, which is not what the Chinese says.

### The graph: a yes, and a borrowed sound

> 唯，**諾也**。从口隹聲。
> *"唯 is a yes. From 口 (kǒu — mouth), with 隹 (zhuī — a short-tailed bird) for the sound."* — 說文解字

The bird gives only the sound. The meaning is the mouth: 唯 is a word you say, the quick *yes* given in answer. The book uses that sense once. Ch 20 asks 唯之與阿，相去幾何, *"between a respectful yes and a careless yeah, how far apart are they?"*, and 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) glosses the pair 同為應對, *"both are answers."*

**"Only" is a different word that borrowed the graph for its sound.** The particle was written several ways: 唯 with the mouth, 惟 with the heart, 維 with the thread, and in early bronze writing the bare 隹 (*zhuī*), the bird alone. In the dated formulas of Western Zhou bronzes it stands first, as *it was*: 唯三月初吉 (*wéi sān yuè chū jí*), *"it was the third month, at 初吉 (chū jí — the month's first auspicious phase)."* That is a word that presents something as the very thing. *Only* says the same and adds *nothing else*.

So the graph argues nothing about *only*. Reading 隹 beside 口 as a picture of *solely* is reading a sound as a sense. The case for *only* rests on how the book and its commentators use the word.

### What the book does with it

| job | lines | English |
|---|---|---|
| **only** — one thing singled out | 2 · 8 · 15 (twice) · 22 · 41 · 53 · 59 · 67 · 70 · 71 · 72 · 75 · 77, and 惟 twice at 21 | *only* |
| **it is** — the copula, presenting the very thing | 42: 人之所惡，唯孤、寡、不穀 | carried by English *is* |
| **yes** — the assent, 說文's sense | 20: 唯之與阿 | *yes* |

**Fourteen of the sixteen lines are *only*.** The other two are the word's other two jobs, the copula and the yes.

### 夫唯: only this, and therefore that

Nine lines run one frame. 夫 (*fú*) opens a statement and has no English of its own; ch 31 records two lines where it had been inflated into an imperative. 是以 (*shì yǐ*) and 故 (*gù*) are both *therefore*.

| ch | 夫唯 X | 是以 / 故 Y |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 弗居 (*fú jū* — does not stay) | 是以不去 (*therefore it does not leave*) |
| 8 | 不爭 (*bù zhēng* — does not contend) | 故無尤 (*therefore no blame*) |
| 15 | 不可識 (*bù kě shí* — could not be read) | 故強為之容 (*therefore I force a likeness*) |
| 15 | 不盈 (*bù yíng* — not full) | 故能蔽不新成 (*therefore they can stay covered, not newly made*) |
| 22 | 不爭 (*does not contend*) | 故天下莫能與之爭 (*therefore no one in the world can contend with them*) |
| 67 | 大 (*dà* — great) | 故似不肖 (*therefore it looks like nothing*) |
| 70 | 無知 (*wú zhī* — no knowing) | 是以不我知 (*therefore they do not know me*) |
| 71 | 病病 (*bìng bìng* — sick of the sickness) | 是以不病 (*therefore not sick*) |
| 72 | 不厭 (*bù yàn* — do not press down) | 是以不厭 (*therefore they do not tire of you*) |

**In seven of the nine, what follows 唯 is a *not*.** Not staying, not contending twice, not readable, not full, no knowing, not pressing down. The frame says that a refusal, and nothing besides it, is the cause. That is the book's argument about 無 (*wú* — what is not there) in the form of a sentence. A pattern like this is a reason to look, not a ruling (`process/principles/distribution-locates.md`). The ruling rests on 唯 itself. The pattern is why the loss of *only* costs so much.

### The commentators keep it exclusive

**河上公 reaches for 獨 (*dú* — alone) four times,** three times beside 唯, doubling the exclusive where one would have done, and once in its place:

- **59.** 夫**獨**愛民財、愛精氣，則能先得天道也. *"Only by cherishing the people's wealth and cherishing the vital essence can one obtain the Tao of nature first."*
- **67.** 唯**獨**名德大者為身害，故佯愚似若不肖. *"Only for one whose name for integrity is great does it become a harm to the body, and so they feign foolishness and look like nothing."*
- **72.** 夫唯**獨**不厭精神之人. *"Only the person who does not press down on the vital spirit."*
- **75.** 夫唯**獨**無以生為務者. *"Only those who do not make living their business."*

**王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) writes the frame himself, in his own prose.** On ch 70: 唯深，故知之者希也, *"only because it is deep, those who know it are few."* That is 唯…故, *only… therefore*, used by a third-century reader to explain the line, and it means *only because*. On ch 59: 唯重積德、不欲銳速，然後乃能使早服其常, *"only by heavily accumulating integrity, not wanting sharp speed, can one then bring about early acceptance of the constant."*

Neither commentator ever reads 唯 as mere emphasis.

### Why "only because"

**The *only* is 唯's.** The *because* is 是以's or 故's, moved forward. Chinese puts its *therefore* at the head of the second clause. English puts its *because* at the head of the first. Nothing is supplied: every word renders a character (`process/principles/renders-no-character.md`).

**The inversion is how English says 唯.** A fronted *only* turns the clause after it round: *only because it is great **does it** look like nothing*. Without the turn, *only because* drifts toward *merely because*, the dismissive sense, as in *only because I was tired*. With it, the line can mean nothing but the sole cause.

**The hardest lines decide it.** Ch 67 says the Tao looks like nothing **because** it is great, and greatness is not an occasion: *"only **when** it is great"* would make it sometimes small. Ch 15's first instance is past: the speaker forces a likeness because the ancients **could not** be read. *When* fails both. *Only because* fits all nine.

### What is set aside, and why

**"because"** alone keeps the cause and loses the *only*. A sole cause becomes a cause.

**"precisely because"** is an intensifier, not an exclusive. It says X is exactly the reason, which is not the same as saying nothing else is. It also cannot serve the four lines where 唯 stands before its subject (41, 59, 75, 77): *precisely the Tao is masterful at lending* does not say that nothing else is. 唯 would need a second English there, and one character would have two.

**"only when"** turns a cause into a condition. 是以 and 故 are causal, and *when* breaks at ch 67 and ch 15 as above. It also makes the frame sound like a rule with a timetable, *only when you do X will Y happen*, where the Chinese states how a thing works.

**"alone"** is 獨's. Ch 20 prints it six times, *I alone*, and ch 25's 獨立 (*dú lì* — standing alone) reads *solitary*. 河上公's 獨 is evidence that he heard 唯 as exclusive. It is not a rendering for 唯 (`process/principles/commentary-is-not-a-rendering.md`).

**"simply"** restricts the wrong way. It means *plainly, without fuss*, and diminishes the verb instead of singling out the noun: *simply follows the Tao* is a manner of following. 惟道是從 (21) is the same construction as 唯施是畏 (53), the object put first and singled out: *only the Tao is what it follows*.

**"it is only because X that Y"**, the cleft, keeps everything and costs six English words a line. It turns verse into argument.

| Chinese | English | form |

| 惟恍惟惚 (21) | *only vague, only elusive* | 惟 before a predicate, twice |
| 人之所惡，唯孤、寡、不穀 (42) | *What most people hate is to be called orphaned…* | the copula, in English *is* |
| 唯之與阿 (20) | *a respectful yes* | the assent, a declared flexion |

**夫 stays silent.** It is a topic particle, and English *now* at the head of a line reads as time.

### What no rule can enforce

**"alone" is ruled out for 唯 and cannot go on its `forbidden:` list.** 獨 stands beside 唯 in ch 20 and prints *alone* there six times.

**"because" and "when" are ruled out as renderings of the frame and cannot be forbidden at all.** They are the English of 以 (*yǐ* — because), 故, 是以 and 則 (*zé* — then) across the same chapters.

**The frame itself is held by no rule.** `check_locks.py`'s repeated-formula rule indexes segments of four or more characters shared by two or three chapters. 夫唯 is two characters in eleven. The reader holds it: wherever 夫唯 is followed by 是以 or 故, the line opens *Only because*.

**Of the set-aside words, only *precisely* is one the build can hold, and it is on the `forbidden:` list.** No other character in these chapters claims it. See `process/principles/ruling-and-enforcement-are-two-facts.md`.

### Left open

**The double negatives are the real cost.** Six of the nine lines put a *not* on both sides, and English inversion makes that heavy: *"only because you do not oppress them do they not tire of you."* The Chinese is just as doubled, 不厭…不厭, but Chinese wears a doubled negative lightly and English does not. No rendering that keeps 唯 avoids it. Dropping 唯 is the only way out, and that is the loss this entry exists to refuse.

**Cause and condition, for a general *you*.** In ch 71 and ch 72 the subject is anyone, and for anyone English hears *because* and *when* as nearly the same claim. The Chinese marks the frame causal, and 王弼 restates ch 72 causally: 不自厭，是以天下莫之厭, *"they do not make themselves loathsome, therefore no one in the world loathes them."* A reader may still hear a rule. That is the reader's to hear, not the English's to impose.

**The copula at ch 42.** 人之所惡，唯孤、寡、不穀 presents the three titles as the very things people hate, and English *is* carries the presenting without the pointing. *None other than* would point, and would make a plain line emphatic. The verse keeps it plain.
