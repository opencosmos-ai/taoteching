## 足 (Zú) — Enough

*Say "dzoo," rising. Seventeen lines across twelve chapters: once a foot, and everywhere else the measure a life stands on.*

**Locked: 足 → "enough," and 不足 (bù zú) → "not enough" in every line; "foot" where it is the body part (64).** *(Shalom's calls, 2026-11-20.)* *(Not "sufficient" or "deficient," which put a clinic or a ledger where the book has a plain word. Not "too little," which drops* enough *at the one chapter where nature restores it. Not "runs short," which borrows 寡 (guǎ — few)'s word.)* 知足 (*zhī zú* — knowing you have enough) is its own entry, `glossary/zhizu-知足.md`.

### The graph: the leg down to the ground

> 足，人之足也。在下。从止、口。
> *"足 is a person's foot. It is below. From 止 (zhǐ — a foot) and 口 (kǒu — an enclosure)."*

**The oracle-bone graph is a knee above a foot: the whole leg, ending where it meets the earth.** The Shuowen's second sentence is the one to keep, 在下 (*zài xià* — "it is below"): the lowest part of a standing person, the part that touches the ground. `glossary/zhizu-知足.md` → *The graph* has the full account. **Where the picture stops:** how the foot came to mean *enough* is not settled. The book holds both senses near each other: the foot is what a person stands on, and enough is what a life stands on. The resonance is real; the etymology is not proven.

> *The thousand-mile journey begins beneath your feet.*
>
> 千里之行，始於足下。
>
> — chapter 64

### Seventeen lines, and enough is always a measure

> *Serve as the world's valley, and the ever-present integrity is enough at last,*
>
> 為天下谷，常德乃足，
>
> — chapter 28

> *These three, taken as cultural form, are not enough.*
>
> 此三者以為文不足，
>
> — chapter 19

> *Look at it. There is not enough to see.*
> *Listen for it. There is not enough to hear.*
>
> 視之不足見，
> 聽之不足聞，
>
> — chapter 35

> *Broad integrity seems not enough.*
>
> 廣德若不足，
>
> — chapter 41

> *Where there is not enough trust, there is no trust.*
>
> 信不足焉，
>
> — chapter 23

**足 never names a quantity. It names the line where a thing has what it needs**, and the book's whole moral is placed on that line. Integrity comes to *enough* in the valley (28). The three cut-off things are *not enough* (19). The Tao offers *not enough to see* (35), and broad integrity *seems not enough* (41), because what is enough of itself does not show. Wang Bi says it in one line at chapter 20: 自然已足，益之則憂 (*"what is so of itself is already enough; add to it and you worry"*).

**And where there is not enough trust, there is none.** 信不足焉，有不信焉 closes chapters 17 and 23 in the same words. Wang Bi at 17 makes it a law of bodies: 夫御體失性則疾病生…信不足焉，則有不信，此自然之道也 (*"when the body is driven against its nature, illness arises… where trust is not enough there is no trust: this is the way of nature"*). A shortfall does not stay partial. The English opens both clauses on *there is*, as the Chinese closes both on 焉 (*yān* — there) (Shalom's line, 2026-11-20).

### Chapter 77: nature restores enough

> *The way of nature*
> *takes from where there is more than enough*
> *and mends where there is not enough.*
>
> 天之道，
> 損有餘而
> 補不足。
>
> — chapter 77

> *The way of people is not like this.*
> *It takes from those who do not have enough*
> *and offers it up to those who have more than enough.*
>
> 人之道，則不然，
> 損不足以
> 奉有餘。
>
> — chapter 77

**The chapter is 知足 made cosmic.** Chapters 33, 44 and 46 tell a person to know when they have enough; chapter 77 says nature already does, taking from 有餘 (*yǒu yú* — more than enough) and mending 不足. Heshang Gong: 天道損有餘而益謙，常以中和為上 (*"the way of nature takes from more than enough and adds to the humble, always holding the balanced middle highest"*), and of people, 損貧以奉富 (*"they take from the poor to offer to the rich"*). **His gloss on the chapter's last question keeps the same pair:** 誰能居有餘之位…以奉天下不足者乎 (*"who can hold a place of more than enough… and offer it to those in the world who do not have enough?"*).

**So the English keeps the pair in every line, *more than enough* and *not enough*** (Shalom's call, 2026-11-20). The chapter had read *too much / too little* in three lines and *more than enough* in the fourth. *Too little* was crisper, and it dropped *enough* from the one chapter where nature restores it.

### 不足以 (bù zú yǐ): *not enough to*, which is *cannot*

> *If it weren't laughed at, the Tao wouldn't be what it is.*
>
> 不笑不足以為道。
>
> — chapter 41

> *The moment you meddle, you cannot win it over.*
>
> 及其有事，不足以取天下。
>
> — chapter 48

**不足以 + verb is idiom: *not enough to do it*, so *cannot do it*.** The English renders the idiom, not the parts. *"It is not enough to win it over"* would read as a matter of degree, where the Chinese means it cannot be done.

### What is set aside

**"Sufficient," "sufficiency," "insufficient."** Latinate and clinical. The book's word is the plainest one there is, and 知足's entry already forbids *sufficiency* for the compound.

**"Deficient," "deficiency."** A ledger or a diagnosis. Rejected for chapter 77 in 2026-08 for the same reason (`notes/translation.md` → *Ch 77*), and it would put a verdict on what the Chinese only measures.

**"Too little," "too much."** Crisp, and it broke chapter 77's thread: nature does not trim excess to a standard; it restores *enough*. Also, *too much* was the English for 有餘 (*yǒu yú*), which is 餘 (*yú* — surplus)'s compound and has no entry; it now reads *more than enough* in all five of its lines at 20 and 77.

**"Runs short," "short."** *Short* is 寡 (*guǎ* — few)'s word at chapter 63 (寡信, *"short on trust"*, `glossary/xin-信.md`). *Runs short* gave chapters 17 and 23 a link to 63 that the Chinese does not make.

**"Satisfied," "content" for 足 alone.** The feeling, where 足 is the measure. *Contentment* belongs to the compound 知足 at chapter 46, where knowing makes it a state of the person.

### Working register

| line | form | English |
|---|---|---|
| 常德乃足 (28) | predicate | *is enough* |
| 此三者以為文不足 (19) · 廣德若不足 (41) | 不足, predicate | *not enough* |
| 信不足焉 (17, 23) | 不足, of a noun | *there is not enough trust* |
| 視之不足見 · 聽之不足聞 (35) | 不足 + verb | *not enough to see / hear* |
| 有餘 / 不足 (77 ×3) | the pair | *more than enough / not enough* |
| 不足以 + verb (41, 48) | idiom | *cannot* / *wouldn't be* |
| 知足 (33, 44, 46) · 知足之足 (46) | the compound | `glossary/zhizu-知足.md`: *knowing you have enough* · *contentment* |
| 足下 (64) | the body part | *beneath your feet* |

### What no rule can enforce

**知足 and 足 are two entries over one character.** The compound's *contentment* at chapter 46 is 足 inside 知足, and `concordance.py --english "contentment"` credits the compound. That is by design.

**"Deficient" is 缺 (quē — chipped)'s at chapter 45**, 大成若缺 (*dà chéng ruò quē* — *"great completeness seems deficient"*), which holds no 足. The list forbids it only where 足 stands, so it fires there as a notice and nowhere as an error.

**有餘 (yǒu yú) has no entry.** *More than enough* now reads in all five of its lines at 20 and 77, and nothing holds it there. At chapter 53, 財貨有餘 (*cái huò yǒu yú*) reads *excess wealth and goods*, and 餘 alone takes three more Englishes (24 *leftover*, 54 *abundant*, 79 *remains*). `concordance.py 餘` is the check.

### Left open

**餘 (yú — surplus) may want an entry.** It stands in six chapters with five Englishes, and its compound 有餘 is now one English everywhere but chapter 53's *excess*. Whether 53's ornate court, gorged and armed, *has more than enough wealth and goods* is a line for Shalom.

**The foot and the measure.** The book puts the foot at chapter 64 and the measure everywhere else, and never sets them side by side. Whether a reader in Laozi's time heard the foot inside *enough* is not known.
