## 利 (Lì) — Benefit

*Say "lee," falling. Ten lines across nine chapters. What a thing gives: the harvest the blade brings in, and once the blade's own edge.*

**Locked: 利 → "benefit," and "beneficial" in 利器 (lì qì); *sharp* at chapter 53.** *(Shalom's calls: beneficial tools at 36 and 57, 2026-11-19; chapters 11 and 19 and the lock, 2026-11-20.)* **害 (*hài* — harm), its opposite, is covered here.** *(Not "profit," which puts a merchant in a farmer's word. Not "advantage," which makes it a position over others. Not "gain," which is 得 (dé — get)'s.)*

### The graph: a blade and the grain it cuts

**The early graph is grain beside a blade: 禾 (*hé* — a grain stalk) and 刂 (*dāo* — a knife), the harvest brought in** (`dong-chinese`). In an agricultural world the harvest was what work gave back. The Shuowen keeps the blade and reads its edge:

> 利，銛也。从刀。和然後利，从和省。
> *"利 is sharp. From 刀 (dāo — a knife). Harmony, and then benefit: from part of 和 (hé — harmony)."*

**So the word holds both the edge and what the edge brings in**, and the book uses both. The same 利 gives the sound to 犁 (*lí* — the plough). **Where the picture stops:** the book's benefit is never bought or counted. It is what water does for the countless things, what nature does and the sage's way imitates.

### Ten lines, and benefit is what is given

> *Water is masterful at benefiting the countless things and does not contend.*
>
> 水善利萬物而不爭，
>
> — chapter 8

> *The way of nature benefits and does not harm.*
> *The way of the sage acts and does not contend.*
>
> 天之道，利而不害；
> 聖人之道，為而不爭。
>
> — chapter 81

> *Of these two, one benefits and one harms.*
>
> 此兩者，或利或害。
>
> — chapter 73

> *They cannot be benefited, and cannot be harmed.*
>
> 不可得而利，不可得而害；
>
> — chapter 56

**The book's first and last 利 belong to water and nature, and both give.** Water benefits the countless things and does not contend (8); the way of nature benefits and does not harm (81), and Heshang Gong reads it as birth: 天生萬物愛育之令長大，無所傷害 (*"nature births the countless things, loves and rears them, and lets them grow, harming none"*). **Wherever 利 and 害 stand together, they are one scale**: Heshang Gong at 73, 活身為利，殺身為害 (*"keeping the body alive is benefit, killing it is harm"*); Wang Bi at 56, 可得而利則可得而害也 (*"whatever can be benefited can be harmed"*), the same shape as his prized and cheap in the next line (`glossary/gui-jian-貴賤.md`).

### Chapter 19: abandon benefit, and the people benefit

> *Cut off sagehood, abandon cleverness, and the people benefit a hundredfold.*
> *Cut off humaneness, abandon duty, and the people return to devotion and tenderness.*
> *Cut off skill, abandon benefit, and there are no thieves.*
>
> 絕聖棄智，民利百倍；
> 絕仁棄義，民復孝慈；
> 絕巧棄利，盜賊無有。
>
> — chapter 19

**The chapter prints 利 twice, and the English now does too** (Shalom's call, 2026-11-20, where the third line had read *profit*). Give up 利, and the people 利 a hundredfold: the paradox is the Chinese's. Wang Bi says why each thing given up is a good: 聖智，才之善也；仁義，人之善也；巧利，用之善也 (*"sagehood and knowing are the best of talent; humaneness and duty the best of persons; skill and benefit the best of use"*). Heshang Gong hears greed in the third, 棄利者，塞貪路，閉權門也 (*"abandoning benefit blocks the road of greed and closes the gate of power"*), and the harvest in the first, 農事修 (*"the farming is in order"*). *Profit* would have taken his side, and made the third good an evil before the chapter gave it up.

### Chapter 11: what presence gives

> *So presence gives the benefit,*
> *and absence gives the use.*
>
> 故有之以為利，
> 無之以為用。
>
> — chapter 11

**利 against 用 (*yòng* — use).** The hub, the vessel and the room are each made of something and used for their nothing. Wang Bi: 有之所以為利，皆賴無以為用 (*"presence is what gives benefit; all rely on absence for use"*). Heshang Gong makes 利 the thing itself: 利，物也 (*"benefit means things"*). *Benefit* replaced *advantage* here (Shalom's call, 2026-11-20), so the pot's walls give what water and nature give. 用 stays covered in `glossary/wu-you-無有.md`.

### 利器 (lì qì): beneficial tools, and the sharp sword

> *A state's beneficial tools cannot be shown.*
>
> 國之利器不可以示人。
>
> — chapter 36

> *Wearing ornate, colorful clothes, carrying sharp swords, gorged on food and drink, possessing more than enough wealth and goods —*
>
> 服文綵，帶利劍，厭飲食，財貨有餘；
>
> — chapter 53

**利器 at 36 and 57 reads *beneficial tools*, in Wang Bi's own words at both** (Shalom's call, 2026-11-19): 利國之器 (*"tools that benefit the state"*) and 利己之器 (*"tools that benefit oneself"*) (`notes/translation.md` → *Ch 36, 57*). **利劍 at 53 is a sword's edge, *sharp***, the Shuowen's sense, worn on the hip of a court that has more than enough. It is the one flexion.

### 害 (hài — harm)

> 害，傷也。从宀从口。宀、口，言从家起也。丯聲。
> *"害 is to injure. From 宀 (mián — a roof) and 口 (kǒu — a mouth): roof and mouth, words that arise from the household. 丯 (jiè) for sound."*

**害 reads *harm* in all five of its lines**: set against 利 at 56, 73 and 81, and alone at 35 (*"It comes unharmed"*) and 66 (*"the people are not harmed"*). It has no line that 利 does not explain, and it lives here as 利's opposite. *Hurt*, *injure* and *damage* are its synonyms and are not used.

### What is set aside

**"Profit," "profitable."** The merchant's word, and a verdict: in English *profit* is already a little suspect. The graph is a harvest, and the book's 利 is given by water and nature, which sell nothing.

**"Advantage."** A position over someone. The book's benefit takes nothing from anyone, and in chapter 11 it is what a pot's walls give.

**"Gain."** 得 (*dé* — get)'s, at chapters 22, 39 and 44 (`glossary/de-shi-得失.md`).

**"Interest," "good."** *Interest* is a ledger; *good* is 善 (*shàn* — masterful)'s, where the book names the category.

### What no rule can enforce

**"Benefit" in a chapter without 利.** The gate cannot see it. Chapter 43 read *"the benefit of non-doing"* for 益 (*yì*) until 2026-11-20, when it became *the abundance of non-doing* (`glossary/yi-益.md`); `concordance.py --english "benefit"` is the check, and every line it finds should be 利's.

**害 is a cover**, so nothing gates its *harm*. `concordance.py --english "harm"` is the check; it also matches inside *harmony*, 和 (*hé*)'s word, and credits that entry, so read its *not backed* list as 害's five lines.

### Left open

**The edge and the harvest at chapter 36.** *Beneficial tools* follows Wang Bi. Han Fei reads the state's 利器 as reward and punishment, sharp instruments in a ruler's hand (`notes/translation.md` → *Ch 36*). The English keeps the benefit and lets the edge go.
