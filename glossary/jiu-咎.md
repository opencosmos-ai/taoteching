## 咎 (Jiù) — Fault

*Say "jyo," falling. Two lines, in two chapters, and both times it is self-inflicted.*

**Locked: 咎 → "fault."** *(Not "blame," which is 尤's. Not "calamity," which is 殃's. Not "disaster" either, but that is a ruling no check can hold; see below.)*

### The graph: a person at odds

> 咎，**災**也。从人从各。各者，相違也。
> *"Disaster. From 人 (rén — a person) and 各 (gè — each, apart). 各 means being at odds."* — 說文解字 (*Shuōwén Jiězì*, the Han dictionary)

The graph holds two things: **a person at odds, and the disaster that follows.** 說文 defines 各 on its own as 異辭也 (*"words of difference"*), a mouth and a foot that 有行而止之，不相聽也 — *"set out, and are stopped: they do not heed one another."* The falling-out is in the picture; the falling is in the definition.

### Two lines, both self-inflicted

- **Ch 9** — 富貴而驕，自遺其咎 (*fù guì ér jiāo, zì yí qí jiù* — "wealthy, high and arrogant, one leaves oneself one's own 咎") → *Grow arrogant with wealth and status, and you leave yourself the fault.*
- **Ch 46** — 咎莫大於欲得 (*jiù mò dà yú yù dé* — "no 咎 greater than wanting to get") → *No greater fault than craving to acquire.*

**自遺 (*zì yí* — to leave to oneself) is the character's grammar.** Nobody hands you 咎. The arrogant leave it to themselves at ch 9, and at ch 46 it is the craving's own. 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) on ch 9 names what is left: 而反驕恣，必被禍患也 — *"if instead they are arrogant and unrestrained, they will surely suffer disaster and trouble."* 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) gives it a time limit: 不可長保也 — *"it cannot be kept long."*

### What is set aside

**"disaster"** is 說文's own word, and the book will not let 咎 have it. Ch 46 sets two characters in a parallel couplet, 禍莫大於不知足 / 咎莫大於欲得 (*"no greater disaster than discontent / no greater fault than craving to acquire"*), and the couplet only reads if the two differ. 禍 (*huò*) takes *disaster*.

**"calamity"** belongs to 殃 (*yāng*), at ch 52's 無遺身殃 (*"leave no calamity for the body"*). 說文 makes the two near-twins, defining 殃 as 咎也. The same verb, 遺 (*yí* — to leave behind), stands in both lines: at ch 9 you leave yourself 咎, at ch 52 you leave the body no 殃. The English keeps the verb the same and the nouns apart.

**"blame"** belongs to 尤 (*yóu*), at ch 8, and it points the wrong way. Blame is what other people do to you; 咎 is what you do to yourself. See `you-尤.md`.

### What no rule can enforce

***"disaster"* is ruled out for 咎 but is not on the `forbidden:` list, because it cannot be.** Ch 46 holds both 咎 and 禍, and *disaster* is 禍's word on the line just above. A rule keyed to 咎 would fire on 禍's own line. The couplet is held by the entries and by the reader.

### This lock stays in this book

**The I Ching renders 咎 *blame*, and both rulings are intended.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-10-20.)* There 咎 is the verdict word, 无咎 (*wú jiù* — "no blame") ninety-two times, set against 譽 (*yù* — praise) at its hexagrams 2 and 28. *Blame* and *praise* are English's own pair, and *fault* is kept free there for 過 (*guò* — to overstep). The Laozi is a different context and may use the word differently. So this entry carries `binds: [taoteching]`, and the I Ching, which vendors this glossary's locks as its own, does not inherit it.

### Left open

**The cost of *fault* is the disaster half.** English *fault* says *the failing is yours*. The character says *the trouble is yours*, and 說文 and 河上公 both weight the trouble. *Fault* keeps the person at odds and the self-inflicting grammar, and loses the fall. The misfortune words, 禍 · 凶 (*xiōng* — ill-omened) · 咎 · 殃 · 患 (*huàn* — trouble), are still being sorted as a family (`WORKLIST.md` T2-53), and 咎's English could move with them.
