## 遺 (Yí) — Leave

*Say "yee," rising; in its second word, "way," falling. Three lines in three chapters. In each, something is left: passed to someone, or left behind on the way.*

**Recommended: 遺 → "leave": *leave to* where something passes to someone (9, 52), *left behind* where the speaker is what remains (20).** *(Not "lose," which is 失 (shī — lose)'s. Not "abandon," which is 棄 (qì — cast away)'s, and Heshang Gong's gloss. Not "missed out," which neither commentator reads. Not "bequeath," which gives the graph's second word an English of its own.)*

### The graph: two hands, something falling from them, and the walking

> 遺，亾也。从辵䝿聲。
> *"遺 is gone missing. From 辵 (chuò — walking), with 䝿 (guì — an older form of 貴, dear) for sound."*

**亾 (*wáng* — gone) is the Shuowen's 亡 (*wáng* — gone, fled), defined 逃也 (*"to flee"*): gone from where it was.** No oracle-bone form is listed in the sources read here. Dong Chinese's earliest is a bronze of the early Western Zhou, c. 1000 BCE. **The CUHK Multi-function Chinese Character Database describes the bronze forms as 象兩手持一物，下有數點 (*"two hands holding a thing, with several dots below"*), written beside 辵 or 彳 (*chì* — a step).** Following Zhang Shichao and colleagues, and Chen Jian, it reads the dots as something falling or scattering from the hands, and gives the first sense as 遺落 (*yí luò* — to drop and leave lying). Some bronze forms lack the 貝 (*bèi* — cowrie) that later sits at the bottom.

**What 道 (*dào* — the Tao) is, 遺 drops along.** 道 is 所行道也 (*"the road one walks"*), from 辵 and 首 (*shǒu* — head) (`notes/reading.md` → *道 is a road*). 遺 is built on the same walking, with hands letting something go. One is the way, the other what falls on it. Fourteen characters in the book carry 辵, and 遺 is one (`chapters/015.md` → Notes). And one manuscript writes the two graphs for each other: silk A, at chapter 52 (below).

**Where the picture stops:** in the book's three lines nothing falls from anyone's hands. By the Shuowen the hands and their load are only a sound: 䝿 is 貴 (*guì* — dear) in an older form, and spells the word. 辵 marks a motion, not a road anyone points to. The tie to 道 is a radical and one scribe's graph. Baxter and Sagart reconstruct the two words apart, 遺 with a uvular initial, *\*ɢ*, and 道 with an *\*l*, so the tie is not a shared word.

### One graph, two words: *yí*, to be left behind, and *wèi*, to leave to someone

Dong Chinese gives 遺 two readings in Middle Chinese: *ywij*, now *yí*, "leave; reject," and *ywijH*, now *wèi*, "to present."

- **Han Fei uses the second.** Commenting on chapter 36's *"If you would take from it, you must first give to it,"* he writes 晉獻公將欲襲虞，遺之以璧馬 (*"Duke Xian of Jin, meaning to raid Yu, presented it with jade discs and horses"*). The gift is given before the taking.
- **The pronunciation notes in our Heshang Gong text spell the second at chapters 9 and 52:** 遺，唯季反 (*wéi jì fǎn* — "the initial of 唯 (*wéi* — yes), the final of 季 (*jì* — season)"), which gives *wèi*. At chapter 20 they spell nothing, and his gloss reads *yí*.

**English *leave* holds both words.** *Leave behind* is *yí*: what is left is what remains when someone goes. *Leave to* is *wèi*: what you leave someone, you give them. One English carries the graph's two words, so the book can keep one.

### Three lines, and in each something is left

> *Grow arrogant with wealth and status, and you leave yourself the fault.*
>
> 富貴而驕，自遺其咎。
>
> — chapter 9

> *The crowd all have more than enough.*
> *I alone seem to have missed out.*
>
> 眾人皆有餘，
> 而我獨若遺。
>
> — chapter 20

> *Use the outer light, return to your inner clarity,*
> *and leave no calamity for the body.*
>
> 用其光，復歸其明，
> 無遺身殃；
>
> — chapter 52

**At chapters 9 and 52 something is left to someone. At chapter 20 the speaker is what is left.** The arrogant leave the fault to themselves, 自 (*zì* — self) (`glossary/jiu-咎.md`). The one who returns to clarity leaves no calamity to the body. Both are *wèi* in the Heshang Gong notes. At chapter 20 nothing is handed over. Beside a crowd with more than enough, the speaker seems like what got left. The chapter ends with the same speaker: *"I prize feeding from the mother."*

### What the commentators did with chapter 20: lost, or cast aside

- **Wang Bi:** 眾人無不有懷有志，盈溢胸心，故曰皆有餘也。我獨廓然無為無欲，若遺失之也 (*"Among the crowd there is no one without something held and something willed, brimming in the breast, and so it says they all have more than enough. I alone am open and empty, without doing, without desire, as if I had lost it"*).
- **Heshang Gong:** 我獨如遺棄，似於不足也 (*"I alone am as if cast aside, seeming not to have enough"*).

**Each sets a second character beside 遺 to say what it is:** 失 (*shī* — lose) for Wang Bi, 棄 (*qì* — cast away) for Heshang Gong. Both are a going-from: the thing let fall, and the thing put down and walked away from. ***Left behind* holds both,** since what is dropped and what is set down are each left behind. And it keeps the 若 (*ruò* — seem): the speaker only seems so, and the line passes no verdict on the crowd or on them.

### The silks: 我獨遺 at chapter 20, and 道 for 遺 at chapter 52

**At chapter 20, silk A reads 我獨遺 (*wǒ dú yí* — "I alone am left")**, with no 而 (*ér* — yet) and no 若. Silk B's transcription has no counterpart to the clause. Guodian attests only the chapter's opening and Han Fei does not quote it, so silk A is the oldest witness to this line, and it states what our base only seems (`sources/variants.yaml` fork 134).

**At chapter 52, silk A reads 毋道身央 (*wú dào shēn yāng* — "do not *dào* the body calamity")**, writing 道 where silk B and every transmitted text have 遺 (fork 135). It reads two ways:

- **A loan by sound.** Zhengzhang reconstructs both words with an *\*l-* initial, 遺 *\*lul* and 道 *\*l'uːʔ*, close enough for a scribe to write one for the other. Baxter and Sagart set them apart.
- **道 in its sense *to lead*.** Dong Chinese gives 道 a second reading, "show the way." Then the line says *"do not lead the body into calamity."*

**At chapter 9 the oldest witnesses agree on 遺.** Guodian, as 6dsy transcribes it, reads 貴富驕，自遺咎也 (*guì fù jiāo, zì yí jiù yě* — "high, wealthy and arrogant, one leaves oneself the fault"), and both silks read 貴富而驕，自遺咎也, the same with 而 (*ér* — and). All three lack our base's 其 (*qí* — its). The slips' own graph is not read here.

### What is set aside

**"Lose," "lost."** It is 失 (*shī*)'s English, locked with 得 (*dé* — get) (`glossary/de-shi-得失.md`), and a word already spoken for is not available (`process/principles/already-spoken-for.md`). Wang Bi's 遺失 brings 失 in beside 遺, which is the sign that they are two words.

**"Abandon," "cast aside."** *Abandon* is 棄 (*qì*)'s at chapters 19 and 27, and *cast aside* is Heshang Gong's 遺棄 in English, a gloss put into the verse (`process/principles/commentary-is-not-a-rendering.md`). Both also bring in someone who did the abandoning, and the line names no one.

**"Missed out."** A failure to get one's share. Neither commentator reads it, and it makes chapter 20 the one 遺 line without the character's English.

**"Left out."** Exclusion from a share, a social grievance. Neither commentator reads it either.

**"Lacking," "in want."** Heshang Gong's 似於不足 (*"seeming not to have enough"*) in English. 不足 (*bù zú* — not enough) is 足's, and the line prints no 不足.

**"Bequeath."** The *wèi* word at chapters 9 and 52 in a legal register. It would give the graph's second word an English of its own and part what *leave* holds together.

**"Give," "present."** 與 (*yǔ* — give) is *give* at chapter 36. And the *wèi* lines are no gifts: what is left is a fault, or a calamity, or none.

### Working register

| line | word | form | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| 自遺其咎 (9) | *wèi*, by the Heshang Gong note | to leave something to someone, here oneself | *you leave yourself the fault* |
| 而我獨若遺 (20) | *yí* | a state, under 若 (*ruò* — seem) | *I alone seem left behind* (recommended) |
| 無遺身殃 (52) | *wèi*, by the Heshang Gong note | to leave something for someone | *leave no calamity for the body* |

***Behind* belongs to the verb, not the line.** At chapter 20, bare *"I alone seem left"* would read as *the only one remaining*. *Behind* makes it what is left as others go. *To* and *for* at 9 and 52 carry the *wèi* word's receiver, which the Chinese names: 自 (*zì* — self), 身 (*shēn* — body).

### What no rule can enforce

**The reverse direction.** `forbidden:` holds *bequeath*, *cast aside* and *left out* out of 遺's chapters, and nothing there prints them. *Abandon* stays off the list: it is 棄 (*qì* — cast away)'s English at chapters 19 and 27, and the gate would raise a note at each of its lines. *Leave* and *left* are 遺's English, but *left* also stands in lines with no 遺: chapter 3's *"nothing is left ungoverned,"* and chapters 37 and 48, *"nothing is left undone,"* for 無不 (*wú bù* — "there is nothing not"). Chapter 24's *leftover* is 餘 (*yú*)'s declared English, and *the left* of chapters 31, 34 and 79 is 左 (*zuǒ* — left), another word. No gate sees these. Run `concordance.py --english "left"` when a line elsewhere reaches for it.

### Left open

**Chapter 20's English is Shalom's call.** The recommendation is *"I alone seem left behind,"* for the reasons above. The line now reads *"I alone seem to have missed out."*

**Silk A states what our base only seems.** Its 我獨遺, without 若, would read *"I alone am left behind."* It is the oldest witness to the line, so the tie-break order weighs it, against both commentators, who gloss the base's 若 with their own *as if*. Read from a Wikisource transcription, not yet checked against a print edition (`WORKLIST.md` T2-95).

**Silk A's 道 at chapter 52:** a loan by sound, or *"do not lead the body into calamity."* The two reconstructions disagree about the sound, and no edition's reading of the line is consulted here (fork 135, T2-95).

**The Heshang Gong text pulls two ways at chapter 52.** Its pronunciation note spells *wèi*, while his gloss, 內視存神，不爲漏失 (*"looking within and keeping the spirit, not letting it leak away and be lost"*), reads the *yí* word. The notes are printed in a Song edition, and when they entered the text is not established here.

**Whether *nothing is left undone* (37, 48) keeps its *left*.** It is supplied English, and *leave* is now one character's. Its phrasing belongs with 無為 (*wú wéi* — non-doing), not here.

**The graph before the seal script** is read here only through the CUHK database's description. The forms themselves, and the Guodian slips' graph at chapter 9, are not.
