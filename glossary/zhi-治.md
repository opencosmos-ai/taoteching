## 治 (Zhì) — To Govern

*Say "jr," falling. Twelve lines across nine chapters — the book's ordinary word for what a ruler does, and 說文解字 files it as a river.*

**Locked: 治 → "govern," with *order* at chapters 8 and 64.** *(Never "control," never "manage," never "administer." Never *rule* either — but that one is a ruling no check can hold; see* What no rule can enforce *below.)*

### The graph: a river in Shandong

> 治，**水**。出東萊曲城陽丘山，南入海。从水台聲。
> *"治 is **a watercourse**. It rises at Mount Yangqiu in Quchang, Donglai, and runs south into the sea. From 水 (shuǐ — water), with 台 (tái) as the sound."* — 說文解字

The dictionary's headword sense is not *to govern* at all. **It is the name of a particular river.** Everything the character later means — to regulate, to put right, to keep in working order, to rule a state — grows out of what one does to moving water: dredge it, bank it, let it run where it should run.

That path is not a curiosity of this one word. It is the oldest governing metaphor China has: 大禹**治**水 (*dà yǔ zhì shuǐ*) — Yu the Great **治**-ed the floods, and was given the realm for it. He did it, the story insists, by **channelling** rather than by damming. So when the Laozi reaches for a word for governing, the word it reaches for already carries a theory of governing inside it.

**And the book puts it in the water chapter.** 政善治 stands in chapter 8, which opens 上善若水 (*shàng shàn ruò shuǐ* — *"the most masterful are like water"*). 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) glosses 治 there through water and nothing else: **无有不洗清且平也** — *"there is nothing it does not wash clean and level."* Washing, clearing, levelling. He is reading the river in the character.

Ten of twelve are *govern*, and the two that are not are the two where the object is **not a polity**.

### What 治 is, by what it is not

**說文 defines 亂 (*luàn* — disorder) as 治** — 亂，治也 — so the two characters are each other's definition, and **chapter 64 prints them in one clause**: 為之於未有，**治**之於未**亂**, *"handle it before it exists; order it before it falls into disorder."* That line is the whole argument for keeping the English pair visibly related. See `luan-亂.md`.

**And 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) says what the governing consists of, at chapter 60:**

> 治大國若烹小鮮 → **不擾也。**躁則多害，靜則全真。故其國彌大，而其主彌靜。
> *"治 is **not to disturb**. Agitation (躁 zào) brings much harm; stillness (靜 jìng) keeps the genuine whole. So the larger the state, the stiller its ruler."*

烹小鮮 (*pēng xiǎo xiān*) is cooking a small fish: turn it too often and it falls apart. **So 治 in this book is not intervention.** It is keeping a thing in its course — which is exactly what one does to a river, and exactly what 亂 is the failure of.

### The flexion, and why it exists

**Chapters 8 and 64 take *order* rather than *govern*, and in both the object is not a state.**

At **64**, 治之 (*zhì zhī* — *"治 it"*) has for its object something 王弼 calls 㣲脆 (*wēi cuì* — *"faint and brittle"*): a situation while it is still a thread or two. *Govern it* would be strange English for a brittle small thing, and it would also drop the pairing with 亂 standing three characters away.

At **8**, the line is 政善治, and **政 (*zhèng* — government) is already in it.** 說文 makes them near-twins — 政，正也。从攴从正 (*"政 is 正 (zhèng — upright); from 攴 (pū — a hand holding a rod) and 正, which is also the sound"*) — and modern Chinese fuses the two characters into 政治 (*zhèngzhì*), the ordinary word for *politics*. Chinese can print the doublet; English cannot say *"in government, masterful at governing"* without sounding as though it has stuttered. **The flexion here is forced by English, not by the Chinese**, and it is recorded rather than hidden for that reason.

### What is set aside

**"rule"** puts a person on a throne. 治 is what you do to the thing, not authority over it, and the book is at pains to say the sage does not 宰 (*zǎi* — lord over, ch 10 and 51). *Rule* also imports the ruler-word 王 (*wáng*), which has its own entry.

**"control"** and **"manage"** both invert 王弼's 不擾 (*bù rǎo* — not to disturb). A controlled river is a dammed one, and the metaphor inside the character is the other kind of engineering.

**"administer"** is bureaucratic, which is the register this edition keeps out, and it is 政's territory rather than 治's.

**"order" as the primary** was weighed and declined. It suits 8 and 64 and fails the other ten: *"the people are hard to order"* (65, 75) is not English, and *"ordering a large state is like cooking a small fish"* (60) loses the office the line is about.

### The working register

- of a state or a people → **govern** (治大國 → "governing a large state"; 民之難治 → "the people are hard to govern")
- as the state's own condition → **ungoverned** (無不治 → "nothing is left ungoverned")
- where the object is not a polity → **order** (治之於未亂 → "order it before it falls into disorder")

### 政, settled — and 正, which is not

**政 (*zhèng* — government) is settled as of 2026-09-23: *government* in all three of its lines**, so chapter 8 reads *"In government, masterful at order."* It had been *"In governing"* here and *"the government"* at chapter 58 twice (其政悶悶 · 其政察察).

**The decision turned on part of speech, not on consistency.** 其政 (*qí zhèng* — ***its*** government) can only be nominal, since 其 takes a noun — so the book's own two unambiguous uses of 政 are nouns. And chapter 8's other six first slots are each rendered by **their own** part of speech: 居 (*jū* — to dwell), 與 (*yǔ* — to give) and 動 (*dòng* — to move) as verbs, 心 (*xīn* — heart), 言 (*yán* — speech) and 事 (*shì* — affairs) as nouns. **The stanza alternates three and four, and that mix is the thing to keep.** Making 政 the one slot rendered against its own part of speech was the error, and it is what pushed 治 onto *order* in the first place.

**正 (*zhèng* — upright) is in the room and stays untouched.** 說文 defines 政 *as* 正 — 政，正也。从攴从正, *"from 攴 (pū — a hand holding a rod) and 正, which is also the sound"*; our base at chapter 8 reads 政 where 河上公's reads 正, a fork already logged; and 正 stands at chapter 57 inside another 治 line, 以正治國 (*"govern the state with uprightness"*). **正 is one of the calls Shalom has deferred** (`WORKLIST.md` T4-2, 正/奇), so 政 was settled without reaching into it.

### Left open

**居 (*jū* — to dwell) reads at least four ways and is unrendered twice.** It stands beside 治 in this very stanza, and chapter 8 gives it *settling* — correctly, because 處 (*chǔ*) holds *dwell* four lines above at 處眾人之所惡. But elsewhere 居 is *stay* (ch 2 ×2), *at home* (ch 31), *homes* (ch 72), and **nothing at all at chapter 38**, where 不居其薄 and 不居其華 are folded into 處's verb. `WORKLIST.md` T2-57.

### What no rule can enforce

***"rule" is ruled out in the verse, and it is not on the `forbidden:` list, because it cannot be.*** Those are two different facts: the ruling holds, and the enforcement does not exist. **宰 (*zǎi* — to lord over) stands with 治 in chapter 10**, where 長而不宰 legitimately reads *"growing them without **ruling** them"* — so a rule keyed to 治's presence would fire on 宰's own line. *Control*, *manage* and *administer* are held by the build; *rule* is held only by this entry and by the reader.
