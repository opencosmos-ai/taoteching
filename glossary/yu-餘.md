## 餘 (Yú) — More Than Enough

*Say "yoo," rising. Nine lines across six chapters. What is past what you need: on the table, in the treasury, in a grudge.*

**Locked: 餘 → "more than enough" in 有餘 (yǒu yú), and "leftover" before a noun; *abundant* at chapter 54 and *remains* at 79.** *(Shalom's calls, 2026-11-20, line by line.)* *(Not "excess," which passes the verdict a scene should pass. Not "surplus," a ledger's word. Not "superfluous," which judges what it names.)*

### The graph: food, and plenty of it

> 餘，饒也。从食余聲。
> *"餘 is plenty. From 食 (shí — food), with 余 (yú) for sound."*

**The word is built on food: what is on the table after everyone has eaten.** Chapter 24 still uses it that way, 餘食 (*yú shí* — leftover food), and Wang Bi reads it so: 盛饌之餘 (*"the leftovers of a rich feast"*). **Where the picture stops:** the book's 餘 is mostly not food. It is wealth (53), cleverness and wanting (20, by Heshang Gong and Wang Bi), integrity (54), and a grudge (79). The table is the measure; what is left on it changes.

### 有餘 (yǒu yú): more than enough, and the book's *enough*

> *The crowd all have more than enough.*
>
> 眾人皆有餘，
>
> — chapter 20

> *Wearing ornate, colorful clothes, carrying sharp swords, gorged on food and drink, possessing more than enough wealth and goods —*
>
> 服文綵，帶利劍，厭飲食，財貨有餘；
>
> — chapter 53

> *Who can have more than enough*
> *and offer it up to the world?*
> *Only those who are with the Tao.*
>
> 孰能有餘以奉天下？
> 唯有道者。
>
> — chapter 77

**有餘 is the far side of 足 (*zú* — enough).** Chapter 77 sets them as a pair four times: nature takes from more than enough and mends what is not enough (`glossary/zu-足.md`). Heshang Gong glosses chapter 53's hoard with the other word: 多嗜欲無足時 (*"many cravings, never a time of enough"*). **So the English keeps one phrase for 有餘 in all six of its lines** (Shalom's call, 2026-11-20, at 53, where it had read *excess*): the reader hears the court of chapter 53 and the crowd of chapter 20 as people who have passed the line that 知足 (*zhī zú* — knowing you have enough) knows.

**The book does not condemn the surplus itself.** Chapter 77 asks who can *have* more than enough and offer it to the world, and answers, those with the Tao. Heshang Gong: 誰能居有餘之位…以奉天下不足者乎 (*"who can hold a place of more than enough… and offer it to those in the world who do not have enough?"*). What the book condemns is keeping it: the crowd's (20), the court's (53), the way of people that offers it up to those who already have it (77). *More than enough* states the measure and leaves the verdict to the lines.

### 餘 before a noun: what is left over

> *Measured by the Tao, these are called leftover food and wasted motion.*
>
> 曰餘食贅行，
>
> — chapter 24

> *Settle a great grudge*
> *and some grudge always remains.*
>
> 和大怨，
> 必有餘怨；
>
> — chapter 79

**Before a noun, 餘 is the residue**: the food left on the table (24), the grudge left after a settling (79). Wang Bi on 79: 大怨已至而德和之，其傷不復，故有餘怨也 (*"the great grudge has come and integrity settles it, but the wound does not heal, so grudge remains"*). Heshang Gong has it reach good people afterward. **Chapter 79 keeps *remains*** (Shalom's call, 2026-11-20): the line closes a sentence and wants the weight, and the residue is in the verb.

### Chapter 54: abundant

> *Cultivate this in your family, and its integrity becomes abundant.*
>
> 修之於家，其德乃餘；
>
> — chapter 54

**Heshang Gong's text reads 其德有餘 here**, the compound itself, and glosses 有餘慶及於來世子孫 (*"more than enough blessing, reaching descendants"*). Wang Bi glosses it with 有餘 too, and says 所施轉大 (*"what it extends grows greater"*): a family's integrity has more than enough, so it spills over to the community on the next rung. Han Fei counts it: 家之有餘者益衆 (*"the households with more than enough grow more numerous"*). **The verse keeps *abundant*** (Shalom's call, 2026-11-20), so the rung stays short among its neighbors, *authentic*, *grows*, *bountiful*, *universal*. The ladder's spillover is carried by the next rung instead of the word.

### What is set aside

**"Excess."** A verdict: *excess* is already too much. 有餘 measures and does not judge, and chapter 77 lets the one with the Tao have it. At chapter 29, *excess* renders 甚 (*shèn* — too far), which is a verdict, and keeps the word.

**"Surplus."** A ledger's word, and it fails the pointing test. It also loses the table the graph sets.

**"Superfluous," "redundant."** Judgments that the thing should not be there. Chapter 24's leftover food is not superfluous to the eater; it is what was left.

**"Too much."** Ch 77's English for 有餘 until 2026-11-20, and a verdict: `glossary/zu-足.md` → *What is set aside*.

### Working register

| line | form | English |
|---|---|---|
| 眾人皆有餘 (20) · 財貨有餘 (53) · 有餘 (77 ×4) | 有餘, to have more than needed | *more than enough* |
| 餘食 (24) | before a noun | *leftover* |
| 必有餘怨 (79) | before a noun, closing the line | *remains* (flexion) |
| 其德乃餘 (54) | predicate in the ladder | *abundant* (flexion) |

### What no rule can enforce

**"More than enough" is a phrase built on 足's word.** `concordance.py --english "enough"` credits 足 and lists 餘's lines as not backed. Read them as backed by 餘.

**"Excess" is forbidden only where 餘 stands.** It stays legal for 甚 (*shèn*) at chapter 29.

### Left open

**Chapter 54's spillover.** *Abundant* names a quantity. Heshang Gong's 有餘 and Wang Bi's 所施轉大 name a quantity that overflows to the next rung. The English keeps the rung short and lets the ladder say the rest.

**Chapter 79's residue and chapter 24's.** One construction, 餘 before a noun, reads *leftover* at 24 and *remains* at 79, by Shalom's call. A reader will not hear that the grudge and the food are the same leftovers.
