# Chapter 27

## Translation

Masterful traveling leaves no tracks.  
Masterful speaking is flawless.  
Masterful calculating uses no counting rods.  
Masterful security uses no locks, yet cannot be breached.  
Masterful tying uses no ropes, yet cannot be untied.

Therefore the sage is always masterful at rescuing people, and so no one is abandoned.  
Always masterful at rescuing things, and so nothing is abandoned.  
This is called clarity worn covered.

Therefore, the masterful one naturally teaches the novice.  
And the novice is the raw material of the master.

Not valuing the master and not loving their raw material is a sign of great confusion.  
Even those who appear intelligent are lost. This is the essential secret.

## Notes

**籌策 → "counting rods."** *(2026-10-14, Shalom's call. Was: "abacus.")* 籌策 (*chóu cè*) are counting rods and tally sticks, bamboo laid out to reckon. The abacus is attested centuries after the text.

**是謂襲明 → "This is called clarity worn covered."** *(2026-08-31, Pass C, `WORKLIST.md` T2-10.)* *(was: "This is called inherited clarity.")* **襲 (*xí*) is grave-clothes.** 說文解字: **左衽袍** — *"a robe with the lapel closed to the left."* The living close a robe right over left; the dead are dressed left over right. The graph is 衣 (*yī* — clothing) with 龖 abbreviated for sound, and every later sense — *to double, to succeed to, to fall upon* — descends from **one garment put on over another**. Inheritance is a derived sense (you "put on" what was worn before), and taking it as the primary one lost the layering.

**The chapter is built on marks not left.** It opens 善行無轍跡 (*shàn xíng wú zhé jì* — "masterful traveling leaves no tracks") and runs five masteries that leave nothing behind, then closes here. **The summary line says the clarity is under something** — the same gesture as 被褐懷玉 (*bèi hè huái yù* — "wears coarse cloth, carries jade at the breast," ch 70) and 光而不耀 (*guāng ér bù yào* — "bright but not dazzling," ch 58).

**Paired with ch 52's 是謂襲常, and the pair is why we keep 襲 there.** 明 (*míng* — clear-seeing) and 常 (*cháng* — the ever-present) are bonded by 知常曰明 (*"knowing the ever-present is called clarity"*), verbatim at ch 16 and ch 55; 是謂襲X is one gesture applied to both halves. See `notes/manuscript.md` — ch 52's text is contested and its English had been following the other character.
