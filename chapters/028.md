# Chapter 28

## Translation

Know the rooster, hold to the hen,  
serve as the world's ravine.  
Serve as the world's ravine, and the ever-present integrity never leaves you.  
Return again to the infant.

Know the white, hold to the black,  
serve as the world's pattern.  
Serve as the world's pattern, and the ever-present integrity never varies.  
Return again to the endless.

Know the white, hold to the soiled,  
serve as the world's valley.  
Serve as the world's valley, and the ever-present integrity is enough at last.  
Return again to the uncarved wood.

The uncarved wood scatters, and becomes vessels.  
The sage puts them to use and becomes chief of the officers.  
So the great carving severs nothing.

## Notes

**The chapter is a chain of things you could point at, and the English had been a chain of concepts.** *(2026-09-01, Pass D, `WORKLIST.md` T1-7, T1-15, T1-16, T3-5, and the last of T2-15.)* 說文解字 makes almost every term here a physical object — a bird's father and a bird's mother, a mountain watercourse with no outlet, a spring that runs through to a river, a carpenter's square, a roof's ridgepole, vessels, a knife — and the old English read *assertive energy · yielding energy · deep ravine · **reservoir** · pattern · the limitless · **master orchestrator** · **the great orchestrator***. **The pointing test failed on nearly every line**, and the replacements sat in one register: the mechanistic, with the modern spiritual idiom beside it. ***"Energy" renders no character at all*** — 氣 (*qì* — vital breath) is not in this chapter.

**雄 and 雌 are a rooster and a hen, and the reason is not the graph alone — it is that 牝/牡 have already taken *male* and *female*.** 說文 defines the two pairs in exactly parallel words in two different animal classes: 雄 **鳥父**也 / 雌 **鳥母**也, from 隹 (*zhuī* — a short-tailed bird); 牡 **畜父**也 / 牝 **畜母**也, from 牛 (*niú* — cattle). Our English gives 牝/牡 *the female* and *the male* at ch 6 (是謂玄牝), 55 and 61, so 雌/雄 must have their own words, and the graph says which. **In English ornithology *cock* and *hen* are precisely this pair** — the male and female of any species, as in *a cock pheasant* — and *cock* is unusable in modern English. **The cost: *rooster* is more specific than 雄, which is any male bird.** The pairing is what carries the sense.

**Both commentators read the pair as *position*, not species** — a reading for this note, not a rendering. 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*): 雄先之屬，雌後之屬也。知為天下之先也，必後也。是以聖人後其身而身先也 — *"雄 belongs to the class of going first, 雌 to the class of coming after. To know how to be first in the world, one must be behind. This is why the sage puts their person behind, and their person comes first."* 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*): 雄以喻尊，雌以喻卑 — *"雄 figures the honoured, 雌 the lowly"* — 就雌之柔和，如是則天下歸之，如水流入深谿也, *"come to the hen's softness, and the world returns to you as water flows into a deep ravine."*

**谷 → "valley," and *reservoir* was backwards.** 說文: 谷 is 泉出**通川**為谷 — *"where a spring comes out and **runs through to a river***" — while 谿 is 山瀆**無所通**者, *"a mountain watercourse with **no outlet**."* **The two are distinguished on whether the water gets out**, and a *reservoir* is a man-made tank that holds it in. **谷 is already *valley* in six other chapters** — 6, 15, 32, 39 ×2, 41 — so ch 28 alone was the outlier. 谿 keeps **ravine**; English cannot carry 說文's outlet distinction, and that is what this note is for.

**The template is the chapter, and ours broke it at every joint.** Three stanzas of 知其X，守其Y / 為天下Z / 為天下Z，常德不W / 復歸於V, with only the variables moving. Ours gave **守 three Englishes and then a fourth** (*"attune to"* ×3, then *"we must welcome"*), **復歸於 three shapes** (*"Become like an infant again" · "Return to the limitless" · "Return to the uncarved wood"*), invented ***we must***, and **dropped 常 — a locked term — from all three 常德**. The template now runs unbroken, which is T3-5.

**為天下X → "serve as the world's X," which is 為's own locked English** (*do / handle / serve as*), and it closes the last of T2-15's formula pairs: **ch 22 read *"sets a pattern for the world"*** for the identical 為天下式 and now reads *"serves as the world's pattern."* *Embody* rendered nothing.

**守 → "hold to,"** matching ch 32 (侯王若能守之) and ch 37, and **not pre-empting T2-19**, the holding family. 說文: **守官也。从宀从寸** — *"to hold an office; from 宀 (a roof) and 寸 (a rule of measure)"* — **keeping a charge you are answerable for.** *"Attune to"* inverted the grammar as well as the register: you attune yourself **to** something; you 守 something **as a duty**.

**白/黑 → "the white" and "the black," not *light* and *darkness*.** 光 (*guāng*) already holds **light** at ch 4, 52 and 56, and 明 (*míng*) is locked to **clarity**. 河上公 keeps the pair visual: 白以喻昭昭，黑以喻默默 — *"white figures the bright-bright, black the silent-silent"* — 當復守之以默默，如闇昧無所見, *"one should hold to the silent, as if dim and seeing nothing."*

**常德's three predicates, one at a time.** **不離** → *never leaves you*; 離's own graph is an oriole (說文: 黃倉庚也，鳴則蠶生, *"the oriole; when it sings the silkworms hatch"*) and the parting sense is a loan. **The *you* is supplied because English 離 needs an object, and 河上公 supplies exactly that**: 德常在，不復離於己. **不忒** → *never varies*; 說文 忒，更也 (*"to change"*), 王弼 忒，差也 (*"to err"*) — *varies* takes the graph and leaves the verdict out. **乃足** → *is enough at last*, where **乃 is the *only then*** that marks the triad closing; 足 is 知足's own word. *(河上公 reads 足 as 止: 足，止也。人能為天下谷，德乃止於己 — "integrity then comes to rest in them." A second reading, noted rather than merged.)*

**無極 → "the endless," not "the limitless."** *Limitless* is currently wearing 淵 (*yuān* — a deep pool) at ch 8, and both commentators gloss 無極 by **exhaustion**, not by extent: 王弼 不可窮也 (*"it cannot be exhausted"*), 河上公 歸身於無窮極也 (*"return the body to what has no exhausting limit"*). 說文 on 極 is worth keeping even though no English can carry it: **棟也**, *"the ridgepole of a roof"* — 無極 is a roof with no topmost beam.

**器 → "vessels," which is its lock** (*vessel / tool / implement*) and its English at ch 11, 29, 41 and 67; *"various tools"* invented *various*. **散 → "scatters,"** matching ch 64's 易散, and not *"is carved"* — the carving is 割's job three characters later. 王弼: 樸真也。真散則百行出，殊類生，若器也 — *"樸 is the genuine; when the genuine scatters the hundred conducts come out and different kinds are born, like vessels."*

**則為官長 follows 河上公 on the grammar, and 王弼 diverges.** 河上公: 聖人升用則為百官之元長也 — *"the sage, raised to use, **becomes** head chief of the hundred officers."* 王弼 reads 為 as *wèi*, "for them": 聖人因其分散，故**為之立**官長 — *"the sage, following their dispersion, **establishes** officers and chiefs for them."* **The line above settles it:** 樸散**則為**器 is the identical 則為 construction one line up, and if that is *becomes vessels*, this is *becomes chief*. **The grammar decides where the commentators split** — the ch 4 lesson, run the other way. 長 → *chief* also matches ch 67's 成器長.

**故大制不割 → "So the great carving severs nothing," and it is a paradox that had been erased.** **制 also carries this chapter's word at ch 32** (始制有名 — *"When the carving begins, there are names"*), and 說文 makes it a knife on timber: **裁也。从刀从未** — *"to cut out; from 刀 (a knife) and 未 (a grown tree)."* *(制 read *cutting* in both places until 2026-09-07, when it moved to **carving** so that 樸 — the **uncarved** — and the blade that ends it share one English root. Full argument in ch 32's notes.)* **割 is a different cut** — 說文 剥也, *"to flay, to strip"* — so it takes **sever**; 河上公 has 无所傷割, *"nothing wounded or cut."* The old *"the great orchestrator itself does not carve"* put **one mechanistic English on two different characters** (官長 and 大制) and lost the knife inside both. 王弼: 大制者，以天下之心為心，故無割也 — *"the great carving takes the world's heart as its own heart, and so there is no cutting."*

**The two em-dashes are gone as a side effect, not as a decision.** They stood in *"Embodying the deep ravine of the world — this is where…"* and its twin, both lines now rewritten. **T4-3 stays deferred**; ch 28 simply no longer appears on its list.

**知其榮，守其辱 → "Know the white, hold to the soiled," following both silks and the Zhuangzi.** *(2026-10-25, Shalom's call: "soiled" is the more honest rendering.)* *(was: "Know the glory, hold to the disgrace.")* Our base reads 榮 (*róng* — glory). Both Mawangdui silks read 白 (*bái* — white), and so does the *Zhuangzi*'s quotation of this stanza, which goes straight on to 受天下之詬 (*shòu tiān xià zhī gòu* — "take on the world's reviling"; one edition reads 垢, grime). *(Corrected 2026-10-25: this quoted the variant 垢 (*gòu* — grime) as the text. The *Zhuangzi*'s main text reads 詬 (*gòu* — reviling, shame), and records 垢 as another edition's reading.)* Beside white, 辱 (*rǔ*) is a soiling, as it is in ch 41's *"the whitest white seems soiled,"* and Heshang Gong glosses this very 辱 as 汚濁 (*wū zhuó* — dirty and murky) even in a text that reads glory. *Soiled* keeps both halves: the earth the valley is made of, and the low place in other people's eyes. *Disgrace* kept only the second. **We keep our stanza order**, which ends on the uncarved wood and runs straight into 樸散 (*pǔ sàn* — "the uncarved wood scatters"); the silks put the valley second. **The costs:** two stanzas in a row now open *"Know the white,"* and both commentaries gloss a 榮 the verse no longer prints. The *Huainanzi* (139 BCE) already read glory. `notes/manuscript.md`, `DISCOVERIES.md` §8.

**Not attested at Guodian** (~300 BCE), and **the stanza order is an editorial restoration** — the Yongle Dadian puts the 雄/雌 stanza second. See `notes/manuscript.md`.
