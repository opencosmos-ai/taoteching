# Chapter 41

## Translation

Those highest in service hear the Tao and practice it with diligence.  
Those middling in service hear the Tao and keep some, forget some.  
Those lowest in service hear the Tao and laugh aloud at it.  
If it weren’t laughed at, the Tao wouldn’t be what it is.

Therefore, as the established sayings have it:  
The clear Tao seems murky.  
The advancing Tao seems to retreat.  
The level Tao seems rough.  
The highest integrity seems a valley.  
The whitest white seems soiled.  
Boundless integrity seems not enough.  
Sturdy integrity seems slack.  
Substantial truth seems to waver.

The great square has no corners.  
The great vessel is never completed.  
The great sound is barely heard.  
The great image has no form.

The Tao is hidden and nameless.  
Only the Tao is masterful at lending, and at completing.

## Notes

**昧 (*mèi*) → *murky*, not *dark*.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-09-20. `WORKLIST.md` T2-54.)* 說文 gives the character both senses — 昧爽，旦明也 for the compound (*daybreak*), 一曰**闇**也 for the graph alone (*shut-in dark*) — so *dark* was defensible about 昧 and **unavailable** all the same: it is 玄 (*xuán*)'s English across six lines, including ch 1's 玄之又玄, *"dark, and dark again."* There is no 玄 in this chapter, so the old line made a reader hear the book's deepest word where the text has only the plain antonym of 明. *Dim* is 和 (*hé*, ch 4 and 56) and 冥 (*míng*, ch 21); *faint* is 微 (*wēi*); *dull* is 爽 and 悶. **The chime with 濁 (*zhuó* — *muddy*, ch 15 and 49) was weighed and accepted as resonance rather than an invented link** — the two are distinct enough to stand apart. 雲 (*yún* — cloud) occurs nowhere in the book, so *clouded* was free too and was declined for this.

**And the English must not settle the commentators, who diverge here.** 王弼: 光而不耀, *"bright but not dazzling"* — the light is present and merely unshowy, and he is quoting ch 58 at it. 河上公: 明道之人若**闇昧**，無所見, *"one of the clear Tao seems dark and obscure, as if seeing nothing"* — genuine obscurity. *Murky* holds both: a murky light is still light, and you cannot see through it.


**大器晚成 — our base prints 晚成, and the English follows the witnesses.** 王弼's text reads 晚成 (*wǎn chéng* — "completed late"); Mawangdui A reads 免成 and Guodian 曼成, both **"never completed."** We follow the witnesses, because *never* completes the parallel the other three lines make — 無隅 (no corners), 希聲 (barely heard), 無形 (no form) — each *"the great X lacks its defining mark."* Recorded in `sources/variants.yaml`; reasoning in `notes/manuscript.md`.

**The 建言 catalogue rebuilt, one saying per line.** *(2026-09-06.)* 故建言有之 (*gù jiàn yán yǒu zhī*) introduces a **quotation** — 河上公: 建設也，設言以有道, *"建 is to set up; sayings set up so as to have the Tao"*; 王弼: 建猶立也. What follows is twelve proverbs, and our English had packed them onto five lines while changing shape as it went: *"Bright Tao seems dark"* with no article against *"The highest integrity seems **like** a valley"* with one and an inserted *like*. **`--formulas 41` shows the chapter is almost nothing but frames** — ▢士聞道 ×3, ▢道若▢ ×3, ▢德若▢ ×2, 大▢無▢ ×2, and 大▢若▢ five times across **this chapter and ch 45 together**. A catalogue rendered as prose stops being a catalogue.

**大白若辱 → "The great white seems disgraced."** *(Was: "true purity seems soiled.")* Three faults in four characters. **大 had been replaced by *true***, which is a breach of the lock at `glossary/da-大.md` that **no check can catch** — every rule keys off a forbidden word, and this one is an absence. **白 (*bái*) is *white*, not *purity*** — an abstraction where the Chinese has a colour, and *purity* is separately forbidden for 樸 (*pǔ*). And **王弼 reads the line straight into ch 28**: 知其白守其黑，大白然後乃得 — *"know the white, hold to the black; only then is the great white attained"* — quoting 知其白，守其黑 verbatim. Ch 28 renders those *"Know the white, hold to the black,"* so *"true purity"* had severed a link the commentator states outright. 辱 (*rǔ*) → **soiled** here, against *disgrace* at ch 13 and ch 28 — a licensed split, argued below.

### 大白若辱 → "The whitest white seems soiled" — an English overlay removed

*(2026-09-07, Shalom's call.)* An intermediate rendering read **"The great white seems disgraced."** It was right about the Chinese and wrong about the English.

**The Chinese carries no racial content, and the question is worth answering with evidence.** 白 and 黑 meet in only one place in the book, ch 28's 知其白，守其黑, and 河上公 glosses the pair outright: 白以喻**昭昭**，黑以喻**默默** — *"白 figures **being conspicuous**; 黑 figures **being unnoticed**."* **It is a figure for visibility, and the instruction is 守其黑 — hold to the black.** 說文 agrees on both graphs: 白 is 西方色也。**陰**用事 (the **yin** colour, of autumn and the west), and 黑 is 火所熏之色也, *"the colour of what fire has smoked"* — **soot**. **There is also no 大黑, and could not be:** the 大▢若▢ frame never pairs opposites, it pairs a thing with the look of its own deficiency — 大方無**隅**, 大成若**缺**, 大白若**辱**. 黑 is not a positive in the frame; it is the hiding place.

**But *"the great white"* is a fixed phrase in English** — *great white hope*, coined for a white challenger to Jack Johnson, and *great white hunter* — and it imports to the reader a reading the Chinese does not contain. This edition exists to strip overlays the English adds; the hymn test and the pointing test both missed this one because both are aimed at theology.

**"The whitest white"** removes the phrase, reads naturally, and doubles 白 the way English intensifies a colour. **The cost, which is real and is the reason this note exists: 大 reads as an intensifier here and as *great* in the four lines of block 3** — 大方, 大器, 大音, 大象 — eleven lines below, inside the same catalogue. A reader cannot see that all five share a character. **That is a deliberate exception to the lock at `glossary/da-大.md`, taken because the alternative ships a line that says something the text never said.** It also touches the question that entry leaves open — whether the whole 大▢若▢ frame wants a superlative — which must still be settled across ch 41 and ch 45 together, not from this line.

**辱 → "soiled" here** rather than *disgrace*, on 河上公's warrant in this very line: 大潔白之人若**汙辱**，不自彰顯 — *"a person of great cleanness-and-whiteness is as though **soiled**, not displaying themselves."* At ch 28 辱 pairs with 榮 (*róng* — glory) and is social standing; here it is a mark on a surface. 說文 gives 恥也, shame, from a graph about **missing the ploughing season** and being punished at the field's boundary.

**夷 (*yí*) → "level," not "smooth."** 王弼: 大夷之道因物之性，**不執平以割物**，其平不見，乃更反若纇㘨也 — *"the great level Tao follows the nature of things and does not grasp levelness to cut them; its levelness is not visible, so it looks instead like unevenness."* 割 (*gē* — to sever) ties this to ch 28's 大制不割. **夷 is polysemous across the book** and this is not ch 14's 夷, which is the unseeable one of the three (名曰夷).

### 建 flexes, and this is the argument — do not relitigate it

**建 (*jiàn*) occurs twice in this chapter and takes two Englishes on purpose:** 故**建**言有之 → *"the **established** sayings"*, and **建**德若偷 → *"**Sturdy** integrity."* *(Settled 2026-09-07, Shalom's call.)*

**The character is one thing.** 說文: **立朝律也**。从**聿**从廴 — *"to establish the laws of the court,"* from 聿 (*yù* — a writing brush) and 廴 (*yǐn* — to stride). 王弼 glosses it plainly: 建猶立也, *"建 is 立 — to set up."* 河上公 reads 建言 compositionally too: 建設也，設言以有道, *"建 is to set up; sayings set up so as to have the Tao."*

**They were unified to *established* first, and the unification had to be undone**, because it made the second line unreadable. *"Established integrity"* collapses in English into **long-standing** — a reputation, which is the opposite of what the line says. The saying needs *fully built* against *looks idle*; **建言** needs *received body of maxims*. One English cannot be both, and the consistency was bought at the price of the sense.

**The grammar differs, which is what licenses the flexion.** 建言 is a **fixed compound** — maxims, a received corpus — where 建德 is 建 **modifying a noun**. This is the same licensed-flexion pattern as 事 (*shì* — affairs / to attend to) and 弱 (*ruò* — yielding / weaken): the register holds, the word bends with the grammar.

**The cost, recorded rather than hidden:** a reader cannot see from the English that these two lines share a character. The chapter notes are where that lives; the verse cannot carry it.

**廣 → "boundless," not "broad."** *(2026-09-07, Shalom's call.)* *Broad* states a width without implying abundance, so *broad → not enough* read as no paradox at all. **Boundless says why it seems insufficient: there is no edge to arrive at** — which is 王弼's argument exactly, 廓然無形，**不可滿也**. *(Runner-up: **ample**, which literally means more-than-enough, so *"ample… not enough"* is a flat contradiction — sharper as wordplay, but it only states the paradox where *boundless* explains it.)*

**建德 → "sturdy," and the reason is in the section below.** *Sturdy* sets a solidly-built thing against *slack* and the tension reads at once. **Its cost:** 建 is the **act** of erecting and *sturdy* is the resulting property, so the building is implied rather than named.

**廣德若不足 and 建德若偷 are a matched pair, and the pairing is architectural.** 說文 on 廣: **殿之大屋也**。从**广**黃聲 — *"a great hall of a palace,"* on 广 (*yǎn* — a roof). **Both characters build.** One is the span of the hall, the other the setting-up of the code, and each saying pairs the thing with the look of its own deficiency. **廣 — the hall so wide it can never be filled looks empty:** 王弼, 廣德**不盈**，廓然無形，**不可滿也**, *"broad integrity does not fill; open and formless, it cannot be filled"* (不盈 is ch 15's word, and 盈 is 滿器, a filled vessel); 河上公, 德行廣大之人**若愚須不足**也. **建 — the code so well established that nobody enforces it looks unenforced:** 王弼, 建德者因物自然，**不立不施**, *"sets nothing up and dispenses nothing"*; 河上公, 建設道德之人**若可偷引使空虛**也, *"as though one could rob them and empty them out."* **Extent produces the appearance of deficiency; structure produces the appearance of sloth.** With 上德若谷 above them the 德 sayings are three, not two.

**不足 → "not enough,"** carrying ch 35's settled rendering rather than *insufficient*.

**質真 → "substantial truth."** *(Was "the most solid truth" — the superlative was invented.)* 王弼: 質真者**不矜其真**，故渝 — *"the substantially true does not boast its truth, and so seems to change."*

### Open, and not settled here

**明 (*míng*) reads "clear," which the lock does not license in so many words.** `glossary/ming-明.md` locks 明 to *clear-seeing / clarity* and lists ch 41 among its chapters — and the entry's own body glosses this very line as *"the clear path seems dim."* *Bright* was worse: it is 皦 (*jiǎo*)'s word at ch 14 (*"a bright top"*), so the old line had two characters wearing one English. **The entry needs an adjectival flexion stated in its frontmatter, or this line needs a different word.** *(That entry also glosses 道 as **path** here, which `glossary/dao-道.md` rejects — the glossary-self-check hole, `WORKLIST.md` T5.)*

**夷 and 希 both run back to ch 14, which is where the book names them.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-09-10 — `WORKLIST.md` T2-52.)* 視之不見，名曰夷 and 聽之不聞，名曰希 give the two as *"the invisible"* and *"the inaudible"* — the meanings rather than the words, chosen for that chapter's legibility, with 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) supplying both readings (無**色**曰夷 · 無**聲**曰希). **This chapter is the strongest argument against that choice, and it was made anyway:** 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) defines 希 here by quoting ch 14 outright, 聽之不聞名曰希, so 大音希聲 and 名曰希 are one word by his own hand. The connection is carried in the notes instead of in ch 14's verse.

**希 (*xī*) is left as "barely heard."** 王弼 defines it by quoting ch 14 outright — 聽之不聞名曰希 — so 大音希聲 and ch 14's 名曰希 are **the same word**, and ours reads *the inaudible* there. 希 runs six chapters with four Englishes and is TRIAGE 14b; the graph is 爻 (crossed threads) over 巾 (cloth), **loosely woven fabric you can see through**. Not settled inside a chapter review.

**廣 (*guǎng*) → "broad" here and "reach far" at ch 67.** Two chapters, two Englishes. *Vast* is now free of 大 and could take it, but 廣 owes a decision of its own.

**王弼 explains 大音 and 大象 with the same mechanism he uses for 大 at ch 25.** 有聲則有分，有分則不宫而商矣 — *"if it has sound it has divisions, and divided it is either the note 宫 or the note 商"*; 有形則有分 — *"if it has form it has divisions."* At ch 25 he argued 大有繫則必有分，有分則失其極矣, *"大, once tied, must have divisions; having divisions, it loses its utmost."* **One argument, three places:** whatever is fully itself cannot be a particular instance of itself.

### 士 → "in service", settled across 15, 41 and 68

*(2026-09-07, Shalom's call. Was "student" here, "warrior" at 68, "masters" at 15 — three Englishes for one character, two of them on the **identical phrase** 善為士者.)*

**"Student" fails on the evidence.** 說文: **士，事也** — *"a 士 is one who handles **affairs**."* Service, not study. 河上公 reads them as governors: the middle 士 治身以長存，**治國以太平** — *"governs the body for long life, governs the state for great peace"* — and the lowest as 貪狼多欲, *"greedy as wolves and full of desire."* Nobody in this chapter is at school. Historically a 士 was the **lowest rank of Zhou nobility**, a person with a sword and a post, between the 大夫 (*dà fū* — grandee) and the commoner.

**Why "in service" and not "officer."** *Officer* was the stronger English — concrete, it takes 上/中/下 naturally, and it repairs ch 68, where *"The master warrior is not martial"* cancels itself. It was declined because **it reads as bureaucratic and carries a negative valence for many readers**, where 士 covers the swordsman and the gentleman-in-waiting as well as the official. *(It also collided with 官 at ch 28, *"chief of the officers."*)* **"In service" is wider and costs only compactness** — five English words for two characters, and a weaker noun.

**Note the seam, per standing rule 2:** 士 named a **male** class, and *in service* is deliberately neutral. The male-default is not erased silently; it is recorded here.

**⚠ Ch 68 now has a seam of its own.** 善為士者 is verbatim at ch 15 and ch 68, so all three must read alike — but ch 68's other three lines run the frame 善▢者 as *"The master fighter,"* *"The master of overcoming enemies,"* *"The master of using people."* Its first line now breaks that shape. `WORKLIST.md` T2-47.

### The closing line

**夫唯道，善貸且成 → "Only the Tao is masterful at lending, and at completing."** *(Was: "Yet the Tao alone masterfully provides for the countless things and brings them to completion.")*

**萬物 is not in the line.** *"The countless things"* was supplied by the English.

**貸 (*dài*) is to lend.** The graph is 貝 (*bèi* — a cowrie shell, money) with 代 phonetic; 說文 glosses it 施也 (*to bestow*), but 王弼 rules out exactly the reading our English had: **貸之非唯供其乏而已**，一貸之則足以永終其德，故曰善貸也 — *"as for its lending, **it is not merely supplying what they lack**; one lending suffices to complete their integrity forever, hence 'masterful at lending.'"* *Provides for* **is** 供其乏. **Things hold what they have on loan** — one that never runs out and is never called in. 善 governs both verbs, since 王弼 glosses 善貸 and 善成 separately.

**道隱無名 keeps "hidden," and the character is ch 15's.** 說文 defines 隱 as **蔽也** — the graph we settled at ch 15 as *stay covered*. The Tao does at the end of this chapter what the ancients do in that one.

**⚠ 大笑之 renders 大 adverbially as "aloud,"** which `glossary/da-大.md` does not license among its flexions. The entry needs it added. `WORKLIST.md` T2-48.
