# Chapter 9

## Translation

Rather than keep filling what you hold, stop.  
Hammer a point ever sharper, and it will not keep for long.

Gold and jade fill the hall, and no one can guard them.  
Grow arrogant with wealth and status, and you leave yourself the fault.

The work done, the body withdraws. This is the way of nature.

## Notes

**The chapter's spine is three things you cannot keep, in three different characters, and the English had kept one.** *(Closed 2026-09-20, `WORKLIST.md` T2-51 · T2-1.)* 持 (*chí* — to hold; 說文 握也) opens it at 持而盈之 and was **not rendered**; 保 (*bǎo* — 說文 養也, *to rear*) closes the couplet at 不可長保 and was **not rendered**; only 守 (*shǒu* — 說文 守官也) survived, as *guard*. All three are now on the page, four lines apart and visibly different.

**The verse also invented a vessel, and 器 (*qì* — vessel) is not in this chapter.** It was not arbitrary — 河上公 glosses the line 持滿必傾, *"hold it full and it must tip over,"* which points at the 欹器 (*qī qì*), the tilting vessel of the ancestral temple. But 器 is **locked** to *vessel / tool / implement* across nine chapters, none of them this one, so the image was a locked English on a chapter without its character. **The referent is named grammatically instead** — *what you hold* — per `name-the-referent`, which fires here because 之 (*zhī*) is a light particle where English *it* actively selects.

**不如 (*bù rú* — not as good as) had been flattened to a comma**, and is now the construction it is: *rather than X, stop*.

**保 takes bare *keep* here, a declared flexion** (`glossary/bao-保.md`). 保's other three lines all take a **cherished** object — the Tao at 15 and 62, the three treasures at 67 — and all three are positive. **Ch 9 is the only negative instance and the only one whose object is not cherished**, and *keep safe* in English requires something valued for its own sake. A hammered point being destroyed by overwork is not that. The lock had never been tested here: ch 9's verse rendered 保 nowhere, so the 2026-09-06 decision was argued from this chapter's **gloss table** rather than its verse.

**銳 (*ruì*) is a point, not an edge.** 說文: 芒也。从金兌聲 — *"it is 芒 (máng, the awn of grain), on the metal radical."* A taper to nothing, not a cutting edge. The edge is 刃 (*rèn*), which occurs **once in the whole book**, at ch 50. 王弼 reads it the same way: 既揣**末**令**尖** — *"having tapered the **tip** to make it **pointed**."* And 揣 (*chuǎi*) as *hammer* is 說文's own secondary gloss — 一曰**捶**之, *"one source says: to beat it."*

**What fails is the point, not its sharpness.** 王弼: 勢必**摧**䘐 — *the momentum must snap it*; 河上公: 先揣之，後必**弃捐** — *"work it first, and afterwards it must be discarded."* It breaks or is thrown away; it does not go quietly blunt. **Blunting is 挫's** (*cuò*, 說文 摧也) at 挫其銳, ch 4 and 56 — where it is the **remedy**, not the affliction. An English saying the point *"cannot be kept sharp"* would make ch 9's disease and chapters 4 and 56's cure the same sentence.

**咎 (*jiù*) → *fault*, following our own ch 46.** 說文 gives 災也 (*disaster*), and the graph is 人 (a person) with 各, which 說文 glosses 相違也, *at odds* — the falling-out and its consequence in one word. *Disaster* is nonetheless unavailable: **ch 46 sets 禍 (*huò*) and 咎 in a parallel couplet** — 禍莫大於不知足 / 咎莫大於欲得 — and gives *disaster* to 禍 and *fault* to 咎. The pair is only legible if they differ. 遺 (*yí* — to leave behind) is now rendered, matching ch 20 and ch 52.

**身 → *body*, and 退 restored.** 功遂身退 had read *"cease striving"* — 身 deleted, and *striving* is 爭's (*zhēng*) word. 河上公 reads the line as leaving office: 不**退身**避位則遇於害, *"if one does not withdraw the body and vacate the position, one meets harm."* He quotes the same shape back at ch 44, 名遂則**身退**也, which ties the two chapters together.
