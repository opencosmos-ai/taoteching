# Chapter 47

## Translation

Without leaving home, you know the whole world.  
Without looking out the window, you see the way of nature.  
The further out you venture, the less you know.

Therefore, the sage knows without venturing,  
names without seeing,  
and accomplishes without acting.

## Notes

**不見而名 → "names without seeing."** *(Shalom's call, 2026-09-24. `WORKLIST.md` T1-24.)* *(was: "understands clearly without seeing," over 不見而明.)* **We restore the received text's 名 (*míng* — to name) and stop following the Mawangdui A silk's 明 (*míng* — clear-seeing).** The two are homophones, and this is the one line in the book where they fork.

**Why the fork was reopened.** 明's locked English puts *see* in its predicate form (*see clearly*), and 見 (*jiàn* — to see) is *see* in this chapter's own second line (*"you see the way of nature"*). So a line rendering both characters had to use *see* twice for two characters. The old *understands* dodged that by borrowing 知 (*zhī* — to know)'s English from three characters away. Every alternative was worse: *has clarity* broke the verb triad, and *is clear* is 清 (*qīng* — clear, as water is)'s predicate English at ch 15 and 39. **Then the evidence settled it: both of our commentaries print 名.** 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) reads it plainly: 識物之宗，故雖不見而是非之理可得而名也, *"knowing the ancestor of things, though not seeing, the principles of right and wrong can still be named."* The triad holds with 名 as well as with 明, since each verb is done without its usual means: *knows* without going, *names* without seeing, *accomplishes* without doing. Naming is what you do once you can tell things apart.

**The Chinese in this file and `source/chinese.md` now print 名.** They had printed the silk's 明 since the Google Doc, while `sources/variants.yaml` recorded 名 as the base. The apparatus was right and the text was not.
