# Chapter 31

## Translation

Fine weapons are tools of ill omen.  
Living things recoil from them.  
Those who are with the Tao do not dwell among them.

The noble at home prizes the left;  
using weapons prizes the right.  
Weapons are tools of ill omen, not the noble's tools.  
Used only when unavoidable, calm detachment is best.

Win, but do not glorify it.  
To glorify it is to delight in killing people.  
And those who delight in killing people cannot get their will in the world.

Auspicious occasions honor the left; inauspicious occasions honor the right.  
The deputy general takes the left, the higher general the right,  
which is to say they are placed by the rites of mourning.  
For the crowd of people killed, weep with sorrow and grief.  
A victory in battle is placed by the rites of mourning.

## Notes

*物或惡之 and 故有道者不處 swept 2026-08-28 to match ch 24, which carries both verbatim. Full argument in `chapters/024.md` · Notes.*

**君子居則貴左，用兵則貴右 → "The noble at home prizes the left; using weapons prizes the right."** *(2026-09-07, Pass D. Was: "In times of peace, the sage chooses creation. When the military must be used, they choose destruction.")*

**左 (*zuǒ* — left) and 右 (*yòu* — right) had been deleted and replaced with an interpretation** — while the same two characters four lines later were rendered literally, 吉事尚左，凶事尚右. One chapter, one pair of characters, read twice on opposite principles. `WORKLIST.md` T1-9.

**And the old English reversed the argument.** It has the sage *choosing destruction* once war comes. 河上公 says the opposite in seven characters: 此言**兵道與君子道反**，所貴者異也 — *"this says the way of arms and the way of the 君子 are **opposed**, and what they prize is different."* The very next line agrees — **非君子之器**, *not the 君子's instrument*. **The noble does not switch sides; weapons belong to a different way entirely.**

**左 and 右 are this book's own pair, which is why deleting them cost so much.** 河上公 glosses them with the locked vocabulary: 貴左 is **貴柔弱也** (*prizes the soft and **yielding***, 弱 locked to *yielding*) and 貴右 is **貴剛強也** (*prizes the hard and **strong***, 強 locked to *strong*). He also gives the ritual sense at 吉事尚左: **左生位也**, *"the left is the position of life"*, against 凶事尚右: **陰道殺人**, *"the yin way kills."*

**君子 (*jūn zǐ*) → "the noble," not "the sage."** *(Shalom's call: the neutral word, over *the gentleman*, which would make the Confucian borrowing visible at the cost of the male default.)* **聖人 (*shèng rén*) is the sage**, and *the sage* carried 38 English lines before this sweep. 君子 is literally *a ruler's child* and is the **Confucian** honorific — handing 聖人's English to it makes Laozi's figure and Confucius's the same person, which is the overlay this edition exists to strip. **Swept at ch 26 with this chapter**, its only other appearance. **Note the seam:** 君子 named a male class and *the noble* is deliberately neutral. `WORKLIST.md` T4-7.

**The general-seating passage carries its point again.** 河上公: 偏將軍**卑**而居陽者，**以其不專殺也** — *"the deputy general is **lower in rank** yet occupies the yang side, **because he is not the one in charge of killing**"* — and 上將軍**尊**而居右者，**言其主殺也** — *"the higher general is **honoured** yet occupies the right, **meaning he presides over the killing**."* **The senior officer takes the inferior seat because he is the one who kills**, which is why the sentence closes 言以喪禮處之: this is a funeral seating. **偏 is a deputy, off to one side; 上 is *higher*** as at ch 8, 38 and 41 — the old *"lieutenant general"* and *"supreme commander"* imported modern ranks, and *supreme* is now forbidden for 大 besides.

**Five characters that repeat, and had two Englishes: 以喪禮處之.** It closes both 言以喪禮處之 and 戰勝以喪禮處之, and read *"placed by the rites of mourning"* then *"treated like a funeral rite."* Both now read **placed by the rites of mourning**.

**Three more terms brought back to their locks.** **器** ran *instruments* once and *tools* twice in one chapter; it is locked *vessel / tool / implement*, so **tools** throughout. **美 (*měi*)** ran *revel* then *glorify* in adjacent lines; **glorify** both times, and it answers 佳 (*jiā* — fine) in the chapter's first line, which 河上公 reads as the same warning: 不當**修飾**之, *"one should not embellish them."* **眾** → **the crowd**, per its lock, where 殺人之眾 had read *"when many have been killed."*

**兵 (*bīng*) → "weapons" throughout, and "arms" was not available.** *Arms* renders 臂 (*bì* — the body's arms) at ch 38, 42 and 64. **兵 still reads *military force* at ch 30**, so the character owes a check of its own. `WORKLIST.md` T2-49.

**夫 (*fú*) is a topic particle and had twice been inflated into an imperative** — *"Understand that…"* opened two lines that begin with nothing of the kind.
