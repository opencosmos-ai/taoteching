# Chapter 38

## Translation

The highest integrity does not do integrity, and so it has integrity.  
The lowest integrity does not lose integrity, and so it has none.  
The highest integrity is non-doing, and has nothing it acts for.  
The lowest integrity acts, and has something it acts for.

The highest humaneness acts, and has nothing it acts for.  
The highest duty acts, and has something it acts for.  
The highest ritual acts, and no one answers,  
so it bares its arms and drags them along.

So when the Tao is lost, then integrity is lost.  
When integrity is lost, then humaneness is lost.  
When humaneness is lost, then duty is lost.  
When duty is lost, then ritual is lost.  
Ritual is the thinning of loyalty and trust, and the head of disorder.  
Knowing in advance is the blossom of the Tao, and the beginning of foolishness.

This is why the great person dwells in the thick, not the thin,  
dwells in the substance, not the blossom.  
They let go of what is out there and take what is here.

## Notes

**失道而後德 → 失道而後失德: the ladder is a cascade, and we follow 韓非.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-09-24. `WORKLIST.md` T1-25.)* *(was: "So when the Tao is lost, then integrity. / When integrity is lost, then humaneness…")* **We depart from the base.** Our base prints 失 (*shī* — to lose) once per clause: 失道而後德 (*shī dào ér hòu dé* — "lose the Tao, and after it, integrity"). 韓非 (*Hán Fēi*, d. 233 BCE), the oldest witness to this text, prints it twice, four clauses running: 失道而後失德，失德而後失仁，失仁而後失義，失義而後失禮 (*"lose the Tao, then lose integrity; lose integrity, then lose humaneness; lose humaneness, then lose duty; lose duty, then lose ritual"*).

**Ours was a descent and his is a cascade.** In the descent, each rung *appears* as the one above it goes, so the lesser virtues are what remains after a loss. In the cascade nothing appears and everything is forfeited in turn. **And 韓非 argues his reading rather than just quoting it.** Just before the quotation, in 解老 (*Jiě Lǎo* — "Explaining Laozi"), he gives the chain: 德者，道之功…仁者，德之光…義者，仁之事也…禮者，義之文也, *"integrity is the achievement of the Tao; humaneness is the radiance of integrity; duty is the business of humaneness; ritual is the pattern of duty."* **Each rung is made from the one above it, so it cannot outlast it.** That is why losing the Tao loses integrity.

**It also sharpens the chapter's spine.** Pass D rebuilt ch 38 around 失 (T1-4), and 失 now carries all four clauses of the ladder, as it carries 下德不失德 (*xià dé bù shī dé* — "the lowest integrity does not lose integrity") above. The source table keeps the base text, as every chapter's must. The departure is recorded in `sources/variants.yaml` and `notes/manuscript.md` → *The 韓非 harvest*, as ch 39's 輿 (*yú* — cart) is.

**故去彼取此 → "They let go of what is out there and take what is here."** *(2026-08-30, Pass C, `WORKLIST.md` T2-13.)* *(was: "Rejects surfaces and embraces life's thickness.")* The same five-character closing tag ends ch 12, ch 38 and ch 72, and 彼 (*bǐ* — that, yonder) occurs nowhere else in the book. All three now read alike.

**The old line restated; the Chinese points.** It repeated *thickness* from the line directly above, so nothing was lost in the change — 彼 and 此 (*cǐ* — this) are **deictic**, and their four referents are already on the page: 厚 (*hòu* — thick) and 實 (*shí* — fruit) against 薄 (*bó* — thin) and 華 (*huá* — flowery display). 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) sorts them flatly: 去彼華薄，取此厚實 — *"let go of that flowery thinness; take this thick substance."*

**The spatial English fits this chapter best of the three, because the two lines above are already locational** — 處 (*chǔ* — to dwell) and 居 (*jū* — to reside), each used twice. The 大丈夫 (*dà zhàng fū* — the fully grown person) is being placed somewhere, and the closing line says which of the two places. **A bare *"that / this"* was not an option here either**: four referents cannot be sorted into two pairs by English *that/this*, the same structural reason the bare draft was killed at ch 72.

***"Embraces" freed.*** The word had been rendering **four characters** — 抱 (*bào* — to embrace: ch 19, 22, 42, 54), 守 (*shǒu* — hold fast: ch 16), 有 (*yǒu* — there is: ch 46), and 取 (*qǔ* — to take: here). This was the loosest of the four and comes off free; the remaining two belong to `WORKLIST` T2-19, the holding family.

**Still owed on this stanza, not fixed here.** *"The thickness **of being**"* invents *of being* for 厚, and *"**life's** fruit"* invents *life's* for 實. That is Pass D, and `WORKLIST` T1-4 already carries this chapter.

---

**失 (*shī* — to lose) is this chapter's spine, and the English had deleted it from the one line where it does the work.** *(2026-08-31, Pass D, `WORKLIST.md` T1-4.)* *(was: "Lesser integrity clutches at integrity, therefore it lacks integrity.")* 失 occurs **five times in ch 38 — more than in any other chapter in the book**: 下失德 at the top, then 失道 · 失德 · 失仁 · 失義 down the whole descent. With 失 gone from line 2, the chapter's argument had nothing to stand on — **that the refusal to lose is what produces the losing.** 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) threads it through his longest commentary in the book: 下德求而得之，為而成之… **求而得之，必有失焉；為而成之，必有敗焉** — *"lower integrity seeks and gets it, acts and completes it… seek and get it, and there must be loss; act and complete it, and there must be failure"* — closing 所謂**失道而後德**也, *"this is what is meant by 'lose the Tao, and then integrity.'"*

**And the word that replaced it renders a character that is not in the chapter.** 執 (*zhí* — to grasp) appears **zero times in ch 38**. *Clutches at* came from 王弼's gloss on the line **above** — 無執無用, *"without grasping, without using"* — describing what 上德 **lacks**. That is the *commentaries are evidence, never candidate renderings* rule, and the class of error only `--english` finds. 執 is also unsettled (`WORKLIST` T2-19), and ch 64 pairs it with 失 in one line: 無執故無失.

**河上公 does not read grasping here at all**, which is the second reason the word had to go. For him the difference is **visibility**: of 上德, 其德不見 (*"its integrity is not seen"*); of 下德, 其德可見，其功可稱 (*"its integrity can be seen, its merit praised"*). Two commentators, and *clutches* picked a third thing.

**The mirror it destroyed.** 上德不德 / 下德不失德 is **a matched pair of negatives** — *does not do integrity* / *does not lose integrity* — one letting go, one holding on, both stated as a **not**. *"Isn't concerned with"* made the first an attitude and *"clutches at"* made the second an assertion, so there was no mirror left. ***"Does not do integrity" stays strange on purpose***: 上德不德，是以有德 puts 德 three times in six characters, and smoothing that is how the line was lost.

**無為 → "takes no action" was a forbidden-register breach no check could see.** The lock renders 無為 *non-doing*; the forbidden list held `"non-action"` and `"inaction"` but not `"takes no action"`, so `check_locks --chapter 38` reported **0 errors and 0 info** on this chapter. `"takes no action"` is now on the list and verified firing — the third near-synonym hole in two days, after `"effortless"` and `"without effort"`.

**上 (*shàng* — upper / highest) had two Englishes and one of them a capital.** Five occurrences — 上德 ×2, 上仁, 上義, 上禮 — read *"highest"* twice and ***"Ultimate"*** three times, a capital that standing rule 5 forbids, on **the rung-marker of a ladder**, changing halfway down. **Ch 41 already renders 上德 *"the highest integrity"* and 上士 *"the highest student,"* and `glossary/wei-為.md`'s own table prints *"上仁 highest humaneness."*** The chapter was contradicting its own glossary entry. 下 → *lowest*, and 禮 reads *ritual* in all three places (it had been *"ritualism"* once).

**以為 → "has something it acts for," because both commentators read it concretely and *"deliberate motive"* pointed at neither.** 王弼: **無以為者，無所偏為也** — *"無以為 means there is nothing it acts partially for"* — and of 上仁, 愛之無所偏私, *"it loves without partiality or private favour."* 河上公 is blunter: 無以為 is 言**無以名號為**, *"it does not act for the sake of name and title,"* and 有以為 is 言**以為己取名號**也, *"it acts to get name and title for itself."* The four lines are a 2×2 grid on two axes — *does it act?* and *is there something it acts for?* — and the English now shows the grid. *(The seam I trust least: the Chinese pairs 無為 / 為之 evenly and *"is non-doing" / "acts"* does not. 無為 is a technical term here and 為之 is not, which is the reason, not an excuse.)*

**The 禮 rung breaks the pattern in the Chinese, and now in ours.** 上仁 and 上義 get one line each; **上禮 gets two** — 上禮為之而莫之應，/ 則攘臂而扔之。 The overflow *is* the content: ritual is the rung that cannot stay in its slot. Ours had fused them into one long line.

**扔 → "drags them along," not "forces them."** 說文解字 glosses 扔 as **因也**, *"to draw along"*; 河上公's text reads 仍 and he glosses **攘臂相仍引**, *"bare their arms and pull at one another."* *Forces* renders neither graph — both are about pulling. Fork recorded in `sources/variants.yaml` and `notes/manuscript.md`.

**而後 → "then," not "follows," and *"pure"* is gone.** *"Pure integrity"* invented a word for 德 on the first rung only, breaking the chain. *Follows* was unavailable: it is 從 (*cóng*)'s English at ch 21. The verbless *"when X is lost, then Y"* also lands the descent at roughly one English word per character, which is the shape 失X而後Y has. **The English is agent-free on purpose** — 失 has no subject, and the commentators supply different ones: 河上公 impersonal (道衰而德化生也, *"the Tao declines and the transforming power of integrity is born"*), 王弼 with an implied human agent (不能無為而貴博施, *"unable to be non-doing, they prize broad bestowal"*).

**首 and 始 are two characters and had one English, in adjacent lines.** 而亂之**首** / 而愚之**始** both read *"the beginning of."* **始 (*shǐ*) is *beginning* everywhere in the book** — ch 1, 39, 64 — so ch 38 was borrowing it. 首 (*shǒu*) occurs only twice in the whole book, and 說文 draws it as **a head with the hair on it** (巛象髪). *The head of disorder* keeps it strange in the direction the character points, and *head* was free in the English. *(說文 on 始 is worth keeping for elsewhere: 女之初也 — "a woman's beginning.")*

**華 and 實 are a bonded pair running across two stanzas, and had three Englishes.** 華 (*huá*) read *"flowery imitation"* and *"flowery display"*; 實 (*shí*) read *"life's fruit."* 說文 on 華: **榮也**, *"blossoming."* On 實: **富也。从宀从貫** — *"abundance; a roof over strung cowrie-shells,"* a full house. 河上公 sets them against each other on the 前識 line itself: **失道之實，得道之華** — *"loses the Tao's substance, gets the Tao's blossom."* Both invented words are gone. ***"The fruit, not the blossom" was the better music and was refused***: 華/實 is genuinely the botanical pair and 華而不實 is a classical idiom, but *fruit* against *flower* is heavily churched in English (Matthew 7:16) and that is the hymn test firing. **Substance** is what 華而不實 means, what 說文's full house shows, and what 河上公 glosses (處其實 = 處忠信也).

**前識 → "knowing in advance," because the commentators openly diverge.** 王弼: 前識者，**前人而識**也 — *"to know ahead of others"* — real but premature knowing, 竭其聰明以為前識 (*"exhausting one's hearing and sight to make foreknowledge"*). 河上公: **不知而言知**為前識 — *"to speak as knowing what one does not know."* One genuine, one bogus. *"Predicting the future"* picked neither and narrowed it to prophecy. The English names the practice; the line's own verdict does the judging.

**處 / 居 — one English verb for two, and a Ming collation note licenses it.** Our 王弼 carries 〔案焦竑云古本四句並作處〕, *"Jiao Hong says the old text has 處 in all four,"* so the alternation may not even be original. See `notes/manuscript.md` for why it is **not** in `sources/variants.yaml`.

**大丈夫 → "the great person," and the gendered reference is removed deliberately.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-08-31. Standing rule 2 — this is the rule's default, not a `shaloms-call`; what was missing was the note.)* *(was: "the mature person.")* **The word is emphatically male and there is no gender-neutral reading of it in the Chinese.** 說文 defines 夫 (*fū*) by a ruler and a hairpin: **丈夫也。从大，一以象簪也…人長八尺，故曰丈夫** — *"an adult male; from 大 (a standing person) with the single stroke depicting a hairpin… a person stands eight 尺 tall, therefore we say 丈夫,"* the hairpin being the 冠禮, the capping at twenty. And 丈 is **十尺也。从又持十**, *"ten 尺; a hand holding ten."* **The word is a measurement and a social marker: full height, publicly grown.**

***"The mature person" failed on two counts.*** *Maturity* in English is an **inward** achievement; 丈夫 is **outward and measurable**, and nothing in the character is about an inner state. And it **fails the 1968 test** — it is Allport-and-Maslow vocabulary and would sit unremarked in a paperback with a lotus on the cover. *(Register — no modern spiritual idiom, `notes/translation.md`. *Matures* also already serves 亭之毒之 at ch 51.)*

**Why *great person* and not *a person of full stature*.** 大丈夫 is **a title in an honour system, not a description of a psychological type** — and you cannot tell a personality trait to move somewhere else. *A person of full stature* was still a description. 大 → *great* is this book's own intensifier (大道, 大器, 大象, and the whole 大X若Y series at ch 45), and 說文 on 大 is **大象人形**, *"大 depicts a human form"* — so both content graphs in 大丈夫 are the **same standing figure**, one of them pinned as an adult. *The great person* renders the picture and universalises the marker.

**The seam, named rather than erased.** There is a real reading on which this chapter **borrows the opposition's vocabulary**: 大丈夫 is a **hapax** here; 聖人 (*shèng rén* — the sage) appears in 25 of 81 chapters and **not once in ch 38**; and ch 38 is where the Confucian ladder lives — 禮 in only two chapters book-wide, 義 in three. On that reading Laozi reached for their word for their ideal man and told it where to stand instead, and 孟子's own 大丈夫 turns on exactly that (居天下之廣居，立天下之正位). **It is a reading, not a fact** — 河上公 goes the other way, glossing 大丈夫 as 得道之君, *"the ruler who has attained the Tao,"* our figure and not theirs; and ch 4 taught that a distributional argument is a reason to look, not to conclude. **No English noun can carry the borrowing in any case**, because it rests on a register contrast an English reader has no ear for. **What the English can do is not fight the frame the fourteen lines above have already built** — which a modern psychological category does. **`WORKLIST` now carries a row for the whole family**: 大丈夫 here, 君子 (*jūn zǐ*) at ch 26 and 31, 士 (*shì*) at ch 15, 41 and 68.
