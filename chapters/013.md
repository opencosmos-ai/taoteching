# Chapter 13

## Translation

Favor and disgrace are both a shock.  
Prize great trouble as your own body.

What does it mean that favor and disgrace are both a shock?  
To be favored is to be below.  
Getting it is a shock.  
Losing it is a shock.  
This is what is meant: favor and disgrace are both a shock.

What does it mean to prize great trouble as your own body?  
The reason I have great trouble is that I have a body.  
If I had no body, what trouble would I have?

So one who prizes their body as a way to serve the world can be trusted with the world.  
One who loves their body as a way to serve the world can be trusted with it.

## Notes

**Rewritten 2026-09-05** (Pass D, `WORKLIST.md` D13 · T1-10 · T3-7). Full reasoning in `notes/translation.md`; two forks in `notes/manuscript.md`.

- **One word per character, and the argument appears.** 身 ×6 had four Englishes, 患 ×4 had two, 貴 ×3 had two. Now **身 → body ×6 · 患 → trouble ×4 · 驚 → shock ×5 · 貴 → prize ×3 · 下 → below ×1.**
- 無身 → **if I had no body** (T1-10). *The separate self dissolves* was Advaita, and it broke **有/無 on the one line in the book where the pair is applied to a person**. 王弼: 歸之自然也.
- **The closing couplet — Shalom's call, Reading B.** 以身為天下 → *the body as a way to serve the world*, supported by ch 26's 以身輕天下 (same 以身 + verb + 天下 shape) and by 河上公: 能愛其身，**非為己也**，乃欲為萬民之父母. **The cost is 王弼's whole chapter**, which reads it as immunity rather than devotion: 不以寵辱榮患損易其身，然後乃可以天下付之也. Reading A was drafted and withdrawn because **in English it lands as egotism** — a construal can be defensible in the Chinese and still arrive as a different claim.
- 寄/託 → **trusted with**, both (T3-7). Near-synonyms; **王弼 swaps them in his own commentary**, and 說文 defines each by the other. Two English verbs in a parallel frame read as an escalation the text does not make. 貴/愛 stay varied, because 王弼 *does* distinguish those: 無以**易**其身 (will not sell it) against 無物可以**損**其身 (will not let it be harmed).
- 若驚 → **a shock**, not *cause alarm*: 若 is *like*, not *cause*. 是謂 → **this is what is meant**, not *this is why* — 謂 is *to call*, and eight other chapters read *"This is called."*
- **Open:** T2-1 — 身 is settled inside this chapter only; it still reads three ways at ch 7, 9, 44 and 54.
