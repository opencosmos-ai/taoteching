# Chapter 17

## Translation

About the greatest leaders, little is known.  
Good leaders are widely loved and praised.  
Poor leaders are feared.  
The worst leaders are despised by all.

Where trust runs short, there is no trust.

The greatest leaders speak sparingly.  
When their work is done,  
Everyone says, “We did this by ourselves.”

## Notes

**百姓皆謂 → "Everyone says."** *(2026-10-14, Shalom's call, `glossary/baixing-百姓.md`. Was: "The people all say.")* This is the one line in the book where the people speak in their own voice, and what they say is that they were not governed. It cannot use 民 (*mín*)'s *the people*, the book's word for them as the governed. *Everyone* already holds 皆 (*jiē* — all).

**信不足焉，有不信焉 → "Where trust runs short, there is no trust."** *(2026-08-30, Pass C, `WORKLIST.md` T2-14. Was: "When leaders don't trust the people, no one trusts the leaders.")* Seven characters, verbatim at ch 23, and the two chapters had carried two different Englishes since the first draft. **The old line had the arrow backwards, which mattered more than the mismatch.**

**信 (*xìn* — trust) is not an attitude the ruler holds.** `glossary/xin-信.md` is locked on this: 信 names a **符契** (*fú qì*) — a **split tally**, a token broken in two so the halves can be matched later — so it is *a correspondence that can be checked*, not a warm inner disposition. 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) reads the line accordingly: 君信不足**於下**，下則應之以不信而**欺其君**也 — *"the ruler's trustworthiness is insufficient **toward** those below; those below respond with untrust and **deceive** their ruler."* **The people's deceiving mirrors a ruler who could not be relied on, not a ruler who was suspicious.** *"Leaders don't trust the people"* had the failure running the wrong way, and made 信 the inner state the entry rejects.

**No subjects are supplied, because 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) does not read this as being about a ruler at all.** On this very line, in this very chapter: 夫御體失性則疾病生，輔物失真則疵釁作。信不足焉，則有不信，**此自然之道也**。已處不足，非智之所齊也 — *"govern the body and lose its nature, and sickness arises; assist a thing and lose its truth, and flaws and cracks appear. Where trust is insufficient, there is untrust. **This is the way of things being so of themselves.** Once one stands in insufficiency, it is not something cleverness can set right."* That is **emergence, not reciprocity** — nothing is given back, the deficiency produces its own consequence the way disease follows a violated nature. An agent-free English holds his reading and 河上公's at once; the old one held only 河上公's.

**The near-tautology is the argument.** 不足 (*bù zú* — not enough; 足 is *enough*, as at ch 44 and 46's 知足, "know when you have enough") gives way to 不信 (*bù xìn* — no trust). **A shortfall of trust does not stay partial; it goes to zero.** There is no slightly-healthy body in 王弼's analogy either.

*(Ch 17 is attested at Guodian, ~300 BCE, bundle C, where the slips run continuously into ch 18 with no chapter division.)*
