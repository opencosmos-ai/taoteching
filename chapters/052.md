# Chapter 52

## Translation

The world has an origin, which serves as the mother of the world.  
Find our mother, and you know her children.  
Know her children, return and hold fast to our mother,  
and to the end of your days, no danger will find you.

Block the openings, close the doors, and to the end of your days you will not toil.  
Open the openings, multiply your affairs, and to the end of your days you are beyond saving.

Seeing the small is called clarity.  
Holding to the soft is called strong.

Use the outer light, return to your inner clarity,  
and leave no calamity for the body.

This is called the ever-present worn covered.

## Notes

**見小曰明 → "Seeing the small is called clarity."** *(Shalom's call, 2026-10-15. Was: "To recognize things in their smallest form is clear-seeing.")* The old line rendered 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*)'s gloss, 萌芽未動，禍亂未見為小, *"before the sprouts stir and before disaster shows, that is 'the small'"*, which supplied *things*, *smallest* and *form*. The line has none of them. Bare *the small* keeps the reading open: 小 (*xiǎo*) is also the Tao's own name at ch 34 (可名於小, *"it can be called small"*) and the uncarved wood at ch 32 (樸雖小, *"though small"*). *Recognize* had dropped 見 (*jiàn* — to see), and with it the eye that 明 (*míng* — clear-seeing) completes.

**The two halves are one couplet, and both old readers say so.** 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) answers them in one matched sentence: 見大不明，見小乃明；守強不強，守柔乃強也, *"seeing the great is not clear-seeing; seeing the small is truly clear-seeing. Holding to the strong is not strong; holding to the soft is truly strong."* 韓非子 (*Hán Fēizǐ*, 喻老) gives each half a story, one after the other. For 見小曰明, 箕子 (*Jīzǐ*) saw the tyrant 紂 (*Zhòu*)'s new ivory chopsticks and foresaw the jade cups, the silk and the wine pools that would end the dynasty: 箕子見象箸以知天下之禍, *"箕子 saw the ivory chopsticks and knew the disaster of the world."* For 守柔曰強, 勾踐 (*Gōujiàn*) served the king of 吳 (*Wú*) as his groom and later destroyed him. The English now matches the halves: *Seeing the small* / *Holding to the soft*.

**Why *clarity* and not *clear-seeing*.** 明's lock allows both (*clear-seeing / clarity*), and *seeing* already stands in the line on 見, so the noun loses nothing and avoids saying *seeing* twice, which ch 47's reversal had ruled against (2026-09-24). It also lets the chapter's two 明 match: 復歸其明, four lines down, is already *"return to your inner clarity."* **The cost:** the frame ▢▢曰明 now reads two ways. Ch 16 and 55 say *"Knowing the ever-present is called clear-seeing."* The frame is not verbatim (知常 there, 見小 here), so the rule to repeat what the Chinese repeats does not bind it, but a reader will hear the difference.

**没身不殆 → "and to the end of your days, no danger will find you."** *(2026-08-31, Pass C, `WORKLIST.md` T2-11.)* *(was: "no harm will come to you.")* The reading was right; the word was another character's. ***Harm* belongs to 害 (*hài*)** — ch 35, 56, 66, 73, 81 — and 殆 (*dài*) is **peril**: 說文解字 危也, the graph built on 歺 (*è*), the bare-bone radical of 死 (*sǐ* — to die) and 殃 (*yāng* — calamity). 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) glosses this line in three characters: **不危殆也**.

**This chapter's version was the sound one, and ch 16 has been brought to it.** 没身不殆 is verbatim in both; 没身 (*mò shēn* — "to the end of one's body") is an idiom for **all one's life**, which this chapter already had right while ch 16 read it as *death itself*.

**是謂襲常 → "This is called holding the ever-present covered" — and a manuscript fork that had been driving the English unrecorded.** *(2026-08-31, Pass C, `WORKLIST.md` T2-10.)* *(was: "This is the practice of the ever-present.")* The old line was not a loose rendering of 襲. It was a **faithful rendering of a different character**: 習 (*xí* — to repeat, to drill), which our base text does not print here.

**Both commentaries do print 習.** 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*): 人能行此，是謂**習修**常道 — *"if a person can do this, it is called **practising and cultivating** the constant Tao."* The Siku edition of 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) sets 習 in the lemma with the compilers' note 〔案**習各本作襲**〕 — *"for 習, all editions read 襲."* **We keep the base text and read 襲**, because 是謂襲明 at ch 27 is the identical frame and 明 (*míng* — clear-seeing) / 常 (*cháng* — the ever-present) are bonded by 知常曰明 at ch 16 and ch 55: one gesture, both halves. Reading 習 here dissolves the pair. Recorded in `sources/variants.yaml`; reasoning in `notes/manuscript.md`.

**襲 is 說文's 左衽袍, a robe closed to the left — grave-clothes**, a garment put on over another. Hence *covered*, which is the motion this movement has just described: 用其光，復歸其明 — *"use the outer light, return to your inner clarity."* The shine is put away, not put on display.

**守 read two ways twelve lines apart, and a third "holding" belonged to 襲.** *(2026-09-08, Pass E, `WORKLIST.md` T2-19 — the holding family.)* 守柔曰強 had read *"To **stay with** the soft is called strong"* against 復守其母's *"hold fast to."* Both are now 守's locked *hold to* (`glossary/shou-守.md`), and the chapter's paradox survives it — 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) on this very line: **守強不強，守柔乃強也**, *"holding to the strong is not strong; holding to the soft is truly strong."* Same verb in both halves of his sentence; only the object changes. **是謂襲常 → "This is called the ever-present worn covered,"** matching ch 27's 是謂襲明 exactly and taking *holding* off 襲 (*xí* — one garment worn over another) in a chapter where 守 stands twice.
