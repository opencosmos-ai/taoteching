# Chapter 23

## Translation

Brevity in speech happens of itself.  
A squall does not last out the morning.  
A downpour does not last out the day.  
Who does this? Sky and earth.  
Even sky and earth cannot make it last long.  
How much less a person?

So one whose affairs follow the Tao is the same as the Tao.  
One of integrity is the same as integrity.  
One of loss is the same as loss.

Those the same as the Tao, the Tao too gladly gets them.  
Those the same as integrity, integrity too gladly gets them.  
Those the same as loss, loss too gladly gets them.  
Where trust runs short, there is no trust.

## Notes

**同 (*tóng* — the same) is the antonym of 異 (*yì* — different), and six lines of this chapter had made it a dissolution.** *(2026-09-03, Pass D, `WORKLIST.md` T2-25. Was: *"merges with the Tao / merges with integrity / merges with the loss,"* and *"Those merged with…"* three times more.)* **Chapter 1 prints the pair in one clause** — 此兩者，**同**出而**異**名, *"these two 同 emerge and 異 in name"* — and our ch 1 renders it correctly as *"the **same** origin."* Everywhere else 同 had drifted: *together* (1), *merges with* (4, 23 ×6, 56), *union* (56). **Five Englishes for one character.**

**Both commentators gloss 同 with 同, and neither describes a process.** 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*): 而物得其真，**與道同體**，故曰同於道 — *"things attain their genuineness, **of one body with the Tao**; therefore it says 同於道."* 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*), flatly: 同於道者，所謂**與道同**也 — *"'同於道' means what is called **being the same as the Tao**."*

**The chapter's claim is resonance, not union, and 河上公 says so in the 易經's own words.** His gloss on the closing 信 (*xìn* — trust) line: 物相歸，**同聲相應**，雲從龍，風從虎，水流濕，火就燥，自然之道也 — *"things return to their kind, **like sounds answer each other**: clouds follow the dragon, wind follows the tiger, water flows to the damp, fire goes to the dry — this is the way of being so of itself."* **Every one of those is a pair, two things finding each other by kind.** 王弼 reaches the same place by a different road, glossing 樂得之 with 應 (*yìng* — to answer): 言隨行其所，故同而應之, *"it says: follow the conduct of what you belong to, and so, being the same, it answers you."*

**Identity would have collapsed the pair, which is why *"is the Tao"* was proposed and declined.** 同於道者，道亦樂**得之** needs a person and a Tao standing apart long enough for one to take up the other; read as *"the one who **is** the Tao, the Tao is glad to get,"* there is no one left to receive and no one to be received. A cloud that *is* the dragon has nothing to follow. **And 同 would have become a copula**, leaving ch 56's 玄同 (*xuán tóng* — dark sameness) with nothing to render. *(The proposal was consistent with ch 8's 幾於道 → *"nearly the Tao,"* and the asymmetry is this: 幾 (*jī* — nearly) survives in the English word *nearly*, where 同 would not survive in *is*.)*

***The third line is where the reading pays.*** 失者，同於失 had read *"Whoever loses merges with the loss"* — a mystical surrender. As **"One of loss is the same as loss"** it is the chapter's harder claim: **you are the same as what you keep company with, and that holds for loss exactly as it holds for the Tao.** 河上公 on 失者: 失謂任己失人也 — *"失 means relying on oneself and losing others."*

**事 (*shì* — affairs) is locked, and read *"actions."*** 從事於道者 had been *"the one who **aligns their actions** with the Tao"* and, in an intermediate draft, the clunky *"one who attends to affairs by the Tao."* It now reads ***"one whose affairs follow the Tao."*** **Both commentators define the phrase**: 王弼 從事謂舉動 (*"從事 means conduct, raising and moving"*), 河上公 從，為也。人為事當如道安静，不當如飄風驟雨 (*"從 means to handle; a person handling affairs should be like the Tao, still and calm, not like a squall or a downpour"*). And *aligns* rendered 從 (*cóng*), whose 說文解字 is 隨行也, *"to follow along"* — an alignment metaphor imported onto a following word.

***"One who follows the Tao"* was proposed and declined on the reverse check.** *(Shalom, 2026-09-03.)* **ch 21 already carries that exact English for a different grammar**: 惟道是從 reads *"The greatest integrity simply **follows the Tao**,"* and there 道 genuinely **is** 從's object. Here 從 governs 事 — 從事 is a set compound, 於道 its complement — so the same English would make two unlike constructions look identical. **And 從事 occurs exactly twice in the book**, here and ch 64's 民之從事, which we already render as *their affairs*. **The chapter is about conduct, not allegiance**: *follows the Tao* makes it a question of whose side you are on, where 河上公 makes it a question of how you go about your business.

***"Intensity" and "sustain" rendered nothing.*** 天地尚不能久 is *"even sky and earth cannot make it last long"* — 久 (*jiǔ*) is duration, 尚 (*shàng*) is *even*. **The invented *intensity* was 王弼's 暴疾** (*bào jí* — violent and rapid) lifted out of the commentary into the verse, the ch 24 *wart* error again: **a commentator's gloss is not the text.**

**人 (*rén* — a person) had become *"we."*** 而況於人乎 is *"how much less a person?"* — 河上公: 何况人欲為暴卒乎, *"how much less a person who wishes to act violently and abruptly."* The old *"how much less can **we**?"* invented a first person the line does not have. **This does not touch T4-1**, the deferred 民/人 call: *we* is neither candidate, and restoring *a person* is not choosing between *the people* and *a person*.

**為 (*wéi* — to do) had become *"acts."*** 孰為此者 is *"who does this?"* — 河上公: 孰，誰也。誰為此飄風暴雨者乎？天地所為, *"孰 means who. Who does this squall and downpour? What sky and earth do."* 為's lock is *do / handle / serve as*.

**希言自然 → "Brevity in speech happens of itself," and the 故 that follows is left unrendered.** *(Shalom's line and Shalom's call, 2026-09-03/04.)* The line is an equation — 希言 **is** 自然 — and the English had been *"Speak sparingly, for **things** go of themselves,"* which supplied 物 (*wù* — things), absent from the line. **Three intermediate drafts failed and each failure is worth keeping.** *"Speech barely heard"* named **volume**, which neither commentator does: 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) means quantity — 希言者是**愛言**也, *"being sparing with words"* — and 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) means unremarkable — 無味不足聽之言, *"speech with no flavour, not enough to hear."* **A whisper is neither.** *"Saying little is so of itself, and no one makes it so"* said something but did not earn the 故 (*gù* — therefore). And *"so a squall does not last out the morning"* asserted an inference the English could not carry.

**The chapter's weather decides 希 for 河上公's reading.** 飄風 (*piāo fēng* — a squall) and 驟雨 (*zhòu yǔ* — a downpour) are **too much at once**, and he draws the moral: 言疾不能長，暴不能久也, *"the rapid cannot be long, the violent cannot endure."* **Excess against sparseness, not loud against quiet.** 王弼's audibility reading is not lost to the book — it is 希's English at ch 14 (聽之不聞名曰希 → *"the inaudible"*) and ch 41 (大音希聲 → *"the great sound can barely be heard"*) — but it is not this chapter's argument.

**The storm is the counter-case, not a parallel instance, and both commentators say so.** 王弼: 言**暴疾**美興**不長**也 — *"violent, rapid, splendid arisings do not last long."* 河上公: 言**疾不能長**，**暴不能久**也. **A squall is not an example of natural brevity; it is the violent thing failing** — which is why the chapter closes the movement with a warning, 而況於人乎, *"how much less a person?"* *(An earlier draft of this note had it backwards, calling the storm "the thesis rather than its counter-example." Corrected.)* 王弼 sets the contrast up by glossing 希言 with the Tao's own speech, quoting 道之出言，淡兮其無味也。視之不足見，聽之不足聞 (*"the Tao's issuing forth in words is bland, without flavour; look, and there is not enough to see; listen, and there is not enough to hear"* — ch 35's line, which he calls 下章). **The self-so mode of speech is bland and barely there; a squall is its opposite, and it stops before noon.**

**故 is printed and not rendered, and the warrant is textual rather than stylistic.** The Siku 王弼 — our base — is the **only** witness that has it, and it says so itself: 〔案**河上公注本及各本俱無故字**〕, *"the Heshang Gong commentary edition and all editions lack the character 故."* Our vendored 河上公 confirms it; his lemma opens 飄風不終朝. **We print the base text and let the three statements stand in juxtaposition, as every other witness has them.** 說文解字 makes 故 unambiguously causal — **使爲之也。从攴古聲**, *"to cause something to be done,"* a hand holding a stick (攴 *pū*) over 古 (*gǔ* — old) — so it cannot be softened into a comparison. ***Likewise* was proposed and declined on exactly that**: it would give the book's causal particle, **63 lines across 42 chapters**, a comparative English in one place, and it would make the squall a parallel instance where both commentators make it the counter-case. **The second 故 in this chapter, at 故從事於道者, is rendered** — *So one whose affairs follow the Tao* — because there the inference is the chapter's own conclusion and the English carries it.

**自然 keeps the locked wording.** ch 17 (*by ourselves*) and ch 25 (*being what it is*) already depart from it, so **two of five occurrences do not show the phrase**; *on its own* would have made a sixth and produced exactly the invisibility `glossary/ziran-自然.md` exists to prevent. *Happens of itself* keeps the term visible and is its plain sense: 自 (*zì* — a nose, hence self) plus 然 (*rán* — so). ***"No one's doing"* was an earlier candidate and the reverse check killed it** — *doing* is 無為 (*wú wéi* — non-doing)'s declared rendering, backed by 無為 in ten lines, and there is no 無為 in ch 23.

**The glossary entry had argued for the old line, and it was half right.** `glossary/ziran-自然.md`'s register defended *"for"* on the ground that 自然 is **the ground, not the consequence** — 河上公's 愛言者自然之道. **That reasoning stands and is kept.** What it could not do was supply a subject without inventing 物, and the entry's own verdict on the line — *"English has no predicate slot that fits it; a strain case"* — was right. Per `CLAUDE.md`, when a decision and a file disagree, fix both and say which was wrong: **the diagnosis was right, the rendering was not.** Entry updated.

**得 (*dé* — to get) → *gets*, not *receives*.** 說文: **行有所得也**, *"walking and getting something,"* from 彳 (*chì* — a step) — active acquisition, not passive reception. And the plain word is what makes the third line land: **loss too gladly gets them.**

**Three textual facts, none of them previously logged.** 王弼's own lemma carries a 道者 our base text lacks; 河上公's third line reads 失亦樂**失**之 against our 失亦樂**得**之; and the Siku 王弼 reports a Ming witness without 樂 (*lè* — glad) in all three lines. See `notes/manuscript.md`.

**Contractions removed as incidental, not as a decision.** *doesn't* and *can't* became *does not* and *cannot* when the lines were rebuilt around them. Twelve chapters still carry contractions and none of them is touched here.

**信不足焉，有不信焉 → "Where trust runs short, there is no trust," matching ch 17.** *(2026-08-30, Pass C, `WORKLIST.md` T2-14. Was: "Where trust is withheld, trust is withheld in return," and ch 17 had a different line again.)* **The waiver that stood here is gone with it.** It argued that ch 17 is a chapter about rulers while this one is about 同 (*tóng* — merging), so the two contexts wanted two Englishes. **The commentators cross that split in both directions**, which is what undid it: 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) reads the line at **ch 17** as impersonal natural law — 信不足焉，則有不信，**此自然之道也** (*"where trust is insufficient, there is untrust; this is the way of things being so of themselves"*) — and 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) supplies a **ruler** here at ch 23: 君信不足於下，下則應君以不足也 (*"the ruler's trustworthiness is insufficient toward those below, and those below answer the ruler with insufficiency"*). Each frame appears in the chapter that was said to exclude it.

**河上公 also settles what the line is doing in this chapter, and it is not an appendix.** 此言物相歸，**同聲相應**，雲從龍，風從虎，水流濕，火就燥，自然之道也 — *"this says that things return to their kind, that **like sounds answer each other**: clouds follow the dragon, wind follows the tiger, water flows to the damp, fire goes to the dry — this is the way of being so of itself."* That is the 易經 (*Yìjīng* — Book of Changes) formula for resonance, and it makes the 信 line **the fourth term of this chapter's 同 series** — 同於道者，道亦樂得之 (*"those who merge with the Tao, the Tao gladly receives them"*), and so on through 德 (*dé* — integrity) and 失 (*shī* — loss). Like joins like; trust is the fourth thing that does it.

***"In return" had taken a side.*** 王弼's shape is **emergence, not reciprocity** — his analogy is 御體失性則疾病生 (*"govern the body and lose its nature, and sickness arises"*), where nothing is given back and the deficiency simply produces its own consequence. The English is now agent-free, so both readings stand behind it.

**Bare 德 → "integrity," not "profound integrity."** *(2026-08-31, `WORKLIST.md` T1-5, closed with Pass C.)* Three lines here gave 德 (*dé*) the English that belongs to **玄德** (*xuán dé* — profound integrity), which stands at ch 10, ch 51 and ch 65 and **contains a 玄 this chapter does not have**. 德 is locked to *integrity*; `--english "profound integrity"` now returns only 玄德's four lines.

**It mattered structurally, not just lexically.** The stanza is a **three-term parallel** — 道者同於道；德者同於德；失者同於失 (*"the Tao-person merges with the Tao; the integrity-person merges with integrity; the loss-person merges with loss"*) — and giving the middle term an adjective the other two lack broke the symmetry the chapter is built on. It is the same fault this chapter had once before, when 信 (*xìn* — trust) was given 德's word.

**希 (*xī*) — the thread from ch 14.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-09-10 — `WORKLIST.md` T2-52.)* **Chapter 14 is where the book names this character**: 聽之不聞，名曰希, which our English gives as *"its name is the inaudible."* The graph is 爻 (*yáo* — crossed threads) over 巾 (*jīn* — cloth): **loose-woven fabric you can see through**, which is why rarity and inaudibility are one property in Chinese and two words in English. Ch 14 renders the meaning rather than the word for that chapter's legibility, so the link is carried in the notes — *the inaudible* there and this chapter's sparseness of speech are one character. Also ch 41 (大音希聲), 43, 70 and 74.
