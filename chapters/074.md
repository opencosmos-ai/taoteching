# Chapter 74

## Translation

When the people do not fear death,  
why try to frighten them with death?

Suppose the people always feared death,  
and I could seize whoever steps out of line and kill them.  
Who would still push?

There is always one who does the killing.  
To take their place is to take the place of the master carpenter.  
Of those who carve in place of the master carpenter,  
few fail to cut their own hand.

## Notes

**司殺者 → "one who does the killing", not "executioner" and not "Master of Killing".** The literal gloss's "Master of Killing" fails the hymn test on sight. The two commentators name the killer differently and neither names a deity: 河上公 says 司殺者天 — the one who presides over killing is 天 (*tiān* — nature) — and then spells the mechanism as the seasons, 春生夏長秋□冬藏 (*"spring gives birth, summer grows, autumn [reaps], winter stores"*), closing on 天網恢恢踈而不失, the very line Ch 73 ends on. 王弼 says something else entirely: 為逆順者之所惡忿也，不仁者人之所疾也，故曰常有司殺也 — *"one who goes against the grain is what the compliant hate and resent; the un-humane is what people loathe; therefore it says there is always one who presides over killing."* For him the executioner is the ordinary revulsion of other people. **The fork is logged, not averaged** — see `notes/manuscript.md`. Neither is a person with a job, which is why **"executioner" fails**: the ruler already has one of those, so "there is always an executioner" invites the answer *"yes, mine."* The chapter's whole force depends on the post being filled by something the ruler does not appoint. Our English names an office and no occupant, which both readings can live in.

**孰敢 is "who would still push?", not "who would dare?"** — 敢 (*gǎn*) is locked (`glossary/gan-敢.md`), and the lock earns itself here: Ch 73, one page earlier, reads 勇於敢則殺 (*"bravery in pushing gets you killed"*). 73 and 74 hold 敢 and 殺 in the same sentence, and the joint is what makes 74's threat redundant. "Dare" severs it.

**若使 is a hypothetical and must sound like one.** 河上公 reads the whole chapter as a rebuke, not a policy: 老子傷時王不先道德化之而先刑罰 — *"Laozi grieves that the rulers of his time did not first transform the people with the Tao and integrity, but put punishment first."* Rendered flat, the second stanza reads as a program for executions. "Suppose" carries 若 and keeps it a seduction the third stanza knocks down.

**為奇者 → "whoever steps out of line", not "criminals".** 奇 (*qí*) is *odd, irregular, off-pattern*, and Laozi had 罪 (*zuì* — crime) available and did not use it (he spends it in ch 46). 王弼 glosses 奇 as 詭異亂羣 — *"deviant and disordering the flock."* The thread runs 正/奇, straight and crooked, across 57–58, and in ch 57 奇 is a **military virtue** (以奇用兵, *"wage war with the crooked"*) — so it cannot be a moral-legal category. "Criminals" concedes the ruler's premise: it makes them right about who deserves killing, which is the one thing the chapter never grants. *"Steps out of line"* keeps the geometry of the straight and stays in plain modern English.

**斫 → "carving", not "hewing" — and the 樸 parallel is the point, not a distraction.** 斫 (*zhuó*) is strictly axe-work, and an earlier draft fenced it off from 樸 (*pǔ* — uncarved wood) on the grounds that here the carpenter is competent and the carving legitimate. **That was wrong.** Ch 28 closes 大制不割 — *"the great orchestrator itself does not carve"* — and ch 28 is where 樸散則為器, the uncarved scattered into tools, lives. 74 is the same shop and the same argument one step further on: the great one does not carve, and if you carve in a master's place you cut yourself. Rendering 斫 as *hewing* makes that inaudible to an English reader for the sake of a tool distinction no one needs. Caught by Shalom, 2026-08-25.

**希 (*xī*) — the thread from ch 14.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-09-10 — `WORKLIST.md` T2-52.)* 希有不傷其手 renders **希** as *few*. **Chapter 14 is where the book names this character**: 聽之不聞，名曰希, given there as *"its name is the inaudible."* The graph is 爻 (*yáo* — crossed threads) over 巾 (*jīn* — cloth), **loose-woven fabric you can see through**: scarcity and faintness are one property in the character. Ch 14 renders the meaning rather than the word for that chapter's legibility, so the link is carried here. Also ch 23, 41, 43, 70.
