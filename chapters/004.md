# Chapter 4

## Translation

The Tao is empty, and using it, it never fills.  
Fathomless, it seems to be the ancestor of the countless things.

It blunts the sharpness, untangles the knots,  
dims its own light, is the same as the dust.

Submerged, it seems to be here.  
I do not know whose child it is.  
It seems older than any god.

## Notes

*Rewritten 2026-08-31 — the first Pass D chapter. `WORKLIST.md` T1-2, and it closes T2-14c's deferred question. Four things were wrong and one whole line was missing.*

**道沖 was inverted, and given 器's word.** 沖 (*chōng*) is **empty**; 不盈 (*bù yíng*) is **never fills**. The line read *"The Tao is a vessel that never runs empty"* — **both halves flipped**, and *vessel* is locked to 器 (*qì*). 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) states the mechanism: 沖而用之，用乃不能窮…滿以造實，實來則溢 — *"empty, and using it, the use cannot be exhausted… fill it to make it solid, and when solidity comes it overflows."* **Emptiness is the capacity; the old line made the Tao a reservoir that never drains, which is the opposite metaphysics.** 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) reads 或 as 常 (*cháng* — always): 道常謙虛不盈滿.

**淵 was dropped, and with it a frame.** 淵兮，似X and 湛兮，似X are **two matched frames** around the quatrain; the English rendered only the second, so the symmetry was invisible and the 湛 line arrived as a new topic with nothing to rhyme against. 說文解字 on 淵: 回水也…左右，岸也，中象水皃 — *"eddying water; the left and right are the banks, the middle depicts the water."* A pool of turning water between two banks. 河上公: 道淵深**不可知**也 — *"deep as an abyss and cannot be known"*, which is what **fathomless** means.

**湛 is not "still" — it is submerged.** 說文: **沒也**, *"to sink under"* (水 with 甚 for sound). That matters, because it makes the line **answer the quatrain** rather than start a new thought: the Tao has just blunted its own edge, dimmed its own light and merged with the dust, and now it is **under**.

**兮 (*xī*) never hedges, and that decided where the doubt goes.** Across its twelve occurrences the pattern is **[asserted quality] 兮，[hedged predicate]** — ch 15 runs seven in a row (豫兮**若**冬涉川, 猶兮**若**畏四鄰, 儼兮**其若**客). So 湛 is **stated**; 似 hedges 存 alone. An earlier draft read *"Submerged. It seems to be."*, which let English's ellipsis reach backward and take *submerged* as the missing complement — putting the doubt on the submersion, which the Chinese asserts. *(Caught by Shalom.)* Both frames now take a comma, matching how the manuscript already handles 兮 at ch 15.

**存 is not "exist" and not "remain."** It is **being-still-there**, against 亡 (*wáng* — to perish): ch 41 pairs them, 若存若亡, and 河上公 glosses this line 長存**不亡**. *Remain* imports a **depletion frame the first line explicitly denies** — leftovers are what survive consumption, and 用之或不盈 says the Tao is not consumed. *Exist* is unavailable: it already serves 有 (*yǒu*) at ch 32, 39 and 64, and 有 is half a locked pairing. **"It seems to be here"** reads in English as *presence*, not location, which is exactly 存. *(Shalom's line.)*

**吾不知誰之子 was not translated at all** — *"I do not know whose child it is."* The book's one flat admission of ignorance about the Tao's parentage, and it was missing. **It is also the only unhedged line in the chapter**, which is why the closing line can follow it as a hedge without going slack.

**帝 was erased, and that erasure deleted the chapter's argument.** See `glossary/di-帝.md`, now locked. The short version: **帝 (*dì*) is not a god this text is importing — it is a god the text is demoting**, and English translation has only ever inflated it (*God*, *the Lord*, *the Creator*) or deleted it, as we had. 帝 reaches Laozi as **the Shang high power that the Zhou deposed and replaced with 天** (*tiān*) when they invented 天命 (*tiān mìng* — the Mandate of Heaven) to justify the conquest; and by 288 BCE living kings were grabbing the title (東帝 / 西帝), with 秦始皇帝 fixing it to the throne in 221. **Read with 誰之子 above it, the line takes the floor out of legitimation:** authority in this culture descends — the Zhou king rules as 天子, *the child of 天* — and a thing with **no parent** standing **before any god** leaves the chain of descent with nothing at the bottom.

***"Any god," not "the high god."*** The definite article asserts a **singular supreme deity at the top of a hierarchy**, which the scholarship on Shang religion has not settled — and it smuggles that in where no reader weighs it, since articles are not words people notice. **The definite article confers status exactly as a capital does.** *Any* also makes the stronger claim the argument needs: precede only *the* high god and a claimant can answer *"my line descends from a different power."* *(Caught by Shalom.)*

**象 is the comparison, not the noun — and this was argued the other way first.** 象 (*xiàng*) is the book's word for **image** at 無物之象 (14), 其中有象 (21), 執大象 (35) and 大象無形 (41), and 似 is the dedicated hedge, already used twice here — so a draft read the last line *"An image, older than any god."* **The grammar defeats it: in all four noun uses 象 is marked as one, by 之, by 有, or by 大, and here it stands bare before a noun phrase, which is verb-plus-object.** Both commentaries read it as a comparison. Ch 4 is also 象's first appearance, so an English *image* would land where no reader can hear the thread.

**One sentence, not two, because English cannot strand a subject.** The four phrases are one sentence in the Chinese — 挫其銳，解其紛，和其光，同其塵 — with the subject elided, as classical Chinese elides freely. A draft broke them into two sentences and left the second without one: *"Dims its own light, merges with the dust."* That is the fault standing rule 4 names in its reasoning about em-dashes, reached with a full stop instead of a dash. The couplet now runs on a comma with a **lowercase continuation line**, which is this manuscript's convention at ch 2, 7, 16, 19, 25, 31, 34, 43, 47, 48 and 49. *(Caught by Shalom.)* **Ch 56 needs no such repair**, because its version is imperative and each line stands as a complete sentence — the nouns match, the grammar differs because the subject does.

**同 → *the same as*, not *merges with*.** *(2026-09-03, `WORKLIST.md` T2-26, the rider ch 23 left.)* 同 (*tóng* — the same) is the antonym of 異 (*yì* — different), printed against it in ch 1's own clause 同出而異名, and ch 23's six occurrences were repaired first. **河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) glosses this very line 不當自别殊也** — *"one should not set oneself apart as separate and distinct"* — where 别 (*bié* — to separate) and 殊 (*shū* — distinct) are 同's opposites. **王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) makes the couplet a matched pair**: 和其光 is 無所**特顯** (*"nothing specially displayed"*), 同其塵 is 無所**特賤** (*"nothing specially despised"*) — do not stand out above, do not hold yourself apart from below. ***Merges with* read as self-dissolution**, losing yourself into dust; the Chinese says do not consider yourself too good for the common muck. **The cost:** *is the same as the dust* is the one stative in a run of actions (*blunts, untangles, dims*), which is the price of one English for one character across ch 4, 23 and 56.

**誰之子 → "whose child," never "whose descendant."** 說文解字 on 孫 (*sūn* — grandchild): **子之子曰孫。从子从系。系，續也** — *"the 子 of a 子 is called 孫; from 子 and 系 (to connect), and 系 means to continue."* **Chinese separates the generations, and has a word for descendants — 子孫 — which this book uses at ch 54** (子孫以祭祀不輟). Ch 4 declines it and writes bare 子, whose 說文 entry is simply 象形, a **pictograph of an infant**. The distinction is load-bearing: **天子 (ch 62) is the *child* of 天, one generation, direct** — and ch 52 pairs 母/子, mother and child. *Descendant* would soften the claim into *"its ancestry is long and untraceable"*, when the claim is that **there is no parent at all.**

**The quatrain keeps the Tao as its subject**, closing the question deferred from Pass C. With 道沖 restored the subject chain runs unbroken — Tao → 淵兮 → the four lines → 湛兮 → 吾不知 → 象帝之先 — which is 王弼's reading: 銳挫而無損…同塵而不渝其真, *"the sharp is blunted and nothing is lost… it shares the dust and does not alter its truth."* Ch 56 keeps its imperative; the nouns match, the verb forms differ, and the divergence is now argued rather than inherited.

**Not attested at Guodian** (~300 BCE) — this chapter rests on the silks and later. The 或/若 fork at 湛兮似或存 is recorded in `sources/variants.yaml`: 河上公 reads 似若存, and the Siku 王弼 carries 〔案或一作若〕. Both are hedges of the same force, so nothing turns on it.
