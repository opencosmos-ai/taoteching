# Chapter 64

## Translation

What is at rest is easy to hold.  
What shows no sign is easy to plan for.  
What is brittle is easy to shatter.  
What is faint is easy to scatter.  
Handle it before it exists.  
Order it before it falls into disorder.

The tree the width of both arms is born from a hair's tip.  
The nine-tiered terrace rises from a heap of dirt.  
The thousand-mile journey begins beneath your feet.

Those who handle it ruin it.  
Those who grasp it lose it.  
Therefore, the sage does not do, and so ruins nothing,  
does not grasp, and so loses nothing.

People often ruin their affairs just short of completing them.  
Be as careful at the end as at the beginning,  
and nothing will be ruined.

Therefore, the sage wants the not-wanting  
and does not prize rare goods,  
learns to unlearn,  
brings the crowd back from where they have gone too far,  
and supports the countless things in being so of themselves,  
and never pushes them.

## Notes

**治之於未亂 → *Order it before it falls into disorder*.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-09-21.)* Was *"before it is chaotic."* **This is the clause 說文 describes** — 亂，治也 — and it is the one line in the book that prints both characters together, so the English has to keep them visibly related. *Chaos* has no antonym inside it; *disorder* does. 王弼 reads the counsel as a matter of scale rather than kind: 治之於未亂 → 謂㣲脆也, *"this refers to what is faint and brittle."* Catch the tangle while it is still a thread or two.


**難得之貨 → "rare goods."** *(2026-08-30, Pass C, `WORKLIST.md` T2-14.)* *(was: "and does not prize precious goods.")* 不貴難得之貨 is verbatim ch 3's second line, and the two had been rendered differently. **"Precious" is 貴 (*guì*)'s word** — ch 70, 72, 75 — and 貴 is the verb standing directly over 貨 here, so *"does not prize precious goods"* argued in a circle: if the goods are already precious, the counsel dissolves. 貨 (*huò* — goods) is locked to *goods*; the full argument, and the reading of 貨 against 寶 (*bǎo* — treasure) and 貴, is in `glossary/huo-貨.md`.

**河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) supplies this chapter's image for the whole thread:** 聖人不眩為服，不賤石而貴玉 — *"the sage is not dazzled into adorning themselves; does not despise stone and prize jade."* **賤 (*jiàn* — to hold cheap) and 貴 (to prize) are both things a person does.** Stone and jade differ in what someone does about them, which is the claim the English had been erasing.

**微 (*wēi*) — the thread from ch 14.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-09-10 — `WORKLIST.md` T2-52.)* 其微易散 renders **微** as *faint*. **Chapter 14 is where the book names this character**: 搏之不得，名曰微, given there as *"its name is the intangible."* 說文解字 has 微 as 隱行也, *"to move concealed,"* on 彳 (*chì* — the step radical). The two readings are one thing seen twice: what is too faint to catch is also what has not yet declared itself, which is this chapter's whole argument about acting early. Ch 14 renders the meaning rather than the word for that chapter's legibility, so the link is carried here. Also ch 15 and ch 36.
