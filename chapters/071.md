# Chapter 71

## Translation

To know you do not know is highest.  
To not know yet claim to know is sickness.

Only because you are sick of the sickness are you not sick.  
The sage is not sick because they are sick of the sickness.

## Source

| 原文 | Pinyin | Literal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 知不知，上； | Zhī bù zhī, shàng; | Knowing that one does not know, is superior/highest; |
| 不知知，病。 | Bù zhī zhī, bìng. | Not knowing yet thinking one knows, is a disease/flaw. |
| 夫唯病病，是以不病。 | Fú wéi bìng bìng, shì yǐ bù bìng. | Only by recognizing the disease as a disease, therefore one is not diseased. |
| 聖人不病，以其病病，是以不病。 | Shèng rén bù bìng, yǐ qí bìng bìng, shì yǐ bù bìng. | The Sage is not diseased, because they recognize the disease as a disease, therefore they are not diseased. |

## Notes

**夫唯病病 → "Only because you are sick of the sickness are you not sick."** *(2026-10-19, Shalom's call, `glossary/wei-唯.md`. Was: "Only when…")* 是以 (*shì yǐ* — therefore) is causal, and 河上公 keeps it so: 夫唯能病苦衆人有強知之病，是以不自病也, *"only because they are pained by the crowd's disease of forced knowing do they not sicken themselves."* The next line's 以其病病 (*yǐ qí bìng bìng* — because they are sick of the sickness) is causal too, so both of the chapter's reasons now read *because*.

**上 / 病 is a posture, not a grade.** 上 (*shàng*) is a line with a mark above it: *up, higher*. 病 (*bìng*) is 疒 (*nè* — a person lying against a bed-plank) over a phonetic; *Shuowen* glosses it 疾加也, "illness intensified" — sick enough to be laid up. The pair is standing high against lying down, not best against worst. "Highest" also follows the house rendering of 上 in 上德 (ch 38) and 上士 (ch 41).

**"Claim you know" follows 河上公.** He inserts 言 (*yán* — to say) into both halves: 知道**言**不知，是乃德之上 — "to know the Tao and *say* you do not know is the height of integrity" — and 不知道**言**知，是乃德之病. That turns the chapter from a Socratic epigram about the limits of knowledge into a claim about the gap between what you know and what you profess. 知 (*zhī*) supports it at the radical: 矢 (*shǐ* — arrow) over 口 (*kǒu* — mouth), speech flying straight to the target. The standard interior reading ("and think you know") is available and not wrong; we render toward the exterior one because the commentary, the graph, and chs 56 and 70 all pull that way. See `notes/translation.md`.

**病病 is being pained by it, not diagnosing it.** 河上公: 夫唯能**病苦**衆人有強知之病 — "only because they are *pained and distressed* by the crowd having the disease of forced knowing." Not "recognizes the flaw as a flaw" but aches over it. English "sick of" carries exactly that verbal sense, which is why the doubling survives in one word. Cost: "sick of" can read as impatient. Logged.

**The repetition is the medicine — the lexical repetition.** 病 appears seven times in four lines. English style wants to alternate *sickness / ailment / affliction*; do not. The chapter hammers one syllable and the hammering is the argument.

**The trailing 是以不病 is cut.** The last line runs 聖人不病，以其病病，是以不病 — *A, because B, therefore A.* Classical Chinese closes a period by restating its opening; English keeps the conclusion inside the causal clause, so the restatement is padding in translation and nothing else. Cut by Shalom, 2026-08-24. The cut leaves the last two lines in exact chiasmus. See `notes/translation.md`.

**The mirror in the opening couplet is deliberate.** 知不知 / 不知知 is the same two characters reversed, and the English holds the reversal at the front of the line where the Chinese puts it: *to know you do not know* / *to not know yet claim to know*.
