# Chapter 21

## Translation

The greatest integrity follows only the Tao.  
Whatever the Tao is, it is only vague, only elusive.  
Elusive and vague, and within it there is an image.  
Vague and elusive, and within it there is a thing.  
Deep and dim, and within it there is vital essence.  
Its essence utterly real, and within it there is trust.

From ancient times until today, the Tao's name has never been erased.  
In this name, I observe the origin of the many.  
How do I know the origin of the many is so? By this.

## Notes

**惟道是從 → "follows only the Tao."** *(2026-10-19, Shalom's call, `glossary/wei-唯.md`. Was: "simply follows the Tao.")* 惟 (*wéi*) is 唯 (*wéi* — only) written with the heart radical, and the next line already reads it *only*, twice: 惟恍惟惚, *"only vague, only elusive."* *Simply* restricted the verb, a manner of following. 惟 X 是 V (*wéi X shì V*) fronts the object and singles it out, the construction of ch 53's 唯施是畏 (*wéi shī shì wèi*), *"my only fear would be to stray."*

**以此 → "By this." — and every word we had added to it is gone.** *(2026-08-30, Pass C, `WORKLIST.md` T2-12.)* The line closed *"By this, here, now."* 以此 (*yǐ cǐ*) is 以 (*yǐ* — by means of) + 此 (*cǐ* — this): **two characters, two words.** The addition came from 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*), who glosses the phrase 此，今也 (*cǐ, jīn yě* — "'this' means **now**") and continues 以今萬物皆得道精氣而生 (*"because now the countless things all obtain the Tao's essence-breath and live"*). **A commentator's gloss is evidence for a rendering, not a rendering** — and this one had been lifted straight into the verse.

**It also picked a side the Chinese does not pick.** 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) reads the same two characters the other way: 此上之所云也…言吾何以知萬物之始於無哉，以此知之也 — *"'this' is what was said above… it says: how do I know the countless things begin in no-thing? By this I know it."* That is **anaphora**, pointing back through the descent 象 (*xiàng* — image) / 物 (*wù* — thing) / 精 (*jīng* — vital essence) / 信 (*xìn* — trust). 河上公's *now* is **deixis in time**. The two are incompatible, and a bare *this* is the only English that leaves both standing. The note in `notes/manuscript.md` that claimed the old line "holds both" has been corrected; it held one.

***"Here, now" was also a date stamp.*** It is *Be Here Now* (Ram Dass, 1971) and the mindfulness literature after it — a register that postdates the text by twenty-three centuries and that this book helped create, so it reads back onto it invisibly. Now a standing principle: [`imported-register`](../process/principles/imported-register.md).

**Three chapters, one formula, one English.** 吾何以知…**然**哉？以此 closes ch 21, ch 54 and ch 57, and it had three Englishes — which meant `DISCOVERIES.md` §2, whose whole claim is that the *same* answer is given three times, was invisible in the manuscript it describes. All three now read **"By this."** 河上公 supports the unification from his own side: he glosses ch 21 and ch 57 with the **identical five characters**, 此，今也.

**The cost, accepted: the chapter now ends on two syllables and a full stop.** That thinness is the reading. The answer to the largest question in the book is a **gesture, not an explanation** — every word we were adding made the pointing finger talk.
