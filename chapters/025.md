# Chapter 25

## Translation

Something mixed and complete, born before sky and earth.  
Silent. Empty. Solitary. Unchanging.  
It moves in a circle, never in danger,  
and can be taken as the mother of the world.

I do not know its name.  
Forcing a label on it, I call it the Tao.  
Forcing a name on it, I call it great.

Great means departing,  
departing means going far,  
going far means returning.

Therefore, the Tao is great, the sky is great, the earth is great, and humans are also great.  
Within all bounds there are four great things, and one of them is human.

Humans model themselves on the earth,  
Earth models itself on the sky,  
The sky models itself on the Tao,  
The Tao models itself on being what it is.

## Notes

**On 人 in the four greats — an editorial call, not a manuscript one.** Our base text reads 王 (*wáng* — king) and we render **human**. Every excavated witness reads 王, Guodian (~300 BCE) included; the human reading is transmitted, from 傅奕 (Tang) and 范應元 (Song), and rests on the seam in the received text itself — the next line is 人法地, *humans follow earth*. Corrected 2026-08-20 from an earlier note that had the manuscript evidence backwards. Apparatus: `notes/manuscript.md` · Ch 25.

**周行而不殆 → "It moves in a circle, never in danger" — a dropped clause restored and a borrowed word returned.** *(2026-08-31, Pass C, `WORKLIST.md` T2-11.)* *(was: "Inexhaustible, it can be considered the mother of the world.")* Two faults in one line. **周行 (*zhōu xíng* — "moves in a circle") was not translated at all**, a debt `WORKLIST` already carried. And 不殆 was given *inexhaustible*, which belongs to 窮 (*qióng*) at ch 6 and ch 35.

**殆 (*dài*) is peril, not exhaustion.** 說文解字: 危也. The graph is 歺 (*è*), the bare-bone radical — the death element in 死 (*sǐ* — to die) and 殃 (*yāng* — calamity). 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) reads the line 周行無所不至而**免殆** — *"it circulates and reaches everywhere, yet **escapes peril**"* — with the Siku compilers' collation note 〔案免永樂大典作危〕, *"for 免, the Yongle Dadian reads 危."* **Peril under either reading; exhaustion under neither.**

**The circle matters to the chapter.** 周行 is the motion the next movement names — 大曰逝，逝曰遠，遠曰反 (*"Great means departing, departing means going far, going far means returning"*) — so dropping it left the return with nothing to return along.

**大 (*dà*) → "great," replacing *"vast"* on all four lines.** *(2026-09-06, Shalom's call.)* 大 occurs **seven times in this chapter** — it is the chapter's own name for the Tao — and ch 25 was the only place in the book rendering it *vast*. Everywhere else 大 is **great**, across 41 lines. **The decisive comparison is ch 34, which makes the identical move:** 可名為大 reads *"it can be called great"* while ch 25's 強為之名曰大 read *"I call it the vast"* — the same claim, twice, invisible to a reader. It failed in the other direction too: *vast* was also carrying 廣 (*guǎng* — broad) at ch 41 and 恢 (*huī* — vast) at ch 73, so one English wore three characters while 大 answered to two. Ch 25's *vast* also predates every Pass D decision, all of which landed on *great* — 大象 → *the great image* (35), 大制 → *the great carving* (28), 大丈夫 → *the great person* (38).

**⚠ The sweep is incomplete, deliberately. Ch 67 still reads 大 as *vast* twice**, and swapping it there would expose rather than fix a fault: 天下皆謂我**道**大 (*"everyone says my **Tao** is great"*) is rendered *"Everyone says **I** am vast"* — **道 dropped, and the line handed to the speaker.** *"Everyone says I am great"* would read as boasting the Chinese does not contain. That is a line rewrite, not a term swap. `WORKLIST.md` T2-42.

**混 (*hùn*) → "mixed," settled across ch 14, 15 and 25.** *(2026-09-06.)* Three chapters had three Englishes — *"they **blend** into one"* (14), *"**Blended**, like muddy water"* (15), *"formed in the **undifferentiated**"* (25). 說文: 豐流也。从**水**昆聲 — *"**abundant flow**. From 水 (*shuǐ* — water)."* **混 is a water word**, and the mixing sense comes from a full river carrying everything along, which is what muddy water is. 王弼 here: 混然不可得而知而萬物由之以成 — *"混-ly, it cannot be known, yet the countless things come to completion through it."* This line's 有物混成 is now *"Something mixed and complete"* — undifferentiated and already whole, which is the paradox 混成 states. **Not yet a glossary entry** (`WORKLIST.md` T2-40), so the rendering is settled but unenforced.

**A rider the reverse search exposed: *blend* was also 沖's word.** Ch 42's 沖氣以為和 reads *"**blending** the vital breath into harmony,"* where 沖 (*chōng*) is the character ch 4's Pass D work settled as **empty** — 道沖, *"The Tao is empty."* Freeing *blend* from 混 does not fix ch 42; it isolates it. `WORKLIST.md` T2-41.

**大曰逝，逝曰遠，遠曰反 → three lines, and 逝 → "departing."** *(2026-09-06. Was: "The vast means expanding, expanding means going far, going far means returning" — one line.)*

**逝 is not size, and all three sources say so.** 說文: **往也**, *"to go."* 王弼: **逝行也**…不守一大體而已，周行無所不至，故曰逝也 — *"逝 is 行 (*xíng* — to go)… it does not hold to one great body only; it circulates and reaches everywhere."* 河上公 supplies what the word is doing here: 非若天常在上，非若地常在下，乃復**逝去无常處所**也 — *"not like the sky always above, nor the earth always below; it **goes away and has no constant dwelling place**."* ***Expanding* is growth in place, which is the one thing the line denies.**

**Why *departing* over the plainer *going* (Shalom's call).** The three steps are a journey with three legs — departure, distance, return — and *departing* names its leg where *going* leaves it unspecified, which is exactly 河上公's 无常處所. An earlier objection that English *departed* suggests death was **weak and is withdrawn**: as a gerund inside a travel sequence it carries no such reading, and 逝's own commentary is about ranging, not ending.

**反 → "returning," to match ch 40.** 遠曰反 is the same claim as 反者道之動 (*"Returning is the Tao's movement"*), the book's most famous statement of this idea, and *turning back* would hide the link. 說文 反: 覆也 — *to overturn*, on 又 (*yòu* — a hand). **⚠ *Return* is overloaded book-wide** — it currently carries 反 (25, 40, 65), 復 (16, 28, 52, 59) and 歸 (14, 34), which 說文 keeps distinct: 復 is 往來也, *"going and coming"*, on the step radical 彳; 歸 is 女嫁也, *"a woman marrying in"* — going where one belongs. Three motions, one English. `WORKLIST.md` T2-44.

**遠 → "going far," not bare "far."** *(Shalom's call.)* Bare *far* left the middle step without a verb — *"departing means far"* strands it, the same fault the no-em-dash rule exists to prevent — and it broke the parallel with the gerunds on either side. **The cost, stated:** both commentators gloss 遠 as **extent** rather than travel. 王弼: **遠極也**，周無所不窮極，不偏於一逝 — *"遠 is 極 (*jí* — the utmost); it circles with nothing it does not reach to the utmost, not partial to any one departing."* 河上公: 言遠者**窮乎無窮**，布氣天地無所不通也 — *"far means exhausting the inexhaustible, spreading breath through sky and earth with nothing it does not get through."* *Going far* tilts a reach word slightly toward motion. It is defensible against the chapter's own frame — 周行 (*zhōu xíng* — "moves in a circle") makes the whole sequence a circuit, and 遠 is its far leg — but the tilt is real and is recorded here rather than smoothed over.

**The three lines are three lines in the Chinese, and the chain is anadiplosis** — each opens on the word the last one closed with, 大 → 逝 → 遠 → 反. Run onto one English line, the steps were invisible. This is the circuit 周行 promised nine lines earlier, so the return now has something to return along.

**The naming block rebuilt.** *(2026-09-06. Was: "Its true name, I will never know. Forcing a label upon it, I call it the Tao. / Forcing a description for it, I call it great." — two English lines for three Chinese ones.)*

**說文 explains why a name is the wrong instrument here.** 名: 自命也。从**口**从**夕**。夕者，冥也。**冥不相見，故以口自名** — *"to name oneself. From 口 (*kǒu* — mouth) and 夕 (*xī* — dusk); 夕 means 冥 (*míng* — dark). **In the dark you cannot see one another, so you use the mouth to name yourself.**"* **A name is what you reach for when sight fails.** 王弼 states the chapter's problem in four characters — **名以定形**, *"a name fixes a form"* — and draws the conclusion: 混成無形，不可得而定，故曰不知其名也, *"the mixed-and-complete has no form, cannot be fixed, therefore 'I do not know its name.'"*

**So 吾不知其名 is not a confession of ignorance.** *"Its **true** name, I will **never** know"* invented a true name existing somewhere out of reach, and turned a claim about formlessness into one about the speaker's limits. Both the *true* and the *never* are gone: 不知 is **do not know**.

**名 (*míng*) occurs twice in three lines and had two Englishes** — *"true name"* then *"description."* It is **name** in every other chapter, including ch 1's 名可名，非常名, and runs 21 lines across 9 chapters.

**字 (*zì*) → "label," and 王弼 distinguishes it from 名 outright:** 夫**名以定形，字以稱可言** — *"a 名 fixes a form; a 字 designates what can be spoken."* He then applies it: 吾所以**字**之曰道者，取其可言之稱**最大**也 — *"the reason I 字 it 'Tao' is that I take the greatest of speakable designations"* — and warns that forcing a 名 costs more: 大有繫則必有分，有分則失其極矣, *"大, being tied, must have divisions; having divisions, it loses its utmost."* **字 occurs once in the whole book**, so nothing constrains the choice. *Word* and *speak* were both unavailable — 言 (*yán*) holds those across 11 lines — and *designation*, which is 王弼's own 稱 and exact, is bureaucratic. **Label is plain, free, and deflationary, which is the argument**: *Tao* is only the largest thing that can be said, not the thing. 說文 on 字 is 乳也, *"to nurse"* — 子 (*zǐ* — a child) under 宀 (*mián* — a roof); the designation sense is later and is not read into the line.

**曰 (*yuē*) is "call" in both lines, as it is twice in the Chinese.**

**Left open, deliberately: 強為之▢ is a frame shared with ch 15**, whose 強為之容 reads *"I force a likeness."* Here 強為之名 reads *"Forcing a name on it."* Same frame, two English shapes — raised rather than matched, since ch 15's line closes a movement and this one does not.

**故道大，天大，地大，人亦大 is written almost entirely in one figure.** *(2026-09-08, raised by Shalom.)* 說文 defines 大 as 象**人形**, *the human form* — a person seen head-on, arms and legs spread — and it is a **radical** whose family is made by marking that same body: **天** is a line **above the head** (顛也。从一大, *"the crown of the head"*), **亦** is marks at **both sides** (人之臂亦也, *"a person's armpits,"* borrowed for *also*), **立** is a line **beneath the feet**, and **夫** a line **through the topknot** — a hairpin, the 夫 inside ch 38's 大丈夫.

**So this line holds three members of the family — 天, 大, 亦 — and 說文's entry for 大 quotes the line itself.** 人亦大 is close to tautological in the graphs: 大 *is* a picture of a person and 亦 *is* that person's armpits. **A person is also what the picture of a person means.** The tautology is the argument — **scale here is measured outward from a body, not downward from a heaven**, which is why 天 is locked to *sky* and 大 to the ordinary *great*. Full family in `glossary/da-大.md`; reader-facing in `notes/reading.md` · *Thread · the standing figure*.

**⚠ Two limits, since 說文 rationalises elsewhere.** 大 is **not** 人 with a stroke added, though it looks like it — 說文 makes them independent pictographs of the same subject, 人 in profile (象臂脛之形, *arms and shins*) and 大 head-on. And whether Laozi's readers saw any of this is unknowable; that the characters are built this way is not.
