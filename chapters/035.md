# Chapter 35

## Translation

Grasp the great image, and the world comes to you.  
It comes unharmed, at rest, and utterly even.

Music and food bring a passing guest to a halt.  
The Tao is bland in the mouth. It has no flavor.  
Look at it. There is not enough to see.  
Listen for it. There is not enough to hear.  
Use it. It never runs out.

## Notes

**Rewritten 2026-09-05** (Pass D, `WORKLIST.md` D35 · T1-12; the 執 rider opened at ch 28 on 2026-09-01). Full reasoning in `notes/translation.md`; the two forks in `notes/manuscript.md` and `sources/variants.yaml`.

- **The chapter is one argument.** 王弼 runs 樂與餌 straight into 道之出口 — music and food stop a passer-by, the Tao stops nobody. The old stanza break severed the comparison. 不足 → *there is not enough*, its sense in all six other chapters, not *cannot* (which is ch 14's 不見, without 足). 河上公 reads 足 as 得 and takes the other side; we follow 王弼.
- 執大象 → **grasp the great image**, restoring the mirror with ch 29's 不可執也 — you cannot grasp the world; grasp the image and the world comes. Does not close **T2-19** (the 守/執/保 family, Pass E).
- 大象 → **the great image**, matching ch 41's 大象無形. *Form* is 形's.
- 往 → a verb of going, as at ch 80. *Routes* was mechanistic.
- 安平太 → **at rest, utterly even**. 太 never stands alone in this book, so 安平 is the pair and 太 the intensifier.
- **Open, not closed here:** ch 69 deletes 主/客 entirely, rendering 河上公's gloss (主，先也) as *move first / move second*; and ch 12's 口 → *palate*, which picks eating over speech.
