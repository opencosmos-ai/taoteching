# Chapter 16

## Translation

Go totally empty.  
Hold steadily to stillness.  
The countless things arise together,  
Relying upon this, I observe their return.

Things come to their fullest,  
Each returns again to its root.  
Returning to the root is called stillness.  
This is called returning to what is given.  
Returning to what is given is called the ever-present.  
Knowing the ever-present is called clear-seeing.

Not knowing the ever-present brings reckless action and disaster.

Knowing the ever-present, you contain everything.  
Containing everything leads to impartiality.  
Impartiality leads to supreme authority.  
Supreme authority leads to the sky.  
The sky leads to the Tao.  
The Tao leads to what endures.  
And to the end of your days, no danger will find you.

## Notes

**致虛極，守靜篤 → "Go totally empty. / Hold steadily to stillness."** *(2026-09-05, Pass D, `WORKLIST.md` D16 · T1-6.)* *(was: "Attain emptiness. / Embrace stillness.")* **Two of the couplet's six characters had no English at all.** 極 (*jí* — the far end) and 篤 (*dǔ* — steady) were both dropped, and 守 (*shǒu* — to hold to) was wearing 抱 (*bào* — to embrace)'s word.

**Both lines hide a gait.** 說文解字 makes 篤 **馬行頓遟**, *"a horse's gait, halting and slow,"* and 致 **送詣也** — 夊 (*suī* — a dragging foot) beneath 至 (*zhì* — to arrive), *"to send all the way there."* Not two achievements: one instruction about how far and how slowly. *Attain* and *embrace* had made both of them prizes.

**極 measures the reacher, not the emptiness** — its original graph 亟 (*jí*) is a standing body with a stroke at the crown and one at the heel. That is what dissolves the paradox the line seems to pose. And **虛 must not become *emptiness***: that is śūnyatā's English, and 空 (*kōng*), the character Chinese Buddhism uses for it, has zero occurrences in this book. Ch 16 is the only place 虛 is forced into a noun slot, so it is the only place the import can happen. Full argument in `glossary/ji-極.md` and `glossary/xu-虛.md`; the rejected candidates and the *hold to* decision are in `notes/translation.md`.

**Guodian (~300 BCE) carries this couplet and nothing else from the chapter** — bundle A, unit A.3, standing alone as its own slip-unit. Two forks logged: 致 / 至 and 觀復 / 觀其復, both in `notes/manuscript.md`.

**容 got one English, and the tail-head chain closed.** *(2026-09-05, Pass D, `WORKLIST.md` D16.)* *(was: "Knowing the ever-present creates the capacity to embrace the totality. / Embracing all leads to impartiality.")* 容 (*róng* — to contain) had **two Englishes one line apart** where the Chinese repeats the identical character, breaking 頂真 (*dǐng zhēn* — the tail-head chain) at its first link; the other four links were already clean. 說文解字: 盛也。从宀、谷 — *"to hold, as a vessel holds. From 宀 (mián — a roof) and 谷 (gǔ — a valley)."* Both commentators gloss it with 無所不包 — 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) 無所不包通也, 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) 无所不包容也 — which is what licenses *everything*. The one fix removed three imports: *create* (生 *shēng*'s), *embrace* (抱 *bào*'s), *totality* (nothing's).

**乃 (*nǎi* — then) keeps *leads to*, on Shalom's call.** Both commentators read the chain as conditional consequence, using 則 (*zé* — if/then): 王弼 無所不包通**則**乃至於蕩然公平也, 河上公 無所不包容**則**公正無私. English *leads to* carries that — the sunrise leading to light, not a machine producing an output.

**Two invented words removed.** *"The open sky"* ×2 — 天 (*tiān*) is locked to *sky* as a tier and *open* rendered nothing. And 久 (*jiǔ*) → **what endures**, not *what lasts*: ch 7's 天長地久 splits the pair in one line, 長 (*cháng*) → *lasting*, 久 → *enduring*, and 長且久 repeats it.

**夫物芸芸，各復歸其根 → "Things come to their fullest. / Each returns again to its root."** *(2026-09-05, Pass D, `WORKLIST.md` D16 · T1-13.)* *(was: "Multitudes flourish, / Each returns energy to its root.")* Four faults in two lines. **物 (*wù* — things) had been deleted**, hiding that this is the chapter restating the 萬物 of three lines above. ***Multitudes* is 眾 (*zhòng* — the crowd)'s word** and 眾 has zero occurrences here — invisible to the checker twice over, since 眾's `forbidden:` list carries *"the multitude"* and this line said *Multitudes*. ***Energy* is 氣 (*qì* — vital breath)'s**, also absent, the last survivor of the purge done at ch 28 and ch 10. And **復歸 lost a character**.

**芸芸 is a peak, not a motion.** 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*): 芸芸者華葉盛 — *"芸芸 means flowers and leaves at their fullest."* The line sits immediately before the turn, and that adjacency is the chapter's teaching: maximum display, then the return. *In full leaf* was tried and rejected — 河上公's gloss explains the word vegetally, but 物 is general, and committing the English to foliage makes all things plants. *Prime* was rejected too: it is 壯 (*zhuàng*)'s word, 壯 is absent here, and ch 30 and ch 55 both read 物壯則老 as the identical sentence *"When things reach their prime, they decay"* — borrowing it would make ch 16 look like a quotation.

**復歸 → *returns again*, following ch 28.** The compound runs seven lines (14, 16, 28 ×3, 52) and ch 28's Pass D rewrite settled it to one shape: *"Return again to the infant / the endless / the uncarved wood."* 歸 carries *return*, 復 carries *again*. 說文解字 keeps them distinct — 復 往來也 (*"going and coming"*), 歸 女嫁也 (*"a woman marrying,"* going to the house that is now hers). *Returns home* was proposed and dropped: *home* does render 歸, but it matches no other line in the formula.

**根 is a stump.** 說文: 木株也 — the stock left in the ground when the growth is gone. Not an origin. That is why 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) can gloss the whole movement 凡有起於虛，動起於靜 — *"all presence arises from emptiness, all movement arises from stillness"* — and why 河上公 can say 枯落 (*"wither and fall"*) and 更生 (*"born again"*) in one breath. **歸根 is not a euphemism for dying**: 吾以觀復 has the speaker *watching* the returning, and no one watches their own death.

**是謂復命，復命曰常 → "returning to what is given."** *(2026-09-05, Pass D, `WORKLIST.md` T2-18.)* *(was: "returning to what **was** given.")* One word, and it settles a line four candidates had failed. **復命 is a name, not a further step** — the chapter describes one event and names it four times over, each name becoming the next line's subject: 各復歸其根 → 歸根**曰**靜 → 是謂**復命** → 復命**曰**常 → 知常**曰**明. Nothing new enters at 復命.

**The present tense is the whole of the change.** *What was given* put the giving in the past, in a naming chain whose other members — 靜 (*jìng* — stillness), 常 (*cháng* — the ever-present) — are timeless. *What **is** given* is ongoing: what any thing is given, always. That reads straight into 常 on the next line, where a past-tense clause fought it.

**The giver stays present and unnamed, which is right rather than a compromise.** 命's graph has a mouth in it — 說文解字: 使也。从口从令, and 令 is 發號也, *"to issue a call."* What this edition strips is **naming** the speaker (*mandate* names Heaven, *destiny* names a plot); the passive admits the speech without naming who spoke, exactly as the Chinese does. Full argument, down to the radicals, in `glossary/fuming-復命.md`.

**Three candidates were rejected on evidence.** ***The allotted course*** — *course* is a path you run, and the chapter's event is a thing **finishing** one; it also passes the hymn test, which is the warning sign. ***Returning the charge*** and ***returning the message*** — both take 復命 transitively (hand it back, the envoy idiom), which **neither commentator supports**, and both need an antecedent the chapter never supplies. ***Returning to what is*** — *what it is* is already 自然 (*zì rán* — of itself)'s English at ch 25, and **ch 51 sets 命 and 自然 against each other in one clause**: 夫莫之命而常自然, *"no one 命s it, and it is always 自然."* That rendering would collapse the book's own antithesis.

**Chapter complete** (`WORKLIST.md` D16, closed 2026-09-05).

**没身不殆 → "and to the end of your days, no danger will find you."** *(2026-08-31, Pass C, `WORKLIST.md` T2-11.)* *(was: "Not even death threatens this.")* **This was a mistranslation, not merely a mismatch.** 没身 (*mò shēn* — "to the end of one's body") is an idiom for **all one's life**, which is exactly how ch 52 renders the identical four characters. The old line read 没身 as *death itself* and handed the sentence to the Tao rather than to the person who has just climbed the ladder 公 → 天 → 道 (*gōng* impartiality → *tiān* the open sky → *dào*).

**河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) settles the subject:** 無殃無咎…乃與天地俱没，**不危殆**也 — *"no calamity, no fault… then one **sinks away together with sky and earth**, not in peril."* The sinking is the person's, and so is the safety.

**殆 is 歺 (*è*) — the bare-bone radical, the death element in 死 (*sǐ* — to die) and 殃 (*yāng* — calamity) — with 台 for sound.** 說文解字: 危也, *"peril."* It reads **danger** in all five of its occurrences now (16, 25, 32, 44, 52), and *danger* renders nothing else in the book.
