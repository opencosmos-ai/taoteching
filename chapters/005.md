# Chapter 5

## Translation

Sky and earth are not concerned with humaneness: they treat the countless things as straw dogs.  
The sage is not concerned with humaneness: they treat everyone as straw dogs.

Isn't the space between sky and earth like a bellows?  
Empty but never depleted, the more it moves, the more it yields.

Speaking of this is a dead end.  
Instead, hold to the center.

## Notes

**以百姓為芻狗 → "they treat everyone as straw dogs."** *(2026-10-14, Shalom's call, `glossary/baixing-百姓.md`. Was: "the people.")* *The people* is 民 (*mín*)'s, and 百姓 (*bǎi xìng*) is the whole population. *Everyone* answers the line above it: sky and earth treat the countless things as straw dogs, and the sage treats everyone the same way.

*不仁 revised 2026-08-28 under the 仁 lock (`glossary/ren-仁.md`). The verse had read "lack benevolence," which was wrong twice: the word, and **lack** — 不仁 is a refusal, not a deficit.*

**The line is not about cruelty, and both commentators say so.** 王弼 names what 仁 would require: 仁者必造立施化，**有恩有為** — *"the 仁 one must construct and impose transformation; there is 恩 (ēn — favour with a debt attached) and there is 為 (wéi — doing)."* So 不仁 is the absence of **management that keeps accounts**, not the absence of care.

河上公 is blunter still: 聖人**愛養**萬民，不以仁恩，法天地行自然 — *"the sage **loves and rears** the people, not by humane favour, but modelling sky and earth in acting so of themselves."* The sage of this chapter is not indifferent.

**A 芻狗 (*chú gǒu*) is a dog plaited from straw for a sacrifice** — dressed and honored through the rite, thrown away after. 河上公 gives the point in five characters: 不貴望其報也, *"expecting no repayment."* The straw dog is not contempt. It is what you are to something that plays no favorites and keeps no ledger.

*English "humane" now means chiefly "not cruel," so "are not humane" would deliver the one reading the commentaries forbid. Hence the longer phrase.*

**守中 → "hold to the center," and *in silence* rendered nothing.** *(2026-09-08, Pass E, `WORKLIST.md` T2-19 — the holding family.)* 守 (*shǒu*) is locked to *hold to* (`glossary/shou-守.md`): 說文 守官也，从宀从寸…寸，法度也 — a roof over a regulation, **the only member of the holding family with no hand in its graph**. What you 守 is a post you are answerable for, which is why the English takes a preposition. The line's *"in silence"* rendered no character and is gone; 多言 (*duō yán* — much speech) two lines above already carries the contrast.
