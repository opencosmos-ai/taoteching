# Chapter 56

## Translation

Those who know do not speak.  
Those who speak do not know.  
Block the openings, close the doors.  
Blunt the sharpness, untangle the knots.  
Dim your own light, be the same as the dust.  
This is called profound sameness.

You cannot draw them near nor hold them off,  
neither benefit nor harm them,  
neither honor nor debase them.  
And so, they become the most honored in the world.

## Notes

**同 occurs twice in this chapter, two lines apart, and the English had used two unrelated words.** *(2026-09-03, `WORKLIST.md` T2-26, the rider ch 23 left.)* 和其光，**同**其塵。是謂玄**同** — *"be the same as the dust; this is called profound sameness."* It had read *"merge with the dust"* and *"the profound union,"* hiding the chapter's own move: **the practice and the name for it are one word.**

**同 (*tóng* — the same) is 異 (*yì* — different)'s antonym**, printed against it in ch 1's 同出而異名, and settled at ch 23 on 2026-09-03. **河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) glosses 同其塵 with 同's opposites**: 不當自**别殊**也 — *"one should not set oneself apart as **separate** and **distinct**."* **王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) makes the couplet a matched pair**: 和其光 is 無所**特顯** (*"nothing specially displayed"*), 同其塵 is 無所**特賤** (*"nothing specially despised"*) — do not stand out above the light, do not hold yourself apart from the dust. ***Merge* read as self-dissolution**; the Chinese says do not consider yourself too good for the common muck.

***Union* was another character's English.** The reverse check returns two lines and the other is ch 55's 牝牡之**合** (*pìn mǔ zhī hé* — "the union of female and male"), where 合 (*hé* — to join) genuinely means union. 同 is not 合.

***Profound sameness* because 是謂玄同 is the same formula as 是謂玄德**, which reads *"This is called profound integrity"* at ch 10, 51 and 65. Same 是謂玄X frame, same English shape. **河上公 reads 玄 here as 天**: 玄，天也。人能行此上事，是謂與天**同道**也 — *"玄 means sky; a person who can do the above is said to be of the **same way** as sky"* — which is 同 again, in his gloss of the compound.

***"Share the dust"* was the first candidate and failed on the link** — short, idiomatic, free in the reverse check, but it gives no noun for 玄同, so the two 同 come apart again. **One test decided it: whatever renders 同 at line 5 must still render it at line 6.**

**Left standing, and named: 和其光 → *"dim your own light"* is the commentator's word.** 說文解字 makes 和 **相譍也**, *"to answer one another"* — the same 應 (*yìng* — to answer) as 河上公's 同聲相應 at ch 23 — and *dim* comes from his 當和之使闇昧 (*"blend it, make it dim"*). `glossary/ming-明.md` already glosses ch 4 and ch 56 together this way, so it is settled rather than sloppy; but **和 and 同 are a pair and only one of them has been repaired.**

**挫其銳，解其紛，和其光，同其塵 — ch 4 now matches this chapter, not the reverse.** *(2026-08-31, Pass C, `WORKLIST.md` T2-14.)* These four lines are verbatim at ch 4, which had rendered them *"It blunts sharpness, / Untangles the tangled, / Softens brilliance, / Is one with every grain of dust."* **This chapter's wording and its couplet lineation were kept**, on two grounds. **This chapter is attested complete at Guodian** (~300 BCE, bundle A, unit 4), the oldest witness there is, while **ch 4 is not attested at all**. And `glossary/ming-明.md` had already prescribed this chapter's word for 和其光: *brilliance* is on 明 (*míng* — clear-seeing)'s `forbidden:` list, and 光 (*guāng*) is 明's counter-term — *"the light a thing **emits**… what the sage is told to soften in Chapters 4 and 56 (和其光 — **dim your light**)."*

**What was not unified, deliberately: the subject.** 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) reads the four lines **here** as a person's practice — 含守質也 (*"holding to and keeping one's substance"*), 除爭原也 (*"removing the source of contention"*), and on 和其光 無所特顯則物無所偏爭也 (*"nothing stands out, so things have nothing to contend over"*) — but reads them at **ch 4** with the **Tao** as subject. Both chapters' contexts back him: here the lines follow 塞其兌，閉其門 (*"block the openings, close the doors"*), which is addressed to a person; at ch 4 they sit between two lines about the Tao. 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) reads both chapters as instruction to a person. **The divergence is left standing** — ch 4's subject travels with its Pass D rewrite (`WORKLIST` T1-2). The nouns match in both chapters now; only the verb forms differ.
