# Chapter 3

## Translation

By not exalting the exceptional, the people have no cause to contend.  
By not prizing rare goods, the people do not become thieves.  
By not displaying the desirable, the people's hearts are not thrown into disorder.

This is how the sage governs:  
emptying their hearts and filling their bellies,  
weakening their ambition and strengthening their bones,  
so the people are ever unknowing, ever desireless,  
and the knowers never push to act.  
Do the not-doing, and nothing is left ungoverned.

## Notes

**使民心不亂 → *the people's hearts are not thrown into disorder*.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-09-21. `glossary/luan-亂.md`.)* Was *"not disturbed"*, which read psychologically in a chapter that is political throughout — contending, thieving, governing, 無不治. **亂 is the governance word**, and this chapter holds it with 治 (*zhì*) twice: 聖人之**治** three lines later and 無不**治** at the close. 說文 defines 亂 *as* 治, and the graph is 𤔔, silk between two hands, plus 乙 — *"what orders it."* The hearts are not a mood here; they are the thing that comes untidy when you show people what to want.


**難得之貨 → "rare goods," and the line was circular before.** *(2026-08-30, Pass C, `WORKLIST.md` T2-14.)* *(was: "By not valuing the precious, no one becomes a thief.")* 不貴難得之貨，使民不為盜 (*bù guì nán dé zhī huò, shǐ mín bù wéi dào*) is a **causal claim about what a ruler does** — the goods are hard to get, the ruler prizes them, people steal. *"Do not value the precious"* puts the value in the object, so it argues in a circle and **the ruler drops out of the sentence**, which is the one place the sentence points. 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) keeps the cause where the Chinese has it: 貴貨過用，貪者競趣，穿窬探篋，沒命而盜 — *"prize goods beyond their usefulness, and the greedy race for them: they tunnel through walls, rifle chests, and steal at the cost of their lives."*

**貨 (*huò* — goods) was wearing 貴's word, in a line where 貴 is the verb.** *Precious* belongs to 貴 (*guì*) at ch 70, 72 and 75; here 不貴 is *does not prize*. 貨 is now locked to *goods* book-wide — see `glossary/huo-貨.md`, which reads the three cowrie characters apart: 貨 is 貝 (*bèi* — a cowrie shell) under 化 (*huà* — a body turning over), **a shell that changes hands**; 寶 (*bǎo* — treasure) is a hoard under a roof; 貴 is a position on a price scale.

***"Rare" and not "scarce," and the reason is this chapter's third line.*** *Scarce* means **a need going unmet**. 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) names the goods — 好珎寶，黃金棄於山，珠玉捐於淵, *"fine treasures; gold left in the mountain, pearls and jade abandoned in the deep"* — and **nobody needs jade**. If the wanting were a real shortfall, honestly felt, the ruler could not be blamed for it. But line three says where the wanting comes from: 不見可欲，使民心不亂 — *"do not display what can be desired, and the people's hearts are not disturbed."* **Display, then appetite, then theft.** 說文 agrees at the level of the graph: 盜 (*dào* — thief) is 私利物也。从㳄 — *"to take private profit from a thing; it follows 㳄 (*xián* — drool)"* — **a person salivating over a dish.** The chapter blames the first link.

**The triad was lopsided in the Chinese and our English had flattened it.** The three objects are 賢 (*xián* — the talented, one character), 難得之貨 (four), 可欲 (*kě yù* — what can be desired, two): **the middle line is deliberately the heaviest.** *"The exceptional / the precious / the desirable"* imposed a neat symmetry by shrinking the longest object to two words.

**民 restored to the middle clause.** All three clauses are 使民X — 使民不爭 / 使民不為盜 / 使民心不亂 — and ours had *"the people"* in the first and third and *"no one"* in the second. This uses no word the stanza does not already carry, and **settles nothing about the deferred 民/人 call** (`WORKLIST` T4-1). 不貴 also reads *prizing* now, matching ch 20's 貴食母 and ch 64.

**The second movement had the opposite grammar from the Chinese.** *(2026-08-31, Pass D, `WORKLIST.md` T1-3.)* *(was: "Therefore the sage / Empties hearts and fills bellies / Weakens striving and strengthens the bones of the people. // Without creating desire or cunning, / Without causing the cunning to act, / Without effort, the sage leaves nothing out of order.")* 常使民無知無欲 / 使夫智者不敢為也 / 為無為，則無不治 are built on 使 (*shǐ* — to cause), 使, and 為 (*wéi* — to do / to handle). The English was built on three *"Without"*s, an anaphora nothing in the Chinese repeats. **The Chinese sage acts; the English sage merely abstained.** The Chinese is one continuous movement under 是以聖人之治, and the English had it as two stanzas.

**為無為 → *"Without effort"* broke the 無為 lock on the one line the lock's own entry cites as its proof.** `glossary/wuwei-無為.md`: *"為無為 (3, 63) — three characters: do the not-doing … 無為 is something you **actively do** … continuous, **effortful**, and difficult."* The line now reads ch 63's English exactly — **"Do the not-doing"** — so the book's home formula says one thing in both places. *(`check_locks.py` could not see the breach: its forbidden list held `"effortless action"`, not `"without effort"`. Both `"effortless"` and `"without effort"` are now on 無為's list and verified firing.)*

**治 (*zhì* — to govern / to order) frames the chapter, and had been dropped from the hinge.** 是以聖人之治 — *"therefore, the sage's governing"* — read only *"Therefore the sage."* 治 opens the second movement and is the chapter's last word; the ring was broken. 無不治 now reads **"nothing is left ungoverned,"** which restores an echo the Chinese has: 無不治 here against 無不為 (*wú bù wéi*) at ch 37 and 48, already rendered *"nothing is left undone."* Ch 65 — 民之難治 (*mín zhī nán zhì* — "the people are hard to govern") — already renders 治 as *govern* three times. 說文解字 on 治 is a **river name**, 水 (*shuǐ* — water) with 台 for sound; governing in this word begins as channelling water, not as forcing it.

**The old English read as manipulation, and the Chinese does not.** Every state the movement names is elsewhere the sage's **own**: 無知 (*wú zhī* — without knowing) is asked of the reader at ch 10, 能無知乎 (*"can you be unknowing?"*); 常…無欲 (*cháng… wú yù* — ever without desire) is the reader at ch 1 and the Tao itself at ch 34, both already rendered **"Ever desireless"**; and 不敢為 (*bù gǎn wéi* — does not push to act) is the sage's own posture at ch 64, 67 and 69. **The English had made asymmetric what the Chinese makes symmetrical, and the asymmetry is what read as coercion.** Three collocations did the work — *"keeping X without knowing"* (an English idiom for *behind someone's back*), *"weakening their will"* (the vocabulary of interrogation), *"keeping X from Y"* (restraint imposed). See [`collocation-carries-a-verdict`](../process/principles/collocation-carries-a-verdict.md).

**And the mechanism is subtraction, which ch 57 states outright.** 我無為，而民自化 … 我無欲，而民自樸 — *"I do not do, and the people transform themselves … I am desireless, and the people become plain of themselves."* The operative character is 自 (*zì* — of themselves). All three of stanza 1's 使 clauses take a **not** as their cause, and 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) spells it out: 故可欲不見，則心無所亂也 — *"so when the desirable is not displayed, the heart has nothing to disorder it."* Nothing is put into anyone. The movement's two 使 clauses are now handled as stanza 1 already handles its three: result of the arrangement, not an act performed on persons.

**河上公 reads the movement as a body, not a population.** He opens it 聖人治國與治身同也 — *"the sage's governing of the state and the governing of the self are one and the same"* — and glosses 實其腹 as 懷道抱一守五神 (*"harbor the Tao, embrace the one, guard the five spirits"*) and 強其骨 as 愛精重施，髓滿骨堅 (*"cherish the vital essence and be sparing in spending it; the marrow fills and the bones grow firm"*). *Govern* holds both readings; it does not shut the inner one out. **What is not smoothed:** this is still a ruler arranging the conditions under which other people want things, and 河上公 titles the chapter 安民 (*ān mín* — "settling the people"). Paternalist is not sinister, and the chapter owns the first.

**弱其志 → "ambition," and 弱 keeps *weaken*.** 說文 on 志: 意也, *"intent"* — 心 (*xīn* — heart) with 之 (*zhī* — to go), the heart going toward something. 王弼: 志生事以亂 — *"ambition generates affairs, and so brings disorder"* — and 心虛則志弱也, *"when the heart is emptied, the ambition weakens,"* which is why the four clauses are one gesture and not four. The lock licenses **weaken** wherever 弱 takes an object, and it does here; the coercion was in *will*, not in *weaken*. **志 is still split against ch 33's *will*** — `WORKLIST` T2-7.

**常 → "ever," restoring a dropped lock term.** 常使民無知無欲 had no *ever* at all, the same fault as ch 28's three 常德. The 常 entry licenses plain *ever / always* adverbially, and 常…無欲 is already **"Ever desireless"** at ch 1 and ch 34.

**敢 applied; it had been dropped outright.** 不敢為 → **"never push to act,"** joining ch 30, 64, 67, 69, 73 and 74.

***智者 → "the knowers," and "cunning" is gone from the chapter.*** *"Cunning"* had covered both 知 (*zhī* — to know) and 智 (*zhì* — knowing-how) in consecutive lines, erasing the distinction exactly where the chapter draws it, and it renders no character it owns — it also wears 巧 (*qiǎo*) at ch 19 and 57. **無知 now reads *unknowing***, ch 10's word for the same compound (能無知乎 — *"can you be unknowing?"*, asked of the practitioner), **and 智者 reads *the knowers*.** *(Shalom's word, against two of mine.)*

**The commentators diverge on who the 智者 are, and the English must not settle it.** 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) prints 智者 and glosses 智者謂知為也 — *"the 智者 are those who know how to handle"* — the operators, whom the line restrains. 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) prints 知者 with the phonetic note 知音智 (*"知 is read* zhì"), and glosses 思慮深，不輕言 — *"deep in deliberation, they do not speak lightly"* — almost word for word what he says of the unambiguously approving 知者 at ch 56, 知者貴行不貴言也. **王弼 has two graphs and two figures; 河上公 has one graph and one figure.** *"Those who know how"* and *"the clever"* both pick 王弼's side. ***The knowers* picks neither** — a bare nominalization, which is exactly what 者 (*zhě*) does in Chinese, leaving the line itself to say whether knowing is good here. The fork is recorded in `sources/variants.yaml` and `notes/manuscript.md`.

***"Those who know" was tried and refused.*** It is **知者's English at ch 56** (知者不言 — *"Those who know do not speak"*) **and ch 81** (知者不博), and in both it names the sage's side. The *string* collides; the *family resemblance* does not, and **河上公 makes that resemblance on purpose.** *The knowers* also chimes with *unknowing* one line above — same root, opposite pole — which is what 河上公's text does with one graph twice. **The book-wide 智 split stays open**: disparaged at ch 18, 19 and 65, neutral at ch 33. `WORKLIST` T2-2.

**Still open on this chapter.** 心 (*xīn* — heart) is dropped from 使民心不亂 in stanza 1, where it also sets up 虛其心 in the movement below; and 亂 (*luàn*) reads *perturbed* there against ch 64's *chaotic* for the same character. 說文 glosses 亂 as **治也** — *"to put in order"* — the tangle and the untangling being one act read from both ends, which is the pair this chapter opens and closes on.

**心 restored to line 3, and 亂 → "disturbed."** *(2026-08-31, with T1-3.)* *(was: "By not displaying the desirable, the people are not perturbed.")* 使民心不亂 (*shǐ mín xīn bù luàn*) had **dropped 心 (*xīn* — heart) at its first appearance**, two lines before the chapter empties it — 虛其心. That is the joint that makes 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*)'s anatomy visible: 心懷智而腹懷食，虛有智而實無知也 — *"the heart harbors clever knowing and the belly harbors food: empty what has 智, fill what has no 知"* — and 骨無知以幹, *"the bones have no knowing, and so they are the frame."* **Knowing lives in the heart, which is why 虛其心 and 常使民無知 are one instruction and not two policies.** *Perturbed* rendered nothing at all.

***"Disturbed," and not "disordered" or "confused."*** 亂 elsewhere in the book is *chaos* (18), *disorder* (38), *chaotic* (64), and the 說文解字 gloss is startling — **亂，治也**, *"亂 means to put in order"* — the graph being hands working tangled silk (𤔔) with the tool that untangles it (乙). **So 亂 and 治 are one act read from both ends, and this chapter opens on the first and closes on the second.** English cannot hold that in one word, and the choice was between the ring and the organ. **The organ wins**: *disordered hearts* is clinical and odd, and 亂 here is something done **to** the heart from outside — 王弼: 故可欲不見，則心無所亂也, *"when the desirable is not displayed, the heart has nothing to disorder it."* Display, then appetite, then theft. ***Confused* was refused on a stronger ground**: it puts the damage in the **knowing**, and this chapter has just spent two lines separating what the heart holds from what disturbs it. 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) pairs 亂 with 惑 (*huò* — led astray) — 不邪淫，不惑亂也, *"no depravity or license; no bewilderment or disorder"* — which is the nearest the evidence comes to *confused*, and it is still agitation rather than misunderstanding. *(Shalom's word.)*

***"Not," not "never" — and the triad's negation is still uneven.*** The three opening clauses carry the identical negation, 不爭 / 不為盜 / 不亂, and our English gives them three shapes: *"have no cause to contend" · "do not become thieves" · "are not disturbed."* Plain *not* makes two of the three match. **The remaining offender is line 1**, where *"have no cause to"* asserts a causal claim 不爭 does not make — still open.

**Rider for ch 26.** *Disturbed* now carries 亂 here, and ch 26's *"their equanimity is undisturbed"* is wearing it for 燕處超然 (*yàn chǔ chāo rán* — "dwells at ease, aloof"), which contains no 亂. Ch 26 is already owed a visit for 靜 (`WORKLIST` T2-5) and 君 (T2-6); this goes with them.
