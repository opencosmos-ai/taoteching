# Chapter 10

## Translation

Housing your aliveness and embracing the one, can you keep these from parting?  
Gathering the breath to softness, can you be like an infant?

Washing and clearing the dark mirror, can you be without blemish?  
Loving the people and governing the state, can you be unknowing?

When the gate of the sky opens and closes, can you be the hen?  
Clarity reaching the four quarters, can you be non-doing?

Giving birth to things and rearing them,  
giving birth without possessing,  
acting yet relying on nothing,  
growing them without ruling them –  
this is called profound integrity.

## Notes

**Five of this chapter's Englishes were attached to characters that are not in it.** *(2026-09-01, Pass D, `WORKLIST.md` T1-1.)* 全 (*quán* — whole), 萬物 (*wàn wù* — the countless things), 鑑 (*jiàn* — a mirror) — **all zero occurrences here** — plus 無為 and 無知, each wearing the other's English. **Every one is invisible to `check_locks`**: its evidence gate keys off the character being *present*, so a locked English on a chapter that lacks the character cannot fire. `--english` is the only thing that finds this class.

**The book's two great negations had swapped lines.** 愛民治國，**能無知乎** read *"can you lead without doing?"* and 明白四達，**能無為乎** read *"can you be unknowing?"* — four lines apart, each carrying the other's word. *Unknowing* has not left the chapter; **it has moved up two lines onto 無知**, where ch 3 settled it. 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) reads this 無知 as **無以智**, *"without cleverness,"* and links it outright to ch 19's 絕聖棄智: 玄覽無疵猶絕聖也，治國無以智猶棄智也 — *"'the dark mirror without blemish' is like 'cut off sagacity'; 'governing the state without cleverness' is like 'discard cleverness.'"* 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) reads it differently — 治國者布施惠德，**無令下知**也, *"one governing the state spreads kindness and lets none below know of it"* — but that requires supplying a second party the grammar does not have. **無為 keeps the lock's word**, used predicatively as ch 37 already does (*"The Tao is ever non-doing"*).

**疵 was not mistranslated; it was deleted.** 能無疵乎 asks *"can you be without blemish?"* — 說文解字: **病也**, under 疒, the sickness radical. Ours asked *"can you see what truly is?"*, which renders nothing at all. **國 (*guó* — the state) was dropped too**, from 愛民治**國**, in a chapter whose whole second couplet is about governing.

***"The dark mirror" was challenged and survives, and my objection to it was wrong.*** *(Caught by Shalom.)* I had argued that *mirror* renders 鑑 / 監 (*jiàn*), which our text does not print — the ch 52 襲/習 fault. **說文 defeats that: 覽，觀也。从見、監，監亦聲** — *"覽 means to look at; from 見 (to see) **and 監**, and 監 is also the phonetic."* **監 is a constituent of the very character we print**, and 監 is a person bending over a water basin at their own reflection, the ancestor of 鑑. The mirror is the character's own base, not an imported variant. **And the draft *"the dark seeing"* failed on the verb**: 滌除 (*dí chú* — wash and clear) needs a washable surface, and 河上公 says exactly what gets washed — 當**洗其心**使潔淨也, *"wash the heart and make it clean."* **疵 confirms it**: a blemish sits on a surface, and 王弼's gloss is more surface-like still — 能不以物**介**其明，**疵之**其神乎, *"can you keep things from **coming between** you and your clarity, or **blemishing** your spirit?"* **The cost, recorded:** both commentators gloss 覽 with *activity* verbs (觀 · 覽知萬事 · 極覽), so **the mirror is a reading of the graph, not of the lemma.**

**專 → "gathering," on the graph against a commentator.** 說文 makes 專 a **spindle whorl** — 一曰專，紡專 — thread wound onto a core. 河上公 agrees: 專守精氣使不亂 (*"concentrate and guard the essential breath so it is not disordered"*). **王弼 goes the other way** — 專，**任**也, *"專 means to entrust oneself to"* — nearly the opposite. **The character decides**, per the tie-breaking order, and the divergence is recorded rather than averaged.

**氣 → "breath," not "life energy."** 河上公's reading here is bodily and concrete: 呼吸精氣 (*"breathing the essential 氣"*), and 治身者愛氣則身全 (*"one who governs the body loves the 氣 and the body is whole"*). *Energy* is the register ch 28 has just been cleared of. **The book-wide call is still owed** — `WORKLIST` T2-4, where 氣 also reads *vital breath* (42) and *vital energy* (55).

**能為雌乎 → "can you be the hen?"** — the rider ch 28 left. 為 is *serve as / be*, not *embody*, and *"the flow of the feminine"* invented **flow** and abstracted the bird away. **王弼 ties the hen straight to this chapter's last question:** 雌**應而不倡，因而不為** — *"the hen responds and does not lead off; she goes along and does not act."* 河上公 pairs her with the other female graph — 治身當如**雌牝**安靜柔弱 — and gives the political half: 治國應變和而不唱, *"governing the state, respond to change harmoniously and do not lead off."* **The line's own graph is contested**; see `notes/manuscript.md`.

**嬰兒 → "an infant," closing T2-22.** It read *"a newborn"* here and *"an infant"* at ch 20 and ch 28, while 赤子 (*chì zǐ* — a red child) reads *"a newborn"* at ch 55. One character, one English, each way. 王弼: 能若嬰兒之**無所欲**乎 — *"can you be like an infant that has no wants?"*

***"Things," not "the countless things."*** 生之畜之 has **no antecedent anywhere in this chapter** — 萬物 does not occur — and both commentators supply one: 河上公 道生**萬物**而畜養之, 王弼 則**物**自生. So the English must supply one too, and **物 (*wù* — things)** is what the glossary carries as the secondary character, where 萬物 is a locked compound this chapter does not contain.

**長 → "growing," which also repairs ch 51.** 長而不宰 read *"leading without mastering,"* but 長 (*zhǎng*) here is the causative *make grow* — and **ch 51 already renders it *"grows"* four lines earlier** (長之育之 → *"grows and raises them"*), so that chapter was running two Englishes for one character in one stanza. 河上公: 道**長**養萬物，不**宰割**以為器用 — *"the Tao grows and nourishes them and does not **butcher** them into **vessels** for use"* — 宰 with 割 and 器, both of them ch 28's words. 說文 on 宰: **辠人在屋下執事者。从宀从辛** — *"a criminal under a roof doing service,"* from a roof and 辛, the tattooing knife. **Ch 51 takes the same change**, since T2-15 closed these two as a matched pair and they must stay matched.

**魄 → "aliveness": *"Housing your aliveness."*** *(Shalom's word, 2026-10-15. Full argument: `glossary/po-魄.md`.)* *(was: "housing your soul," from 2026-09-01.)* **"Housing" did the watchlist's work with a verb, and the noun was still the watchlist's word.** Every early source puts 魄 on the body's side: 杜預 (*Dù Yù*) 魄，**形**也, *"魄 is the form"*; 孔穎達 (*Kǒng Yǐngdá*) **附形之靈**為魄, *"the life clinging to the form,"* and then a newborn's senses, moving limbs and cry, 初生之時，耳目心識，手足運動，啼呼為聲，此則魄之靈也; 鄭玄 (*Zhèng Xuán*) **耳目之聰明**為魄, *"the sharpness of ear and eye."* **Aliveness** carries all three, comes in degrees as the 魄 does (左傳 魂魄**強**), and carries no theology, no breath and no energy. **The couplet now names the two halves the 禮記 pairs**: 氣也者神之盛也，魄也者鬼之盛也, *"breath is the fullness of spirit, the 魄 the fullness of ghost,"* aliveness in line 1 and the breath in line 2, and the infant line 2 asks for is 孔穎達's own picture of the 魄. ***Life force*, *vitality* and *vigor* were refused**: the first is the popular English for 氣; *vital* is 精's and 氣's (21, 42, 55); *vigor* leans toward virility, which is 精's at ch 55.

**Two claims below are corrected, and stay where they were made.** The pair 魂魄 is **not a Han invention**: the 左傳 has it (人生始化曰魄，既生魄，陽曰魂) and so does 韓非子 解老 on ch 60 (魂魄去而精神亂). What is Han in 河上公's gloss is the **organ map**, 魂在肝，魄在肺. And **"housing" does not follow 王弼**: his 營魄 is the house and the person dwells in it (言人能處常居之宅, *"a person who can dwell in the house where they always live"*). The direction *housing* takes, a person holding the 魄, is 王逸 (*Wáng Yì*)'s on 楚辭 遠遊's 載營魄而登霞兮 (抱我魂靈而上升也, *"embracing my soul I rise"*) and 河上公's 當愛養之, *"you must cherish and nourish it."* The verb stands on 營's graph and on those readings.

**載營魄 → "housing your soul," and the decisive fact is a character that is not in this book.** *(Shalom's line, 2026-09-01.)* *(was: "Being embodied"; then a draft, "carrying the body's soul.")* **魂 (*hún* — the cloud-soul) occurs zero times in all 81 chapters.** 魄 and 營 each occur once, both here. So 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*)'s reading — 營魄，**魂魄**也…魂在肝，魄在肺, *"營魄 is the 魂 and the 魄… the 魂 is in the liver, the 魄 in the lungs"* — is a **Han two-soul system imported onto a text that never uses the other half of the pair.** That is why a draft rendering *"the body's soul"* read as jargon: it answered a question neither English nor this book asks.

**王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) needs nothing imported, and the graphs back him.** 載猶**處**也。營魄，人之**常居處**也。一，人之**真**也 — *"載 is like 'to dwell.' 營魄 is a person's constant dwelling place. 一 is a person's genuine self."* **A person's constant dwelling place is the body.** 說文解字: **營，市居也。从宮** — a walled residence; **魄，陰神也。从鬼白聲** — the yin spirit, with a ghost for its radical, the one billeted in the flesh, not the one that leaves; **載，乘也。从車** — to ride, with a cart in the graph. **So 營魄 is not two souls. It is one thing: the quarters your animating part is camped in.**

**The rendering does the watchlist's work with a verb rather than a label.** CLAUDE.md flags 精/魄 → *soul*, and the corrective for 魄 is that it is the **corporeal** one. *"The body's soul"* said so with a possessive and read as jargon; **housing** says so with an action, and it renders 營's own graph. ***These*** carries 能無離乎 onto the two things the line has just named, which is how both commentators read it: 王弼 抱一清神，**能常無離乎**; 河上公 抱一使**不離於身**.

**Four candidate Englishes were blocked, and it is worth recording which.** *Spirit* is 神 (*shén*)'s at ch 6, 39, 60. *Flesh* is free and Pauline — *the flesh* against *the spirit*, Romans 8 — the hymn test. *Quarters* is taken four lines below by this chapter's own 四達. And ***body* is 身 (*shēn*)'s** at ch 13, 54 and 76; 身 does not occur in ch 10, so nothing clashed inside the chapter, but the noun was avoided rather than borrowed. ***Embodying* was refused too**: it is this manuscript's junk-drawer verb, wearing 為 at ch 28 until this pass removed it and still wearing 執 at ch 35 (`WORKLIST` T1-12).

**This also gives the chapter its floor.** Question 1 is the bodily ground, question 2 the breath, then the mirror, then governing, then response, then non-doing — **it moves outward from the flesh**, and the old *"Being embodied"* left it starting nowhere.

**天門 keeps *the gate of the sky*, and its strain case gains a second reading.** 河上公 gives both: 天門謂**北極紫微宮** (*"the Purple Tenuity Palace at the north pole"*) and, for the body, 天門謂**鼻孔**，開謂喘息，闔謂呼吸也 — *"天門 means the **nostrils**: 開 is breathing out, 闔 breathing in."* 王弼 takes neither: 天門謂天下之所由從也 — *"that by which the world proceeds."* Logged at `notes/translation.md` → 天 family, strain cases.

**The en-dash stays.** `WORKLIST` T4-3 is deferred, and this line's fault was its words, not its punctuation.
