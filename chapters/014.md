# Chapter 14

## Translation

Look, but you cannot see it – its name is the invisible.  
Listen, but you cannot hear it – its name is the inaudible.  
Reach, but you cannot touch it – its name is the intangible.  
These three defy comprehension.  
Therefore, they mix into one.

It doesn’t have a bright top,  
Or a murky bottom.  
Continuously flowing, it cannot be named  
And returns to no-thing.  
This is the shape of no-shape, the image of no-thing.  
This is called the elusive.

Meet it, and you cannot see its face,  
Follow it, and you cannot see its back.

Grasp the ancient Tao, and steer what is present now.  
Know the primordial source,  
The thread of the Tao.

## Notes

**其上不皦，其下不昧 — 昧 → *murky*.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-09-20. `WORKLIST.md` T2-54.)* Rode in with ch 41's 明道若昧, since one character takes one English. The line had read *"a dark bottom"*, and *dark* belongs to 玄 (*xuán*), which is absent from this chapter. 皦 (*jiǎo*) keeps *bright* and still has no entry.


**執古之道 → "Grasp the ancient Tao," and 搏 gives back the word it had taken.** *(2026-09-08, Pass E, `WORKLIST.md` T2-19 — the holding family.)* 執 (*zhí*) is 說文 **捕罪人也**, *"to seize a criminal"* — a reaching hand and a set of manacles — and it is locked to *grasp* (`glossary/zhi-執.md`). The line had read *"Hold"* on the argument that *grasp the Tao* risks reading as *comprehend the Tao*. **The real collision was inside this chapter:** 搏之不得 (*bó zhī bù dé*) read *"you cannot **grasp** it"* thirteen lines above, so 搏 (*bó* — 說文 索持也, *"to search out and grip"*) was wearing 執's English on 執's own page. That line now reads *"Reach, but you cannot **touch** it."* **得 (*dé* — to get) is the generic completion verb here, not a lexical *obtain*, and 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) proves it from inside his own gloss** — he uses 得 as a bare modal in the two lines above (不可**得**視而見之 / 不可**得**聽而聞之) and frames the triad by what is absent: 無**色**曰夷 · 無**聲**曰希 · 無**形**曰微. Colour, sound, form: sight, hearing, **touch**. Chinese has 見 (*jiàn* — to see) and 聞 (*wén* — to hear) for the first two senses and no dedicated verb for succeeding by hand, so it reaches for 得; English has one. **The cost, recorded:** 得 renders *get* at chapters 13, 23, 31 and 61 and *received* at 39, and this line departs from that. *Grasp* and *hold* were both unavailable — the first is 執's, standing in this chapter, and the second is 持's. **And ch 35's 執大象 was settled as *Grasp the great image* in Pass D**, so keeping *Hold* here split one character across the two chapters that print the same construction: 執 + a name for the Tao + a consequence for the world.

**夷 · 希 · 微 — the chapter names three characters, and the English gives their meanings rather than their words.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-09-10 — `WORKLIST.md` T2-52.)* This stanza is a **definition passage**: 名曰夷 · 名曰希 · 名曰微, *"its name is called X,"* three times. Each of the three is rendered here by what it means in this line, not by the word it carries elsewhere in the book.

| | 說文解字 | here | elsewhere |
|---|---|---|---|
| **夷** (*yí*) | 平也 — *"level."* 大 (a person) with 弓 (a bow) | the invisible | **level** — ch 41, 53 |
| **希** (*xī*) | the graph is 爻 (crossed threads) over 巾 (cloth): **loose-woven fabric you can see through** | the inaudible | **barely heard** — ch 41 · **sparse** — ch 23, 43, 70, 74 |
| **微** (*wēi*) | 隱行也 — *"to move concealed,"* on 彳 (the step radical) | the intangible | **faint** — ch 15, 64 · **hidden** — ch 36 |

**河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) supplies exactly these three readings, and the English follows him:**

> 無**色**曰夷 · 無**聲**曰希 · 無**形**曰微
> *"Colourless is called 夷. Soundless is called 希. Formless is called 微."*

Colour, sound, form — the three ways a thing presents to the senses, and the stanza denies all three in turn. He runs the same sentence three times: 言一無采色，不可得視而見之 · 言一無音聲，不可得聽而聞之 · 言一無形體，不可搏持而得之.

**The alternative was weighed and declined.** Rendering the words themselves — *"its name is the level · the sparse · the faint"* — would turn all three into live threads running through nine other chapters, and **王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) argues for it**: at ch 41 he defines 希 by quoting this stanza outright, 聽之不聞名曰希, which makes 大音希聲 and this line **one word**. It was declined because **it costs this chapter its clarity, and chapter 14 is early** — a reader meeting three bare adjectives here has nothing yet to hang them on. **Carrying the connection is a job for these notes, not for the verse**, and the notes at chapters 15, 23, 36, 41, 43, 53, 64, 70 and 74 each point back here.
