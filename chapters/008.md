# Chapter 8

## Translation

The most masterful are like water.  
Water is masterful at benefiting the countless things and does not contend.  
It dwells in what the crowd loathes,  
and so is nearly the Tao.

In settling, masterful at choosing ground.  
In the heart, masterful at staying deep.  
In giving, masterful at humaneness.  
In speech, masterful at trust.  
In government, masterful at order.  
In affairs, masterful at what can be done.  
In moving, masterful at timing.

Because it does not contend,  
there is no blame.

## Notes

**政善治 → *In government, masterful at order*.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-09-23. `glossary/zhi-治.md`.)* Was *"In governing."* **The decision was about part of speech, not consistency.** The seven first slots of this stanza are a genuine mix in the Chinese, and the English renders each by **its own** part of speech — 居 (*jū* — to dwell), 與 (*yǔ* — to give) and 動 (*dòng* — to move) as verbs; 心 (*xīn* — heart), 言 (*yán* — speech) and 事 (*shì* — affairs) as nouns. **政 was the one slot rendered against its own**, and the book settles it elsewhere: its only other two lines are 其政悶悶 and 其政察察 at ch 58, where 其 (*qí* — its) can take nothing but a noun. 說文 agrees on the register — 政，正也。从攴从正, a hand holding a rod (攴 *pū*) making things straight.

**And it is what had pushed 治 onto *order*.** 政 and 治 are near-twins — modern Chinese fuses them into 政治 (*zhèngzhì* — politics) — so with both wearing verbs the line stuttered. With 政 nominal, 治's flexion to *order* here stands on its own reason: the object is the institution, not a people. 河上公 reads 治 in this chapter through water alone: 无有不洗清且平也, *"there is nothing it does not wash clean and level."*

**居 → *settling*, not *dwelling*, and the reason is four lines above.** 處 (*chǔ*) holds *dwell* at 處眾人之所惡 — *"it dwells in what the crowd loathes"* — in this same chapter, which is the sharpest kind of collision. 說文 separates them cleanly: 居，**蹲也**, *"to squat,"* on 尸 (*shī* — a seated body); 處，**止也。得几而止**, *"to stop; having got a stool (几 *jǐ*), one stops."* 居 is taking up a place; 處 is coming to rest where a seat has been set. **居's other lines are not yet consistent** — `WORKLIST.md` T2-57.


**Nine 善 (*shàn* — masterful), rendered nine ways, and one of them deleted outright.** *(2026-09-02, Pass D, `WORKLIST.md` T2-8 and T3-2.)* The chapter is 善's showcase — nine occurrences in thirteen lines, more concentrated than anywhere else in the book — and the lock had been applied to none of them. What stood instead was *supreme goodness · a solid foundation · limitless depth · pure kindness · trustworthy · good order · skillful · good timing*, plus a **deletion**: 水**善**利萬物 read *"it nourishes the countless things,"* with the 善 gone. **The Chinese is a list of trades; the English had made it a list of virtues.**

**善 governs a verb, everywhere in the book, and that decides this chapter's grammar.** X善Y admits two parses — 善 as a verb taking Y (*"masterful **at** Y"*) or 善 as an adjective modifying Y (*"masterful Y"*) — and nothing in these seven lines settles it. **The other eighteen instances do.** 善行 · 善言 · 善計 · 善閉 · 善結 · 善救 ×2 · 善為 ×2 · 善貸 · 善攝 · 善建 · 善抱 · 善下 · 善戰 · 善勝 ×2 · 善用 · 善應 · 善謀 — *travel, speak, reckon, shut, tie, rescue, handle, give, nurture, establish, embrace, go below, fight, overcome, use, respond, plan.* **Eighteen verbs, zero nouns.** And ch 8 sets the pattern itself four lines above the stanza with 善**利** (*lì* — to benefit), which is unambiguously verbal. The adjectival parse also collapses on its own terms: it yields *masterful order · masterful ability · masterful timing*, which read, and *masterful ground · masterful depth · masterful humaneness · masterful trust*, which do not. **A reading that survives three of seven loses to one that survives all seven.**

**Two of the seven objects name a *place*, and English needs a verb there.** *(Caught by Shalom: *"masterful at ground"* communicates nothing.)* The finding above says 善 governs a **verb** — and the first draft then rendered all seven objects as bare nouns, which is the finding applied to the argument and not to the English. *Masterful at X* requires X to name an **activity**: it holds for 時 (*shí* — timing), 能 (*néng* — able), 仁 (*rén* — humaneness), 信 (*xìn* — trust) and 治 (*zhì* — order), all of them qualities or practices, and it collapses for 地 (*dì* — ground) and 淵 (*yuān* — a deep pool), which are **places**. So a stance verb is supplied at exactly those two: **choosing ground** and **staying deep**.

**This is house practice, not a new licence.** Ch 66 does it already, in the same construction: 善下之 (*shàn xià zhī*) reads *"masterful at **staying** below them"* — 下 (*xià* — below) is a position, and the English supplies the verb. Ch 61's 以靜為下 takes *staying low* the same way. **The supplied word is scaffolding, not a character's English**, which is why *staying* can serve 下 and 淵 without either losing its own word.

**The verbs were chosen against the reverse check, and two obvious ones were rejected by it.** *Keeping trust* was declined because *keep* is 全 (*quán* — whole)'s declared render and the manuscript already carries **nine unbacked *keeps***; *bringing order* was declined because *bring* belongs to 生 (*shēng* — to give birth) and is already unbacked seven times. **擇 (*zé* — to choose) and 選 (*xuǎn* — to select) have zero occurrences in all 81 chapters**, so *choosing* takes nothing from anyone. *Staying deep* follows 說文's 淵: **回水也…左右，岸也**, *"swirling water… left and right are the banks"* — banked water that **stays**, which is why *going deep* was wrong for it and why *limitless* was backwards.

**The cost, named:** *choosing* imports agency the water image does not have (河上公 has water *flowing down* to the ground, not selecting it). What licenses it is that the seven couplets are the **person's** application — 上善之人如水之性 — where agency belongs, as it does to 與 (giving), 言 (speech) and 政 (governing) in the same stanza. And *deep* is 深 (*shēn* — deep)'s adjective in five other lines, with no 深 in this chapter; that overlap rides with T2-24, where 淵 is still open.

**幾 is an adverb of degree, and both drafts before this one made it a verb.** 故幾於道 first read *"And is therefore **one with** the Tao"* — identity asserted where the character asserts a hair's breadth — and the repair over-corrected into *"it **comes near** the Tao,"* which installed a distance and sent water walking across it. *(Caught by Shalom: it implies the Tao is somewhere else.)* **幾於道 has no verb in it at all.** 說文解字 on 幾 (*jī*): **微也。殆也。从𢆶从戍。戍，兵守也。𢆶而兵守者，危也** — *"minute; verging. From 𢆶 (yōu — fine silk threads) and 戍 (shù — a garrison guard). Threads under armed guard: that is danger."* Its second gloss is 殆 (*dài*), the character locked to **danger** on 2026-08-31.

**The manuscript already knew this in three other places, and only ch 8 moved.** Ch 69 輕敵**幾**喪吾寶 reads *"**nearly** costs me my treasures"*; ch 64 **幾**成 reads *"**just short of** completing them"*; ch 20 相去**幾**何 reads *"**how much** distance?"* Three degrees, one journey, and the journey was ours.

**The construction is sameness, not location.** 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) glosses the line 水性**幾與道同** — *"water's nature is **nearly the same as** the Tao"* — where 幾 modifies 同 (*tóng* — the same), an adverb sitting on a stative verb. And the book prints the frame without the degree word **seven times in ch 23**: 同於道 (*tóng yú dào* — "the same as the Tao"). So 幾於道 is 同於道 with a hair's breadth inserted, and 於 (*yú*) is the all-purpose relational particle — *with respect to*, never *toward*. **The Tao is not placed anywhere, so nothing can travel to it.**

***Like the Tao* was proposed and declined on three deletions and a collision.** It drops 故 (*gù* — therefore), which is the chapter's inference and occurs twice here; it drops 幾, the only word keeping the line honest; **and *like* is 若's word, printed in this chapter's own first line** — 上善**若**水. It also moves the claim: *"it dwells in what the crowd loathes, like the Tao"* predicates the **dwelling** of the Tao, where the Chinese predicates **nearness** of water.
**上善 → "the most masterful," following 河上公's person-reading.** *(Shalom's call.)* 上 (*shàng* — highest) is settled at ch 38, where 上德 / 上仁 / 上義 / 上禮 all read *the highest*, so *"Supreme"* was the outlier. And *good* is reserved by the lock for chapters 2, 20 and 58, where the book **attacks** the category (皆知善之為善，斯不善已 — *"when all know good as good, the not-good arises"*) — using it here would make this chapter endorse what ch 2 dismantles. 河上公: **上善之人如水之性** — *"the person of the highest 善 is like the nature of water"* — which is 善人 (*shàn rén* — the masterful) at ch 27, 49 and 62. **The cost:** it decides that 上善 is a person rather than the quality, which the Chinese leaves open. ***True* was considered and rejected** — 真 (*zhēn* — genuine) is a live character in this book (21, 41, 54), and 上 is a graded rank word whose middle rung is printed at ch 41 (上士/中士/下士 — upper, middle and lower 士). *(A first draft of that argument claimed the chapter stages an 上/下 descent lexically. **It does not — 下 (*xià* — below) has zero occurrences here.** The descent is carried by low nouns, 地 and 淵, and by 所惡; the explicit pair lives at ch 66 and ch 76. Caught by Shalom.)*

**利 → "benefiting," and *nourishes* was another character's English.** `--english` found it: *nourish* appears at ch 34 (衣**養**萬物) and ch 51 (**養**之覆之), both 養 (*yǎng* — to nourish), which is **not in this chapter**. 利 (*lì* — to benefit) already reads *benefit* at ch 19, 56, 73 and 81 — including 81's 利而不害, *"benefits and does not harm,"* the same 而 (*ér* — and) construction as this line. 說文 makes it a blade: **銛也。从刀** — *"sharp; from 刀 (dāo — a knife)."*

**惡 is *loathe*, not avoidance.** 處眾人之所惡 had read *"where the crowd will not go."* 河上公 marks the reading with a *fanqie* pronunciation note — **惡烏路反**, fixing it as *wù*, the verb *to detest*, not *è*, the adjective *bad* — and glosses it 衆人惡卑濕垢濁, *"the crowd loathes the low, the damp, the dirty and the turbid."* The line's force is that water occupies what everyone finds disgusting.

**仁 → "humaneness" is simply the lock**, and *"pure kindness"* breached it while inventing *pure*. `check_locks.py` stayed silent because *kindness* is not on 仁's `forbidden:` list — the same blind spot that hid ch 38's *clutches at*. 河上公 on 與善仁: **萬物得水以生，與虚不與盈也** — *"the countless things get water and live; it gives to the empty and does not give to the full."*

**地 → "ground," matching ch 50's 死地** (*sǐ dì* — "deadly ground"). *Solid* and *foundation* each rendered nothing; the reverse check returns ch 8 as the only line in the book carrying *foundation*.

**能 → "what can be done," built from *can*.** 能 (*néng* — able) has **32 lines in 17 chapters, and 31 of them are the plain modal *can*** — 能無離乎, 故能長生, 天下莫能與之爭. This is the **only** place in the book where it is a noun, and *ability* was a Latinate abstraction imported for that one line, stacking two nouns of the same kind beside *masterful*. 說文 makes it a bear — **熊屬。足似鹿…能獸堅中，故稱賢能** (*"a kind of bear, feet like a deer… the 能 beast is solid within, therefore we call the worthy-and-capable 賢能"*) — so the sense runs from **packed-in capacity** to capability, and nothing in it moves. 河上公 reads the line as versatility: **能方能圓，曲直隨形** — *"it can be square, it can be round; bent or straight, it follows the shape."* ***Action* was proposed and fails twice**: 動 (*dòng* — to move) is the action word in the very next line, and *action* already wears 行 (*xíng* — to conduct oneself), 作 (*zuò* — to start up), 事 (*shì* — affairs, which is locked) and nothing at all across four other chapters.

**"Always blameless" lost both halves.** 常 (*cháng* — the ever-present) has **zero occurrences here**, so *always* rendered nothing. And 尤 (*yóu* — blame) is a **hapax**, its only appearance in all 81 chapters, meaning fault-finding rather than moral purity; *blameless* fails the hymn test. 河上公: **天下无有怨尤水者也** — *"there is no one in the world who resents or blames water."* Kept agentless, as 無尤 is, where 河上公 supplies the agents.

**夫唯不爭 now matches ch 22**, which shares the formula: *"Because they do not contend…"* there, *"Because it does not contend…"* here.

**居 and 處 both land on dwelling words, and the seam is named rather than forced.** 說文 makes them near-twins — 處 (*chǔ*) is **止也。得几而止** (*"to stop; to find a stool and stop"*), 居 (*jū*) is **蹲也** (*"to squat"*) — and ch 38 already uses them as stylistic variants in parallel clauses (see `notes/manuscript.md`). Here 處 keeps *dwells*, its English at ch 24, 31, 38 and 76, and 居 takes ***settling***, which is 說文's squat and leaves the two distinguishable.

**Left open: 淵 (*yuān*) has three Englishes and none of them is what it is.** *Fathomless* (4), *limitless depth* (8), *the deep abyss* (36). 說文: **回水也…左右，岸也** — *"swirling water… left and right are the banks."* **It has banks**, which is why a fish cannot leave one at ch 36, and why *limitless* is precisely backwards. *Depth* is a holding move; the term is now on `WORKLIST.md` as T2-24.

**A fork logged, which may not be a fork.** 河上公's text prints 正善治, our base prints 政善治. See `notes/manuscript.md`.

**河上公 reads the ground line through the feminine**, and it is worth a reader's note rather than a rendering: 水性善喜於地…**有似於牝**，動而下人也 — *"water's nature delights in the ground… **it resembles the female** (牝 *pìn* — the female animal), moving downward, below people."* That puts ch 8 in the same family as ch 6's 玄牝 (*xuán pìn* — the dark female) and ch 61's 牝常以靜勝牡 (*"the female always overcomes the male by stillness"*).
