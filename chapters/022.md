# Chapter 22

## Translation

Bend, and stay whole. Be wronged, and be made straight.  
Be hollowed out, and be filled. Be worn out, and be renewed.  
Hold little and gain. Hold much and be confused.

Therefore the sage embraces one thing and serves as the world's pattern.

They do not show themselves off, and so they see clearly.  
They do not insist they are right, and so their rightness shows.  
They do not claim the work, and so the work is theirs.  
They do not boast, and so they endure.

Because they do not contend, no one in the world can contend with them.  
When the ancients said "bend, and stay whole," were those empty words?  
These words hold. Return to them. Stay whole.

## Notes

**不自矜 → "They do not boast."** *(2026-10-14, Shalom's call. Was: "They do not make themselves great.")* *Great* is 大 (*dà*)'s locked English and there is no 大 in the line. The old rendering took the commentary gloss 矜，大也 as its English. *Boast* is 矜's word at ch 30 (果而勿矜), and ch 24 mirrors this line, so the three now agree.

*Revised 2026-08-27, from the `WORKLIST.md` debt on 不自是. Full argument for all five decisions in `notes/translation.md` · Ch 22.*

**王弼 maps stanza three onto stanza one, one-to-one.** 不自見其明**則全**也 · 不自是**則其是彰**也 · 不自伐**則其功有**也 · 不自矜**則其德長**也 — the four 不自X (*bù zì X* — "does not self-X") lines are the four opening paradoxes restated as conduct. The chapter is one argument told twice, and **what you refuse to claim is what comes to you**. Note Wang Bi repeats the character: 不自是則其**是**彰也.

**抱一 → "embraces one thing," a deliberate de-mystification.** 王弼: **一，少之極也** — *"the one is the extreme of 少 (*shǎo* — few)"* — anchored in the line above, 少則得，多則惑. The least you can hold is one. *"The one"* now reads in English as a chosen-one trope, so the definite article was doing damage the Chinese never does; 一 (*yī*) is a single stroke, a tally mark. A **per-chapter call, not a lock** — 一 keeps *the one* at ch 14, 39, 42. See `glossary/yi-一.md`.

**明 → *see clearly*, retiring "luminous."** `glossary/ming-明.md` already used this line as its worked example; the verse was stale against its own entry. 河上公: 聖人**不以其目視**千里之外也，乃**因天下之目以視** — *"the sage does not see a thousand li with their own eyes; they see by means of the world's eyes."* Reception, not emission.

**式 (*shì*) is a template, not a moral example.** 弋 (*yì* — a driven stake) with 工 (*gōng* — a carpenter's square). 王弼: 式猶則之也. 河上公: 法式也. Ch 28's 常德**不忒** (*tè* — to deviate) proves it: you can only deviate from a standard.

**誠全而歸之 had no English at all** — the line read *"Try and see for yourself."* 全 (*quán* — whole) is the word the chapter opens on and quotes back, so restoring it forced 曲則全 → *"Bend, and stay whole."*

**誠 answers 虛言.** 豈**虛言**哉！**誠**全而歸之 — 虛言 (*xū yán* — empty words) answered by 誠, which is 言 (*yán* — words) + 成 (*chéng* — to complete): **words made good**. Hence *"These words hold,"* not a bare *in truth*.

**全 is preservation, not repair.** 河上公 on 曲則全: 則**全其身**也, *"then keeps their body whole."* The chapter is about not getting broken in the first place, so neither *makes you whole* nor *restores your wholeness* can stand — both presuppose the loss. **歸 → return**, the book's word for it in seven chapters, and the person is the one who moves (歸根 · 復歸於樸 · 復歸於嬰兒).

**之 → *them*, the words — and *home* was deliberately refused.** 歸 does carry homecoming; every destination this book gives it is the original state. But **home is not in the line**, and supplying it would hand the reader a conclusion the text lets them reach. The verse delivers the words; the depth belongs to the reader's own arrival. See `DISCOVERIES.md` §5. 河上公's filial reading (歸之於父母, from the *Xiaojing*) is declined as a Han Confucian import.

**Owed elsewhere:** ch 28 carries 為天下式 twice and needs the same 式 treatment; ch 24's 自伐 → *"boasts"* may want 伐 → *claim*. Both in `WORKLIST.md`.

**為天下式 → "serves as the world's pattern."** *(2026-09-01, with ch 28, closing `WORKLIST.md` T2-15.)* *(was: "sets a pattern for the world.")* The formula stands here and three times at ch 28, and the two chapters had rendered it two ways. **為 (*wéi*) is locked to *do / handle / serve as*** — *sets… for* read it as the other tone, 為 (*wèi* — for the sake of), which turns a role into an intention. The sage does not **make** a pattern for anybody; the sage **is** one.
