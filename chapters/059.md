# Chapter 59

## Translation

When governing people and serving nature, nothing compares to conserving.  
Conserving is called early accord,  
early accord is called a heavy accumulation of integrity,  
a heavy accumulation of integrity is when nothing cannot be overcome,  
when nothing cannot be overcome, no one knows its limit,  
when no one knows its limit, you can have the state.  
Have the mother of the state, and you can last long.

This is called the Tao of deep roots, firm base, long life, and enduring vision.

## Notes

**早服 → "early accord," twice.** *(2026-10-14, Shalom's call. Was: "returning early.")* 服 (*fú*) has no sense of return, and no witness recorded here reads 復 (*fù* — return). All three commentators read it as accord or taking in. 王弼: 早服常也 (*"early 服 is constancy"*). 河上公: 服得也 (*"服 is to obtain"*). 韓非 (*Hán Fēi*): 夫能令故德不去，新和氣日至者，蚤服者也 (*"one who keeps their old integrity from leaving while new harmonious breath arrives each day is one who 蚤服"*). *Submit* was the literal column's word and reads as defeat.

**有國之母 → "Have the mother of the state," and 有 stops reading two ways in adjacent lines.** *(2026-09-08, Pass E, `WORKLIST.md` T2-19 — the holding family.)* The lines had read 可以**有**國 → *"one can **maintain** the state"* and 有國之母 → *"**Holding** the mother of the state"* — the same character, two Englishes, one line apart, and neither of them 有's. 有 (*yǒu*) is locked in `glossary/wu-you-無有.md`, and *hold* belongs to 持 (`glossary/chi-持.md`). Both now read **have**, so the repetition the Chinese builds the couplet on is audible. **有's transitive uses stay open** beyond the settled 有道者 → *those who are with the Tao* (24, 31, 77).
