# Chapter 33

## Translation

One who knows others is clever.  
One who knows themselves is clear-seeing.

One who overcomes others has force.  
One who overcomes themselves is strong.

One who knows they have enough is wealthy.  
One who walks with strength has purpose.

One who does not lose their place is enduring.  
One who dies but does not perish is long-lived.

## Notes

**The four couplets, set on the chapter's own grammar.** *(2026-10-30, Shalom's call. `WORKLIST.md` T3-21.)* *(was: "is intelligent," "sees clearly," "knows they have enough is wealthy," "forces themselves onward has will," "does not lose their rootedness, endures," "has long life.")* Every line is ▢者▢ (*▢ zhě ▢* — "one who ▢ is ▢"). Six predicates are one bare word, 智 (*zhì* — clever), 明 (*míng* — clear-seeing), 強 (*qiáng* — strong), 富 (*fù* — wealthy), 久 (*jiǔ* — lasting) and 壽 (*shòu* — long-lived), and two add 有 (*yǒu* — has): 有力 (*yǒu lì* — has strength) and 有志 (*yǒu zhì* — has intent). The English now does the same: *is* + one word for the six, *has* only for the two.

**Couplets 1 and 2 rank; couplets 3 and 4 praise.** The first two set 人 (*rén* — others) against 自 (*zì* — oneself), and Wang Bi ranks them in so many words: 用其智於人，未若用其智於己也 — *"using your cleverness on others is not as good as using it on yourself."* Couplets 3 and 4 drop the marker, and he praises both lines of each: 知足自不失，故富也 — *"one who knows enough does not lose themselves, and so is rich"*; 勤能行之，其志必獲 — *"one who keeps at it diligently surely attains their intent."* So the two lines of couplet 3 stand as equals, not as a contrast.

**Line by line.** 智 → *clever*, the lesser rung, as 智 reads *cleverness* at 18 and 19 (`WORKLIST.md` T2-2 still owes the character). 明 → *is clear-seeing*, a declared flexion in `glossary/ming-明.md`. 知足 keeps *knows they have enough*: *knows enough* is heard as knowledge, and *sufficiency* is ruled out (`glossary/zhizu-知足.md`). 富 → *wealthy*, as it has read since the first draft. 強行 → *walks with strength*: the Shuowen makes 行 (*xíng* — to go) 人之步趨也 (*"a person walking and hurrying"*), the 強 of line 4's *is strong* carries into the going as the Chinese reuses it, and no 自 (*zì* — oneself) stands in the line, so no *themselves*. 志 → *purpose*: the Shuowen's 意也 (*"intent"*), where the heart is headed, and here a good thing to have, unlike chapter 3's *ambition*. 所 (*suǒ* — place) → *place*: nothing in the line is a root, and Wang Bi reads 不失其所，必獲久長 (*"not losing their place, they surely last long"*). 久 → *is enduring*, as the bare 久 reads at chapter 7. 壽 → *is long-lived*, since no 有 stands in the line.
