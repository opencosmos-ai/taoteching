# Chapter 15

## Translation

The ancients who were masterful in service  
were faint, subtle, dark, and unobstructed,  
so deep they could not be read.  
Precisely because they could not be read,  
I force a likeness:

Careful, like wading across a river in winter.  
Hesitant, like someone wary of the neighbors on all four sides.  
Dignified, like a guest.  
Loosening, like ice about to melt.  
Solid, like uncarved wood.  
Open, like a valley.  
Mixed, like muddy water.

Who can be muddy, and by stilling it slowly come clear?  
Who can be at rest, and by moving it slowly come to life?

Those who keep the Tao safe like this have no wish to be full.  
Precisely because they are never full,  
they can stay covered, and never newly made.

## Notes

**猶兮若畏四鄰 → "Hesitant, like someone wary of the neighbors on all four sides."** *(2026-08-31, Pass C, `WORKLIST.md` T2-11.)* *(was: "Watchful, aware of danger from all sides.")* **The danger was invented.** There is no 殆 (*dài* — danger) in this chapter and no threat in the Chinese, so no check could ever have seen it; it surfaced only because `--english "danger"` had to be run before locking that word elsewhere.

**河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) supplies what the line is actually about:** 其進退猶猶如拘制，若人犯法**畏四鄰知之**也 — *"their advancing and retreating is hesitant as if held back, like someone who has broken the law **fearing the four neighbours will find out**."* **Not peril from every side — the fear of being seen.** 鄰 (*lín*) is a neighbour, and 四鄰 is the ordinary phrase for the households around you.

**畏 (*wèi*) flexes here, and it is worth marking.** It reads **fear** in six other lines (17, 20, 53, 72, 74 ×2), always fear *of a thing* — authority, death, what people fear. Here 河上公's gloss is 畏四鄰**知之**, fear *that they will find out*: social exposure, not a threat. *"Fearing the neighbors"* would name them as the danger; *"wary of"* keeps the watching, which is the chapter's whole register. 畏 has no glossary entry yet, and this is the line that will decide its shape.


**The chapter was reworked whole on 2026-09-06** *(Pass D)*, because its closing line had stopped meaning anything. What follows is what changed and why.

**故能蔽而不新成 → "they can stay covered, and never newly made."** *(was: "They can become weathered, and have no interest in appearing new.")* **We had been reading 敝 (*bì* — worn out) where our base text prints 蔽 (*bì* — to cover, to screen).** Same sound, different character. 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) prints 蔽 and glosses it 蔽者**匿光榮**也 — *"蔽 means **hiding one's radiance and glory**"* — and glosses the second half 新成者**貴功名** — *"the newly-completed means **prizing merit and fame**."* The line is about staying out of sight, not about patina.

**The 說文解字 (*Shuōwén Jiězì*, c. 100 CE) settles what kind of covering this is, and it is not a person hiding.** 蔽 is built on 艸 (*cǎo* — grass, growing plants): 蔽蔽，**小艸**也。从艸敝聲 — *"蔽蔙: **small grasses**. From 艸, with 敝 as the phonetic."* The covering is **vegetation** — undergrowth, foliage, shade. Its twin 敝 is built on 㡀 (*bì* — cloth in tatters) and 攴 (*pū* — to strike): 帗也。一曰**敗衣** — *"a cloth; one definition says **worn-out clothing**."* **One is plant cover, the other is a rag.** That the two share a phonetic is exactly why manuscripts confuse them, and it is why our base's choice of 蔽 has to be honoured rather than quietly read as 敝.

**This also answers the motive question, by dissolving it.** 能 (*néng*) is **can**, a capacity, not a wish — the line does not say these people *want* to be hidden, and reading it that way puts a strategy into a text that has none. 盈 is 滿**器**也 — *"a full **vessel**"*, on the 皿 (*mǐn* — dish, container) radical. A full vessel is a finished vessel, and a finished thing is a thing there is something to say about. Someone who never fills up has nothing that needs showing, so **being overlooked costs them nothing.** That is a capacity, not a virtue, and the chapter recommends no concealment anywhere in its seven similes.

**新 (*xīn*) is an axe, and that is why this line sits six lines below 樸.** 說文: 取木也。从**斤**新聲 — *"**to take wood**. From 斤 (*jīn* — an axe)."* The *new* sense is derived from freshly-cut timber. 樸 (*pǔ* — uncarved wood) is wood the axe has not finished with; 新成 is wood it has. **The etymology is not the reading** — 新 plainly means *new* by the Warring States, and we render it so — but it explains why the chapter's last image answers its middle one.

**The strongest evidence against this reading, and why it does not win.** Ch 22 has 敝則新 (*bì zé xīn* — "worn out, then new"), which we render *"Be worn out, and be renewed"* — the worn-out character paired with 新, looking very like ch 15's formula. But our base prints **敝** at ch 22 and **蔽** at ch 15; the text distinguishes them, and the worn-out character was available. And under the wear reading the two chapters collide: worn-out-becomes-new is the good outcome at 22, worn-out-and-not-new the good outcome at 15. Under the covered reading there is no clash.

**保此道者 → "Those who guard the Tao like this."** *(was: "One who holds the Tao.")* 此 (*cǐ* — this) is **anaphoric** — it points back at the two 孰能 (*shú néng* — who can) questions just asked, and 河上公 expands the phrase to 保**此徐生**之道, *"guards this Tao of gradually bringing to life."* English *"this Tao"* would partition instead of point, implying a second Tao somewhere. Attaching the pointer to the manner keeps the reach and loses the implication. 保 (*bǎo*) also moves from "holds" to **"guard"**, matching ch 9's 不可長保.

**不可識 (*bù kě shí*) appears twice, in adjacent lines, and had two Englishes** — *"fathomed"* then *"known"*. Now **"could not be read"** both times. This is `DISCOVERIES.md` §6's fault in miniature.

**An intermediate rendering, *"could not be made out,"* was retired within a day, and the reason is worth keeping.** It read as slang, and worse, it **invented a parallel**: *made out* in lines 3 and 4 chimed with *newly made* in the last line, but 識 (*shí*) and 新成 (*xīn chéng*) share no character and no root. **Where the Chinese does not repeat, the English must not either** — the mirror of `DISCOVERIES.md` §6, and nothing in the harness looks for it. `--formulas` finds a flattened repetition; an invented one is invisible. `WORKLIST.md` T5-14.

**識 is the speech-side knowing, and four candidates were tested against the whole manuscript before *read* won.** 說文: 常也。一曰**知**也。从**言**戠聲 — *"constant; one definition says **to know**. From 言 (*yán* — speech)."* Built on the speech radical, 識 is telling **what a thing is** — identification, not sensation.

- **"named" is the one to refuse hardest.** 名 (*míng* — name) runs 21 lines across 9 chapters, and **ch 14, the adjacent chapter, has 繩繩兮不可名 — *"it cannot be named."*** 不可名 and 不可識 are the same grammar one chapter apart, on **different characters**. An English that read *"could not be named"* here would assert a formula the text does not have — the invented-repetition fault (`WORKLIST.md` T5-14) in a far worse place than the one it replaced.
- **"defined"** is free of collisions but wrong in register: you define a term, not a person, and it turns the ancients into concepts. Abstraction drift, per the overlay watchlist.
- **"clearly perceived"** fails twice — 明 (*míng*) is locked to *clear-seeing / clarity* across twelve chapters, and 清 (*qīng*) takes *clear* four lines below in *"come clear"* — and *perceive* is sensory where 識 is not.
- **"known"** is 說文-licensed and has no local clash, since 知 (*zhī*) is absent from ch 15. But **"know" carries 58 English lines**, nearly all of them 知, and 識 appears in only two chapters. Handing the book's most-worked verb to a rare character muddies the reverse direction for no gain.

**"read" is free** — the only two hits on a whole-manuscript reverse search are false positives (*thread*, *already*) — plain, and exactly 識's job: telling what something is. It is metaphorical applied to people, but it is standard English rather than idiom, which *"made out"* was not.

**夫唯 (*fū wéi*) likewise appears twice** — 夫唯不可識 and 夫唯不盈 — and had *"Because they cannot be known"* against *"Because they don't seek to become full."* Both are now **"Precisely because"**, and the invented *seek* is gone: 不盈 is simply *not full*.

**靜 and 安 had been swapped.** 孰能濁以**靜**之徐清 had 靜 (*jìng* — stillness) as *"sit"*, while 孰能**安**以動之徐生 gave *"still"* to 安 (*ān* — at rest). 靜 is **stillness** in seven other chapters. The two questions are a strict grammatical mirror — 孰能 [state] 以 [action] 之 徐 [result] — and *"move in ways that gradually stir life"* padded the second out of parallel with the first. Now: *"by stilling it slowly come clear"* / *"by moving it slowly come to life."* 生 (*shēng*) lands as **come to life**, not "stir life."

**涉 (*shè*) is 氵 (water) + 步 (*bù* — a pair of footprints): walking *through* water.** *"Crossing a frozen river"* let the crossing be made on ice or a bridge, which removes the reason for the caution — the danger is the cold water and the untested footing, and the whole simile depends on it. *"Fording"* carried that but reads as arcane; **"wading across"** keeps the legs in the river and supplies the direction English wants, which bare *"wading a river"* leaves open. *(Shalom's calls, 2026-09-06.)*

**Three smaller restorations in the similes.** 將 (*jiāng* — about to) had been dropped from 冰之將釋: the ice is **about to** melt, and the not-yet is the point in a chapter of not-yet things. 涉 (*shè*) is to **ford** or wade, so 冬涉川 is crossing a river *in winter*, not crossing a *frozen* river — the danger is the cold water, not thin ice. And all seven similes now carry **"like"**, matching 若 (*ruò*), which appears in all seven; *"as if wary of"* had broken the series at the second line.

**微妙玄通 → "faint, subtle, dark, and unobstructed."** *(was: "subtle-fine and dark-penetrating —", then "connected through", then briefly "went everywhere".)* Four characters, four parallel English terms, no hyphenated compounds and no em-dash — the dash was flagged by `check_locks.py`.

**通 (*tōng*) is a road word, which is what the first attempts missed.** 說文: 達也。从**辵**甬聲 — *"to get through (達). From 辵 (*chuò* — the walking-and-road radical)."* It is **motion past obstruction**, not a static link; *connected* drifted toward the mechanistic register the watchlist names, and *went everywhere* kept the reach but dropped the barrier, describing the result rather than the thing.

**通 shares both its radical and its definition with 道.** 說文 on 道: 所行道也。从**辵**从𩠐。**一達謂之道** — *"the road one walks. From 辵 and 首 (*shǒu* — head). **A single 達 — a single getting-through — is called a 道.**"* So 道's definition **contains** 達, and 通's definition **is** 達. They are siblings in one field: passage. **The honest limit:** 甬 is merely phonetic in 通, so the character was not built to evoke 道 and this is no pun. But the semantic kinship is real, and it means the last quality in this opening line already stands in the field the chapter's fourth block names outright at 保此道者.

**That kinship is why the passage sense had to be kept, and it decided the word.** The near candidates were all spoken for: **open** is 曠 seven lines below (*"Open, like a valley"*) plus 兌 at 10, 52 and 56; **clear** is 明 (locked, twelve chapters) and 清 four lines below; **unblocked** is 塞 at 52 and 56 (*"Block the openings"*); **far-reaching** takes *far* from 遠 at 25 and 65. *Obstruct* is free on a whole-manuscript reverse search.

**Why the Latinate register is accepted here but was refused for 識.** *Identified* was rejected because it imports a **wrong operation** — Latin *idem facere*, matching a thing against a known set — on top of its register. *Unobstructed* imports the **right** one: a road with nothing in it, which is precisely 辵 + 達. Register alone does not decide a word when the semantics are exact. It also keeps the line's four items grammatically parallel, as the Chinese is, where *went everywhere* put a verb phrase after three adjectives. **The one risk** is that English *unobstructed* often describes what one can see *through*, which would contradict 深不可識 in the very next line; the *dark* immediately before it blocks that reading.

**A thread worth pulling later: the book is full of road-words.** Besides 道 and 通, the 辵 radical carries 遠 (*yuǎn* — far), 還 (*huán* — return), 逝 (*shì* — pass away), 過 (*guò* — pass over), 隨 (*suí* — follow), 迎 (*yíng* — meet), 迷 (*mí* — go astray), 進 (*jìn* — advance), 遇 (*yù* — encounter), 遂, 遺 and 達. Not yet a note in `notes/reading.md`; recorded here so it is not lost.

**士 (*shì*) → "in service," settled 2026-09-07 across ch 15, 41 and 68** *(Shalom's call; the full argument is in ch 41's notes).* 古之善為士者 opens this chapter; 善為士者 opens ch 68; 上士 / 中士 / 下士 run through ch 41. The manuscript currently renders 士 as **"masters"** here, **"student"** at 41, and **"warrior"** at 68 — three words for one character, and ch 68's *"The master warrior is not martial"* cancels itself. A 士 is a man in service at court, the lowest rank of the nobility, which fits this chapter's deferential and unreadable figure exactly. 說文 gives 士，事也 — *one who handles affairs* — so *student* was never available; *officer* was declined as bureaucratic. **Note the seam:** 士 named a male class, and the English is deliberately neutral.

**古之善為▢者 is shared with ch 65**, which reads *"The ancients who were masterful at the Tao."* The frame now matches; only the object differs, as in the Chinese. *(`--formulas 15`.)*

**Not checked, and worth checking: whether the opening character is contested.** Guodian (~300 BCE) carries this chapter complete, and `sources/variants.yaml` records no forks for it, which means nobody has looked rather than that none exist. 河上公 glosses 士 as 謂得道之君也, *"this refers to a ruler who has attained the Tao"* — importing 道 to make 士 work, which is the kind of strain that sometimes marks a variant underneath. Logged as an open question, not as a claim.

**保此道者 → "Those who keep the Tao safe like this."** *(2026-09-08, Pass E, `WORKLIST.md` T2-19 — the holding family.)* *(was: "Those who guard the Tao like this.")* 保 (*bǎo*) is 說文 **養也**, *"to rear"* — 人 (*rén* — a person) beside an abbreviated 孚 (*fú*), a hand over a child — and it is locked to *keep safe* (`glossary/bao-保.md`). **The earlier move to *guard* was argued from ch 9's literal gloss table, not its verse**, which at the time rendered 保 nowhere and gave *guard* to **守** (莫之能守). Two characters, one English, in the chapter cited as the match. *(Ch 9 was rebuilt on 2026-09-20 and now renders 保 as bare* **keep** *— a declared flexion, and the lock's first real test there.)* 此 stays anaphoric, pointing back at the two 孰能 questions — 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*): 保**此徐生**之道, *"keeps safe this Tao of gradually bringing to life."*

**微 (*wēi*) — the thread from ch 14.** *(Shalom's call, 2026-09-10 — `WORKLIST.md` T2-52.)* 微妙玄通 renders **微** as *faint*. **Chapter 14 is where the book names this character**: 搏之不得，名曰微, which our English gives as *"its name is the intangible."* 說文解字 has 微 as 隱行也, *"to move concealed,"* built on 彳 (*chì* — the step radical) — something going by without being caught. 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) glosses ch 14's line 無**形**曰微, *"formless is called 微."* Ch 14 renders the meaning rather than the word for the sake of that chapter's legibility, so the link is carried in the notes: *the intangible* there and *faint* here are one character. Also ch 36 (是謂微明) and ch 64 (其微易散).
