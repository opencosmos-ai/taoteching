# Chapter 78

## Translation

In all the world nothing is softer and more yielding than water,  
yet at attacking the hard and the strong nothing overcomes it,  
for nothing can take its place.

The yielding overcomes the strong.  
The soft overcomes the unbending.  
No one in the world fails to know this,  
yet no one can follow it.

So the sage says:  
whoever takes on the filth of the state  
is the keeper of the soil and the grain.  
Whoever takes on the ill omen of the state  
is the ruler of the world.

Words on the mark seem like their opposite.

## Notes

**柔之勝剛 → "The soft overcomes the unbending."** *(2026-10-14, Shalom's call. Was: "the hard.")* This chapter held *hard* for two characters a few lines apart: 攻堅強 (*"attacking the hard and the strong"*, 堅) and 柔之勝剛 (剛). 堅 keeps *hard*. `glossary/ruo-弱.md`.

- 以其無以易之 — 易 is read as *exchange / replace*, not *easy*. 河上公 marks the tone explicitly (易入聲) and glosses 無以易於水, "nothing to substitute for water." 易 means *easy* everywhere else in the book.
- 社稷主 — 社 (altar of the soil) + 稷 (millet, the grain god) is a metonym for the state, but 國 already stands in the same line. Rendered for the substance, "the keeper of the soil and the grain," not the furniture. See notes/translation.md.
- 莫能行 held to Ch 70's *follow*, so the inversion of 天下莫能知，莫能行 is audible.
