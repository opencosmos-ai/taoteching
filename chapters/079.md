# Chapter 79

## Translation

Settle a great grudge  
and some grudge always remains.  
How could that count as masterful?

So the sage grasps the left half of the tally  
and never asks for the other.

Those with integrity keep the tally.  
Those without integrity keep score.

The way of nature plays no favorites.  
It stays with those who are masterful.

## Notes

**餘怨 (*yú yuàn*) is the *unhealed* remainder, not the small one.** 餘 is 食 (food) with 余 — surplus, what is still on the table — and it answers whether the transaction closed, not how much is left. 王弼 says what the remainder is: 以致大怨已至，而德和之，**其傷不復**，故有餘怨也, *"a great grudge has already arrived, and one uses integrity to compose it — the wound does not heal — and so there is leftover grudge."* 復 (*fù*) is return-to-whole, ch 16's word. A drafted *"some small grudge"* was rejected on this: the next line is a dismissal, and it only earns itself if the residue is disqualifying rather than minor. It also costs the thread to ch 77, where 有餘 is the surplus nature always drains. The text's own repetition — 大**怨** … 餘**怨** — is left bare in English for the same reason.

**和 (*hé* — harmonize) → "settle."** The chapter runs one continuous accounting metaphor — grudge, surplus, tally, score — and *settle* is the English verb that sits inside it. "Reconcile" is more literal and more abstract; it steps outside the image the chapter is built from.

**善 (*shàn*) reads *masterful* in both its slots** — 為善 and 善人 — per the lock. Rendering either as *good* rebuilds the moralized chapter the lock exists to prevent. 王弼 licenses the skill reading directly: 左契防怨之所由生也, *"the left tally guards against that from which grudges arise"* — the masterful move is prevention, not repair.

**徹 (*chè*) → "keep score," not "taxes."** 王弼 and 河上公 converge independently: 徹司人之過也, *"徹 is overseeing people's faults"*; 無德之君背其契言司人所失, *"the ruler without integrity turns their back on the tally and oversees what people fall short in."* The graph agrees — 彳 (a step) with 攴 (a hand holding a stick): *striking through, seeing through.* The Zhou tithe sense of 徹 is real elsewhere; neither commentator reaches for it here.

**契 (*qì*) is a split stick, and the verse has to say so.** The graph is 丯 (notch marks) with 刀 (*dāo* — a knife): terms notched across bamboo or wood, then split lengthwise, one half to each party. Neither half proves anything alone — the claim becomes real only when the halves are brought together and matched. 河上公 describes the procedure: 刻契**合符**以為信也, *"they notched a tally, matched the halves, and that served as trust."* So *"holds their half of the tally / and never asks for the other"* teaches the object inside the line, which lets the bare word **tally** carry line six without a footnote.

**左 (*zuǒ* — left) is dropped from the verse and kept here.** It encoded which party held which half, and we cannot say which: the source table's literal gloss calls 左契 the *debtor's* tally, which cannot be right — 而不責於人 (*ér bù zé yú rén*, and does not press claims on people) only means something if the sage is the one who *could* collect, and 責 is 朿 (a thorn) over 貝 (a cowrie), to dun for payment. Neither 王弼 nor 河上公 states which half is which. So *left* raises a question in English that the poem cannot answer, on a point we hold only by inference. The editorial call stands; the word does not.

**執 (*zhí*) → *holds*, 司 (*sī*) → *keep*.** 執 is a hand grasping; 司 is administering an office, and it governs both 契 and 徹. The English keeps the two verbs apart so the parallel in lines six and seven belongs to 司 alone.

**天道 (*tiān dào*) → "the way of nature,"** following ch 47 and ch 77. 天 is sky and the natural, never "Heaven" — see `glossary/tian-天.md`. **無親** (*wú qīn*) is literally *has no kin*; "plays no favorites" is what it means in use.

**常與善人 is not a reward.** 河上公 identifies the 善人 of the last line with the tally-holder of line four: 唯與善人則與司契者也, *"it is only with the masterful person, that is, with the one who keeps the tally."* The line points back into the poem, not out to a moral economy.

**執左契 → "grasps the left half of the tally," and 左 comes back.** *(2026-09-08, Pass E, `WORKLIST.md` T2-19 — the holding family.)* The line had read *"the sage holds their half of the tally,"* dropping 左 (*zuǒ* — left) — the character Pass D had just restored at ch 31. 執 (*zhí*) is locked to *grasp* (`glossary/zhi-執.md`), and this is one of the two places the book uses it in its plain legal sense: a 契 (*qì* — 說文 大約也, *"a great covenant"*) split in two, the halves matched to prove a debt. 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*): 古者聖人執左契合符信也，無文書法律，刻契合符以為信也 — *"in antiquity the sage held the left tally and matched the token of trust; there were no documents or statutes, so they carved tallies and matched them to make trust."*

**Whose half the left half was is left open, deliberately.** The traditions disagree — on one reading the sage holds the creditor's half and declines to press it, on the other they hold the debtor's half and have no claim to press. 而不責於人 is pointed under the first and merely descriptive under the second. 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) says only 左契防怨之所由生也, *"the left tally prevents what gives rise to grudges."* The English renders the character and leaves the side to the reader.
