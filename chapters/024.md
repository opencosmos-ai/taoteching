# Chapter 24

## Translation

Those who stand on tiptoe do not stand.  
Those who take long strides get nowhere.

Those who show themselves off do not see clearly.  
Those who insist they are right convince no one.  
Those who claim the work have none.  
Those who boast do not endure.

Measured by the Tao, these are called leftover food and wasted motion.  
Living things recoil from them.  
Those who are with the Tao do not dwell among them.

## Notes

**自矜者不長 → "Those who boast do not endure."** *(2026-10-14, Shalom's call. Was: "Those who make themselves great.")* The mirror of ch 22, changed with it; see that chapter's note.

*Rewritten 2026-08-28. Ch 24 is the exact negative of ch 22's 不自X (*bù zì X* — "does not X themselves") stanza, which was rebuilt 2026-08-27; the two had shared no English at all.*

**The mirror is in the four verbs, not in matching predicates.** 見 (*jiàn* — to display) · 是 (*shì* — to affirm as right) · 伐 (*fá* — to claim) · 矜 (*jīn* — to magnify oneself). Ch 22 has 不自X，故Y (*"does not X themselves, therefore Y"*); ch 24 has 自X者不Y (*"one who X's themselves does not Y"*). **The stanza's logic is: grasp at a thing and lose it.**

**The subjects genuinely differ between the two chapters, and an earlier draft of this repair forced them together with a colon.** In ch 22 what becomes evident is *their rightness* — 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) supplies it: 不自是則其**是**彰也 (*bù zì shì zé qí shì zhāng yě* — "not affirming themselves, then their rightness is made evident"). In ch 24 what fails to appear is **the person**: 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) reads 衆共蔽之，使不得彰明 (*zhòng gòng bì zhī, shǐ bù dé zhāng míng* — "the crowd together obscures them, so they cannot be made evident"). Hence *"convince no one"* rather than a possessive.

***"Go unseen" was tried and rejected, because in this book it is praise.*** Ch 17's best rulers are barely known, ch 41 has 道隱無名 (*dào yǐn wú míng* — "the Tao is hidden and nameless"), ch 70's sage wears coarse cloth over jade. What this person grasps at is recognition, so the loss must read as failure. *(Caught by Shalom.)*

**跨 (*kuà*) is an overstretched step, not a stride.** 足 (*zú* — foot) beside 夸 (*kuā* — to boast): a step so long it straddles. *"Those who stride do not walk"* left a near-synonym of *walk* and the paradox evaporated. 河上公 supplies the fix by glossing 不行 (*bù xíng*) as 使不得行 (*shǐ bù dé xíng* — "so they cannot get anywhere"). 王弼 states the principle for both opening lines: 物尚進則失安 (*wù shàng jìn zé shī ān* — "a thing that presses forward loses its footing").

**餘食贅行 → "leftover food and wasted motion," and the commentators diverge here.** 贅 (*zhuì*) is 敖 over 貝 (*bèi* — a cowrie shell); 說文 glosses it 以物質錢也 (*yǐ wù zhì qián yě* — "to pawn a thing for money"), hence **superfluous, an appendage**. 王弼 reads it physically — 肬贅 (*yóu zhuì* — a wart) — and 餘食 as 盛饌之餘也，本雖美，更可薉也 (*shèng zhuàn zhī yú yě, běn suī měi, gèng kě huì yě* — "the leftovers of a rich feast: fine to begin with, they turn foul"). 河上公 reads it morally: 贅，貪也 (*zhuì, tān yě* — "贅 means greed"). **Both are glosses, not the text**, and neither word is rendered here: *wasted motion* keeps 贅's plain sense and ties the summary back to the chapter's own opening, since tiptoe and overstretched steps *are* superfluous motion. 河上公's moral reading is declined on the naturalistic razor.

**物 → "living things," and 或 is dropped.** 物 (*wù*) cannot be inert here — 河上公: 物無有不**畏**惡也 (*wù wú yǒu bù wèi wù yě* — "there is nothing that does not **fear** and detest them"), and rocks do not fear. *The material world* was an import and ch 31's *all creatures* narrowed too far. 惡 read *wù* is aversion, not hatred, so **recoil**. 或 (*huò* — "perhaps, some") is a hedge we drop deliberately, on 河上公's near-universal reading.

**The last two lines are verbatim in ch 31 and were swept to match**, closing two `repeated-formula` warnings. 有道者 (*yǒu dào zhě* — "one who has the Tao") already read *"one who holds the Tao"* at ch 77.

**有道者 → "those who are with the Tao," and neither *have* nor *hold* survived.** 有 (*yǒu*) is 又 (*yòu* — a hand) over 肉 (*ròu* — flesh), a hand holding meat — but its force in 有道 is **existential, not possessive**: ch 46's 天下有道 (*tiān xià yǒu dào*) is *"there is Tao in the world,"* not *"the world owns the Tao."* Two things ruled out the obvious renderings. **The book refuses 有 as a way of relating** — 生而不有 (*shēng ér bù yǒu* — "bears and does not possess") stands in ch 2, 10 and 51 — so *have the Tao* sits one syllable from a contradiction. And **hold** was already doing duty for 守 (*shǒu* — hold fast to), 執 (*zhí* — grasp) and 保 (*bǎo* — keep) in four other chapters, including ch 15's 保此道者, which read *"one who holds the Tao"* for a different character. *With* claims nothing, and it survives ch 23's mutuality: 同於道者，道亦樂得之 (*tóng yú dào zhě, dào yì lè dé zhī* — "those who merge with the Tao, the Tao gladly receives them"). Swept to ch 31 and ch 77.

***"Dwell in the Tao" was proposed and refused on the hymn test*** — it is Psalm 23 and John 15 cadence — **and on placement**: 處 (*chǔ*) is the dwelling word and it belongs to the second clause, so moving *dwell* to 有's slot would spend the right word in the wrong place. *"Where there is Tao, these have no place"* was the strongest alternative and was declined because it drops 者 (*zhě* — "the one who"), and the chapter has spent eight lines contrasting two kinds of **person**. 河上公 keeps the person: 言有道之人不居其國也.

**故 (*gù* — therefore) is deliberately not rendered.** English carries the inference by adjacency, and the three closing lines read better as three flat verdicts than as a chain. The omission also travels: in ch 24 this is the final line, where *so* sums up what needs no summing; in ch 31 the same sentence sits third, where *so* would mark an inference the reader has not yet been given.
