# Chapter 77

## Translation

The way of nature:  
is it not like stringing a bow?  
The high is pressed down, the low is lifted.  
What is too much is drawn off. What is too little is filled in.

The way of nature  
takes from where there is too much  
and mends where there is too little.  
The way of people is not like this.  
It takes from those who have too little  
and offers it up to those who have too much.

Who has more than enough,  
and offers it up to the world?  
Only those who are with the Tao.

Therefore the sage acts yet relies on nothing.  
The work is done, and they do not stay in it.  
They have no wish to appear exceptional.

## Notes

**張弓 — stringing the bow, not drawing it.** 河上公 glosses 言張弓和調之如是乃可用 — "stringing the bow and tuning it into balance, thus, and then it can be used." 和調 (*hé tiáo* — harmonize and tune) is the bowyer's act, not the archer's: pressing the upper limb down and lifting the lower is how a string is seated. 王弼 names the mechanism as 均 (*jūn* — to make even) — 不欲示其賢以均天下, "does not display their 賢, so as to level the world." Aim would make the chapter about targeting; bracing makes it about tension brought into balance, which is what the four following lines describe.

**The bow is 弱's own graph.** 弱 (*ruò* — yielding) is two 弓 (*gōng* — bow). 河上公's gloss on these lines reads 夫抑高舉下，損強益弱，天之道也 — "pressing down the high and raising the low, diminishing the strong and increasing the yielding: this is the way of nature." The chapter that gave the 弱 lock its argument is the chapter whose central image is the bow itself.

**奉 is tribute.** 奉 (*fèng*) is 廾 (two raised hands) holding an offering up — the verb for giving to a superior. 河上公: 世俗之人損貧以奉富，奪弱以益強 — "worldly people diminish the poor to offer up to the rich, seize from the yielding to increase the strong." Rendered *offer up* in both occurrences so the reversal in the third block lands on the same verb. This is Ch 75's charge about taxes, given a cosmology.

**補 is patching cloth** — 衤 (garment) + 甫. Rendered *mends*, not "supplements."

**天之道 → the way of nature**, per `glossary/tian-天.md`; consistent with ch 9, 73, and the 天 entry's own reading of this chapter.

**為而不恃 → acts yet relies on nothing.** 恃 (*shì*) is 忄 (heart) + 寺 — **the heart putting its weight on something**; *Shuowen*: 恃，賴也 (*"恃 is 賴, to rely"*). Ch 2, 10 and 51 read *presuming*, an inference from the graph rather than a rendering of it; Shalom rejected it here on 2026-08-26 and **all four were swept to one formula the same day**, along with ch 34's positive 恃. *Lean on* is the truer picture but 倚 (*yǐ*) already holds it at ch 58. Full argument in `glossary/shi-恃.md`.

**功成而不處 → the work is done, and they do not stay in it**, matching ch 2's 功成而弗居. 處 (*chù*) is **to dwell, to occupy a place** — 河上公: 功成事就不處其位, *"the work done, they do not occupy the position."* Not "take credit": that is the *next* clause's business (其不欲見賢), and collapsing the two makes the coda redundant. 功成 is impersonal — the work completes — not "they complete their work".

**其不欲見賢** — 見 stands for 現 (*xiàn* — to show). 賢 threads to ch 3's 不尚賢 ("the exceptional").

**Transcription lacuna, not a variant.** The Siku 王弼 text carries □ where 補 stands twice (…不足者□之 / …而□不足). 河上公 has 補/𥙷 in both places and our base text reads 補. This is damage in the 1782 transcription, not a witness fork; nothing recorded in `sources/variants.yaml`.
